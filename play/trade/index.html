Hawkeye Play · Chapter 1 · Political · Economic · Legal

Trade or tariff

Two countries trade every year. Each year, each can keep its market open or put up a tariff. What happens depends on whether they expect to meet again.

The rules

Each round, both countries choose at the same time. Points are made up. Both open: 3 each. Both tariff: 1 each. One tariffs while the other stays open: the one with the tariff gets 5 and the open one gets 0. This is the payoff pattern of the prisoner’s dilemma.

What the model typically shows

Takeaway

In a single round of this game, putting up a tariff scores more whatever the other side does. When the same two sides expect to meet many times, strategies based on reciprocity can start cooperating, do well against a wide range of other strategies, and resist being displaced once they are common.

Sources for the model:

  • Axelrod, R. & Hamilton, W. D. (1981). The Evolution of Cooperation. Science 211(4489), 1390–1396 (opens in a new tab)
  • Axelrod, R. (1984). The Evolution of Cooperation. New York: Basic Books.

What this model leaves out

  • Real trade involves many goods, many countries and domestic politics, not one yes-or-no choice.
  • Real countries can talk, sign agreements and go to dispute panels; here they only see each other’s last move.
  • The points are invented and the same for both sides.
