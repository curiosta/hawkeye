Hawkeye Play

How economies hold together

Three short games about trading, sharing and borrowing. No economics background needed: every word is explained as you go.

Hawkeye Play is a set of small games about how economies hold together. An economy is all the making, buying, selling, working, saving and borrowing that people, businesses and governments do. You don’t need to know any economics to play. Every chapter explains its words the first time they come up.

How each chapter works

  1. Read the situation. A short everyday story (two neighbouring farmers, a village pasture, a family loan) sets the scene, and a box explains every word you will meet.
  2. Play a few rounds yourself. You make a choice. The page then tells you what happened and why.
  3. Watch the same rules run many times. The computer plays out many rounds in a moment, so you can see where the rules lead over time.
  4. Change one setting. Move a slider and see how the result changes.
  5. Read what it means. Each chapter ends with its main lesson in plain words, what it means in real life, and what the game leaves out.

What is a model?

Each game is a model: a deliberately simple version of a real situation, kept small so you can see how one rule leads to a result. Real life has far more going on. The numbers in the games are made up. They show what the model does under the settings you pick. They do not say which setting is the right one.

Reading the charts

Line charts show how something changes over time. Time runs from left to right along the bottom, and the amount goes up the side. Bar charts compare totals: a longer bar means more points. Under every chart, Data table opens the same numbers as a table, which also works with screen readers.

More chapters are planned. Each one reports what its model does; none recommends a policy.

The chapters

Play them in any order. Each one starts from scratch and explains its own words.

  • Chapter 1 · Political · Economic · Legal

    Trade or tariff

    Two countries sell goods to each other every year. Each year, each one decides: keep its market open, or put up a tariff (a tax on goods coming in from the other country). What happens depends on whether they expect to meet again.

    What you do: You play six rounds against a hidden strategy, then watch five strategies compete.

    Model: Repeated prisoner’s dilemma (Axelrod & Hamilton 1981; Axelrod 1984)

  • Chapter 3 · Environmental · Social · Legal

    The shared pasture

    Four herders share one village pasture. The grass grows back, but only so fast. Each season, each herder decides how much their animals eat: a modest share or a large one. How long does the grass last?

    What you do: You herd for ten seasons alongside three others, then watch thirty seasons play out.

    Model: Common-pool resources (Hardin 1968; Ostrom 1990)

  • Chapter 5 · Economic · Political

    Interest and growth

    A government owes money equal to 60% of everything its country produces in a year. Every year interest is due on that debt, and every year the economy grows by some amount. Does the debt get bigger or smaller compared with the economy?

    What you do: You set the budget for five years, then watch thirty years under the settings you choose.

    Model: Public debt dynamics (Escolano 2010; Blanchard 2019)
