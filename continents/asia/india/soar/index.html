A Aspirations

India has no single national vision law. The goal of a developed India by 2047, called Viksit Bharat, is stated in the Prime Minister’s speeches, Budget speeches and NITI Aayog meetings. Climate goals are stated in India’s commitments under the Paris Agreement. The Aspirations below quote those texts, each labelled with its status.

A developed India by 2047

Political statement: Prime Minister’s Independence Day speech, 15 August 2022

Speaking of 'Panch Pran', the first vow is for the country to move ahead with a big resolve. And that big resolution is of a developed India; and now we should not settle for anything less than that. The Prime Minister asked: we must resolve to make India a developed nation in the next 25 years (Prime Minister’s Office, 15 August 2022 (opens in a new tab)). Panch Pran means five vows.

Nine Budget priorities for Viksit Bharat

Budget speech to Parliament, 23 July 2024

In the interim budget, we promised to present a detailed roadmap for our pursuit of 'Viksit Bharat'. In line with the strategy set out in the interim budget, this budget envisages sustained efforts on the following 9 priorities for generating ample opportunities for all. (Budget speech 2024-25 (opens in a new tab), paragraph 7).

The Centre and the States as “Team India”

NITI Aayog Governing Council, 9th meeting, 27 July 2024 (release of 26 July 2024)

Achieving the vision of a 'Viksit Bharat' by 2047 will require a collaborative approach between the Centre and State governments. (NITI Aayog, PIB 26 July 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Infrastructure for the 2047 vision

Economic Survey 2025-26 (January 2026)

Sustaining investment momentum while aligning infrastructure with efficiency, sustainability and competitiveness will continue to remain key towards supporting India's medium-term growth and its long-term vision of Viksit Bharat @2047. (Economic Survey 2025-26 (opens in a new tab), chapter 9, page 339).

Climate targets for 2030

Updated Nationally Determined Contribution under the Paris Agreement, approved by the Cabinet on 3 August 2022

As per the updated NDC, India now stands committed to reduce Emissions Intensity of its GDP by 45 percent by 2030, from 2005 level and achieve about 50 percent cumulative electric power installed capacity from non-fossil fuel-based energy resources by 2030. (Cabinet decision, PIB 3 August 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

Net zero by 2070

Long-term goal stated by the Government, 3 August 2022

The update is also a step towards achieving India's long term goal of reaching net-zero by 2070. (PIB, 3 August 2022 (opens in a new tab)). Net zero means that emissions are balanced by removals.

How this SOAR analysis works

For a country, the Aspirations are its official goal statements, quoted word for word. Where a country has no single national vision document, the page names the official documents it quotes instead.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
