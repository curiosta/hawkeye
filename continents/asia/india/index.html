T

Threats

External · harmful

Imported oil and critical minerals

  • 88.7% of crude oil imported. India imported 88.7% of the crude oil it used in 2025-26 (provisional figure), up from 84.4% in 2020-21. This share is measured against consumption (PPAC Ready Reckoner FY 2025-26 (opens in a new tab), Petroleum table, row 7).
  • Ten minerals fully imported. A Ministry of Mines committee found that India relied on imports for 100% of ten critical minerals in 2020, including lithium, cobalt and nickel. The report says India is 100% import dependent for certain elements (Ministry of Mines, Critical Minerals for India, June 2023 (opens in a new tab), Table 1).

The European Union’s carbon border charge

  • Full rules from 1 January 2026. The European Commission states: As of 1 January 2026, CBAM is applicable under its definitive regime. CBAM is the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism. It covers imports such as iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity, and introduces obligations for importers, including authorisation requirements, reporting obligations and the purchase and surrender of CBAM certificates (European Commission (opens in a new tab)).
  • EUR 82.32 a certificate in the third quarter of 2026. The German authority that runs CBAM in Germany lists certificate prices of EUR 75.36 for the first quarter of 2026 and EUR 82.32 for the third quarter. It says The purchase and repurchase of CBAM certificates will start in February 2027. (DEHSt, CBAM certificates (opens in a new tab)). No official figure for the cost to Indian exporters has been confirmed at source.

Hotter years

  • 2025 was the eighth warmest year. In 2025, all India annual mean land surface air temperature was 0.28°C higher than the 1991–2020 long-term average. This made 2025 the eighth warmest year since nationwide temperature records began in 1901. The warmest year on record was 2024, when temperatures across India were 0.65°C above the long-term average. (India Meteorological Department, 1 January 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

The northern border

  • “Stable yet sensitive”. The Ministry of Defence review of 2025 says: Situation along Northern borders remains stable yet sensitive. After the agreement on disengagement achieved in Depsang and Demchok in 2024, 2025 witnessed reduced deployment levels of PLA both opposite the Northern borders and in traditional training areas. (Ministry of Defence, Year End Review 2025 (opens in a new tab)). PLA is the Chinese People’s Liberation Army.
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Services exports and a European trade deal

  • US$387.5 billion of services exports. In FY25, services exports reached an all-time high of USD 387.5 billion, registering a robust 13.6 per cent (YoY) growth. The Economic Survey links this to Global Capability Centres, which are offices that foreign firms run in India for their own global work (Economic Survey 2025-26 (opens in a new tab), para 4.57, page 170).
  • A US$100 billion investment pledge. India’s Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement with Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland took effect on 1 October 2025. TEPA commits USD 100 billion in investments and 1 million direct jobs over 15 years, the first binding pledge of its kind in any Indian FTA. (PIB, 11 October 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Green hydrogen and solar factories

  • 5 million tonnes a year of green hydrogen. National Green Hydrogen Mission is expected to lead to development of 5 MMT Green Hydrogen production capacity per annum by 2030. MMT means million metric tonnes (PIB, 6 December 2023 (opens in a new tab)). This is a target.
  • ₹19,500 crore for solar panel factories. In September 2022, the Cabinet approved a second round of the incentive scheme for high-efficiency solar panels, with an outlay of Rs.19,500 crore for achieving manufacturing capacity of Giga Watt (GW) scale in High Efficiency Solar PV Modules (PIB, 21 September 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

Battery cell factories

  • 50 GWh of planned capacity. An incentive scheme was approved in May, 2021 with a total outlay of ₹18,100 crore to establish 50 GWh of domestic Advanced Chemistry Cell manufacturing capacity. Out of the total targeted 50 GWh capacity, 40 GWhhas been awarded to four beneficiary firms. The firms reported ₹3,237 crore of investment by 31 December 2025 (PIB, 6 February 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Few women in paid work

  • 40.0% of women in the labour force. Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR) in usual status (ps+ss) for persons of age 15 years and above observed as 59.3% during 2025 and remained stable compared to 2024. The same for male and female stood at 79.1% and 40.0%, respectively, in 2025. (PLFS Annual Report 2025, PIB 27 March 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social

Child stunting

  • 29.3% of young children stunted. Stunting (low height for age) among children under five years declined substantially from 35.5% to 29.3%, reflecting improvement in long-term nutritional outcomes. The figures are from the National Family Health Survey: 2019-21 and 2023-24 (NFHS-6, PIB 29 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Poverty

  • 11.28% multidimensionally poor in 2022-23. A NITI Aayog discussion paper reports a significant decline in multidimensional poverty in India from 29.17% in 2013-14 to 11.28% in 2022-23. It says 24.82 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty in last nine years. (PIB, 15 January 2024 (opens in a new tab)). Multidimensional poverty counts lack of health, education and living standards, not only income.

Also in PESTLE · Social

Over-used groundwater

  • 10.8% of areas over-exploited. In the 2025 groundwater assessment, the share of assessment units in the “over-exploited” group declined from 17.2% to 10.8% since 2017 (Ministry of Jal Shakti, PIB 6 August 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in GAP · Rural tap water, 2028

Rural work tied to farming

  • 57.7% of rural workers in agriculture. Rural employment is dominated by agricultural workers (57.7 per cent) and self-employment (62.8 per cent), with women workers showing relatively higher participation in them. (Economic Survey 2025-26 (opens in a new tab), para 12.9, page 471). The survey does not give the period for these figures.

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Digital public infrastructure

  • Over 24,162 crore UPI payments. UPI is the Unified Payments Interface, a national system for instant payments by phone. From FY 2016-17 to FY 2025-26: Annual transaction volume increased from 2 crore transactions to over 24,162 crore transactions. (PIB factsheet, 24 August 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 145.32 crore Aadhaar numbers. Aadhaar is India’s 12-digit digital identity number. The UIDAI dashboard shows Aadhaar Generated 145.32 Cr and 2,632.51 crore electronic identity checks (eKYC) (UIDAI Aadhaar Dashboard, updated 28 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological

A large working-age population

  • Nearly 65% of working age around 2030. According to the UN projections, India's demographic dividend is expected to peak around 2030, when nearly 65 per cent of the population will fall within the 15-59 years group. (Economic Survey 2025-26 (opens in a new tab), para 12.2, page 468). This is a projection.

Fast growth

  • Real GDP up 7.7% in 2025-26. The growth rate in Real GDP during 2025-26 is estimated at 7.7% as compared to 7.1 % in 2024-25. Real GDP is at constant prices. Nominal GDP, at current prices, was ₹346.36 lakh crore, up 8.9%. These are Provisional Estimates on the new base year 2022-23 (MoSPI, PIB 5 June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Half of power capacity from non-fossil sources

  • 51.93% non-fossil. On 31 December 2025, non-fossil sources made up 266.79 GW of India’s 513.73 GW of installed power capacity (Annexure). India has achieved a landmark in its energy transition journey by reaching 50% of its installed electricity capacity from non-fossil fuel sources in June, 2025 (Ministry of Power, PIB 16 March 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
