India TOWS

Pairs internal factors (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external ones (Opportunities, Threats) into four kinds of response.

  • SWOT
  • PESTLE
  • GAP
  • TOWS

Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that India has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Use digital public systems to grow services exports

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Digital public infrastructure · SWOT · Opportunities · Services exports and a European trade deal

      Use India’s existing digital public systems, including Unified Payments Interface and Aadhaar, to lower the cost of expanding services exports under trade deals such as TEPA. Official Indian programmes already support digital public infrastructure. This recommendation is about pairing that strength with the services-export opportunity, and learning from a documented case elsewhere.

      Analogous case. Estonia built a national digital identity and a secure public data exchange. The OECD writes that Estonia is a leader in e-government and has an innovative ICT sector (OECD Economic Surveys: Estonia 2024 (opens in a new tab), p. 16). The same survey says Estonia has prospered through participation in regional value chains, particularly in machinery and equipment, wood products and fast-growing exports of ICT services (p. 46).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India already runs digital public systems at a much larger scale, including over 24,162 crore Unified Payments Interface payments and 145.32 crore Aadhaar numbers (live SWOT). Those tools can cut the cost of expanding services exports, which reached US$387.5 billion in FY25. The analogy is limited because Estonia is a small, high-income member of the European Union. The OECD also notes that traditional sectors in Estonia lag in the use of digital technologies, and that a large gender pay gap persists (p. 16). India’s informal firms and many languages add friction that Estonia did not face at this scale.

    • Build more of the solar supply chain on existing non-fossil capacity

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Half of power capacity from non-fossil sources · SWOT · Opportunities · Green hydrogen and solar factories

      Use India’s non-fossil power capacity as the base for a deeper domestic solar manufacturing chain, from cells and modules toward earlier stages of the chain. Official Indian programmes already fund high-efficiency solar factories. This recommendation is a pairing with that opportunity, and a lesson from a documented build-out elsewhere.

      Analogous case. China treated solar manufacturing as a strategic industry and built domestic demand at the same time. The International Energy Agency reports: China has invested over USD 50 billion in new PV supply capacity – ten times more than Europe − and created as many as 300 000 manufacturing jobs across the solar PV value chain since 2011. Today, China’s share in all the manufacturing stages of solar panels (such as polysilicon, ingots, wafers, cells and modules) exceeds 80%. The IEA also writes: These policies have contributed to a cost decline more than 80%, helping solar PV to become the most affordable electricity generation technology in many parts of the world. (IEA, Special Report on Solar PV Global Supply Chains, revised August 2022 (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary). A 9 August 2022 corrigendum updates figure 1.8 on wafer sizes. It does not change this share or investment wording.

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India reached 51.93% non-fossil installed power capacity on 31 December 2025. The Cabinet approved Rs.19,500 crore for high-efficiency solar panel factories (live SWOT and documented responses). A deeper domestic chain can serve Indian demand and exports. The 51.93% figure is installed capacity, not the share of electricity generated. The analogy is limited by scale. China’s investment and manufacturing capacity are much larger, and the IEA warns that the level of geographical concentration in global supply chains also creates potential challenges that governments need to address. India starts against an established low-cost competitor.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Cut oil imports by electrifying road transport with non-fossil power

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Half of power capacity from non-fossil sources · SWOT · Threats · Imported oil and critical minerals

      Shift more road transport to electric vehicles that run on non-fossil power, so that oil import dependence can fall as the vehicle fleet changes. Official Indian programmes already support electric mobility and non-fossil capacity. This recommendation pairs that strength with the oil-import threat, and uses a documented outcome from other large markets.

      Analogous case. The IEA records a realised drop in oil use from electric vehicles. In 2025, the global fleet of EVs avoided the consumption of around 1.7 million barrels of oil per day (mb/d), primarily in countries that have implemented fuel economy and CO2 standards, such as China and the European Union. In China, EVs still accounted for nearly 55% of all car sales and Chinese automakers supplied 60% of global electric car sales in 2025. (IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026 (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary). The 2025 outlook also records a realised earlier step: oil displacement growing by 30% to over 1.3 mb/d in 2024 (IEA, Global EV Outlook 2025 (opens in a new tab), Outlook for energy demand).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India already has a large and rising share of non-fossil installed capacity, so more electric vehicles can run on cleaner power than in a coal-heavy system. The live threat is 88.7% crude-oil import dependence. The main limit is that 51.93% is a capacity share, not a generation share, and charging still draws on the actual mix of the grid. India also imports lithium, cobalt and nickel in full. A large shift to electric vehicles will move part of the import risk from oil to those minerals unless domestic battery materials catch up.

    • Cut the carbon in export metals to meet the EU carbon border charge

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Half of power capacity from non-fossil sources · SWOT · Threats · The European Union’s carbon border charge

      Use non-fossil power, and later green hydrogen, to cut the carbon in steel and other export metals that will face the EU carbon border charge. Official Indian programmes already support green hydrogen. This recommendation pairs that strength with the border-charge threat, and uses a documented first industrial step from Sweden.

      Analogous case. Sweden’s state-owned miner LKAB and the steelmaker SSAB ran the HYBRIT project. The Government Offices of Sweden record: SSAB, LKAB produced the world's first hydrogen-reduced DRI (Direct Reduced Iron) for fossil free steel production as part of HYBRIT (Government Offices of Sweden, Annual Report for State-owned Enterprises 2021 (opens in a new tab), LKAB section). The Swedish Energy Agency later stated that the demonstration plant has potential for cutting Sweden´s carbon emissions by 1.8 million tonnes annually and can produce 1.35 million tonnes of fossil-free sponge iron annually at full operation, after the EU Innovation Fund granted the project € 108 million for the demonstration phase (Swedish Energy Agency, 14 December 2023 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India already has non-fossil capacity and an official green hydrogen programme, and the EU charge is already in force from 1 January 2026 (live SWOT). Low-carbon metals can protect access to the EU market. The 1.8 million tonne figure is a potential cut at full operation, not a result already delivered. Sweden is a small, high-income economy with different ore, power prices and state-owned firms. Indian blast-furnace capacity and coal use are on a much larger scale.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Open services-export jobs to more women

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Few women in paid work · SWOT · Opportunities · Services exports and a European trade deal

      Use the growth of services exports to raise women’s participation in paid work, with attention to job quality and pay. Official Indian programmes already promote women’s employment and skills. This recommendation pairs the live weakness in female labour-force participation with the services-export opportunity.

      Analogous case. The Philippines built a large information-technology and business-process sector with high female employment. The ILO reports: As of 2019, the IT-BPM sector employed over 1.1 million employees, and women represented 53 per cent of total jobs, but faced a 13 per cent pay gap, largely representing the lower paying and skilled positions they occupy. (ILO Research Brief, June 2020 (opens in a new tab), Box 1, p. 5).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India’s female labour-force participation stands at 40.0% in 2025 (live SWOT), and services exports are large and growing. Remote and office-based export work can bring more women into paid jobs where travel and safety barriers are high. The analogy is limited by the pay and skill gap in the Philippine case, and by the ILO’s warning about automation risk in parts of that sector. A rise in headcount alone will not close India’s participation gap if new jobs stay low-paid or insecure.

    • Use trade-linked public capacity to cut child stunting

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Child stunting · SWOT · Opportunities · Services exports and a European trade deal

      Direct part of the fiscal and administrative capacity that comes with growth and trade toward a targeted cut in child stunting. Official Indian programmes already work on nutrition. This recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from a documented national campaign, not a claim that India has no nutrition policy.

      Analogous case. Peru ran a focused national effort against stunting during a period of growth. The World Bank records: Peru more than halved its stunting rates in eight years from 28 percent in 2008 to 13 percent in 2016. The same book states that it was targeted policies that played a pivotal role, while poverty fell from 58.7 percent in 2004 to 22.7 percent in 2014 (World Bank, Standing Tall, 2017 (opens in a new tab)). These years follow the book’s opening and Chart A1, not the foreword’s 2005-2016 span, which is inconsistent with those figures.

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India’s child stunting rate is 29.3% (live SWOT), close to Peru’s starting point in 2008. Growth and a larger trade base can fund targeted nutrition work. The analogy is limited because Peru is smaller, and the World Bank stresses targeted policy, not growth alone. There is no official figure in the live sources that earmarks TEPA revenue for nutrition. Transfer depends on state delivery, not only on export receipts.

    • Offer rural wage work in solar, battery and related industry

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Rural work tied to farming · SWOT · Opportunities · Green hydrogen and solar factories · SWOT · Opportunities · Battery cell factories

      Open wage work in solar, battery and related industry to people now tied to farming, so that rural livelihoods can diversify as those factories grow. Official Indian programmes already support manufacturing and rural employment. This recommendation pairs the live rural-work weakness with those factory opportunities.

      Analogous case. Viet Nam moved a large share of workers out of farming over two decades. The 2019 Labour Force Survey states: Agricultural workers accounted for 34.5% of the workforce in 2019, declined by 27.7 percentage points compared to 2000 (Viet Nam GSO, Labour Force Survey 2019 (opens in a new tab), p. 26, Table 2.5). The 2000 share was 62.2%. By 2022, The employed persons in agriculture, forestry and fisheries reached 13.9 million people (27.5%). (Viet Nam NSO, Labour Force Survey 2022 (opens in a new tab), point 8).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India still has 57.7% of rural workers tied to farming (live SWOT), and official plans already support solar and battery factories. Those factories can offer wage work near some rural districts. The analogy is limited because Viet Nam’s shift also ran through garments, electronics and foreign investment, not only clean-energy factories. A factory job is not automatic for a farm worker without skills, transport and a nearby plant. Some regions will see little of this work.

  • WT Responses that reduce weaknesses and limit threats

    • Set binding limits on over-used groundwater as years get hotter

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Over-used groundwater · SWOT · Threats · Hotter years

      Set and enforce binding limits on groundwater extraction in over-exploited blocks, and recover water for aquifers, as heat and drought stress rise. Official Indian programmes already address water. This recommendation pairs the live groundwater weakness with hotter years, and uses a documented basin-scale recovery effort.

      Analogous case. Australia’s Murray-Darling Basin Plan set recovery targets for water in a drying climate. The 2025 evaluation overview states: As at 31 March 2025, the total amount recovered to bridge the gap is 2,068.9 GL/y (99.7% of the recovery target) with 6.7 GL/y remaining. It also states: The condition of the Basin environment is much better than it would have been had the Basin Plan not been implemented. Implementation is not yet complete. The public summary records a social cost: In some smaller and more remote irrigation-dependent communities, water recovery has generated adjustment pressures. (MDBA, 2025 Basin Plan Evaluation Overview (opens in a new tab); MDBA, 2025 Basin Plan Evaluation (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. India already lists 10.8% of assessment units as over-exploited (live SWOT), and 2025 was among the warmest years on the Indian record. Binding limits can protect aquifers as heat rises. The analogy is limited because the Basin Plan is a single federal basin with a quantified recovery target, and even there implementation is not complete. Indian groundwater is governed across many states and aquifers. Recovery can also put pressure on irrigation-dependent communities, as the Australian evaluation itself records.

    • Reuse treated wastewater for farms to ease groundwater and heat stress

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Over-used groundwater · SWOT · Threats · Hotter years

      Reuse treated wastewater for irrigation in districts where treatment plants already exist, so that farms can use less well water in hotter years. Official Indian programmes already promote wastewater reuse in some cities. This recommendation pairs the same groundwater weakness with hotter years, and uses a documented national reuse outcome.

      Analogous case. Israel combined regulation, reuse and irrigation reform in a water-stressed climate. A 2023 report by the United States Environmental Protection Agency, after a visit hosted with the Israel Water Authority and other Israeli ministries, states: After drastic infrastructure, regulatory, and institutional reform, Israel transformed from one of the world’s most water-stressed countries to a water secure country, becoming a global leader in agricultural water reuse. Today, nearly 90 percent of Israel’s treated wastewater is reused for irrigation purposes. (US EPA EPA-822-S-23-001, March 2023 (opens in a new tab), Background/Purpose).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Reuse can replace some well water in Indian irrigation districts and ease over-exploited blocks in hotter years. The analogy is limited because Israel is small and coastal, and large desalination plants are central to its drinking-water supply. That coastal desalination model does not transfer to most inland Indian aquifers. Energy use, cost and state-level water institutions will limit the pace and the place where reuse pays off.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
