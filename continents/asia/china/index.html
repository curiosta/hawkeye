T

Threats

External · harmful

Warming faster than the world average

  • 0.31°C warmer every 10 years. The China Meteorological Administration reports: 1961年至2025年，中国年平均气温每10年升高0.31℃，高于同期全球平均升温速率。 From 1961 to 2025, China’s average yearly temperature rose by 0.31°C every 10 years, faster than the global average over the same period (Hawkeye’s translation). The north is warming faster than the south (China Meteorological Administration, Blue Book on Climate Change 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 2025 was about 1.0°C above normal. 2025年，全国平均气温较常年偏高约1.0℃，位列1901年以来两个最暖年份之一. In 2025 the national average temperature was about 1.0°C above normal, one of the two warmest years since 1901 (Hawkeye’s translation) (CMA (opens in a new tab)).

Floods and storms

  • 166.6 billion yuan of flood losses in 2025. Flood, waterlogging and geological disasters caused a direct economic loss of 166.6 billion yuan. Typhoons hit China 10 times in 2025. (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).

A harder world for trade

  • Protectionism named in the national plan. The plan describes the outside world like this: 单边主义、保护主义抬头，霸权主义和强权政治威胁上升，国际经济贸易秩序遇到严峻挑战. Unilateralism and protectionism are rising, the threat of hegemonism and power politics is growing, and the international economic and trade order faces severe challenges (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab), chapter 1).
  • A trade surplus of 8,509.4 billion yuan. The surplus of trade in goods reached 8,509.4 billion yuan. Goods exports grew 6.1% in 2025 (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).

A growing nuclear arsenal

  • Up to 620 nuclear warheads. SIPRI writes that China is also in the middle of a significant modernization and expansion of its nuclear arsenal, which is estimated to have increased from 600 to up to 620 warheads during the year. It adds: The modernization and further deployment of nuclear weapons raise potential risks related to nuclear escalation. (SIPRI Yearbook 2026, Summary (opens in a new tab)). These are SIPRI estimates. China does not publish its warhead numbers.
  • The second largest military spender. China, the second largest military spender ($336 billion), accounted for 12 per cent of the world total in 2025 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026, Summary (opens in a new tab)).

O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Artificial intelligence across the economy

  • The “AI Plus” plan. 全面实施“人工智能+”行动，加强人工智能同科技创新、产业发展、文化建设、民生保障、社会治理相结合. Fully carry out the “AI Plus” action, and join artificial intelligence with science, industry, culture, public services and governance (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab), chapter 13).
  • Industrial robots up 28%. Output of industrial robots was 773,000 sets in 2025, 28.0% more than in 2024 (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab), table of industrial output).

Room to grow spending at home

  • A goal to lift household consumption. The plan sets the aim that 居民消费率明显提高，内需拉动经济增长主动力作用持续增强. The share of household consumption rises clearly, and demand at home becomes the main driver of growth (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab), chapter 3).
  • Over half of trade is with Belt and Road partners. Trade with countries along the Belt and Road was 23,601.8 billion yuan, an increase of 6.3 percent over that of the previous year, accounting for 51.9 percent of the total value of imports and exports. (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Coal still supplies half of all energy

  • 51.4% of energy from coal. The consumption of coal accounted for 51.4 percent of the total energy consumption, 1.8 percentage points lower than that of 2024. Total energy use rose 3.5% in 2025 (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).
  • Almost a third of world fossil CO2. China emitted 12,289 Mt of fossil CO2 in 2024, 31.8% of the world total (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)). See the shares table below.

A shrinking and ageing population

  • 3.39 million fewer people in 2025. By the end of 2025, the total number of national population … reached 1,404.89 million, a decrease of 3.39 million over that at the end of 2024. There were 7.92 million births in 2025 and 11.31 million deaths (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).
  • 23.0% aged 60 or over. At the end of 2025, 323.38 million people were aged 60 and above, 23.0% of the population. 15.9% were 65 or over (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab), table 1).
  • About one child per woman. The UN estimates the fertility rate at 1.02 children per woman in 2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social

Property slump and weak demand

  • Property investment down 20.6%. From January to September, the investment in real estate development was 5,336.4 billion yuan, a year-on-year decrease of 20.6% (NBS, 20 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). It had already fallen 17.2% in 2025 (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).
  • Risks named in the plan. 有效需求不足，供强需弱矛盾突出. Demand is not strong enough, and supply is much stronger than demand (Hawkeye’s translation). The plan also says 防范化解房地产、地方政府债务、中小金融机构等风险任务繁重. The work of preventing and defusing risks in property, local government debt and small and medium financial institutions is heavy (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab), chapter 1).
S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

The world’s largest build-out of wind and solar power

  • 1,202 GW of solar and 640 GW of wind. At the end of 2025, the installed wind power generation capacity was 640.01 million kilowatts, up by 22.9 percent and the installed solar power generation capacity was 1,201.73 million kilowatts, up by 35.4 percent. (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)). One GW is one million kilowatts.
  • About 40% of electricity from clean sources. The electricity generated by clean energy such as hydropower, nuclear power, wind power and solar power was 4,248.1 billion kilowatts-hours, up by 14.4 percent over the previous year. That is about 40% of the 10,575 billion kilowatt-hours generated in 2025 (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab), Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • 16.5 million new energy vehicles made. The output of new energy vehicles reached 16.524 million, up by 25.1 percent over the previous year (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)). New energy vehicles are mainly electric and plug-in hybrid cars.

Also in GAP · Wind and solar capacity, 2035

High and rising research spending

  • 2.80% of GDP on research. Expenditures on research and experimental development activities (R&D) were 3,926.2 billion yuan in 2025, up by 8.1 percent over that of 2024, accounting for 2.80 percent of GDP. (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological

The largest economy in Asia

  • US$19.6 trillion in 2025. China’s GDP is the largest in Asia and 16.6% of the world economy at market exchange rates (IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 (opens in a new tab), Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • 5.0% growth in 2025. According to preliminary estimates, the gross domestic product (GDP) … in 2025 was 140,187.9 billion yuan, up by 5.0 percent over the previous year. (NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué (opens in a new tab)). In the third quarter of 2026, GDP was 4.5% higher than a year earlier (NBS, 21 October 2026 (opens in a new tab), table 2).
