China TOWS

Pairs internal factors (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external ones (Opportunities, Threats) into four kinds of response.

Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that China has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Use the record wind and solar build-out to meet more energy demand at home

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · The world’s largest build-out of wind and solar power · SWOT · Opportunities · Room to grow spending at home

      Steer more of China’s record wind and solar capacity toward electricity demand at home, including industry and road transport, as official policy already seeks to grow domestic spending. Official Chinese programmes already control carbon and support consumption. This recommendation pairs that clean-power strength with the domestic-demand opportunity.

      Analogous case. Portugal, a smaller economy, raised the renewable share of electricity production while cutting energy import dependence. The Directorate-General for Energy and Geology (DGEG) states: A dependência energética em 2024 foi de 64,3%, tendo descido 2,4 p.p. face a 2023. Energy dependence in 2024 was 64.3%, 2.4 percentage points lower than in 2023. O contributo das fontes de energia renovável (%FER) no consumo final bruto de energia, em 2024, foi de 36,5%, sendo o contributo das fontes de energia renovável na produção de eletricidade (%FER-E), de 65,8%. The renewable share of electricity production was 65.8% in 2024. (DGEG, Balanço Energético Nacional 2024 (opens in a new tab), published 31 October 2025).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. China already has 1,202 GW of solar and 640 GW of wind installed at the end of 2025 (live SWOT, capacity not generation). Using more of that power at home can support domestic activity and displace some coal-fired use. The 51.4% coal share of total energy in 2025 is a live weakness, so capacity does not by itself change the energy mix. Portugal is much smaller, still had 64.3% energy import dependence in 2024, and started from a different fuel mix. A 2.4 percentage-point fall in dependence is a modest step.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Use the wind and solar build-out to cut emissions as the country warms and faces floods

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · The world’s largest build-out of wind and solar power · SWOT · Threats · Warming faster than the world average

      Use the record wind and solar build-out as the core of a faster cut in greenhouse-gas emissions, as China warms faster than the world average and faces floods and storms. Official Chinese carbon-control programmes already exist. This recommendation pairs that strength with the climate threat.

      Analogous case. The European Environment Agency, in its 9 November 2026 press release on the Trends and Projections report, states: In 2025, net EU greenhouse gas emissions under the scope of the European Climate Law dropped by 39% below 1990 levels, and This drop also came while gross domestic product and populations grew by 74% and 8% respectively over the same period. The figures for 2025 are preliminary. The same release records that the pace of cuts slowed in 2025, and that year-on-year emissions rose in domestic transport and buildings (EEA, 9 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. China is warming faster than the world average, and it already hosts the world’s largest wind and solar capacity (live SWOT). The EU case shows that emissions can fall while output grows. The 2025 figures are preliminary. China’s energy system is still 51.4% coal, and the live wind and solar figures are capacity, not generation. The EU started with a carbon market and slower energy-demand growth than China in recent decades. Transport and buildings remain hard, even in the EU.

  • WT Responses that reduce weaknesses and limit threats

    • Cut coal’s share of total energy, following a documented end of coal power in another large economy

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Coal still supplies half of all energy · SWOT · Threats · Warming faster than the world average

      Cut coal’s share of total energy, not only the share of electricity, as the country warms faster than the world average. Official Chinese programmes already control coal use. This recommendation pairs the live coal weakness with that climate threat, and uses a documented end of coal-fired power in the United Kingdom.

      Analogous case. The UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero records that The last UK coal-fired power station closed in England in September 2024. In the same official document: In September 2024, Ratcliffe-on-Soar, the last coal-fired power station in the UK, closed. UK fossil fuel generation decreased by 16 per cent compared to 2023. Coal-fired generation’s share of UK electricity was only 0.7 per cent in 2024, a record low. The low carbon share of total UK generation stood at its highest value on the time series at 64.7 per cent. Renewables’ share rose 3.9 percentage points to 50.4 per cent. (DESNZ, Electricity generation and supply, 2020 to 2024 (opens in a new tab)). Energy Trends December 2024 confirms: Ratcliffe-on-Soar closed at the end of the third quarter on 30 September 2024. (DESNZ, Energy Trends December 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Coal still supplied 51.4% of China’s total energy consumption in 2025, even after a 1.8 percentage-point fall (live SWOT). The UK case shows that a large economy can take coal-fired power to a close, with a record low-carbon share of generation. The main limit is scale and scope. In 2024 coal was already only 0.7% of UK electricity, and the official sources describe electricity generation, not coal’s share of all energy. China’s 51.4% figure is for total energy, including industry and heat. The UK also remains a net importer of electricity. Official Chinese coal-control programmes already exist and are not replaced by this pairing.

Documented national responses 6

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • Controls on total carbon emissions and on coal and oil use

    Adopted by 15th Five-Year Plan Outline, chapter 47 (March 2026)

    推动煤炭和石油消费达峰，单位GDP能耗下降10%左右. Bring coal and oil use to a peak, and cut energy use per unit of GDP by about 10% (Hawkeye’s translation). The plan also aims to 扩大全国碳排放权交易市场覆盖范围, widening the national carbon market (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Coal still supplies half of all energy

  • Support for having and raising children

    Adopted by 15th Five-Year Plan Outline, chapter 37 (March 2026)

    发挥育儿补贴和婴幼儿照护、子女教育个人所得税专项附加扣除政策作用. Use childcare subsidies and income tax deductions for infant care and children’s education (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · A shrinking and ageing population

  • Care for older people and a later retirement age

    Adopted by 15th Five-Year Plan Outline, chapter 40 (March 2026)

    稳妥实施渐进式延迟法定退休年龄. Carefully carry out a gradual rise in the statutory retirement age (Hawkeye’s translation). The plan also aims to 推行长期护理保险, rolling out long-term care insurance (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · A shrinking and ageing population

  • A new model for property

    Adopted by 15th Five-Year Plan Outline, chapter 44 (March 2026)

    加快构建房地产发展新模式，健全多主体供给、多渠道保障、租购并举的住房制度. Speed up a new model for property, with housing supplied by many providers and both renting and buying supported (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Property slump and weak demand

  • Curbing hidden local government debt

    Adopted by 15th Five-Year Plan Outline, chapter 52 (March 2026)

    坚决遏制新增地方政府隐性债务，优化中央和地方政府债务结构，加快推动地方政府融资平台改革转型. Firmly stop new hidden local government debt, improve the mix of central and local debt, and speed up reform of local government financing vehicles (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Property slump and weak demand

  • A special drive to boost consumption

    Adopted by 15th Five-Year Plan Outline, chapter 15 (March 2026)

    深入实施提振消费专项行动，增强居民消费能力，改善消费意愿. Carry out the special action to boost consumption, so that households can and want to spend more (Hawkeye’s translation) (15th Five-Year Plan Outline (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Opportunities · Room to grow spending at home

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
