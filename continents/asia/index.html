T

Threats

External · harmful

Rising military spending and five nuclear-armed states

  • Almost three-tenths of world military spending. Countries in Asia spent US$838.8 billion on their armed forces in 2025, 29.1% of the world total of US$2,887 billion; China US$335.5 billion and India US$92.1 billion (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s sum)
  • 1,130 nuclear warheads. Five Asian countries held nuclear warheads in January 2026: China 620, India 190, Pakistan 170, Israel 90 and North Korea 60, 9.3% of the world’s 12,187 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • China’s arsenal is growing. SIPRI estimates that China’s nuclear arsenal increased from 600 to up to 620 warheads during 2025, as part of a significant modernization and expansion (SIPRI Yearbook 2026, Summary (opens in a new tab), ch. 8)
  • Strategic competition. Asia and Oceania continued to be marked by intensifying strategic competition between China and the USA (SIPRI Yearbook 2026, Summary (opens in a new tab), ch. 5)

SIPRI’s own “Asia and Oceania” region is not the same as the UN Asia region used for the totals here; Hawkeye’s sums cover the UN Asia region.

Also in PESTLE · Political

Fast warming and glacier loss

  • About twice Asia’s own earlier trend. Asia has warmed faster than the global average in recent decades, with the warming trend during 1991–2025 approximately twice that observed during 1961–1990. (WMO, 17 June 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Every monitored glacier shrank. All 23 monitored glaciers in High-mountain Asia lost mass, driven by above-average temperatures and below-average winter snow. (WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Water security. This threatens long-term water security in the world’s most heavily populated region and leads to an upsurge in hazards – with multiple glacial lake outburst floods and glacier collapses being recorded in 2025. (WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab))

WMO compares the 1991–2025 trend with Asia’s own 1961–1990 trend, not with the global rate of warming.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Floods, heat and drought in 2025

  • Millions affected. Dangerous heat, devastating rainfall and flooding, and severe drought affected millions of people across Asia in 2025, exacting a heavy human and economic toll (WMO, 17 June 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Monsoon and cyclone floods. Exceptional monsoon and tropical cyclone-related rainfall caused devastating flooding in many countries, including Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Viet Nam. (WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Pakistan. In Pakistan, monsoon flooding was associated with more than 1,000 deaths and left over 3 million people requiring assistance. (WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Viet Nam. In Viet Nam, prolonged flooding linked to multiple weather systems resulted in at least 200 deaths and economic losses of US$ 1.9 billion. (WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab))

These are 2025 examples. No continent-wide loss total was confirmed at source, so none is shown.

O

Opportunities

External · helpful

The RCEP free-trade partnership

  • What it aims to do. The objectives of this Agreement are to: (a) establish a modern, comprehensive, high-quality, and mutually beneficial economic partnership framework to facilitate the expansion of regional trade and investment and contribute to global economic growth and development (RCEP Agreement (opens in a new tab), Art. 1.3)
  • In force since 2022. Signed on 15 November 2020 and in force since 1 January 2022 (RCEP Agreement (opens in a new tab))
  • Fifteen parties. The ten original ASEAN members plus China, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Australia and New Zealand; 13 of the 15 are in Asia (RCEP Agreement (opens in a new tab))
  • Over a quarter of world output. The 15 parties produced US$32,306.6 billion in 2025, 27.3% of world GDP (IMF WEO, Oct 2026 (opens in a new tab)), and have 2,321.7 million people, 28.2% of the world; the 13 Asian parties hold 47.3% of Asia’s population (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s sums)

Also in PESTLE · Political

Renewable power in South-East Asia

  • A third of capacity. Renewables made up 33.5% of ASEAN’s installed power capacity in 2023 (ASEAN Centre for Energy, 17 October 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • The 2025 target. APAEC Phase II set aspirational targets of 23% share of RE in Total Primary Energy Supply and 35% share of RE in ASEAN installed power capacity by 2025 (APAEC Phase II (opens in a new tab))
  • The 2030 target. The successor plan aims at 45% RE Share in Installed Power Capacity by 2030 (ASEAN Centre for Energy, 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • A new plan adopted. We welcomed the adoption of the ASEAN Plan of Action for Energy Cooperation (APAEC) 2026–2030 as the strategic blueprint to guide ASEAN’s energy priorities (Chairman’s Statement, 47th ASEAN Summit (opens in a new tab), para 77)

ASEAN figure: ten members at the time of the data. Timor-Leste joined as the 11th member in October 2025. RE = renewable energy. The 2025 outcome has not been reported yet (TBC).

Mobile networks and road links

  • Wider mobile coverage. Advancements are particularly evident in digital infrastructure, with a growing share of the population covered by a mobile network. (ESCAP, Asia and the Pacific SDG Progress Report 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • A 145,000 km highway network. The Asian Highway Network now comprises over 145,000 km of roads passing through 32 countries. (ESCAP, Asian Highway (opens in a new tab))
  • An agreed plan. Parties adopt the network as a coordinated plan for the development of highway routes of international importance which they intend to undertake within the framework of their national programmes. (Intergovernmental Agreement on the Asian Highway Network (opens in a new tab), Art. 1; in force 4 July 2005)

The mobile-network line is for ESCAP’s Asia-Pacific region, which also covers the Pacific and the Russian Federation. Progress already made on industry and infrastructure is under Strengths.

W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

The largest share of fossil CO2

  • More than half of greenhouse gases. ESCAP says the region remains one of the largest polluters in the world, accounting for more than half of the global greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. Both issues contribute to an alarming regression on climate action (Goal 13). (ESCAP, SDG Progress Report 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Six-tenths of fossil CO2. Countries in Asia emitted 23,403 million tonnes of fossil CO2 in 2024, 60.6% of the world’s 38,599 million tonnes; 4.9 tonnes per person against a world average of 4.7 (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s sums)
  • A third of the historical total. 625,477 million tonnes emitted from 1850 to 2024, 33.9% of the world’s cumulative fossil CO2 (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s sum)
  • The largest emitters. China 12,289, India 3,193, Japan 962, Indonesia 812 and Iran 793 million tonnes in 2024 (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab))

The first point is for ESCAP’s Asia-Pacific region; the CO2 figures are for the UN Asia region.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Climate policy

Hunger

  • 323 million people undernourished, and falling. The prevalence of undernourishment (PoU) in Asia fell from 7.9 percent in 2022 to 6.7 percent, or 323 million people, in 2024. (FAO, 28 July 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Rising in western Asia. in western Asia an estimated 12.7 percent of the population, or more than 39 million people, may have faced hunger in 2024. (FAO, 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • The 2030 target. SDG target 2.1: By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round (UN global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))

The FAO figures come from The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2025.

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Hunger

Public finances and growth targets off track

  • Taxes and debt service. However, there are concerning regressions in the proportion of domestic budgets funded by domestic taxes, debt service as a proportion of exports and use of country-owned results frameworks (ESCAP, SDG Progress Report 2025 (opens in a new tab), Goal 17)
  • Decent work and growth. The goal of decent work and economic growth (Goal 8) has the highest number of targets with negative trends, significantly hindering overall progress. (ESCAP, 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Fossil fuel subsidies. For example, increases in fossil fuel subsidies has hindered progress in sustainable consumption and production (Goal 12). (ESCAP, 2025 (opens in a new tab))

Scope: ESCAP’s Asia-Pacific region also covers the Pacific islands, Australia, New Zealand and the Russian Federation, not the UN Asia region alone.

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Most of the world’s people

  • 58.7% of humanity. 4,835,320,060 people on 1 July 2025, out of a world population of 8,231,613,070 (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab))
  • The two largest countries. India 1,463,865,525 (30.3% of Asia) and China 1,416,096,094 (29.3%) (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab); shares are Hawkeye’s arithmetic)
  • Living longer than the world average. Life expectancy at birth 75.0 years in 2025, against 73.5 for the world; Japan 85.0 (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab))
  • Densely settled. 154 people per km², against 63 for the world, across 51 UN areas including 47 sovereign states (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Social

Progress on industry and infrastructure

  • No regressing targets. Significant progress has been made on industry, innovation and infrastructure (Goal 9), which has no regressing targets in 2024. (ESCAP, Asia and the Pacific SDG Progress Report 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Alongside better health. Significant achievements have been made on industry, innovation and infrastructure (Goal 9) and good health and well-being (Goal 3), driven by expanded access to mobile networks and remarkable improvements in maternal, infant and child health. (ESCAP, 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • The target being tracked. SDG target 9.2: Promote inclusive and sustainable industrialization and, by 2030, significantly raise industry’s share of employment and gross domestic product, in line with national circumstances, and double its share in least developed countries (UN global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))

Scope: ESCAP’s Asia-Pacific region also covers the Pacific islands, Australia, New Zealand and the Russian Federation, not the UN Asia region alone.

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · Industry

A South-East Asian nuclear-weapon-free zone

  • No nuclear weapons. Each State Party undertakes not to, anywhere inside or outside the Zone: (a) develop, manufacture or otherwise acquire, possess or have control over nuclear weapons; (b) station or transport nuclear weapons by any means; or (c) test or use nuclear weapons. (Bangkok Treaty (opens in a new tab), Art. 3(1))
  • In force since 1997. Signed on 15 December 1995; in force since 27 March 1997 (Bangkok Treaty (opens in a new tab))
  • Ten states parties. The ten ASEAN members that signed it; whether Timor-Leste has joined is TBC. None of them holds nuclear warheads (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab), January 2026)
