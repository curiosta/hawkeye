Indonesia TOWS

Pairs internal factors (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external ones (Opportunities, Threats) into four kinds of response.

Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Indonesia has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Use a young workforce and RCEP trade to shift more jobs toward industry and services

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · A young, large population · SWOT · Opportunities · The RCEP trade agreement

      Use Indonesia’s young population, and RCEP trade, to shift more jobs toward industry and services as the economy grows. Official Indonesian development and trade programmes already exist. This recommendation pairs that population strength with the trade opportunity.

      Analogous case. Viet Nam moved a large share of workers out of farming over two decades. The 2019 Labour Force Survey states: Agricultural workers accounted for 34.5% of the workforce in 2019, declined by 27.7 percentage points compared to 2000 (Viet Nam GSO, Labour Force Survey 2019 (opens in a new tab), p. 26, Table 2.5). The 2000 share was 62.2%. By 2022, The employed persons in agriculture, forestry and fisheries reached 13.9 million people (27.5%). (Viet Nam NSO, Labour Force Survey 2022 (opens in a new tab), point 8).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Indonesia has a young, large population and steady growth, and RCEP is a live opportunity (live SWOT). Viet Nam documented a large, official fall in the agricultural share of employment, from 62.2% in 2000 to 34.5% in 2019 and 27.5% in 2022. The analogy is limited because Viet Nam’s shift also ran through garments, electronics and foreign investment, not only regional trade rules. A trade agreement does not automatically create nearby factory jobs or skills.

    • Link nickel output to clean-energy manufacturing funded by transition finance

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · The world’s largest nickel producer · SWOT · Opportunities · Finance for the energy transition

      Link Indonesia’s nickel output to cleaner processing and to battery and related manufacturing, using energy-transition finance. Official Indonesian programmes already include the mining law, carbon pricing and the Just Energy Transition Partnership plan. This recommendation is a pairing, not a claim that no downstream policy exists. It also notes the live WTO dispute on the ore-export ban.

      Analogous case. The IEA reports: China has invested over USD 50 billion in new PV supply capacity – ten times more than Europe − and created as many as 300 000 manufacturing jobs across the solar PV value chain since 2011. Today, China’s share in all the manufacturing stages of solar panels (such as polysilicon, ingots, wafers, cells and modules) exceeds 80%. Those policies contributed to a cost decline more than 80% (IEA, Special Report on Solar PV Global Supply Chains, revised August 2022 (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Indonesia mined about two-thirds of world nickel in 2025, and transition finance is a live opportunity (live SWOT). The IEA solar case shows that a strategic manufacturing build-out can create jobs and cut costs. The analogy is limited because the IEA case is solar modules, not nickel processing, and because the live SWOT already records a WTO panel finding against Indonesia’s ore-export prohibition. Energy-intensive nickel processing will raise, not cut, emissions if it runs on a high-carbon grid. The live weakness is slow growth of renewables.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Cut the carbon in nickel and other export metals to meet border-carbon rules

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · The world’s largest nickel producer · SWOT · Threats · Trade disputes and carbon border taxes

      Cut the carbon in nickel processing and related export metals, so that border-carbon charges and trade disputes do less harm to export access. Official Indonesian programmes already include carbon pricing and the enhanced NDC. This recommendation pairs nickel strength with that trade threat.

      Analogous case. Sweden’s state-owned miner LKAB and the steelmaker SSAB ran the HYBRIT project. The Government Offices of Sweden record: SSAB, LKAB produced the world's first hydrogen-reduced DRI (Direct Reduced Iron) for fossil free steel production as part of HYBRIT (Government Offices of Sweden, Annual Report for State-owned Enterprises 2021 (opens in a new tab), LKAB section). The Swedish Energy Agency later stated that the demonstration plant has potential for cutting Sweden´s carbon emissions by 1.8 million tonnes annually and can produce 1.35 million tonnes of fossil-free sponge iron annually at full operation, after the EU Innovation Fund granted the project € 108 million for the demonstration phase (Swedish Energy Agency, 14 December 2023 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Indonesia is the world’s largest nickel miner, and carbon border taxes are a live threat (live SWOT). HYBRIT shows a documented first industrial step toward fossil-free metal reduction, with EU innovation funding. The 1.8 million tonne figure is a potential at full operation, not a result already delivered. The analogy is limited because HYBRIT is iron, not nickel, and Sweden is a small, high-income economy with different ore, power prices and state-owned firms. Indonesian processing scale and grid carbon intensity differ sharply. The WTO ore-ban dispute is a separate live legal fact.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Use trade-linked public capacity, and targeted policy, to cut child stunting

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Child stunting · SWOT · Opportunities · The RCEP trade agreement

      Direct part of the capacity that comes with growth and RCEP trade toward a targeted cut in child stunting. Official Indonesian programmes already include Perpres 72/2021 on accelerating the reduction of stunting, with a 21.50% baseline in 2023. This recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from a documented national campaign, not a claim that Indonesia has no stunting policy.

      Analogous case. Peru ran a focused national effort against stunting during a period of growth. The World Bank records: Peru more than halved its stunting rates in eight years from 28 percent in 2008 to 13 percent in 2016. The same book states that it was targeted policies that played a pivotal role, while poverty fell from 58.7 percent in 2004 to 22.7 percent in 2014 (World Bank, Standing Tall, 2017 (opens in a new tab)). These years follow the book’s opening and Chart A1, not the foreword’s 2005-2016 span, which is inconsistent with those figures.

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Indonesia’s stunting baseline is 21.5% in 2023, between Peru’s 28% starting point in 2008 and its 13% result in 2016, and RCEP is a live opportunity (live SWOT). Peru more than halved stunting in eight years, and the World Bank stresses targeted policy, not growth alone. The analogy is limited because Peru is smaller, and there is no official figure in the live sources that earmarks RCEP gains for nutrition. Official Indonesian targets of 18.8% for 2025 and 14.2% for 2029 are targets, not results already delivered.

    • Use transition finance to speed renewable power, which is still growing slowly

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Slow growth of renewables · SWOT · Opportunities · Finance for the energy transition

      Use Just Energy Transition Partnership and related finance to speed renewable power, including grid, mini-grid and industry supply. Official Indonesian programmes already include Perpres 112/2022 and the JETP plan. This recommendation pairs the live slow-renewables weakness with that finance opportunity.

      Analogous case. Kenya’s official energy compact records a national result: Kenya has made significant strides in expanding electricity access, with national access levels rising from approximately 30% in 2014 to over 75% (65% on grid, 0.84% mini grid and 9.2% Standalone PV) as at 2024. This progress has largely been complemented by a strong focus on renewable energy development, which now accounts for 82% of the country’s total installed electricity generation capacity and 93% of actual energy consumption. (Kenya National Energy Compact 2025–2030 (opens in a new tab), figures as at 2024).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Indonesia’s live weakness is slow growth of renewables, and transition finance is a live opportunity. Kenya recorded a rise in electricity access from about 30% in 2014 to over 75% in 2024, with 82% renewable installed capacity and 93% of electricity consumption. The analogy is limited because Kenya’s compact is mainly about access and a geothermal-rich mix, while Indonesia’s live issue is the pace of renewable growth in a large, already partly electrified system, plus nickel-processing demand. The same Kenyan source records leftover rural access and a large clean-cooking gap. Geothermal and grid finance are not evenly transferable.

  • WT Responses that reduce weaknesses and limit threats

    • Speed renewable power so the energy system adds less to sea-level and sinking-land risk

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Slow growth of renewables · SWOT · Threats · Rising seas and sinking land

      Speed renewable power, so that Indonesia’s energy system adds less to the warming that raises seas and, with groundwater use, sinking land. Official Indonesian programmes already include renewable-power and carbon-pricing rules, and the enhanced NDC. This recommendation pairs the live slow-renewables weakness with that climate threat.

      Analogous case. The European Environment Agency, in its 9 November 2026 press release on the Trends and Projections report, states: In 2025, net EU greenhouse gas emissions under the scope of the European Climate Law dropped by 39% below 1990 levels, and This drop also came while gross domestic product and populations grew by 74% and 8% respectively over the same period. The figures for 2025 are preliminary. The same release records that the pace of cuts slowed in 2025, and that year-on-year emissions rose in domestic transport and buildings (EEA, 9 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Renewable growth is still slow, and rising seas with sinking land are a live threat (live SWOT). The EU case shows that emissions can fall 39% below 1990 while output grows. The 2025 figures are preliminary. The analogy is limited because the EU started from a different energy mix and a carbon market, and because land subsidence also depends on groundwater and local planning, not only on power-sector emissions. Transport and buildings in the EU still saw emissions rise in 2025. A continental emissions cut does not at once halt sea-level rise already locked in.

Documented national responses 9

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • National Long-Term Development Plan 2025–2045 (UU 59/2024)

    Adopted by House of Representatives (DPR) and the President, law of 13 September 2024

    Undang-Undang No. 59 Tahun 2024 Tentang Rencana Pembangunan Jangka Panjang Nasional Tahun 2025-2045 dalam mendukung perwujudan Visi Indonesia Emas 2045, Negara Kesatuan Republik Indonesia yang Bersatu, Berdaulat, Maju, dan Berkelanjutan. (unofficial translation: “Law No. 59 of 2024 on the National Long-Term Development Plan 2025-2045, in support of the Golden Indonesia 2045 Vision: a Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia that is United, Sovereign, Advanced and Sustainable.”) (Bappenas, Indonesia Emas 2045 (opens in a new tab)). Its five main targets are on the SOAR page.

  • The new capital, Nusantara (UU 3/2022, amended by UU 21/2023)

    Adopted by DPR and the President, law of 15 February 2022, amended 31 October 2023

    Ibu Kota Nusantara memiliki visi sebagai kota dunia untuk semua yang dibangun dan dikelola dengan tujuan untuk: 1) menjadi kota berkelanjutan di dunia; 2) sebagai penggerak ekonomi Indonesia di masa depan; dan 3) menjadi simbol identitas nasional yang merepresentasikan keberagaman bangsa Indonesia, berdasarkan Pancasila dan Undang-Undang Dasar Negara Republik Indonesia Tahun 1945. (unofficial translation: “The capital Nusantara has a vision as a world city for all, built and managed with the aims of: 1) becoming a sustainable city of the world; 2) acting as a driver of Indonesia’s future economy; and 3) becoming a symbol of national identity that represents the diversity of the Indonesian nation, based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution.”) (UU 3/2022, BPK abstract (opens in a new tab)). The budget figures in the research were not confirmed (TBC).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Rising seas and sinking land

  • Mineral and Coal Mining Law (UU 3/2020)

    Adopted by DPR and the President, law of 10 June 2020, later amended

    Dalam Undang-Undang ini juga dilakukan pengaturan kembali terkait kebijakan peningkatan nilai tambah Mineral dan Batubara, divestasi saham, (unofficial translation: “This law also re-regulates the policy on adding value to minerals and coal, share divestment,”) (UU 3/2020, BPK abstract (opens in a new tab))

    Related SWOT · Strengths · The world’s largest nickel producer · SWOT · Threats · Trade disputes and carbon border taxes

  • Accelerating renewable power (Perpres 112/2022)

    Adopted by President, regulation of 13 September 2022

    Dalam rangka transisi energi sektor ketenagalistrikan, Menteri ESDM menyusun peta jalan percepatan pengakhiran masa operasional PLTU yang dituangkan dalam dokumen perencanaan sektoral. (unofficial translation: “For the energy transition in the power sector, the Minister of Energy and Mineral Resources draws up a roadmap to speed up ending the operation of coal-fired power plants, set out in a sector planning document.”) (Perpres 112/2022, BPK abstract (opens in a new tab)). The wording on new coal plants and a 2050 date was not confirmed (TBC).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Slow growth of renewables

  • Accelerating the reduction of stunting (Perpres 72/2021)

    Adopted by President, regulation of 5 August 2021

    percepatan penurunan stunting yang dilaksanakan secara holistik, integratif, dan berkualitas. (unofficial translation: “the acceleration of stunting reduction, carried out in a holistic, integrated and quality way.”) (Perpres 72/2021, BPK abstract (opens in a new tab))

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Child stunting · GAP · Stunting among children under five down to 14.2% by 2029

  • Carbon pricing (Perpres 98/2021, replaced by Perpres 110/2025)

    Adopted by President, Perpres 98/2021 of 29 October 2021, revoked by Perpres 110/2025

    The BPK record lists Perpres 98/2021 as no longer in force and replaced by Perpres No. 110 Tahun 2025 tentang Penyelenggaraan Instrumen Nilai Ekonomi Karbon dan Pengendalian Emisi Gas Rumah Kaca Nasional (unofficial translation: “Presidential Regulation No. 110 of 2025 on carbon economic value instruments and national greenhouse gas emission control”) (Perpres 98/2021, BPK record (opens in a new tab)). The date Perpres 110/2025 entered into force was not confirmed (TBC).

  • Enhanced Nationally Determined Contribution

    Adopted by Government of Indonesia, submitted to the UNFCCC in September 2022

    The target was further increased from 29 percent to 31.89 percent unconditionally and from 41 percent to 43.20 percent conditionally, as documented in the Enhanced NDC that was submitted to the Secretariat of the UNFCCC in September 2022. (KLHK, FOLU Net Sink 2030 (opens in a new tab)). A Second NDC was reported in October 2025. Its targets have not been checked (TBC).

  • Just Energy Transition Partnership investment plan

    Adopted by Government of Indonesia with its JETP partners, plan launched 21 November 2023

    Quoted in the SWOT Opportunities (JETP Indonesia Secretariat (opens in a new tab)): US$21.6 billion committed, and a conditional target of 250 Mt of CO2 and 44% renewables for on-grid power by 2030.

    Related SWOT · Opportunities · Finance for the energy transition

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
