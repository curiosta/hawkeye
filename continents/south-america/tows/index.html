South America TOWS

Pairs internal factors (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external ones (Opportunities, Threats) into four kinds of response.

Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that South America has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Grow solar and wind on South America’s already high renewable share

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · A high share of renewable energy · SWOT · Opportunities · Fast growth in solar and wind

      Grow solar and wind on top of South America’s already high renewable share, including manufacturing where it pays off. Official national energy programmes already exist. This recommendation pairs that renewable strength with the live solar-and-wind opportunity.

      Analogous case. The IEA reports that China invested over USD 50 billion in new PV supply capacity and created as many as 300 000 manufacturing jobs across the solar PV value chain since 2011, and that these policies contributed to a cost decline more than 80% (IEA, Special Report on Solar PV Global Supply Chains, revised August 2022 (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. South America already has a high renewable share, and solar and wind are growing fast (live SWOT). Lower global module costs, which the IEA links to that build-out, can speed installation. The analogy is limited because the IEA also warns about geographic concentration, and because much of South America’s existing renewable share is hydropower, which faces drought risk. A manufacturing chain on the Chinese scale is not a realistic near-term copy for every country in the region.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Pay for verified Amazon protection before a tipping point is crossed

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · The Escazú Agreement on environmental rights · SWOT · Threats · The Amazon’s point of no return

      Pay for independently verified forest protection and restoration in the Amazon, using environmental-rights rules already agreed under Escazú. Official forest and rights instruments already exist. This recommendation pairs that governance strength with the live Amazon-tipping threat, and uses a documented national reversal of forest loss.

      Analogous case. The World Bank records that Costa Rica is the first tropical country in the world to have reversed deforestation, with forest cover now close to 60% after a low of 40% in 1987, and a first results-based payment of US$16.4 million for 3.28 million tons of verified emission reductions in 2018 and 2019 (World Bank, 16 November 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. The Amazon tipping threat is live, and Escazú is a live regional rights agreement (live SWOT). Paying for verified results can change incentives for landholders, as in Costa Rica. The analogy is limited because Costa Rica is small, and the Amazon is transboundary and far larger. One results-based payment does not test protection at Amazon scale, and land rights, enforcement and commodity demand remain harder in several Amazon states.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Use targeted support, not growth forecasts alone, to cut concentrated poverty

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Concentrated income and falling social spending · SWOT · Opportunities · The EU–MERCOSUR trade agreement

      Use targeted household and place-based support, alongside the EU-MERCOSUR trade agreement, to cut concentrated income poverty and protect social spending. Official social and trade instruments already exist. This recommendation pairs the live social-spending weakness with that trade opportunity.

      Analogous case. The World Bank records that the number of people in China with incomes below $1.90 a day has fallen by close to 800 million over 40 years, and that the approach combined broad-based transformation with targeted support to disadvantaged areas and later to households (World Bank, 1 April 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. South America still has concentrated income and, in the live SWOT, falling social spending, while EU-MERCOSUR is a live trade opportunity. The documented lesson is that targeted support mattered alongside growth and trade openness. China’s scale, starting poverty rate and institutions differ sharply from South American economies. A trade agreement does not by itself reverse falling social spending, and the $1.90 line is not the same as the region’s own inequality measures.

Documented regional responses 7

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • MERCOSUR (Treaty of Asunción)

    Adopted by Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay; signed 26 March 1991

    Sets up a common market with free movement of goods, services and factors of production, a common external tariff and coordinated economic policies (Tratado de Asunción (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). Quote on the SWOT page.

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · MERCOSUR’s common tariff still has national exceptions · SWOT · Opportunities · The EU–MERCOSUR trade agreement

  • Ushuaia Protocol on democratic commitment

    Adopted by MERCOSUR States Parties, Bolivia and Chile; signed 24 July 1998

    Makes democratic institutions an essential condition for integration among its parties (Protocolo de Ushuaia (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). Quote on the PESTLE page.

  • MERCOSUR Structural Convergence Fund (FOCEM)

    Adopted by MERCOSUR Common Market Council, Decision CMC 18/05

    El Fondo para la Convergencia Estructural del MERCOSUR (FOCEM), creado por la Decisión CMC Nº 45/04, está destinado a financiar programas para promover la convergencia estructural; desarrollar la competitividad; promover la cohesión social, en particular de las economías menores y regiones menos desarrolladas, y apoyar el funcionamiento de la estructura institucional y el fortalecimiento del proceso de integración. (unofficial translation: “FOCEM, created by Decision CMC 45/04, is to fund programmes to promote structural convergence, develop competitiveness, promote social cohesion, especially of the smaller economies and less developed regions, and support the institutional structure and the strengthening of integration.”) Annual contributions total US$100 million: Argentina 27%, Brazil 70%, Paraguay 1%, Uruguay 2% (Decision CMC 18/05 (opens in a new tab), Arts. 1 and 6). The decision ran for ten years from the first contribution (Art. 22); its current status was not checked.

  • Andean Community (Cartagena Agreement)

    Adopted by Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru

    Aims at balanced and harmonious development of its members through integration and economic and social cooperation, and at reducing external vulnerability and development gaps (Acuerdo de Cartagena (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). Quotes on the PESTLE and SOAR pages.

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Concentrated income and falling social spending

  • Amazon Cooperation Treaty

    Adopted by Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Guyana, Peru, Suriname and Venezuela; signed in Brasília on 3 July 1978

    Las Partes Contratantes convienen en realizar esfuerzos y acciones conjuntas para promover el desarrollo armónico de sus respectivos territorios amazónicos, de manera que esas acciones conjuntas produzcan resultados equitativos y mutuamente provechosos, así como para la preservación del medio ambiente y la conservación y utilización racional de los recursos naturales de esos territorios. (unofficial translation: “The Contracting Parties agree to undertake joint efforts and actions to promote the harmonious development of their Amazon territories, so that these joint actions produce equitable and mutually beneficial results, and to preserve the environment and conserve and rationally use the natural resources of those territories.”) (Amazon Cooperation Treaty (opens in a new tab), Art. I)

    Related SWOT · Threats · The Amazon’s point of no return

  • Belém Declaration common agenda

    Adopted by Leaders of the eight Amazon Cooperation Treaty states, 9 August 2023

    A new common cooperation agenda for the Amazon, including urgent action to prevent its point of no return and the fight against deforestation (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), paragraph 1). Quote on the SWOT page.

    Related SWOT · Threats · The Amazon’s point of no return · SWOT · Opportunities · A shared Amazon observatory

  • Amazon Indigenous Peoples’ Mechanism

    Adopted by Leaders of the eight Amazon Cooperation Treaty states, 9 August 2023

    Establecer el Mecanismo Amazónico de los Pueblos Indígenas, para fortalecer y promover el diálogo entre los gobiernos y los pueblos indígenas amazónicos para la gestión y coordinación sobre asuntos que conciernen a los pueblos indígenas y contribuyan a los objetivos de la OTCA; (unofficial translation: “To establish the Amazon Indigenous Peoples’ Mechanism, to strengthen and promote dialogue between governments and Amazonian Indigenous peoples on the management and coordination of matters that concern Indigenous peoples and contribute to ACTO’s objectives;”) (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), paragraph 6)

Sources checked 2026-11-12. Spanish quotations are given as published; English translations are unofficial. Sources that cover all of Latin America and the Caribbean are labelled.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a continent, the responses are documented instruments of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, each named with its members and the date it was adopted.

Every documented regional response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
