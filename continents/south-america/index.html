T

Threats

External · harmful

The Amazon’s point of no return

  • Named by the eight Amazon states. Meeting in Belém on 9 August 2023, the leaders of the parties to the Amazon Cooperation Treaty agreed on a common agenda that includes la acción urgente para evitar el punto de no retorno en la Amazonía, el combate a la deforestación y a los ilícitos en la región (unofficial translation: “urgent action to prevent the point of no return in the Amazon, and the fight against deforestation and illegal activities in the region”) (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), paragraph 1, p. 8)
  • The pressures they listed. The declaration’s preamble names la crisis climática, la pérdida de biodiversidad, la contaminación del agua, de los suelos, la deforestación y los incendios forestales, el aumento de la desigualdad, de la pobreza y del hambre, con el propósito de evitar que la Amazonía llegue a un punto de no retorno (unofficial translation: “the climate crisis, biodiversity loss, pollution of water and soils, deforestation and forest fires, and rising inequality, poverty and hunger, with the aim of preventing the Amazon from reaching a point of no return”) (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), preamble)
  • Who the eight are. Las Repúblicas de Bolivia, del Brasil, de Colombia, del Ecuador, de Guyana, del Perú, de Suriname y de Venezuela. (Amazon Cooperation Treaty, 1978 (opens in a new tab), opening line)
  • Forest cover is falling. Forest covered 50.94% of South America’s land area in 2015 and 48.59% in 2025 (UN SDG Global Database, series AG_LND_FRST (opens in a new tab), FAO data).

The declaration names the risk. It does not report a measured threshold, and none is shown here.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · SDG 15.1 forests · GAP · SDG 15.5 threatened species · GAP · ACTO 2030 targets

Glacier loss and water supplies

  • A country with no glaciers left. 2024 was the warmest, or second warmest year on record, which had devastating impacts on glaciers. With the loss of the Humboldt glacier, the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela became the second country in the world to lose all of its glaciers. (WMO, State of the Climate in Latin America and the Caribbean 2024 (opens in a new tab))
  • A key message two years running. Glacier retreat threatens long-term water supplies appears among the key messages of both the 2024 and the 2025 reports (WMO, 2025 (opens in a new tab); WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • 2025. In 2025, the region experienced record-breaking heat, persistent drought, devastating tropical cyclones, accelerating glacier retreat and rising seas, with severe consequences for communities, food systems and economies across the region. (WMO, State of the Climate in Latin America and the Caribbean 2025 (opens in a new tab))

Scope: the source covers all of Latin America and the Caribbean, not South America alone.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Venezuelan displacement

  • 7.64 million people from Venezuela abroad. At the end of 2025, UNHCR counted 7,640,872 people from Venezuela outside the country: 417,010 refugees, 1,181,156 asylum-seekers and 6,042,706 other people in need of international protection (UNHCR Refugee Data Finder (opens in a new tab), end-2025).
  • Most are in South America. 6,548,354 of them, about 86%, were in the other 11 South American countries (Hawkeye’s sum of the UNHCR country figures).
  • The largest hosts. Colombia 2,828,195; Peru 1,641,305; Brazil 732,272; Chile 669,408; Ecuador 441,063 (refugees, asylum-seekers and other people in need of international protection from Venezuela, end-2025; UNHCR (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social

A slower world economy

  • The outside picture for 2026. A global economic downturn is expected for 2026, with growth of 2.9%, the lowest rate since 2022. Geopolitical rivalries and the disruption of energy supplies have increased the prices of oil, fertilizers, and transportation, while the persistence of relatively high international interest rates and the appreciation of the dollar could worsen financing conditions for emerging economies. (ECLAC, Economic Survey 2026 press release, 20 August 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Part of the regional slowdown. It also indicates that a declining international context―marked by lower worldwide growth, greater geopolitical tensions, financial volatility, and pressures in energy markets―partly explain the deceleration predicted for 2026. (ECLAC, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • The projection for South America. South America is expected to grow 2.5% in both 2026 and 2027. (ECLAC, 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Hunger and food prices

  • Hunger is one of the region’s hardest goals. El logro del ODS 2, referido a la erradicación del hambre, también se enfrenta a grandes desafíos: la desnutrición y la malnutrición persisten, y la volatilidad de los precios de los alimentos agrava la inseguridad alimentaria. (unofficial translation: “Achieving SDG 2, on ending hunger, also faces major challenges: undernourishment and malnutrition persist, and volatile food prices worsen food insecurity.”) (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Scope: the source covers all of Latin America and the Caribbean, not South America alone.
  • Four hunger targets stalled. In CEPAL’s 2026 chart for Latin America and the Caribbean, targets 2.1, 2.2, 2.4 and 2.c are in the group marked La tendencia es de estancamiento o retroceso (unofficial translation: “The trend is stagnation or regression”) (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab), figure)
  • Undernourishment in South America. 3.8% of people in 2015 and 3.5% in 2025. It rose between 2015 and 2024 in Bolivia (16.1% to 19.5%), Ecuador (8.8% to 10.8%), Suriname (9.5% to 12.7%) and Paraguay (under 2.5% to 4.1%) (UN SDG Global Database, series SN_ITK_DEFC (opens in a new tab), FAO estimates).
  • Weather and food systems. Extreme weather and climate hits agro-food systems is a key message of WMO’s 2025 report for the region (WMO, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Opportunities

External · helpful

The EU–MERCOSUR trade agreement

  • Applied since 1 May 2026. The agreement became binding under international law upon the start of its provisional application on 1 May 2026, following the completion of all the required internal procedures by the parties. (European Commission, DG Trade (opens in a new tab))
  • All four MERCOSUR countries. As all four Mercosur countries completed their domestic procedures in March, the iTA will be provisionally applied as of 1 May. The four are Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay; the agreements were signed on 17 January 2026 (European Commission (opens in a new tab))
  • Not yet fully in force. The iTA will enter fully into force after the European Parliament has given its consent. The EMPA will have to be ratified by all EU Member States before entering into force. (European Commission (opens in a new tab); iTA = interim Trade Agreement, EMPA = EU-Mercosur Partnership Agreement)

Fast growth in solar and wind

  • Up 30% in a year. Solar and wind experienced a remarkable 30% increase in capacity and generation compared to 2023. (WMO, 2025 (opens in a new tab), 2024 data). Scope: the source covers all of Latin America and the Caribbean, not South America alone.
  • Renewable targets met. El ODS 7 constituye un ejemplo de progreso, dado que varias de sus metas, como las relacionadas con la participación creciente de las energías renovables y la mejora de la eficiencia energética, ya se han alcanzado. (unofficial translation: “SDG 7 is an example of progress, as several of its targets, such as those on the growing share of renewable energy and improved energy efficiency, have already been met.”) (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab), Latin America and the Caribbean)
  • A rising share. Renewables supplied 33.37% of South America’s total final energy consumption in 2015 and 38.78% in 2023 (UN SDG Global Database, series EG_FEC_RNEW (opens in a new tab)).

The current renewable share is listed under Strengths; this item covers the growth.

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · SDG 7.2 renewables

Marine protection on track

  • Progress CEPAL records. Por su parte, el ODS 14 presenta algunos avances en lo que respecta a la conservación de las zonas costeras y marinas y la regulación de las subvenciones a la pesca, lo que consolida el compromiso de los países con la sostenibilidad de los océanos. (unofficial translation: “SDG 14, for its part, shows some progress in conserving coastal and marine areas and regulating fisheries subsidies, consolidating countries’ commitment to ocean sustainability.”) (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Scope: the source covers all of Latin America and the Caribbean, not South America alone.
  • Three ocean targets met or likely. Targets 14.5, 14.6 and 14.7 are in the group marked La meta se alcanzó o es probable que se alcance con la tendencia actual (unofficial translation: “The target has been met or is likely to be met on the current trend”); target 14.2 is in the stagnation or regression group (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab), figure)
  • Large new protected waters. Share of territorial waters in marine protected areas, 2015 to 2025: Chile 4.4% to 41.2%; Colombia 2.1% to 41.1%; Brazil 1.6% to 26.6% (World Bank WDI, ER.MRN.PTMR.ZS (opens in a new tab), UNEP-WCMC data).

This is progress already recorded. It is listed under Opportunities, as in Hawkeye’s research notes; it could also be read as a strength.

Also in GAP · SDG 14.5 marine areas

A shared Amazon observatory

  • Decided in 2023. The eight Amazon states decided to Institucionalizar el Observatorio Regional de la Amazonía (ORA), en la estructura de la OTCA, con el fortalecimiento de sus diferentes módulos en los temas económicos, sociales, ambientales y culturales, como un instrumento permanente de monitoreo, consolidación de informaciones, datos y conocimiento, avalados por los Estados Parte (unofficial translation: “institutionalise the Amazon Regional Observatory (ORA) within ACTO’s structure, strengthening its economic, social, environmental and cultural modules, as a permanent instrument for monitoring and consolidating information, data and knowledge endorsed by the States Parties”) (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), paragraph 8, p. 10)
  • Real-time access for members. La OTCA facilitará el acceso en tiempo real al ORA y a sus distintos módulos a los Estados Parte y sus instituciones (unofficial translation: “ACTO will facilitate real-time access to the ORA and its modules for the States Parties and their institutions”) (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), paragraph 8)

Whether the observatory now operates as the declaration describes was not checked.

W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Low investment and informal work

  • A structural brake on growth. ECLAC states that the main limitation for regional growth is structural in nature: low levels of investment, decreased dynamism in formal job creation, and high and persistent labor informality. (ECLAC, 20 August 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Almost half of workers are informal. The region is also facing an undynamic, segmented and exclusionary labor market, with high levels of informality that affect 47% of the region’s employed persons. (ECLAC, Social Panorama 2025 press release (opens in a new tab))
  • Formalisation slowed after 2013. This process has been less robust since 2014, along with the deceleration of investment and stalling of productivity. Currently, nearly half of employed people in the region continue to work in informal activities. (ECLAC, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Little fiscal room. Regarding fiscal matters, the stabilization of gross public debt at approximately 52% of GDP in Latin America has not been sufficient to rebuild the fiscal space. (ECLAC, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Workers’ share of output fell. The labour share of GDP in South America was 55.1% in 2015 and 53.6% in 2025 (UN SDG Global Database, series SL_EMP_GTOTL (opens in a new tab), ILO estimates).

Scope: the ECLAC sources cover Latin America and the Caribbean (debt: Latin America); the labour-share figure is for South America.

Also in PESTLE · Economic · GAP · SDG 10.4 labour share

Concentrated income and falling social spending

  • The top tenth gets a third. ECLAC warns that income concentration continues to be extreme in Latin America, since the wealthiest 10% obtains 34.2% of total income, while the poorest 10% only gets 1.7% (ECLAC, 26 November 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • The highest Gini of any world region. Although it exhibits a slow downward trend, the average Gini coefficient for Latin America and the Caribbean is the highest of all regions in the world – and is only lower than a subregion of Africa (sub-Saharan Africa) – surpassing that of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) by 14 percentage points, according to ECLAC. (ECLAC, 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Social spending per person fell in South America. In South America, social spending per capita fell by 1.4% on average in 2024, in contrast to the 3.5% increase seen in 2023. (ECLAC, 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Poverty at a record low, extreme poverty above 2014. 25.5% of Latin Americans (162 million people) lived in income poverty in 2024, the lowest since comparable data began; extreme poverty affected 9.8% (62 million), 2.1 percentage points higher than the rate registered in 2014 (ECLAC, 2025 (opens in a new tab))

Scope: income, poverty and Gini figures cover Latin America (Gini: Latin America and the Caribbean); the social-spending figure is for South America.

Also in PESTLE · Social

MERCOSUR’s common tariff still has national exceptions

  • A common market due by 1994. Los Estados Partes deciden constituir un Mercado Común, que deberá estar conformado al 31 de diciembre de 1994, el que se denominará "Mercado Común del Sur" (MERCOSUR). (unofficial translation: “The States Parties decide to establish a Common Market, which is to be in place by 31 December 1994 and will be called the ‘Southern Common Market’ (MERCOSUR).”) It includes El establecimiento de un arancel externo común (“the establishment of a common external tariff”) (Tratado de Asunción (opens in a new tab), Art. 1, signed 26 March 1991)
  • Exceptions renewed in 2025. Cada Estado Parte podrá mantener una Lista Nacional de Excepciones (LNE) al Arancel Externo Común (AEC), en los siguientes términos: (unofficial translation: “Each State Party may keep a National List of Exceptions to the Common External Tariff, on the following terms:”) Argentina and Brazil up to 100 tariff codes each until 31 December 2028; Paraguay up to 649 until 31 December 2030; Uruguay up to 225 until 31 December 2029 (Decision CMC 01/25, Montevideo, 25 June 2025 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1)
  • A further temporary list. Article 2 allows each State Party a temporary list of up to 50 more codes para la adaptación al nuevo contexto internacional (“to adapt to the new international context”), to the same end dates (Decision CMC 01/25 (opens in a new tab), Art. 2)
  • National incorporation. Esta Decisión deberá ser incorporada por los Estados Partes. (“This Decision must be incorporated by the States Parties.”) Uruguay did so by Decreto 211/025 of 13 October 2025 (IMPO (opens in a new tab), Art. 7). Incorporation by the other three was not checked.

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

A high share of renewable energy

  • Nearly 69% of the energy mix. A positive development in the report was the increasingly important role of renewable energy – at nearly 69% of the region’s energy mix. (WMO, 2025 (opens in a new tab), 2024 data). Scope: the source covers all of Latin America and the Caribbean, not South America alone.
  • Twice the world’s share of final energy use. Renewables supplied 38.78% of South America’s total final energy consumption in 2023, against 18% worldwide (UN SDG Global Database, series EG_FEC_RNEW (opens in a new tab)). This is a different measure from WMO’s “energy mix”.
  • Highest shares. Paraguay 62.68%, Uruguay 60.47% and Brazil 50.18% of total final energy consumption in 2023 (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · SDG 7.2 renewables

A small share of fossil CO2

  • 2.9% of the world’s fossil CO2. South America emitted 1,110 million tonnes in 2024, out of 38,599 million tonnes worldwide, while home to 5.3% of the world’s people (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s sums).
  • About half the world average per person. 2.5 tonnes per person in 2024, against 4.7 worldwide (Hawkeye’s division of the same totals by UN population estimates).
  • 2.6% of all fossil CO2 since 1850. 47,489 million tonnes over 1850–2024, out of 1,844,551 worldwide (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Fossil fuel and cement CO2 only; emissions from land-use change, such as deforestation, are not included in these figures.

No nuclear weapons

  • None of the nine nuclear-armed states. SIPRI counts 12,187 warheads in January 2026, held by Russia, the United States, China, France, the United Kingdom, India, Pakistan, Israel and North Korea. No South American country is among them (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 2% of world military spending. The 12 countries spent US$56.3 billion in 2025, 2.0% of the world total; SIPRI has no 2025 figure for Venezuela (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s sum).

The Escazú Agreement on environmental rights

  • What it does. Adoptado en Escazú, Costa Rica, el 4 de marzo de 2018, el Acuerdo Regional tiene por objetivo garantizar la implementación plena y efectiva en América Latina y el Caribe de los derechos de acceso a la información ambiental, participación pública en los procesos de toma de decisiones ambientales y acceso a la justicia en asuntos ambientales (unofficial translation: “Adopted in Escazú, Costa Rica, on 4 March 2018, the Regional Agreement aims to guarantee the full and effective implementation in Latin America and the Caribbean of the rights of access to environmental information, public participation in environmental decision-making and access to justice in environmental matters”) (CEPAL, Acuerdo de Escazú (opens in a new tab))
  • In force since 2021. Tras alcanzar los requisitos establecidos en su artículo 22, el Acuerdo de Escazú entró en vigor el 22 de abril de 2021. (unofficial translation: “Having met the requirements of its article 22, the Escazú Agreement entered into force on 22 April 2021.”) (CEPAL (opens in a new tab))
  • Seven South American parties. Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Guyana and Uruguay are parties. Brazil, Paraguay and Peru have signed but not ratified. Suriname and Venezuela have not signed (UN Treaty Collection (opens in a new tab), status read 19 October 2026).

People and longevity

  • 438 million people. 438,105,374 people in 2025, 5.3% of the world’s population (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab), 1 July 2025 estimate)
  • Living longer than the world average. Life expectancy at birth of 76.6 years in 2025, against 73.5 worldwide (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab))
  • Highest life expectancy. Chile 81.5 years, Uruguay 78.5, Peru 78.1, Colombia 78.1, Ecuador 77.8 (2025; UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab))
  • Space. 24.9 people per km², against 63.1 worldwide (2025; UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab))
