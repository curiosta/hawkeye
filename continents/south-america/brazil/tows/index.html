Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Brazil has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Grow solar manufacturing and generation on Brazil’s already high renewable electricity share

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Mostly renewable electricity · SWOT · Opportunities · Fast growth in solar power

      Use Brazil’s already high renewable electricity share as the base for further solar generation and, where it pays off, for more of the solar supply chain. Official Brazilian energy programmes already exist, and solar capacity is already growing fast. This recommendation pairs that strength with the solar opportunity.

      Analogous case. The IEA reports: China has invested over USD 50 billion in new PV supply capacity – ten times more than Europe − and created as many as 300 000 manufacturing jobs across the solar PV value chain since 2011. Today, China’s share in all the manufacturing stages of solar panels (such as polysilicon, ingots, wafers, cells and modules) exceeds 80%. Those policies contributed to a cost decline more than 80% (IEA, Special Report on Solar PV Global Supply Chains, revised August 2022 (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Renewables already made up 86.8% of Brazil’s electricity mix in 2025, and solar capacity rose 33.7% in the latest official year on the live SWOT. Lower global module costs, which the IEA links to the Chinese build-out, can support that growth. The analogy is limited because the IEA also warns about geographic concentration, and because much of Brazil’s existing renewable share is hydropower, which faces drought risk. A manufacturing chain on the Chinese scale is not a realistic near-term copy.

    • Lock in record-low Amazon clearing with verified payments as EU trade opens

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Lowest Amazon deforestation on record · SWOT · Opportunities · A trade deal with the European Union

      Use verified forest payments, and the new EU trade framework, to lock in the record-low Amazon clearing rate. Official Brazilian programmes already include the Amazon deforestation plan and the EU trade agreement. This recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from a documented national reversal of forest loss, not a claim that Brazil has no forest policy.

      Analogous case. The World Bank records Costa Rica’s result: It is the first tropical country in the world to have reversed deforestation. Its highly biodiverse tropical rainforests now cover close to 60% of the country, having shrunk as low as 40% in 1987. Costa Rica also received a first Forest Carbon Partnership Facility payment of $16.4 million for independently verified emission reductions, for reducing 3.28 million tons of carbon emissions in 2018 and 2019 (World Bank, 16 November 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Amazon clearing fell to 3,335 km² in the August 2025-July 2026 year, the lowest in the series that starts in 1988, and the EU trade deal is a live opportunity (live SWOT). Costa Rica shows that long-running payments and later results-based carbon finance can go with a reversal of deforestation. The analogy is limited because Costa Rica is small, and the Amazon is far larger and transboundary in part. One FCPF payment does not test protection at Amazon scale. Commodity demand and land rights remain harder. The live Cerrado threat also shows that a fall in Amazon clearing can sit beside still-high clearing in another biome.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Extend the Amazon payment approach to the Cerrado, where clearing remains larger

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Lowest Amazon deforestation on record · SWOT · Threats · Clearing in the Cerrado

      Extend verified payment and enforcement tools that helped bring Amazon clearing to a record low, so that Cerrado clearing can keep falling from 5,517 km². Official Brazilian forest programmes already exist. This recommendation pairs that Amazon strength with the live Cerrado threat.

      Analogous case. The World Bank records Costa Rica’s result: It is the first tropical country in the world to have reversed deforestation. Its highly biodiverse tropical rainforests now cover close to 60% of the country, having shrunk as low as 40% in 1987. Costa Rica also received a first Forest Carbon Partnership Facility payment of $16.4 million for independently verified emission reductions, for reducing 3.28 million tons of carbon emissions in 2018 and 2019 (World Bank, 16 November 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Cerrado clearing in the latest official year on the live SWOT was still larger than Amazon clearing, even after a 23.8% fall. A payment model documented in Costa Rica can change landholder incentives in a second biome. The analogy is limited because the Cerrado is a savanna, not the tropical rainforest of Costa Rica or the Amazon, and agriculture is also Brazil’s largest source of emissions (a live weakness). A forest-payment scheme does not automatically change cattle and soya incentives in the Cerrado. Costa Rica is much smaller.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Use targeted transfers, not the trade deal alone, to cut remaining poverty

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Poverty and inequality · SWOT · Opportunities · A trade deal with the European Union

      Use targeted household support, alongside the EU trade agreement, to cut the remaining poverty rate. Official Brazilian programmes already include Bolsa Família, and the live SWOT already records a fall from 27.3% to 23.1% in 2024. This recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from a documented targeted-support case, not a claim that Brazil has no transfer policy.

      Analogous case. A joint World Bank study with Chinese institutions states: Over the past 40 years, the number of people in China with incomes below $1.90 per day – the International Poverty Line as defined by the World Bank to track global extreme poverty– has fallen by close to 800 million. With this, China has contributed close to three-quarters of the global reduction in the number of people living in extreme poverty. At China’s national poverty line, the number of poor fell by 770 million over the same period. The report points to two pillars: broad-based economic transformation, and targeted support to places and later to households (World Bank, 1 April 2022 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Brazil still had 23.1% of people in poverty in 2024 on a US$6.85 line, and the EU trade deal is a live opportunity (live SWOT). The Chinese case shows that targeted support mattered alongside growth and trade openness. The $1.90 line is not the same as Brazil’s US$6.85 line, and China’s starting conditions and institutions differ. A trade agreement does not by itself close inequality. Bolsa Família already operates in this area and is acknowledged.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
