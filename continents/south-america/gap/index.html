CEPAL’s 2026 assessment for Latin America and the Caribbean: De las 134 metas evaluadas, el 61% presenta resultados positivos en cuanto a la trayectoria observada. Sin embargo, se prevé que solo en el 19% de los casos se logrará alcanzar los umbrales estipulados para 2030. (unofficial translation: “Of the 134 targets assessed, 61% show positive results in their observed trajectory. However, only 19% are expected to reach the thresholds set for 2030.”) (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The status labels below are CEPAL’s, for that whole region. The values are for South America where the UN publishes a South America figure, and for Latin America and the Caribbean where it does not.

Targets and latest values

Hunger (SDG 2.1)

Target
“2.1 By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
3.8% of people undernourished in South America (2015); Latin America and the Caribbean 5.1% (UN SDG Global Database, SN_ITK_DEFC (opens in a new tab))
Latest
3.5% (2025); Latin America and the Caribbean 4.8% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab), FAO estimates)
Distance
Not computed. “End hunger” is not a single number, and FAO does not publish values below 2.5% for countries.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean: target 2.1 is in the group marked La tendencia es de estancamiento o retroceso (“the trend is stagnation or regression”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Renewable energy (SDG 7.2)

Target
“7.2 By 2030, increase substantially the share of renewable energy in the global energy mix” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
33.37% of total final energy consumption in South America (2015) (UN SDG Global Database, EG_FEC_RNEW (opens in a new tab))
Latest
38.78% (2023); Latin America and the Caribbean 33.21%; world 18% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. The target sets no number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean: target 7.2 is in the group marked La meta se alcanzó o es probable que se alcance con la tendencia actual (“the target has been met or is likely to be met on the current trend”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Labour share of GDP (SDG 10.4)

Target
“10.4 Adopt policies, especially fiscal, wage and social protection policies, and progressively achieve greater equality” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
55.1% of GDP in South America (2015) (UN SDG Global Database, SL_EMP_GTOTL (opens in a new tab))
Latest
53.6% (2025); Latin America and the Caribbean 49.2%; world 52.6% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab), ILO estimates)
Distance
Not computed. The target sets no number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean: target 10.4 is in the group marked La tendencia es de estancamiento o retroceso (“the trend is stagnation or regression”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Marine protected areas (SDG 14.5)

Target
“14.5 By 2020, conserve at least 10 per cent of coastal and marine areas, consistent with national and international law and based on the best available scientific information” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Latin America and the Caribbean, 2015: 39.37% of marine Key Biodiversity Areas inside protected areas (UN SDG Global Database, ER_MRN_MPA (opens in a new tab)); 2.97% of territorial waters protected (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab))
Latest
2025: 44.10% of marine Key Biodiversity Areas; 22.24% of territorial waters. Chile 41.2%, Colombia 41.1%, Brazil 26.6% of territorial waters (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab); World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. Neither published series measures “coastal and marine areas” exactly as the target words it, and no South America total is published.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean: target 14.5 is in the group marked La meta se alcanzó o es probable que se alcance con la tendencia actual (“the target has been met or is likely to be met on the current trend”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Forest area (SDG 15.1)

Target
“15.1 By 2020, ensure the conservation, restoration and sustainable use of terrestrial and inland freshwater ecosystems and their services, in particular forests, wetlands, mountains and drylands, in line with obligations under international agreements” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Forest was 50.94% of South America’s land area (2015) (UN SDG Global Database, AG_LND_FRST (opens in a new tab))
Latest
48.59% (2025); Latin America and the Caribbean 47.03% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab), FAO data)
Distance
Not computed. The target sets no forest-area number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean: target 15.1 is in the group marked La tendencia es la correcta, pero el avance es demasiado lento para alcanzar la meta (“the trend is right, but progress is too slow to reach the target”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Threatened species (SDG 15.5)

Target
“15.5 Take urgent and significant action to reduce the degradation of natural habitats, halt the loss of biodiversity and, by 2020, protect and prevent the extinction of threatened species” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Red List Index for Latin America and the Caribbean 0.77446 (2015; 1 = no species threatened, 0 = all extinct) (UN SDG Global Database, ER_RSK_LST (opens in a new tab))
Latest
0.76671 (2025); world 0.74491 (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab)). No South America figure is published.
Distance
Not computed. “Halt the loss” would mean an index that stops falling; it has fallen since 2015.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean: target 15.5 is in the group marked La tendencia es de estancamiento o retroceso (“the trend is stagnation or regression”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

MERCOSUR common market and tariff

Target
A common market by 31 December 1994, including El establecimiento de un arancel externo común (“the establishment of a common external tariff”) (Tratado de Asunción (opens in a new tab), Art. 1)
Baseline
Treaty signed 26 March 1991 by Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay
Latest
National exception lists to the common tariff renewed on 25 June 2025: up to 100 tariff codes each for Argentina and Brazil until 2028, 649 for Paraguay until 2030 and 225 for Uruguay until 2029, plus temporary lists of up to 50 each (Decision CMC 01/25 (opens in a new tab), Arts. 1–2)
Distance
Not computed. Exceptions are allowed until 2028–2030 under the 2025 decision.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Amazon states’ common 2030 deforestation targets

Target
The Belém Declaration stresses la urgencia de acordar metas comunes al 2030 para el combate a la deforestación (“the urgency of agreeing common 2030 targets to fight deforestation”), con el ideal de llegar a la deforestación cero en la Región (“with the ideal of reaching zero deforestation in the Region”) (Declaración de Belém (opens in a new tab), preamble, 9 August 2023)
Baseline
No common numeric target was set in 2023
Latest
No ACTO text setting a common numeric 2030 target was found. The 2025 Declaración de Bogotá commits to work on deforestation mediante metas cuantificables, basadas en la mejor evidencia científica disponible (“through quantifiable targets based on the best available scientific evidence”), in line with national climate plans (Declaración de Bogotá, 22 August 2025 (opens in a new tab), paragraph 7)
Distance
Not applicable. No common target has been agreed.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 17–19 October 2026. Spanish quotations are given as published; English translations are unofficial. Sources that cover all of Latin America and the Caribbean are labelled.

How this GAP analysis works

For a continent, the targets are those set by the regional or treaty bodies that govern it, plus the global targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
