Political 2 points

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • Military spending. US$954.4 billion in 2025, 33.1% of the world total of US$2,887 billion and 3.1% of GDP; US$1,004.9 billion in 2024 (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database, v1.2 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Nuclear warheads. 5,042 in January 2026, 41.4% of the world’s 12,187: a military stockpile of 3,700 (1,770 of them deployed) and 1,342 retired (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab)).

Economic 3 points

Economy and finance

  • Output. GDP was US$30,861.3 billion in 2025, US$29,381.0 billion in 2024 and US$27,825.9 billion in 2023 (BEA, 30 September 2026 (opens in a new tab), after the 2026 annual update). The 2025 figure matches the IMF’s.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · The largest economy · SWOT · Opportunities · Trade with partner countries and foreign purchases of US services

Economy and finance

  • Deficits. The federal deficit was US$1,832.8 billion in fiscal 2024, US$1,775.4 billion in fiscal 2025 and US$1,992.5 billion in fiscal 2026 (Monthly Treasury Statement (opens in a new tab)). As a share of GDP (Hawkeye’s arithmetic with BEA GDP as revised in 2026): 6.3% in 2024 and 5.8% in 2025; 2026: TBC.
  • Debt. Debt held by the public was US$28,307 billion on 30 September 2024 and US$32,425 billion on 30 September 2026 (Treasury, Debt to the Penny (opens in a new tab)).

Social 4 points

Population and demographics

  • Population. 347,275,808 people on 1 July 2025, with a median age of 38.5 years and life expectancy at birth of 79.6 years (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab), medium variant). The Census Bureau estimate for the same date is 341,784,857 (Census Bureau, Vintage 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Life expectancy, national figure. Life expectancy for the U.S. population in 2023 was 78.4 years, an increase of 0.9 year from 2022. (CDC NCHS Data Brief 521 (opens in a new tab)). The 2024 figure has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Human development

  • Human development. HDI 0.938, rank 17 of 193, very high (data year 2023) (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Poverty. The official poverty rate fell 0.5 percentage points to 10.2% in 2025. There were 34.5 million people in poverty in 2025. On the Supplemental Poverty Measure the rate was 13.1%, not statistically different from 2024 (Census Bureau, 15 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Technological 2 points

Technology and AI

  • Artificial intelligence policy. Executive Order 14179 of 23 January 2025, Sec. 2: It is the policy of the United States to sustain and enhance America's global AI dominance in order to promote human flourishing, economic competitiveness, and national security. (90 FR 8741 (opens in a new tab)). It followed the revocation on 20 January 2025 of the 2023 order on AI safety, Executive Order 14110 (Executive Order 14148 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 2(ggg)). Later AI measures have not been checked for this page (TBC).

Technology and AI

  • Semiconductor fund. The CHIPS Act of 2022, Sec. 102(a)(1): There is established in the Treasury of the United States a fund to be known as the “Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors (CHIPS) for America Fund” (referred to in this subsection as the “Fund”) for the Secretary of Commerce to carry out sections 9902, 9904, and 9906 of the William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021 (15 U.S.C. 4652, 4654, and 4656; Public Law 116-283). (P.L. 117-167 (opens in a new tab), Division A). Award status and changes since 2025 have not been checked for this page (TBC).

Legal 4 points

Economy and finance

  • Spending caps, now expired. The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 capped discretionary budget authority for fiscal 2024 at $886,349,000,000 (revised security) and $703,651,000,000 (revised nonsecurity), and for fiscal 2025 at $895,212,000,000 and $710,688,000,000 (P.L. 118-5 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 101). The caps end with fiscal 2025; see the GAP page.
  • Debt limit raised in 2025. Public Law 119-21 of 4 July 2025, Sec. 72001: The limitation under section 3101(b) of title 31, United States Code, as most recently increased by section 401(b) of Public Law 118-5 (31 U.S.C. 3101 note), is increased by $5,000,000,000,000. (P.L. 119-21 (opens in a new tab)).

Climate change / global warming

  • Out of the Paris Agreement. The UN treaty depositary records: On 27 January 2025, the Government of the United States of America notified the Secretary-General of its decision to withdraw from the Agreement which took effect on 27 January 2026 in accordance with article 28 (1) and (2) of the Agreement (UN Treaty Collection (opens in a new tab), endnote 7).
  • Leaving the UN climate convention. On 27 February 2026, the Government of the United States of America notified the Secretary-General of its decision to withdraw from the Convention, which will take effect on 27 February 2027 in accordance with article 25 (2) of the Convention. (UN Treaty Collection (opens in a new tab), endnote 9). The withdrawal was directed by a presidential memorandum of 7 January 2026 (91 FR 2281 (opens in a new tab)).

Environmental 3 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Emissions. 4,904 Mt of fossil CO2 in 2024, 12.7% of the world total, and 434,696 Mt since 1850, 23.6% of the world total and the largest of any country (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); shares are Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Per person. 14.2 tonnes in 2024, three times the world average of 4.73 t (GCB 2025 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s arithmetic with UN WPP 2024 population).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · The largest cumulative emitter of fossil CO2

Energy transition

  • Clean electricity. Nuclear and renewable sources made 40.8% of utility-scale electricity in 2024 (nuclear 18.1%, renewables 22.7%) and 41.8% in 2025 (17.7% and 24.1%) (US EIA, Electric Power Monthly, Table 1.1 (opens in a new tab); shares are Hawkeye’s arithmetic; small-scale solar not included). The withdrawn 2035 goal is on the GAP page.

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a country, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups: politics and government, the economy, society and population, technology, laws, and the environment. Official national sources come first.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact. Spanish quotations are given as published, with unofficial English translations.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
