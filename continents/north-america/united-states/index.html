T

Threats

External · harmful

Costly weather and climate disasters

  • 27 billion-dollar disasters in 2024. In 2024, there were 27 individual weather and climate disasters with at least $1 billion in damages, trailing only the record-setting 28 events analyzed in 2023. These disasters caused at least 568 direct or indirect fatalities, which is the eighth-highest for these billion-dollar disasters over the last 45 years (1980-2024). The cost was approximately $182.7 billion. (NOAA Climate.gov, 10 January 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A series that ends with 2024. NOAA’s own data give 27 events, a cost of US$182.7 billion adjusted for inflation, and 568 deaths for 2024 (NOAA NCEI (opens in a new tab)). NOAA has retired the series with no updates beyond calendar year 2024, so 2024 is its last year. An official figure for 2025 has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

State-sponsored cyber actors in critical infrastructure

  • An assessment by three agencies. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), National Security Agency (NSA), and Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) assess that People’s Republic of China (PRC) state-sponsored cyber actors are seeking to pre-position themselves on IT networks for disruptive or destructive cyberattacks against U.S. critical infrastructure in the event of a major crisis or conflict with the United States. (CISA, NSA and FBI, advisory AA24-038A, 7 February 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Compromises confirmed. The U.S. authoring agencies have confirmed that Volt Typhoon has compromised the IT environments of multiple critical infrastructure organizations—primarily in Communications, Energy, Transportation Systems, and Water and Wastewater Systems Sectors—in the continental and non-continental United States and its territories, including Guam. (AA24-038A (opens in a new tab)). Later advisories (2025–26) have not been checked for this page (TBC).

Large and growing foreign nuclear arsenals

  • Russia 5,420 and China 620 warheads. In January 2026 Russia held 5,420 nuclear warheads and China 620, out of a world total of 12,187 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), table of world nuclear forces).
  • China’s arsenal growing. The USA and Russia together possess almost 86 per cent of all nuclear warheads and both have extensive nuclear modernization programmes under way. China is also in the middle of a significant modernization and expansion of its nuclear arsenal, which is estimated to have increased from 600 to up to 620 warheads during the year. (SIPRI (opens in a new tab)). The US arsenal is under Strengths below.

Overdose deaths from trafficked synthetic opioids

  • About 38,500 deaths in 2025. Synthetic opioids other than methadone, mainly illicit fentanyl, were involved in 38,506 of 70,735 US overdose deaths in 2025 (54%), down from 74,792 in 2023 (CDC NCHS, provisional data as of 14 October 2026 (opens in a new tab); the share is Hawkeye’s arithmetic). Provisional counts change with each monthly release.
  • Overdose deaths falling. Provisional data from CDC's National Center for Health Statistics indicate there were an estimated 69,973 drug overdose deaths in the United States during 2025—a decrease of almost 14% from the 81,313 deaths estimated in 2024. (CDC NCHS, 13 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Trafficked by foreign cartels. The Drug Enforcement Administration’s 2025 threat assessment covers the violent domestic and international drug trafficking organizations responsible for producing and trafficking the drugs poisoning our communities, including the Sinaloa Cartel and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel (CJNG), which the US has designated Foreign Terrorist Organizations (DEA, 15 May 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Where the drugs and their chemical precursors are made has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Dependence on imported critical minerals

  • 13 critical minerals fully imported. Of the 58 mineral commodities included in the Final 2025 List of Critical Minerals (excluding metallurgical coal and uranium), the United States was 100% net import reliant for 13 mineral commodities, and an additional 20 critical mineral commodities (including 14 lanthanides, which are listed under rare earths) had a net import reliance greater than 50% of apparent consumption. (USGS, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 8).
  • China the leading supplier. China was the leading supplier of eight of these mineral commodities and was a major supplier of six others. (USGS (opens in a new tab)). Across all nonfuel minerals, imports were more than half of US consumption for 54 in 2025. Earlier editions used a list of 50 critical minerals, so the counts are not a trend.

Opportunities

External · helpful

Foreign demand for US liquefied natural gas

  • The largest LNG exporter. LNG exports from the United States increased by 26% to 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025, a larger increase than from any other country, according to our Natural Gas Monthly. U.S. exports amounted to 26% of the global total in 2025, up from 21% in 2024. (US Energy Information Administration, 14 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Qatar, the next largest, exported 10.6 billion cubic feet a day (Hawkeye’s comparison of EIA figures).
  • Europe the main buyer in 2024. Europe (including Türkiye) remained the primary destination for U.S. LNG exports in 2024, accounting for 53% (6.3 Bcf/d) (US EIA, 27 March 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Gulf supply cut in 2026. The EIA also reports: Qatari exports have fallen in 2026 due to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz since February 28, which has cut off approximately 20% of global LNG supplies. (US EIA (opens in a new tab)).

Trade with partner countries and foreign purchases of US services

  • Mexico and Canada the largest goods partners. In 2025, two-way goods trade with Mexico was US$872.8 billion (15.6% of the US total) and with Canada US$719.5 billion (12.8%); China followed with US$414.7 billion (7.4%) (BEA and Census, FT-900, Exhibit 14 (opens in a new tab); exports plus imports, Census basis, Hawkeye’s arithmetic). These figures have since been revised; the revised values have not been confirmed for this page (TBC).
  • 2024 for comparison. Mexico US$839.9 billion (15.8%), Canada US$762.1 billion (14.3%) and China US$582.5 billion (10.9%) (US Census Bureau, Top Trading Partners, December 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Foreign purchases of US services. Exports of services increased $82.1 billion to $1,234.9 billion in 2025. Other business services increased $26.2 billion. Charges for the use of intellectual property increased $21.9 billion. Financial services increased $14.3 billion. The services surplus rose by $27.6 billion, or 8.9 percent, to $339.5 billion. (BEA, 19 February 2026 (opens in a new tab); since revised, TBC).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Australia’s purchase of US submarines (AUKUS)

  • Up to three Virginia-class submarines. US law allows sales to Australia: the President is authorized to transfer up to two Virginia Class submarines from the inventory of the Department of the Navy to the Government of Australia on a sale basis, and transfer not more than one additional Virginia Class submarine to the Government of Australia on a sale basis (National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 1352(a)).
  • Paid for by Australia. Any expense incurred by the United States in connection with a transfer of a vessel authorized under subsection (a) shall be charged to the Government of Australia (Sec. 1352(c)), and The President may accept from the Government of Australia contributions of money made by the Government of Australia for use by the Department of Defense in support of non-nuclear related aspects of submarine security activities (Sec. 1353(a)) (P.L. 118-31 (opens in a new tab)). How much Australia has paid, and the outcome of the 2025 Pentagon review of AUKUS, have not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

The largest cumulative emitter of fossil CO2

  • 434,696 Mt since 1850. The United States emitted 434,696 Mt of fossil CO2 from 1850 to 2024, 23.6% of the world total of 1,844,551 Mt and more than any other country; China is next, at 285,087 Mt (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); sums and share are Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • 12.7% of the world’s emissions in 2024. 4,904 Mt in 2024, out of 38,599 Mt worldwide, or 14.2 tonnes per person against a world average of 4.73 t (GCB 2025 (opens in a new tab); per person: Hawkeye’s arithmetic with UN WPP 2024 population). See the GAP page for the withdrawn 2030 pledge.

Federal deficits and debt

  • A US$1.99 trillion deficit in fiscal 2026. This issue includes the final budget results and details a deficit of $1.992 trillion for Fiscal Year 2026. (Monthly Treasury Statement, September 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The deficit was US$1,775.4 billion in fiscal 2025, 5.8% of GDP (Hawkeye’s arithmetic with BEA GDP). The 2026 deficit as a share of GDP has not been confirmed at an official source for this page (TBC).
  • 6.4% of GDP in fiscal 2024. Year-end data from the September 2024 Monthly Treasury Statement of Receipts and Outlays of the United States Government show that the deficit for FY 2024 was $1.8 trillion; $138 billion higher than the prior year’s deficit. As a percentage of GDP, the deficit was 6.4 percent, an increase from 6.2 percent in FY 2023. (Treasury and OMB, 18 October 2024 (opens in a new tab)). On GDP as revised in 2026, Hawkeye’s arithmetic gives 6.3%.
  • US$32.4 trillion held by the public. On 30 September 2026 debt held by the public was US$32,425.0 billion and total public debt outstanding US$40,171.8 billion (Treasury, Debt to the Penny (opens in a new tab)). The 2024 statement records that borrowing from the public grew from 96 percent of GDP at the end of FY 2023 to 98 percent of GDP at the end of FY 2024 (Treasury and OMB (opens in a new tab)).

Poverty

  • About 1 in 10 people in 2025. In 2025, the official poverty rate fell 0.5 percentage points to 10.2 percent, the third consecutive annual decline and one of the lowest rates on record (Figure 1). There were 34.5 million people in poverty in 2025. (Census Bureau, Poverty in the United States: 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 13.1% on the supplemental measure. The SPM rate in 2025 was 13.1 percent, not statistically different from 2024 (Census Bureau (opens in a new tab); SPM is the Supplemental Poverty Measure). The UN poverty yardstick is on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Poverty (UN yardstick)

People without health insurance

  • 26.7 million uninsured all year. In 2025, 26.7 million people (7.9%) were uninsured for the entire year, not statistically different from 2024. People are considered uninsured if, for the entire year, they were not covered by any type of insurance. (Census Bureau, 15 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 8.0% in 2023. That means 8.0 percent of people were uninsured for the entire calendar year. That was 26.4 million people (Census Bureau, Health Insurance Coverage in the United States: 2023 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in GAP · Health coverage (UN yardstick)

Lead pipes in drinking-water systems

  • An estimated 4 million lead service lines. With the latest data from 2025, EPA estimates that there are 4 million lead service lines across the country. These are 3 million reported lead service lines and 1 million lines of unknown material that EPA predicts are lead (EPA, November 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A new count, not pipes replaced. EPA’s 2023 survey said that the total projected LSL in the United States is 9.2 million (EPA, April 2023 (opens in a new tab)). The 2025 estimate uses water systems’ own inventories rather than a sample survey, so the fall from 9.2 million to 4 million reflects a change in counting method, not progress in replacing pipes. EPA states: There is no safe level of lead exposure. (EPA (opens in a new tab)). The replacement deadline is on the GAP page.

Strengths

Internal · helpful

The largest economy

  • US$30.9 trillion in 2025. GDP was US$30,861.3 billion in 2025 and US$29,381.0 billion in 2024, a rise of 5.0% in current dollars (BEA, 30 September 2026 (opens in a new tab), after the 2026 annual update; the rise is Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • 26.0% of world GDP. The IMF gives the same 2025 figure, 26.0% of the world’s US$118,485.3 billion and more than any other economy; China is next, at US$19,626.2 billion (IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 (opens in a new tab); the share is Hawkeye’s arithmetic).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

The largest military spending and nuclear arsenal

  • A third of world military spending. The USA’s military spending fell by 7.5 per cent to $954 billion in 2025, while China’s spending rose by 7.4 per cent to an estimated $336 billion. The USA remained the biggest spender, accounting for a third of all military spending in 2025, but the gap to the next largest spenders is shrinking. (SIPRI Fact Sheet, April 2026 (opens in a new tab)). In SIPRI’s database the figure is US$954,387 million, 33.1% of the world total and 3.1% of GDP; the US share was 4.3 percentage points lower than in 2024 (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)).
  • 5,042 nuclear warheads. In January 2026 the United States held 5,042 warheads, 41.4% of the world’s 12,187; 3,700 were in the military stockpile, 1,770 of them deployed (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab); share is Hawkeye’s arithmetic). SIPRI’s note on modernisation programmes is quoted under Threats above.

Also in PESTLE · Political

Large research and development spending

  • US$937 billion of R&D in 2023. The science statistics agency reports that research and experimental development (R&D) performed in the United States totaled $937 billion in 2023 (figure 1). The estimated total for 2024, based on performer-reported expectations, is $993 billion. (NCSES, InfoBrief NSF 26-314, 27 February 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Some 2023 data are preliminary and the 2024 figures are estimates.
  • Business funding at 2.54% of GDP. Since 2014, federal funding for R&D has remained at or below 0.70% of GDP, accounting for 0.63% of GDP in 2024. By contrast, business R&D funding, which was below 1.00% of GDP through the 1960s, had reached 1.81% in 2014 and increased to 2.54% by 2024. (NCSES (opens in a new tab)). An international ranking has not been checked for this page (TBC).

Very high human development

  • HDI 0.938, 17th of 193. The Human Development Index was 0.938 in 2023, in the very high category and 17th of 193 countries. In North America only Canada (0.939) ranks higher (UNDP Human Development Report 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Life expectancy near 80 years. 79.3 years in 2023 according to the HDR 2025, and 79.6 years in 2025 according to the UN (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
