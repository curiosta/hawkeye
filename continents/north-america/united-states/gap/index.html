United States GAP

Where things stand now compared with an officially agreed target.

Read this page with three facts in mind. Rows 1–4 are former federal targets. The executive orders behind rows 2–4 were revoked on 20 January 2025 (Executive Order 14148 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 2), and the 2030 climate pledge in row 1 lapsed when the US withdrawal from the Paris Agreement took effect on 27 January 2026 (UN Treaty Collection (opens in a new tab)). They are shown with the latest values so the record is complete. Rows 7–9 are UN yardsticks, not US Government targets. On 4 March 2025 the US mission to the UN stated: Therefore, the United States rejects and denounces the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development and the Sustainable Development Goals, and it will no longer reaffirm them as a matter of course. (US Mission to the UN, remarks as prepared (opens in a new tab)). Rows 5, 6 and 10 come from US laws and rules. Where a figure has not yet been confirmed at source, the cell says TBC.

Targets and latest values

Net emissions 50–52% below 2005 by 2030 (Paris pledge, withdrawn)

Target
The nationally determined contribution of the United States of America is: To achieve an economy-wide target of reducing its net greenhouse gas emissions by 50-52 percent below 2005 levels in 2030. (US NDC, April 2021 (opens in a new tab)). Withdrawn: the pledge lapsed with the US exit from the Paris Agreement, effective 27 January 2026 (UN Treaty Collection (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
2005: net emissions of 6,536.4 million tonnes CO2 equivalent (EPA, Inventory of U.S. Greenhouse Gas Emissions and Sinks 1990–2023 (opens in a new tab), Table ES-2). The NDC cited 6,635 Mt from the 2021 edition of the inventory and said the value may be adjusted.
Latest
2023: Net emissions, including emissions and sinks from the LULUCF sector, were 5,257.4 MMT CO2 Eq. in 2023. (EPA (opens in a new tab)). That is 19.6% below 2005 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic). A 2024 inventory figure has not been confirmed at source (TBC).
Distance
30.4–32.4 percentage points short of the 50–52% cut (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, 2023 against 2005). The target no longer applies.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

100% carbon-pollution-free electricity by 2035 (withdrawn)

Target
National goal in the NDC: Electricity: The United States has set a goal to reach 100 percent carbon pollution-free electricity by 2035 (US NDC, April 2021 (opens in a new tab)), withdrawn with the Paris exit. For federal operations only, Executive Order 14057 set 100 percent carbon pollution-free electricity on a net annual basis by 2030, including 50 percent 24/7 carbon pollution-free electricity (EO 14057 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 102(a)(i)); revoked on 20 January 2025 (EO 14148 (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
2020: 39.2% of utility-scale generation from nuclear and renewable sources (nuclear 19.7%, renewables 19.5%) (US EIA, Electric Power Monthly, Table 1.1 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s arithmetic). The NDC said approximately 39 percent.
Latest
2025: 41.8% (nuclear 17.7%, renewables 24.1%); 2024: 40.8% (US EIA (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s arithmetic; small-scale solar not included).
Distance
58.2 percentage points short of 100% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, 2025). The target no longer applies.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Half of new cars zero-emission by 2030 (revoked)

Target
Executive Order 14037, Sec. 1: setting a goal that 50 percent of all new passenger cars and light trucks sold in 2030 be zero-emission vehicles, including battery electric, plug-in hybrid electric, or fuel cell electric vehicles (EO 14037 (opens in a new tab)). Revoked on 20 January 2025 (EO 14148 (opens in a new tab)). Public Law 119-21 then ended the federal clean vehicle credit for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025 (P.L. 119-21 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 70502).
Baseline
2020 share: TBC (not yet confirmed at source).
Latest
PEVs were 9.1% of annual passenger vehicle sales in 2025, down from 9.9% in the annual sales of 2024. (Argonne National Laboratory (opens in a new tab)). PEVs are battery electric plus plug-in hybrid vehicles; fuel cell vehicles are counted separately.
Distance
Not computed: the latest figure excludes fuel cell vehicles, which the target includes. The target no longer applies.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

30% of lands and waters conserved by 2030 (revoked)

Target
Executive Order 14008, Sec. 216(a): to achieve the goal of conserving at least 30 percent of our lands and waters by 2030 (EO 14008 (opens in a new tab)). Revoked on 20 January 2025 (EO 14148 (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
2020: protected areas covered 11.8% of land area and 37.4% of territorial waters (World Bank WDI, land (opens in a new tab); marine (opens in a new tab); data from UNEP-WCMC’s World Database on Protected Areas).
Latest
2025: 12.9% of land and 19.0% of territorial waters (same sources). The marine series falls from 41.1% in 2019 to 19.0% in 2021 with no reason given, so it is treated as a break in the series. The national figure from the US Protected Areas Database is TBC.
Distance
17.1 percentage points short on land and 11.0 points short on waters (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, 2025, against 30%; the marine comparison spans the series break). The target no longer applies.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Universal broadband availability (BEAD programme)

Target
The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act set up a grant programme to bridge the digital divide and provides: There is authorized to be appropriated to the Assistant Secretary to carry out the Program $42,450,000,000. (P.L. 117-58 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 60102(b)). The agency running it states: The BEAD Program was established to ensure universal broadband availability across the United States and to serve as the final federal broadband deployment program. (NTIA, 25 August 2026 (opens in a new tab)). No numeric coverage target or date has been confirmed in the law.
Baseline
TBC (not yet confirmed at source).
Latest
TBC. Programme status, not coverage: all 56 state and territorial final proposals approved by NTIA, 55 approved by NIST and 54 award agreements signed (NTIA dashboard, 8 September 2026).
Distance
Not computed: no baseline or latest coverage figure has been confirmed.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Replace lead service lines within 10 years (rule compliance date 1 November 2027)

Target
EPA’s Lead and Copper Rule Improvements: Water systems are required to complete replacements within 10 years of the LCRI compliance date. (Federal Register, 30 October 2024 (opens in a new tab)). The compliance date is 1 November 2027, so the 10-year window ends in about 2037 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic). Any revision of the rule in 2025–26 has not been confirmed (TBC).
Baseline
2023: the total projected LSL in the United States is 9.2 million (EPA, April 2023 (opens in a new tab); LSL means lead service line).
Latest
2025: With the latest data from 2025, EPA estimates that there are 4 million lead service lines across the country. (EPA, November 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed: the 2025 estimate uses water systems’ own inventories instead of a sample survey, so the fall from 9.2 million to 4 million is a change in counting method, not pipes replaced. A count of replacements has not been confirmed (TBC).
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Halve the share of people in poverty by 2030 (UN SDG 1.2, a yardstick the US rejects)

Target
SDG target 1.2: By 2030, reduce at least by half the proportion of men, women and children of all ages living in poverty in all its dimensions according to national definitions (UN Statistics Division (opens in a new tab)). A UN yardstick: the US Government rejects the 2030 Agenda (see the note above).
Baseline
2015: 13.5% (43.1 million people) on the official poverty measure (Census Bureau, historical poverty table 2 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2025: 10.2% (34.5 million) (Census Bureau, Poverty in the United States: 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Halving the 2015 rate gives 6.75%; the 2025 rate is 3.45 percentage points above it (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Universal health coverage (UN SDG 3.8, a yardstick the US rejects)

Target
SDG target 3.8: Achieve universal health coverage, including financial risk protection, access to quality essential health-care services and access to safe, effective, quality and affordable essential medicines and vaccines for all (UN Statistics Division (opens in a new tab)). A UN yardstick: the US Government rejects the 2030 Agenda (see the note above). No numeric US coverage target has been found.
Baseline
TBC (not yet confirmed at source).
Latest
In 2025, 26.7 million people (7.9%) were uninsured for the entire year, not statistically different from 2024. (Census Bureau, 15 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed: the target sets no number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
For 2023 the Census Bureau wrote that the uninsured rate remained near historic lows at 8.0 percent (Census Bureau (opens in a new tab)). This is a description, not an assessment against the SDG.

End preventable deaths of newborns and children under 5 (UN SDG 3.2, a yardstick the US rejects)

Target
SDG target 3.2: By 2030, end preventable deaths of newborns and children under 5 years of age, with all countries aiming to reduce neonatal mortality to at least as low as 12 per 1,000 live births and under-5 mortality to at least as low as 25 per 1,000 live births (UN Statistics Division (opens in a new tab)). A UN yardstick: the US Government rejects the 2030 Agenda.
Baseline
2015: under-5 mortality 6.8 per 1,000 live births (UN IGME, via World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2024: under-5 mortality 6.5 per 1,000 (UN IGME (opens in a new tab)). The provisional infant mortality rate for 2024 was 5.52 infant deaths per 1,000 live births, not significantly different from the rate in 2023 (5.61); neonatal mortality was 3.66 per 1,000 in 2024 (CDC NCHS, VSRR No. 42, January 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Both numeric thresholds are met: under-5 mortality is 18.5 per 1,000 below the 25 threshold, and neonatal mortality 8.34 per 1,000 below the 12 threshold (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, 2024).
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Caps on discretionary budget authority, fiscal 2024–2025 (expired)

Target
The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023, Sec. 101, capped new budget authority for fiscal 2024, for the revised security category, $886,349,000,000 in new budget authority, with $703,651,000,000 for revised nonsecurity; and for fiscal 2025 at $895,212,000,000 and $710,688,000,000 (P.L. 118-5 (opens in a new tab)). The caps covered fiscal 2024 and 2025 only.
Baseline
TBC (not yet confirmed at source).
Latest
TBC: whether enacted appropriations for fiscal 2024–25 stayed within the caps has not been confirmed. Context: the federal deficit was US$1,832.8 billion in fiscal 2024, US$1,775.4 billion in fiscal 2025 and US$1,992.5 billion in fiscal 2026 (Monthly Treasury Statement (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published.

Sources checked 20 October 2026. Quotations are given as published; in quoted US laws, inner quotation marks are shown as typographic quotes.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
