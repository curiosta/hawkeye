Documented national responses 12

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • National Security Strategy, November 2025

    Adopted by the President (Executive Office of the President), under 50 U.S.C. 3043; published December 2025. It replaced the October 2022 strategy

    First and foremost, we want the continued survival and safety of the United States as an independent, sovereign republic whose government secures the God-given natural rights of its citizens and prioritizes their well-being and interests. (p. 3) Cultivating American industrial strength must become the highest priority of national economic policy. (p. 4) (National Security Strategy of the United States of America, November 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The date it was sent to Congress has not been confirmed at an official source (TBC).

  • CHIPS Act of 2022 (Division A of Public Law 117-167)

    Adopted by Congress; signed 9 August 2022. In force, as amended

    Sec. 102(a)(1): There is established in the Treasury of the United States a fund to be known as the “Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors (CHIPS) for America Fund” (referred to in this subsection as the “Fund”) for the Secretary of Commerce to carry out sections 9902, 9904, and 9906 of the William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021 (15 U.S.C. 4652, 4654, and 4656; Public Law 116-283). (P.L. 117-167 (opens in a new tab)). “CHIPS and Science Act” is the popular name; the statutory short title of Division A is “CHIPS Act of 2022”. In 2025, Public Law 119-21 raised the advanced manufacturing investment credit from 25 percent to 35 percent (Sec. 70308) and rescinded $850,000,000 of the Act’s wireless fund balances (Sec. 40011) (P.L. 119-21 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (Public Law 117-169)

    Adopted by Congress; signed 16 August 2022. In force, heavily amended in 2025

    Long title: To provide for reconciliation pursuant to title II of S. Con. Res. 14. (P.L. 117-169 (opens in a new tab)). The Act has no purpose section. Public Law 119-21 of 4 July 2025 ends or phases out many of its tax credits (Secs. 70501–70515; for example, the clean vehicle credit ends for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025) and rescinds unspent funds (Secs. 60001–60024, including Sec. 60002, REPEAL OF GREENHOUSE GAS REDUCTION FUND) (P.L. 119-21 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related GAP · Half of new cars zero-emission by 2030 (revoked)

  • Public Law 119-21 (H.R. 1, known as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act)

    Adopted by Congress; signed 4 July 2025

    Long title: To provide for reconciliation pursuant to title II of H. Con. Res. 14. It has no purpose section. Sec. 72001: The limitation under section 3101(b) of title 31, United States Code, as most recently increased by section 401(b) of Public Law 118-5 (31 U.S.C. 3101 note), is increased by $5,000,000,000,000. (P.L. 119-21 (opens in a new tab)). It also ends or phases out clean-energy tax credits (Secs. 70501–70515) and rescinds unspent Inflation Reduction Act funds (Secs. 60001–60024).

    Related GAP · Half of new cars zero-emission by 2030 (revoked) · GAP · Caps on discretionary budget authority, fiscal 2024–2025 (expired)

  • Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (Public Law 117-58)

    Adopted by Congress; signed 15 November 2021

    Sec. 1(a): This Act may be cited as the “Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act”. Long title: To authorize funds for Federal-aid highways, highway safety programs, and transit programs, and for other purposes. (P.L. 117-58 (opens in a new tab)). Its authorizations cover fiscal years 2022 through 2026, which ended on 30 September 2026; any extension has not been confirmed (TBC).

    Related GAP · Universal broadband availability (BEAD programme)

  • National Defense Authorization Acts (annual)

    Adopted by Congress; fiscal 2024 act signed 22 December 2023; the current act, for fiscal 2026 (Public Law 119-60), approved 18 December 2025

    Sec. 1 of the 2024 act: This Act may be cited as the “National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024”. (P.L. 118-31 (opens in a new tab)). The fiscal 2026 act’s long title: To authorize appropriations for fiscal year 2026 for military activities of the Department of Defense, for military construction, and for defense activities of the Department of Energy, to prescribe military personnel strengths for such fiscal year, and for other purposes. (P.L. 119-60 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · The largest military spending and nuclear arsenal

  • AUKUS submarine sales to Australia (NDAA for fiscal 2024, Sec. 1352)

    Adopted by Congress; signed 22 December 2023, effective one year after enactment

    Sec. 1352(a): the President is authorized to transfer up to two Virginia Class submarines from the inventory of the Department of the Navy to the Government of Australia on a sale basis, and transfer not more than one additional Virginia Class submarine to the Government of Australia on a sale basis pursuant to section 21 of the Arms Export Control Act (22 U.S.C. 2761) during the 20-year period beginning on the date of the enactment of this Act, to implement the trilateral security partnership between Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States (P.L. 118-31 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Opportunities · Australia’s purchase of US submarines (AUKUS)

  • Executive Order 14179, Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence

    Adopted by the President; signed 23 January 2025 (90 FR 8741). It followed the revocation of Executive Order 14110 on 20 January 2025

    Sec. 2: It is the policy of the United States to sustain and enhance America's global AI dominance in order to promote human flourishing, economic competitiveness, and national security. (EO 14179 (opens in a new tab)). Sec. 4 ordered an AI action plan within 180 days; the plan has not been checked for this page (TBC). The 2023 order on AI safety was revoked by (ggg) Executive Order 14110 of October 30, 2023 (Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence) in the list of revoked actions in EO 14148 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 2.

  • Executive Order 14162, Putting America First in International Environmental Agreements

    Adopted by the President; signed 20 January 2025 (90 FR 8455)

    Sec. 3(a): The United States Ambassador to the United Nations shall immediately submit formal written notification of the United States' withdrawal from the Paris Agreement under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. Sec. 3(e): The U.S. International Climate Finance Plan is revoked and rescinded immediately. (EO 14162 (opens in a new tab)). The withdrawal took effect on 27 January 2026 (UN Treaty Collection (opens in a new tab)). It replaced the 2021 pledge to cut net emissions 50–52% below 2005 by 2030.

    Related GAP · Net emissions 50–52% below 2005 by 2030 (Paris pledge, withdrawn)

  • Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 (Public Law 118-5)

    Adopted by Congress; signed 3 June 2023. Its caps and debt-limit suspension have expired

    Long title: To provide for a responsible increase to the debt ceiling. It capped discretionary budget authority for fiscal 2024–25 (Sec. 101) and suspended the debt limit for the period ending on January 1, 2025 (Sec. 401(a)) (P.L. 118-5 (opens in a new tab)). The limit was raised by $5 trillion in July 2025 (P.L. 119-21 (opens in a new tab), Sec. 72001).

    Related GAP · Caps on discretionary budget authority, fiscal 2024–2025 (expired)

  • Federal Reserve Act, Section 2A (12 U.S.C. 225a)

    Adopted by Congress; added by act of 16 November 1977, amended 1978, 1988 and 2000

    The Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Federal Open Market Committee shall maintain long run growth of the monetary and credit aggregates commensurate with the economy's long run potential to increase production, so as to promote effectively the goals of maximum employment, stable prices, and moderate long-term interest rates. (Federal Reserve Board (opens in a new tab)).

  • Energy Act of 2020 (Division Z of Public Law 116-260)

    Adopted by Congress; approved 27 December 2020

    Sec. 101(a): This division may be cited as the “Energy Act of 2020”. (P.L. 116-260 (opens in a new tab)). It has no objective sentence. Its titles cover nuclear energy (II), renewable energy (III), carbon management (IV), carbon removal (V) and industrial technologies (VI).

Sources checked 20 October 2026. Quotations are given as published; in quoted US laws, inner quotation marks are shown as typographic quotes.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Placing a response in a cell is Hawkeye’s only editorial step. Responses are not ranked.
