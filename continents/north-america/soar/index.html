A Aspirations

Hawkeye found no single vision document for the whole of North America. Goals are stated by the bodies its countries belong to. The Aspirations below are theirs, in their own words, labelled with their members.

USMCA: freer, fairer trade in the region

Trade agreement of the United States, Mexico and Canada, 84.1% of North America’s population ·, in force 1 July 2020, not renewed at the 2026 joint review and still in force

REPLACE the 1994 North American Free Trade Agreement with a 21st Century, high standard new agreement to support mutually beneficial trade leading to freer, fairer markets, and to robust economic growth in the region; PRESERVE AND EXPAND regional trade and production by further incentivizing the production and sourcing of goods and materials in the region; (USMCA, Preamble (opens in a new tab))

CARICOM: the objectives of the Community

Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas ·, 15 member states, 2.9% of North America’s population

The Community shall have the following objectives: (a) improved standards of living and work; (b) full employment of labour and other factors of production; (c) accelerated, co-ordinated and sustained economic development and convergence; (Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas (opens in a new tab), Article 6)

No current Community strategic plan was found. The 2015–2019 plan has expired (TBC).

SICA: a region of peace, freedom, democracy and development

Protocol of Tegucigalpa, 1991 ·, eight members, 10.5% of North America’s population

EL SISTEMA DE LA INTEGRACION CENTROAMERICANA tiene por objetivo fundamental la realización de la integración de Centroamérica, para constituirla como Región de Paz, Libertad, Democracia y Desarrollo. (unofficial translation: “The Central American Integration System has as its fundamental objective the integration of Central America, to make it a Region of Peace, Freedom, Democracy and Development.”) (Protocolo de Tegucigalpa (opens in a new tab), Article 3)

How this SOAR analysis works

For a continent, the Aspirations are goal statements of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, quoted word for word and labelled with each body’s members.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
