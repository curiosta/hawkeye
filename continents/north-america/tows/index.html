Documented regional responses 8

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA)

    Adopted by United States, Mexico and Canada, 84.1% of North America’s population, in force 1 July 2020

    REPLACE the 1994 North American Free Trade Agreement with a 21st Century, high standard new agreement to support mutually beneficial trade leading to freer, fairer markets, and to robust economic growth in the region; PRESERVE AND EXPAND regional trade and production by further incentivizing the production and sourcing of goods and materials in the region; (USMCA, Preamble (opens in a new tab))

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A large economy · GAP · USMCA car rules of origin

  • The USMCA joint reviews

    Adopted by The USMCA Free Trade Commission (the three parties), under Article 34.7, first review 1 July 2026

    The United States did not agree to renew the USMCA in its current form. As a result, the USMCA is not renewed. However, the Agreement remains in force pending resolution of these issues or until the Agreement’s termination. (USTR, 1 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)) the Commission will meet to conduct a joint review annually until each of the three Parties confirms its wish to extend the term of this Agreement for another 16-year period or the term of the Agreement expires, whichever is earlier. (Federal Register, 5 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Comments for the 2027 review are due by 12 January 2027 (USTR, 2 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Statements by Canada and Mexico have not been checked (TBC).

  • Environmental Cooperation Agreement (CEC)

    Adopted by United States, Mexico and Canada, signed 30 November to 18 December 2018, in force with the USMCA

    The objectives of this Agreement are to: (a) seek ways to modernize and enhance the effectiveness of environmental cooperation between the Parties, building upon their long history of environmental cooperation; and (c) strengthen cooperation between the Parties to conserve, protect, and enhance the environment and address environmental challenges and priorities; (Environmental Cooperation Agreement (opens in a new tab), Article 1)

  • Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas (CARICOM)

    Adopted by Caribbean Community, 15 member states (12 sovereign states in North America and Montserrat), 2.9% of North America’s population, opened for signature 5 July 2001

    The Community shall have the following objectives: (a) improved standards of living and work; (b) full employment of labour and other factors of production; (c) accelerated, co-ordinated and sustained economic development and convergence; (Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas (opens in a new tab), Article 6). The date it entered into force has not been confirmed at source (TBC).

  • CARICOM food import bill initiative, extended to 2030

    Adopted by Caribbean Community, announced 25 February 2025

    Quoted in the SWOT Threats (CARICOM, 25 February 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Price shocks and imported food · GAP · CARICOM food import bill (25 by 2025)

  • Caribbean Disaster Emergency Management Agency (CDEMA)

    Adopted by Participating states of the Caribbean, signed 2 July 2008 in St John’s, Antigua and Barbuda, in force 4 July 2013

    CDEMA shall have the following objectives, mobilising and coordinating disaster relief; mitigating or eliminating, as far as practicable, the immediate consequences of disasters in Participating States; providing immediate and coordinated response by means of emergency disaster relief to any affected Participating State; (CDEMA Agreement (opens in a new tab)). The number of participating states has not been confirmed (TBC).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Hurricanes and costly disasters

  • Protocol of Tegucigalpa (SICA)

    Adopted by Central American Integration System, eight members, 10.5% of North America’s population, signed 13 December 1991, in force 23 July 1992

    EL SISTEMA DE LA INTEGRACION CENTROAMERICANA tiene por objetivo fundamental la realización de la integración de Centroamérica, para constituirla como Región de Paz, Libertad, Democracia y Desarrollo. (unofficial translation: “The Central American Integration System has as its fundamental objective the integration of Central America, to make it a Region of Peace, Freedom, Democracy and Development.”) (Protocolo de Tegucigalpa (opens in a new tab), Article 3)

    Related GAP · SICA regional climate strategy

    Adopted by All 33 Latin American and Caribbean states, including the 21 in North America other than the United States and Canada, done at Mexico City, 14 February 1967

How this TOWS matrix works

For a continent, the responses are documented instruments of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, each named with its members and the date it was adopted.

Every documented regional response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
