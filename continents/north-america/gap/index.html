North America GAP

Where things stand now compared with an officially agreed target.

The UN publishes no single figure for North America. It reports three parts: Northern America (the United States, Canada, Bermuda, Greenland and Saint Pierre and Miquelon), Central America (including Mexico) and the Caribbean. Each value below names the part it covers. CEPAL’s 2026 assessment for Latin America and the Caribbean: De las 134 metas evaluadas, el 61% presenta resultados positivos en cuanto a la trayectoria observada. Sin embargo, se prevé que solo en el 19% de los casos se logrará alcanzar los umbrales estipulados para 2030. (unofficial translation: “Of the 134 targets assessed, 61% show positive results in their observed trajectory. However, only 19% are expected to reach the thresholds set for 2030.”) (CEPAL, 2026 (opens in a new tab)). That assessment does not cover the United States or Canada. Three rows track regional agreements: the USMCA car rules, the CARICOM food import goal and the SICA climate strategy.

Targets and latest values

Hunger (SDG 2.1)

Target
“2.1 By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Share of people undernourished, 2015: Caribbean 12.7%, Central America 6.3%, Northern America below 2.5% (UN SDG Global Database, SN_ITK_DEFC (opens in a new tab))
Latest
2025: Caribbean 16.6%, Central America 5.1%, Northern America below 2.5%. World 7.8% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab), FAO estimates)
Distance
Not computed. “End hunger” is not a single number, and FAO does not publish values below 2.5%. In the Caribbean the share rose by 3.9 points from 2015 to 2025 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean (this covers Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, not the United States or Canada): target 2.1 is in the group marked La tendencia es de estancamiento o retroceso (“the trend is stagnation or regression”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Renewable energy (SDG 7.2)

Target
“7.2 By 2030, increase substantially the share of renewable energy in the global energy mix” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Renewable share of total final energy consumption, 2015: Northern America 10.57%, Central America 16.27%, Caribbean 19.93% (UN SDG Global Database, EG_FEC_RNEW (opens in a new tab))
Latest
2023: Northern America 13.52%, Central America 18.57%, Caribbean 18.45%. World 18% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. The target sets no number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean (this covers Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, not the United States or Canada): target 7.2 is in the group marked La meta se alcanzó o es probable que se alcance con la tendencia actual (“the target has been met or is likely to be met on the current trend”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)). The Caribbean share fell from 2015 to 2023.

Labour share of GDP (SDG 10.4)

Target
“10.4 Adopt policies, especially fiscal, wage and social protection policies, and progressively achieve greater equality” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Labour share of GDP, 2015: Northern America 58.7%, Central America 35.5%, Caribbean 48% (UN SDG Global Database, SL_EMP_GTOTL (opens in a new tab))
Latest
2025: Northern America 56.7%, Central America 38.9%, Caribbean 48.1%. World 52.6% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab), ILO estimates)
Distance
Not computed. The target sets no number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean (this covers Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, not the United States or Canada): target 10.4 is in the group marked La tendencia es de estancamiento o retroceso (“the trend is stagnation or regression”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)).

Marine protected areas (SDG 14.5)

Target
“14.5 By 2020, conserve at least 10 per cent of coastal and marine areas, consistent with national and international law and based on the best available scientific information” (UN SDG global indicator framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
2015: Northern America 33.28% of marine Key Biodiversity Areas inside protected areas (UN SDG Global Database, ER_MRN_MPA (opens in a new tab)). The World Bank’s North America group (the United States, Canada and Bermuda) protected 16.49% of its territorial waters (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab))
Latest
2025: Northern America 35.95% of marine Key Biodiversity Areas. Territorial waters protected, World Bank North America group 14.63%, Mexico 22.6%, Latin America and the Caribbean 22.24%, world 17.46% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab), World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. The two series measure different things, and neither is the target’s own measure of “coastal and marine areas”.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CEPAL, for Latin America and the Caribbean (this covers Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, not the United States or Canada): target 14.5 is in the group marked La meta se alcanzó o es probable que se alcance con la tendencia actual (“the target has been met or is likely to be met on the current trend”) (CEPAL 2026 figure (opens in a new tab)). No agency assessment for Northern America was found.

USMCA car rules of origin

Target
For a passenger vehicle or light truck, (d) 75 percent under the net cost method, beginning on January 1, 2023, or three years after the date of entry into force of this Agreement, whichever is later, and thereafter. (USMCA, Appendix to Annex 4-B, Article 3.1 (opens in a new tab)). The agreement entered into force on 1 July 2020.
Baseline
Under NAFTA, for passenger cars and light trucks: for a producer's fiscal year beginning on the day closest to January 1, 2002 and thereafter, 62.5 percent under the net cost method (NAFTA, Article 403(5)(a) (opens in a new tab))
Latest
The USITC timeline marks July 2023 as when Staging for RVC and LVC requirements ended for light vehicles without alternative staging. The 13 approved alternative staging plans affected less than one-quarter of models produced in USMCA countries in 2024. Nearly all alternative staging plans end by mid-2025. (USITC, 2025 report (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. The required share rose from 62.5% to 75% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 12.5 points). No official source found gives the share of vehicles that meet the 75% rule.
Assessment by the tracking agency
USITC: the ROOs had concentrated effects on the U.S. automotive industry, and a negligible impact on the overall U.S. economy. (USITC press release with the 2025 report (opens in a new tab)). The USITC covers effects in the United States only.

CARICOM food import bill (25 by 2025)

Target
The 25 by 2025 Initiative , which targeted the reduction of the Region’s more than $6B food import bill by 25 percent by yearend, has now been extended to 2030. (CARICOM, 25 February 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Heads of Government agreed the extension on 21 February 2025.
Baseline
Between 2018 and 2020, CARICOM’s food import bill reached approximately US$13.7 billion (about 15 percent of Regional GDP). (CARICOM, 28 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The February 2025 release gives “more than $6B”. The two CARICOM figures differ, and neither source explains the difference.
Latest
TBC. No food import bill after 2020 was found in a CARICOM source. CARICOM reports output instead: 24 percent growth in regional food production between 2022 and 2024 (CARICOM, 28 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. There is no latest value for the import bill.
Assessment by the tracking agency
CARICOM’s own view: Today, we can confidently say that the initiative has moved beyond aspiration and is delivering tangible results. (CARICOM Assistant Secretary-General, 28 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)). CARICOM has 15 member states. Two of them, Guyana and Suriname, are in South America.

SICA regional climate strategy

Target
The strategy’s mitigation objective: Fortalecer y generar acciones regionales para promover la mitigación y reducción de emisiones de GEI y la transición de las economías bajas en carbono en los países del SICA (unofficial translation: “Strengthen and generate regional actions to promote mitigation and reduction of greenhouse gas emissions and the transition to low-carbon economies in the SICA countries”) (CCAD, ERCC 2018-2022 (opens in a new tab)). It sets no regional number. The only numbers in it are what it asks of developed countries: Reducción de emisiones al menos en un 45% para el año 2020 y un 95% para el año 2050. (“Emission cuts of at least 45% by 2020 and 95% by 2050.”)
Baseline
Fossil CO2 from the eight SICA members (Belize, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama), 2015: 80.1 Mt (Hawkeye’s sum of Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab) national data)
Latest
2024: 102.7 Mt, with the Dominican Republic 33.1 Mt and Guatemala 19.9 Mt the largest (Hawkeye’s sum of Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab) national data)
Distance
Not computed. The strategy sets no number for SICA countries. Emissions rose by 28% from 2015 to 2024 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
No assessment by a tracking agency was found. The plan of action covered 2018 to 2022. A successor plan is TBC.

Sources checked 20 October and 12 November 2026. Spanish quotations are given as published. English translations are unofficial.

How this GAP analysis works

For a continent, the targets are those set by the regional or treaty bodies that govern it, plus the global targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
