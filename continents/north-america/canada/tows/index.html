Canada TOWS

Pairs internal factors (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external ones (Opportunities, Threats) into four kinds of response.

Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Canada has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Use mostly clean electricity to expand clean-power trade with other G7 partners

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Mostly clean electricity · SWOT · Opportunities · Trade deals with every other G7 country

      Use Canada’s mostly clean electricity, and existing trade deals with every other G7 country, to expand clean-power and clean-technology trade. Official Canadian programmes already include the Clean Electricity Regulations and the Major Projects Office. This recommendation is about clean-power trade, not a call to expand oil-export routes, which sit in tension with the live oil-and-gas emissions weakness.

      Analogous case. Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (amended 11 November 2025), states that Norway had record power production of 158 TWh in 2024 and et eksportoverskudd (eksport minus import av kraft) på 18,4 TWh (an export surplus of 18.4 TWh). Vann- og vindkraft er dominerende i norsk kraftproduksjon, og står samlet for rundt 98 prosent av produksjonen. (Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Canada already generates 60% of electricity from hydropower in the 2021 figure on the live SWOT, and was the third-largest hydro generator in 2023. Trade deals with every other G7 country are a live opportunity. Norway shows that a hydro-and-wind system can run a large annual export surplus (18.4 TWh in 2024). The analogy is limited because the 60% hydro figure is for 2021, Norway’s 98% hydro-and-wind mix is higher, and the 2024 Norwegian surplus followed high reservoir inflows. Interconnection, distance and partner demand will limit how much Canadian power can move. Official clean-electricity rules already exist and are not replaced by this pairing.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Use clean electricity to cut oil-and-gas emissions as the country warms and wildfires grow

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Mostly clean electricity · SWOT · Threats · Warming at about twice the global rate

      Use Canada’s mostly clean electricity to electrify more transport, buildings and some industry, so that emissions fall as the country warms at about twice the global rate and faces record wildfires. Official Canadian programmes already include large-emitter carbon pricing and the vehicle standard. This recommendation pairs that electricity strength with the warming threat.

      Analogous case. The Norwegian Ministry of Transport, in “Norway is electric” (last updated 15 January 2025), states: At the end of 2024, electric vehicles (EVs) held an 88% market share in the market for new passenger cars in Norway, resulting in EVs making up 28% of the total passenger car stock. For new urban busses, the EV market share reached 72% by the end of 2024. The same page records a large fiscal cost for the incentives (Norwegian Ministry of Transport, 15 January 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (amended 11 November 2025), states: Energibruken i veitransport gikk ned 3,7 prosent i 2024 selv om de totale kjørelengdene for norsk-registrerte kjøretøy var omtrent uendret fra året før. Road-transport energy use fell 3.7% in 2024 even though total distances driven were about unchanged. Electric vehicles and assumed electric driving by plug-in hybrids stod for til sammen 29 prosent av de totale kjørelengdene i 2024, against 26% in 2023 and 2% in 2015 (Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The European Environment Agency, in its 9 November 2026 press release on the Trends and Projections report, states: In 2025, net EU greenhouse gas emissions under the scope of the European Climate Law dropped by 39% below 1990 levels, and This drop also came while gross domestic product and populations grew by 74% and 8% respectively over the same period. The figures for 2025 are preliminary. The same release records that the pace of cuts slowed in 2025, and that year-on-year emissions rose in domestic transport and buildings (EEA, 9 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Canada has a mostly clean electricity system, warms at about twice the global rate, and faces record wildfires (live SWOT). Norway shows that electric vehicles can take most new-car sales and go with a fall in road-energy use. The EU shows that net emissions can fall 39% below 1990 while GDP grows. The 2025 EU figures are preliminary. The limits are important. Norway has a still-cleaner power mix and costly incentives. Electrifying Canadian oil and gas production is not the same as ending the sector’s 30% share of national emissions (a live weakness). Emissions cuts do not at once reduce wildfire seasons already under way.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Cut the oil-and-gas share of emissions with the same kind of phase-out already documented for coal power

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Oil and gas: the largest source of emissions · SWOT · Opportunities · Trade deals with every other G7 country

      Use trade with other G7 countries, and existing carbon pricing, to cut the oil-and-gas share of Canadian emissions, rather than treating new fossil-export routes as the main response to that weakness. Official Canadian programmes already include large-emitter carbon pricing and the vehicle standard. This recommendation pairs the live emissions weakness with G7 trade, and uses a documented end of coal-fired power in the United Kingdom.

      Analogous case. The UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero records that The last UK coal-fired power station closed in England in September 2024. In the same official document: In September 2024, Ratcliffe-on-Soar, the last coal-fired power station in the UK, closed. UK fossil fuel generation decreased by 16 per cent compared to 2023. Coal-fired generation’s share of UK electricity was only 0.7 per cent in 2024, a record low. The low carbon share of total UK generation stood at its highest value on the time series at 64.7 per cent. Renewables’ share rose 3.9 percentage points to 50.4 per cent. (DESNZ, Electricity generation and supply, 2020 to 2024 (opens in a new tab)). Energy Trends December 2024 confirms: Ratcliffe-on-Soar closed at the end of the third quarter on 30 September 2024. (DESNZ, Energy Trends December 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Oil and gas accounted for 30% of Canadian emissions in 2024, 208 Mt, and G7 trade deals are a live opportunity (live SWOT). The UK closed its last coal-fired power station on 30 September 2024, with coal at 0.7% of electricity that year and a record 64.7% low-carbon share. The main limit is scope. The UK case is coal-fired electricity, already a small residual share, not a large oil-and-gas extraction sector. Canada is a major oil and gas producer, and a power-sector phase-out does not transfer as a package to wells, sands and pipelines. Official carbon pricing already applies to large emitters and is acknowledged.

  • WT Responses that reduce weaknesses and limit threats

    • Cut oil-and-gas emissions as Canada warms at about twice the global rate

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Oil and gas: the largest source of emissions · SWOT · Threats · Warming at about twice the global rate

      Cut oil-and-gas emissions, the largest national source, as Canada warms at about twice the global rate. Official carbon pricing and electricity rules already exist. This recommendation pairs that weakness with the warming threat.

      Analogous case. The European Environment Agency, in its 9 November 2026 press release on the Trends and Projections report, states: In 2025, net EU greenhouse gas emissions under the scope of the European Climate Law dropped by 39% below 1990 levels, and This drop also came while gross domestic product and populations grew by 74% and 8% respectively over the same period. The figures for 2025 are preliminary. The same release records that the pace of cuts slowed in 2025, and that year-on-year emissions rose in domestic transport and buildings (EEA, 9 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Oil and gas were 30% of Canadian emissions in 2024, and warming at about twice the global rate is a live threat, together with record wildfires (live SWOT). The EU cut net emissions 39% below 1990 while GDP grew. The 2025 figures are preliminary. The analogy is limited because the EU started from a different production structure, without a comparably large oil-and-gas extraction share, and because transport and buildings in the EU still saw emissions rise in 2025. Emissions cuts do not at once reverse warming already observed in Canada.

  • Rebuilding the Canadian Armed Forces

    Adopted by Prime Minister of Canada, 9 June 2025

    With this increase, Canada will achieve NATO’s 2% target this year. The plan includes Developing new drones and sensors to monitor the seafloor and the Arctic. (Prime Minister of Canada, 9 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related GAP · Core defence spending of 3.5% of GDP by 2035

  • Defence spending in Budget 2025

    Adopted by Government of Canada, Budget 2025 (4 November 2025)

    With $30 billion over five years, Budget 2025 represents the largest defence investment in decades. We will meet our NATO 2% commitment this year, modernize NORAD, reinforce our Arctic defences (Budget 2025 speech (opens in a new tab)). NORAD is the North American Aerospace Defense Command, run jointly with the United States.
