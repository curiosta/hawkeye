T

Threats

External · harmful

Tariffs from the largest trading partner

  • US tariffs rose from about 2% to 17%. Budget 2025 states: The United States, our largest trading partner, is fundamentally reshaping all of its trading relationships and putting up trade barriers, with its average tariffs across all countries rising from around 2 per cent at the end of 2024 to 17 per cent today. It adds that A 35 per cent tariff also applies to non- Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA) compliant goods. (Budget 2025, Economic and fiscal overview (opens in a new tab)).
  • About one-third of income from exports. The Budget describes Canada as a country where about one-third of national income comes from exports (Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)). In 2023, Canada exported 4.0 million barrels a day of crude oil, nearly 80% of production, and Nearly all these volumes are exported to the United States (U.S.). (Canada Energy Regulator (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Record wildfires

  • 15 million hectares burned in 2023. Canada’s 2023 wildfire season was the most destructive ever recorded. By the end of the year, more than 6,000 fires had torched a staggering 15 million hectares of land. In an average year, about 2.5 million hectares burn (Natural Resources Canada (opens in a new tab)).

Warming at about twice the global rate

  • 1.7°C warmer since 1948. Both past and future warming in Canada is, on average, about double the magnitude of global warming. Northern Canada has warmed and will continue to warm at even more than double the global rate. The report adds that Annual average temperature over northern Canada increased by 2.3°C since 1948. (Canada’s Changing Climate Report, 2019 (opens in a new tab)).
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

New export routes for energy

  • A new pipeline and a new gas terminal. Budget 2025 states: Newly added energy export capacity, such as the Trans Mountain expansion and LNG Canada, has cushioned some of the decline in exports. LNG means liquefied natural gas. The Budget also names the Port of Churchill Plus project, that has the potential to expand export capacity in the North through Hudson Bay (Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Trade deals with every other G7 country

  • Free trade with all G7 partners. The Budget 2025 speech says Canada has unmatched market access, as the only country in the G7 with free trade agreements with every other G7 nation (Budget 2025 speech, 4 November 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The G7 is the group of seven large advanced economies. This is the Government’s own statement.
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Oil and gas: the largest source of emissions

  • 30% of national emissions. In 2024, the oil and gas sector was Canada’s largest source of GHG emissions, accounting for 30% of total national emissions, with 208 megatonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Mt CO 2 eq) emitted (Environment and Climate Change Canada (opens in a new tab)). GHG means greenhouse gas.
  • 16.6 tonnes per person. Between 1990 and 2024, the amount of GHGs emitted per person decreased 24% from 22.0 to 16.6 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO 2 eq) per person (ECCC (opens in a new tab)). This counts all greenhouse gases. The fossil CO2 figure in the shares table counts carbon dioxide from fossil fuels only.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Greenhouse gases, 2030

Slow productivity growth

  • 0.3% a year over the past decade. Budget 2025 states: Over the past decade, Canada's productivity performance has been persistently weak. In this time, productivity grew by only 0.3 per cent annually—less than one-third the pace of the previous two decades (Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Productivity here means output per hour worked.
S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Mostly clean electricity

  • 60% of electricity from hydropower. More than half of the electricity in Canada (60%) is generated from hydroelectricity . This figure is for 2021. In 2023, Canada was the third largest generator of hydroelectricity in the world. (Canada Energy Regulator (opens in a new tab)).
  • Over 80% from non-emitting sources. The Government states that Canada is already a global leader in clean electricity, with over 80% of its power coming from non-emitting sources. (ECCC, 12 February 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Non-emitting sources include hydro, nuclear, wind and solar power.

Large oil reserves

  • 11% of the world’s oil reserves. Canada holds some of the largest oil reserves in the world—11% of the world’s reserves. Only Venezuela and Saudi Arabia had more as of 2020. (Canada Energy Regulator (opens in a new tab)).
  • Fourth largest oil producer. Canada produced 5.1 million barrels per day (MMb/d) of crude oil in 2023, an increase of 1.9% from 2022, and Canada was ranked as the fourth largest oil producer in the world in 2023. (CER (opens in a new tab)).

Highest human development score in North America

  • Human Development Index 0.939. Canada ranks 16th of 193 countries and first among the 23 sovereign states of North America, for the data year 2023 (UNDP Human Development Report 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The index combines life expectancy, schooling and income.
