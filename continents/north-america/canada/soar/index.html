A Aspirations

Canada has no single national vision document. The net-zero goal for 2050 is set in law. The Budget and the Prime Minister state the economic and defence goals. The Aspirations below quote those texts, each labelled with its status.

Net zero by 2050

In law: Canadian Net-Zero Emissions Accountability Act, section 6 (assented 29 June 2021)

The national greenhouse gas emissions target for 2050 is net-zero emissions. (Canadian Net-Zero Emissions Accountability Act (opens in a new tab)). Net zero means that emissions are balanced by removals.

Emissions 45 to 50% below 2005 by 2035

Nationally Determined Contribution under the Paris Agreement, submitted 11 February 2025

On December 12, 2024, the Government of Canada announced a target to reduce emissions by 45-50% below 2005 levels by 2035 (ECCC, 12 February 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Canada Strong

Budget 2025 speech to Parliament, 4 November 2025

As I said earlier, this budget will unleash one trillion dollars in total investment over the next five years. (Budget 2025 speech (opens in a new tab)). The Budget names two fiscal anchors: Balancing day-to-day operating spending with revenues by 2028–29 and Maintaining a declining deficit-to-GDP ratio (Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Defence and security at 5% of GDP by 2035

Speech by the Prime Minister at the NATO Summit, 25 June 2025

Canada is already beginning work toward a target of 3.5% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by 2035. We’re also working to ensure that we are spending 1.5% of our GDP in defence and security-related infrastructure. (Prime Minister of Canada, 25 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

30% of land and ocean conserved by 2030

Government target, reported by Environment and Climate Change Canada

Canada has set a target to conserve 25% of its lands and 25% of its oceans by 2025, and 30% of each by 2030. (ECCC (opens in a new tab)).

How this SOAR analysis works

For a country, the Aspirations are its official goal statements, quoted word for word. Where a country has no single national vision document, the page names the official documents it quotes instead.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
