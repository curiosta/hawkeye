T

Threats

External · harmful

Heavy reliance on one export market

  • 83.7% of non-oil exports go to the United States. In January–December 2025, the United States took 83.70% of Mexico’s non-oil exports and the rest of the world 16.30% (INEGI, trade balance, 27 January 2026 (opens in a new tab), Cuadro 3; preliminary figures).
  • A treaty review in 2026. The trade agreement with the United States and Canada (USMCA, called T-MEC in Mexico) came into force on 1 July 2020. It states: On the sixth anniversary of the entry into force of this Agreement, the Commission shall meet to conduct a “joint review” of the operation of this Agreement, review any recommendations for action submitted by a Party, and decide on any appropriate actions. (USMCA, Article 34.7(2) (opens in a new tab), p. 34-2). Mexico’s Ministry of Economy opened a public consultation for the review on 17 September 2025 (DOF, 17 September 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The outcome of the July 2026 review has not yet been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Rapidly strengthening Pacific hurricanes

  • A category 5 landfall at Acapulco. It is estimated that Otis made landfall in Acapulco around 0645 UTC as a 140-kt category 5 hurricane. The storm strengthened by 90 knots in 21 hours before landfall in October 2023 (US National Hurricane Center, Tropical Cyclone Report: Hurricane Otis (opens in a new tab)).
  • The costliest on record for Mexico. Otis caused at least 52 fatalities and an estimated $12–16 billion (USD) of total damage in Mexico, making it the costliest tropical cyclone on record for Mexico and one of the most expensive natural disasters in Mexico's history. (NHC, 2024 (opens in a new tab)). The report also lists 32 people missing; the damage range comes from reinsurers’ estimates.

Firearms trafficked from the United States

  • Two-thirds of traced guns came from the US. The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) reported that 68 percent (82,785) of firearms recovered in Mexico and traced from January 2020 to December 2024 had a U.S. source. (US Government Accountability Office, 28 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Out of 122,084 traces. The same report says ATF completed traces for 122,084 firearms recovered in Mexico in that period. The figures cover only firearms that Mexican authorities submitted to ATF for tracing (GAO-26-108061 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Political

Large numbers of asylum applications

  • A record in 2023, then fewer. Mexico received 140,979 asylum applications in 2023, 78,966 in 2024 and 70,482 in 2025, from all countries of origin (UNHCR Refugee Data Finder (opens in a new tab)). The figure published by Mexico’s own refugee agency (COMAR) has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).
  • Hundreds of thousands in need of protection. At the end of 2025 Mexico hosted 152,002 refugees, 173,532 asylum-seekers and 64,160 other people in need of international protection (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)). See the share-of-world-totals table below.

Shared river water owed under the 1944 treaty

  • About half the five-year delivery. Under the 1944 Water Treaty, Mexico delivers Rio Grande water to the United States in five-year cycles. For the cycle that ended on 24 October 2025, the US Section of the International Boundary and Water Commission reports U.S. Accumulated Five-Year Cycle Deliveries - 884,861 AF (1,091 MCM ) as of October 24, 2025, against an obligation of 1,750,000 acre-feet (2,158.6 million m³) (IBWC, Current Cycle report (opens in a new tab)). That is about 51% of the total (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Drought on the Mexican side. At the end of 2024, 38.1% of Mexico was in moderate to exceptional drought (CONAGUA, Drought Monitor, 31 December 2024 (opens in a new tab)); see the Weaknesses below. The position in the 2025–2030 cycle has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Opportunities

External · helpful

Foreign investment, trade and visitors

  • Record foreign investment. Con un monto de 40 mil 871 millones de dólares (mdd) de Inversión Extranjera Directa, México registró un record histórico al cierre anual por quinto año consecutivo. (unofficial translation: “With US$40,871 million of Foreign Direct Investment, Mexico recorded an all-time annual high for the fifth year in a row.”) (Ministry of Economy, 23 February 2026 (opens in a new tab); 2025, preliminary). The 2024 figure was revised to US$36,872 million.
  • The United States invests most. US investors put in US$15,877 million in 2025, about 39% of the total (Hawkeye’s arithmetic from the same report).
  • The largest US trading partner. In 2024 Mexico was the United States’ largest trading partner, with total goods trade of US$839.9 billion (15.8% of US trade), and the largest source of US goods imports, at US$505.9 billion (15.5%) (US Census Bureau, Top Trading Partners, December 2024 (opens in a new tab)). The 2025 page has not been confirmed (TBC).
  • More international visitors. Mexico received 47,786,706 international tourists in 2025, 6.1% more than in 2024 (45,038,754). International visitors spent US$34,991.6 million, of which tourists spent US$31,715.4 million (INEGI, International Travellers Survey, 12 February 2026 (opens in a new tab), Cuadro 2; preliminary).

World Bank backing for infrastructure

  • A US$500 million guarantee. The World Bank's Executive Board today approved a credit enhancement instrument of up to US$500 million for Mexico, working through the national development bank BANOBRAS and its infrastructure fund FONADIN (World Bank, 18 December 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Private money expected. The bank lists among the expected results The mobilization of approximately US$1 billion in private capital for the country's infrastructure. (World Bank (opens in a new tab)). The operation supports Plan México; see the SOAR page.

A US partnership on semiconductors

  • An assessment, first. The U.S. Department of State will partner with the Government of Mexico to explore opportunities to grow and diversify the global semiconductor ecosystem under the International Technology Security and Innovation (ITSI) Fund, created by the CHIPS Act of 2022. The initial phase includes a comprehensive assessment of Mexico's existing semiconductor ecosystem and regulatory framework, as well as workforce and infrastructure needs. (US Department of State, 28 March 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A global fund. The ITSI Fund’s US$500 million ($100 million a year for five years) is for all partner countries, not for Mexico alone. What followed the 2024 assessment has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

A high homicide rate

  • 33,241 homicides in 2024. Durante 2024 se registraron 33 241 homicidios en México. (unofficial translation: “During 2024, 33,241 homicides were recorded in Mexico.”) (INEGI, 1 August 2025 (opens in a new tab); preliminary).
  • About 26 per 100,000 people. Durante 2024 se obtuvo, de manera preliminar, una tasa de 25.6 homicidios por cada 100 mil habitantes a nivel nacional. (unofficial translation: “In 2024 a preliminary national rate of 25.6 homicides per 100,000 inhabitants was obtained.”) The rate was 46.0 for men and 5.6 for women (INEGI (opens in a new tab)). Wider crime figures are on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Political · PESTLE · Legal · GAP · Crime

Most workers in informal jobs

  • 54.4% informal. Por su parte, la tasa de informalidad laboral se ubicó en 54.4 por ciento. En igual mes de 2024, fue de 54.3 % (unofficial translation: “For its part, the labour informality rate stood at 54.4 per cent. In the same month of 2024, it was 54.3%”) (INEGI, National Survey of Occupation and Employment, March 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 1). These are March figures: about 32.5 million people were in informal work.
  • Registered jobs are fewer. Formal jobs registered with the social security institute are on the Strengths below; they cover only part of all employment.

Also in PESTLE · Economic

A heavily indebted state oil company

  • US$85.2 billion of debt. Al 31 de diciembre, la deuda financiera se ubicó en USD 85.2 mil millones, una disminución de 12.7% respecto al cierre de 2024. (unofficial translation: “At 31 December, financial debt stood at USD 85.2 billion, a decrease of 12.7% from the end of 2024.”) (PEMEX, fourth-quarter 2025 results, 27 February 2026 (opens in a new tab); unaudited).
  • A plan for its finances. The government’s 2025–2035 strategic plan for PEMEX is listed on the TOWS page.

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Drought and water stress

  • More than a third in drought at the end of 2024. Al término del año 2024 el 38.1% de México tiene sequía de moderada a excepcional (D1 a D4) (unofficial translation: “At the end of 2024, 38.1% of Mexico has moderate to exceptional drought (D1 to D4)”) (CONAGUA, Drought Monitor, 31 December 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Less after the 2026 rains. Al 30 de septiembre de 2026, la sequía de moderada a extrema (D1 a D3) ocupó el 12.2 % del territorio nacional, lo que representó un incremento de 1.9 puntos porcentuales respecto al 15 de septiembre del mismo año. (unofficial translation: “At 30 September 2026, moderate to extreme drought (D1 to D3) covered 12.2% of the national territory, an increase of 1.9 percentage points from 15 September of the same year.”) (CONAGUA, Drought Monitor, 30 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • High water stress. Withdrawals of fresh water were 44.9% of available resources in 2022 (World Bank, World Development Indicators (opens in a new tab), latest year).

Low spending on research

  • 0.25% of GDP. Mexico spent 0.25% of GDP on research and development in 2024, after 0.27% in 2023 and 0.26% in 2022 (World Bank, World Development Indicators (opens in a new tab), updated 8 October 2026).

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Falling poverty

  • 38.5 million people in poverty in 2024. En 2024, en México 38.5 millones de personas se encontraban en situación de pobreza multidimensional; 7.0 millones estaban en situación de pobreza extrema (unofficial translation: “In 2024, 38.5 million people in Mexico were in multidimensional poverty; 7.0 million were in extreme poverty”) (INEGI, 13 August 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • From 41.9% to 29.6%. The share of people in poverty was 41.9% (51.9 million) in 2018 and 29.6% in 2024. Entre 2022 y 2024, la población en situación de pobreza multidimensional pasó de 46.8 a 38.5 millones: la disminución fue de 8.3 millones de personas. (unofficial translation: “Between 2022 and 2024, the population in multidimensional poverty went from 46.8 to 38.5 million: a decrease of 8.3 million people.”) (INEGI (opens in a new tab)). This is the first measurement by INEGI, which took over the task from CONEVAL. The target is on the GAP page.

Protected land and sea

  • About a quarter of the sea and an eighth of the land. De la superficie total del país, se protegen 23,096,563.19 hectáreas, de superficie terrestre, esto corresponde al 11.76% de la superficie terrestre protegida. En cuanto a la superficie marina, se protegen 74,904,155.39 hectáreas, esto corresponde al 23.78% de superficie marina protegida. (unofficial translation: “Of the country’s total area, 23,096,563.19 hectares of land are protected, which is 11.76% of the land area. As for the marine area, 74,904,155.39 hectares are protected, which is 23.78% of the marine area.”) (CONANP, protected areas information system (opens in a new tab)).
  • 232 federal protected areas. The national protected areas commission lists 232 decreed federal protected areas (CONANP (opens in a new tab)). The 2030 target is on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Protected areas

A large export base and record formal jobs

  • US$664.8 billion of exports in 2025. Para 2025, el valor de las exportaciones totales fue de 664 837 mdd, cifra que se integró de exportaciones no petroleras, por 643 592 mdd y de petroleras, por 21 246 mdd. (unofficial translation: “For 2025, total exports were worth US$664,837 million, made up of non-oil exports of US$643,592 million and oil exports of US$21,246 million.”) Exports were US$617,677 million in 2024 (INEGI, 27 January 2026 (opens in a new tab); preliminary).
  • Mostly manufactured goods. Durante 2025, la estructura del valor de las exportaciones de mercancías fue la siguiente: bienes manufacturados, 91.6 %; productos petroleros, 3.2 %; bienes agropecuarios, 3.1 %; y productos extractivos no petroleros, 2.1 por ciento. (unofficial translation: “During 2025, the value of merchandise exports was made up as follows: manufactured goods, 91.6%; oil products, 3.2%; agricultural goods, 3.1%; and non-oil extractive products, 2.1 per cent.”) (INEGI (opens in a new tab)).
  • A record number of registered jobs. Al 30 de septiembre de 2026, se tienen registrados ante el Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social (IMSS) 22,927,276 (...) puestos de trabajo, la cifra más alta desde que se tiene registro. (unofficial translation: “At 30 September 2026, 22,927,276 (...) jobs are registered with the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS), the highest figure since records began.”) 87.1% are permanent, and the total rose by 355,594 (1.6%) over twelve months (IMSS, Comunicado 539/2026 (opens in a new tab)). Most workers are still informal; see the Weaknesses above.

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Home of the Latin American nuclear-weapon-free zone

  • The Treaty of Tlatelolco. The treaty, signed in Mexico City on 14 February 1967, commits its parties to peaceful use only: Las Partes Contratantes se comprometen a utilizar exclusivamente con fines pacíficos el material y las instalaciones nucleares sometidos a su jurisdicción, y a prohibir e impedir en sus respectivos territorios: a. El ensayo, uso, fabricación, producción o adquisición, por cualquier medio, de toda arma nuclear (unofficial translation: “The Contracting Parties undertake to use exclusively for peaceful purposes the nuclear material and facilities under their jurisdiction, and to prohibit and prevent in their respective territories: a. The testing, use, manufacture, production or acquisition, by any means, of any nuclear weapon”) (Treaty of Tlatelolco (opens in a new tab), Article 1(1)).
  • Depositary and host. Mexico is the treaty’s depositary government (Article 27(2)), and the treaty body OPANAL is based in Mexico City: La sede del Organismo será la ciudad de México. (unofficial translation: “The seat of the Agency shall be Mexico City.”) (Article 7(4) (opens in a new tab)). Mexico holds no nuclear warheads (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
