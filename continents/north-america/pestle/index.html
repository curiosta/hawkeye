North America PESTLE

The large outside forces acting on a place, in six groups: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental.

  • SWOT
  • PESTLE
  • GAP
  • TOWS
  • SOAR

Political 4 points

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • About US$1 trillion. North America’s states spent US$1,009.0 billion on their militaries in 2025, 34.9% of the world total, and the United States US$954.4 billion of it (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)).
  • 5,042 nuclear warheads, all held by the United States. 41.4% of the world’s 12,187 in January 2026 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), the share is Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Russia 5,420 and China 620 warheads. In January 2026 Russia held 5,420 nuclear warheads and China 620, out of a world total of 12,187 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), table of world nuclear forces).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Military capacity · SWOT · Threats · Foreign nuclear arsenals

Governance and cooperation

  • All 33 Latin American and Caribbean states. Todos los 33 Estados de la América Latina y el Caribe son Partes en el Tratado de Tlatelolco y, por lo tanto, Miembros del OPANAL. Unofficial translation: all 33 Latin American and Caribbean states are parties to the Treaty of Tlatelolco and so members of OPANAL (OPANAL (opens in a new tab)). 21 of them are in North America: every state except the US and Canada.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A nuclear-weapon-free zone

Economic 6 points

Economy and finance

  • US$35.8 trillion. The 24 economies with IMF data had a combined GDP of US$35,813.0 billion in 2025, 30.2% of the world’s US$118,485.3 billion (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The IMF publishes no figure for Cuba.
  • Mostly the United States. The US accounts for US$30,861.3 billion, 86.2% of the total. Canada US$2,321.2 billion, Mexico US$1,835.0 billion (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Nearly half of workers. The region is also facing an undynamic, segmented and exclusionary labor market, with high levels of informality that affect 47% of the region’s employed persons. (ECLAC, 26 November 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Scope. These ECLAC figures cover all of Latin America and the Caribbean, including South America. No North America figure was found (TBC).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A large economy · SWOT · Weaknesses · Informal work (Latin America and the Caribbean)

Water and food security

  • Energy, fertilizer and transport prices (Latin America and the Caribbean). Geopolitical rivalries and the disruption of energy supplies have increased the prices of oil, fertilizers, and transportation, while the persistence of relatively high international interest rates and the appreciation of the dollar could worsen financing conditions for emerging economies. (ECLAC, 20 August 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • So far, less on food. The same release adds: The recent price shock has been mainly concentrated on energy and fertilizers, with a more limited transmission to food than in previous incidences. (ECLAC (opens in a new tab))

Social 8 points

Population and demographics

  • 78.1 years. Life expectancy at birth across North America was 78.1 years in 2025, weighted by population, against 73.5 for the world (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab), Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • A wide range. Northern America 79.9 years, Central America 75.3, the Caribbean 73.4. Among the states, Canada 82.9 and Haiti 65.3 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Long lives

Human development

  • The richest tenth gets 34.2%. ECLAC warns that income concentration continues to be extreme in Latin America, since the wealthiest 10% obtains 34.2% of total income, while the poorest 10% only gets 1.7% (ECLAC, 26 November 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Poverty. 25.5% of the Latin American population (162 million people) were living in situations of income poverty in 2024 (ECLAC (opens in a new tab)). These figures include South America.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Unequal incomes (Latin America)

Water and food security

  • 17.5% undernourished. Finally, the Caribbean had the highest prevalence among the three subregions, estimated at 17.5 percent, which has remained relatively unchanged over the past three years. (FAO, Regional Overview 2025 (opens in a new tab), 2024 nowcast)
  • Haiti most of all. Haiti had the highest prevalence of undernourishment, estimated at 54.2 percent (FAO (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Hunger in the Caribbean

Pandemics and health

  • About 38,500 US deaths in 2025. Synthetic opioids other than methadone, mainly illicit fentanyl, were involved in 38,506 of 70,735 US overdose deaths in 2025 (54%) (CDC NCHS, provisional data as of 14 October 2026 (opens in a new tab), the share is Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Canada. There was a total of 57,509 apparent opioid toxicity deaths reported between January 2016 and March 2026, and Of all apparent opioid toxicity deaths so far in 2026 (Jan to Mar), 36% involved fentanyl and 70% involved fentanyl analogues (Public Health Agency of Canada, updated 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Synthetic opioids

Technological 3 points

Technology and AI

  • 3.31% of GDP. Northern America spent 3.31% of GDP on research and development in 2023, against 1.92% for the world. The United States 3.45%, Canada 1.81% (UN SDG Global Database, UNESCO data (opens in a new tab)).
  • Far lower to the south. Latin America and the Caribbean 0.57%, Mexico 0.27% (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab)). No Central America or Caribbean figure is published.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Research spending (Northern America)

Natural resources

  • The largest LNG exporter (United States). LNG exports from the United States increased by 26% to 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025, a larger increase than from any other country, according to our Natural Gas Monthly. U.S. exports amounted to 26% of the global total in 2025, up from 21% in 2024. (US Energy Information Administration, 14 July 2026 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · Foreign demand for US liquefied natural gas

Legal 4 points

Economy and finance

  • A trade agreement for three countries. The USMCA between the United States, Mexico and Canada entered into force on 1 July 2020. REPLACE the 1994 North American Free Trade Agreement with a 21st Century, high standard new agreement to support mutually beneficial trade leading to freer, fairer markets, and to robust economic growth in the region; PRESERVE AND EXPAND regional trade and production by further incentivizing the production and sourcing of goods and materials in the region; (USMCA, Preamble (opens in a new tab))
  • Not renewed at the 2026 review. The United States did not agree to renew the USMCA in its current form. As a result, the USMCA is not renewed. However, the Agreement remains in force pending resolution of these issues or until the Agreement’s termination. (USTR, 1 July 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Yearly reviews from now on. the Commission will meet to conduct a joint review annually until each of the three Parties confirms its wish to extend the term of this Agreement for another 16-year period or the term of the Agreement expires, whichever is earlier. (Federal Register, 5 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Comments for the 2027 review are due by 12 January 2027 (USTR, 2 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Statements by Canada and Mexico have not been checked (TBC).

Governance and cooperation

  • Central American integration. EL SISTEMA DE LA INTEGRACION CENTROAMERICANA tiene por objetivo fundamental la realización de la integración de Centroamérica, para constituirla como Región de Paz, Libertad, Democracia y Desarrollo. (unofficial translation: “The Central American Integration System has as its fundamental objective the integration of Central America, to make it a Region of Peace, Freedom, Democracy and Development.”) (Protocolo de Tegucigalpa (opens in a new tab), Article 3). SICA has eight members, 10.5% of North America’s population (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, UN WPP 2024). The member list has not been confirmed at sica.int (TBC).

Environmental 5 points

Climate change / global warming

  • 10.0 tonnes a person. North America emitted 6,084 Mt of fossil CO2 in 2024, 15.8% of the world total and 10.0 tonnes per person, the most of any continent (world 4.7 tonnes) (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A large historical share. 500,370 Mt since 1850, 27.1% of the world total, third after Asia and Europe. The United States is 86.9% of it (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · The highest emissions per person

Natural and cosmic hazards

  • The first Category 5 landfall in Jamaica. This was exemplified by Hurricane Melissa in October 2025 - the first Category 5 hurricane on record to make landfall in Jamaica. It led to 45 deaths and economic losses of approximately 8.8 billion US dollars, more than 41% of GDP. (WMO, 18 May 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Rising seas. Along Atlantic-facing coasts, rates of sea level rise are exceeding the global average in parts of the tropical Atlantic and Caribbean. (WMO (opens in a new tab))
  • 27 billion-dollar disasters in the United States in 2024. In 2024, there were 27 individual weather and climate disasters with at least $1 billion in damages, trailing only the record-setting 28 events analyzed in 2023. These disasters caused at least 568 direct or indirect fatalities, which is the eighth-highest for these billion-dollar disasters over the last 45 years (1980-2024). The cost was approximately $182.7 billion. (NOAA Climate.gov, 10 January 2025 (opens in a new tab)). NOAA has retired the series, so 2024 is its last year. No single loss total for the whole continent was found.

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a continent, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups. Where a continent has no permanent population, government or national economy, as with Antarctica, the groups follow what its governing treaties and official bodies report on.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Official sources, and labelled analysis. Hawkeye reports official and authoritative sources, quoted or attributed, with a link. Documented goals and programmes appear as those bodies stated them. TOWS strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice to any person or firm. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
