Documented regional responses 9

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • The Antarctic Treaty

    Adopted by The 12 original signatories, and the Parties that have acceded since. There are now 58 Parties, of which 29 are Consultative Parties, which take decisions. Signed in Washington on 1 December 1959. Entered into force in 1961

    The preamble records the Parties Recognizing that it is in the interest of all mankind that Antarctica shall continue for ever to be used exclusively for peaceful purposes and shall not become the scene or object of international discord; Article I(1): Antarctica shall be used for peaceful purposes only. There shall be prohibited, inter alia, any measures of a military nature, such as the establishment of military bases and fortifications, the carrying out of military maneuvers, as well as the testing of any type of weapons. (The Antarctic Treaty (opens in a new tab)). The Secretariat states: The Antarctic Treaty was signed in Washington on 1 December 1959 by the twelve countries whose scientists had been active in and around Antarctica during the International Geophysical Year (IGY) of 1957-58. It entered into force in 1961 and has since been acceded to by many other nations. The total number of Parties to the Treaty is now 58. (Antarctic Treaty Secretariat (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Peaceful use only; no nuclear explosions · SWOT · Strengths · Claims frozen and science shared

  • Protocol on Environmental Protection to the Antarctic Treaty (Madrid Protocol)

    Adopted by The Consultative Parties at the time of adoption, and later Parties. Signed in Madrid on 4 October 1991. Entered into force in 1998

    Article 2: The Parties commit themselves to the comprehensive protection of the Antarctic environment and dependent and associated ecosystems and hereby designate Antarctica as a natural reserve, devoted to peace and science. Article 7: Any activity relating to mineral resources, other than scientific research, shall be prohibited. (Environmental Protocol (opens in a new tab)). The Secretariat’s note that the Protocol does not expire in 2048 is quoted on the SWOT Strengths page (Antarctic Treaty Secretariat (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A natural reserve devoted to peace and science · SWOT · Strengths · Mining banned except for science

  • Convention on the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources

    Adopted by The Contracting Parties. Drawn up at Canberra on 20 May 1980

    Article II(1): The objective of this Convention is the conservation of Antarctic marine living resources. Article II(2): For the purposes of this Convention, the term ‘conservation’ includes rational use. (CAMLR Convention (opens in a new tab)). The krill catch limit and the marine protected area network are tracked on the GAP page.

    Related SWOT · Threats · Krill fishing pressure · SWOT · Weaknesses · Marine protection short of its target; krill catch limits not spread out · SWOT · Opportunities · Three proposed marine protected areas · GAP · Krill catch and the trigger level · GAP · Marine protected area network

  • Conservation Measure 91-04 (2011): general framework for marine protected areas

    Adopted by Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources, adopted 2011

    The aim of a representative network of marine protected areas by 2012 is quoted on the GAP page (Conservation Measure 91-04 (2011) (opens in a new tab), preamble).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Marine protection short of its target; krill catch limits not spread out · SWOT · Opportunities · Three proposed marine protected areas · GAP · Marine protected area network

  • Annex VI to the Environmental Protocol (liability arising from environmental emergencies)

    Adopted by Measure 1 (2005), adopted at the 28th Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meeting, Stockholm, 17 June 2005. Not yet in force

    The rule that the annex enters into force only when every entitled Consultative Party has approved it is quoted on the GAP page (depositary status list (opens in a new tab)). At ATCM 48, the Meeting called on all Parties that had not yet implemented Annex VI to do so as a matter of priority. (ATCM 48 Final Report (opens in a new tab), paragraph 252).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Liability annex not in force; the all-party approval rule · GAP · Liability annex (Annex VI) entering into force

  • Climate Change Response Work Programme

    Adopted by Committee for Environmental Protection, as set out in the ATCM XLVI Final Report, Appendix 2, and kept under review. The work plan runs from CEP 2025 to CEP 2029

    The vision is quoted on the GAP page (CCRWP (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Ice loss and record-low sea ice · GAP · Climate Change Response Work Programme

  • Paris Declaration, ATCM XLIII (2021)

    Adopted by Antarctic Treaty Consultative Parties, adopted at ATCM XLIII, Paris, 2021. A political declaration, not a treaty

    The encouragement to Treaty Parties that have not yet joined the Environmental Protocol is quoted on the GAP page (Paris Declaration, ATCM XLIII (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A natural reserve devoted to peace and science · GAP · Environmental Protocol membership

  • Decision 5 (2026): Multi-year Strategic Work Plan

    Adopted by Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meeting 48, Hiroshima, 12 to 21 May 2026. The plan annexed to the Decision will no longer be current at the end of ATCM 49

    The Representatives, Reaffirming the values, objectives and principles contained in the Antarctic Treaty and its Protocol on Environmental Protection; decided to adopt the Plan annexed to the Decision (ATCM 48 Final Report (opens in a new tab), Decision 5 (2026)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Claims frozen and science shared

  • Antarctic Climate Change and the Environment: 2026 Update

    Adopted by Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR), working paper WP 31 to ATCM 48. Scientific advice, not a binding instrument

    Key message: Abrupt changes are already underway in the Antarctic region with others on the verge of taking place. A regime shift has reduced Antarctic sea-ice extent far below its natural variability of past centuries. Themes from recent research include that, without a reduction in emissions, Antarctic environments will continue along a pathway of substantial changes, where it is increasingly likely that these changes will be unable to be reversed even within centuries. (SCAR, ACCE 2026 Update (opens in a new tab)). SCAR encourages Antarctic Treaty Parties to continue their efforts to support research to understand the changing Antarctic environment across physical and living systems. (SCAR, ACCE 2026 Update (opens in a new tab), Recommendations).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Ice loss and record-low sea ice

How this TOWS matrix works

For a continent, the responses are documented instruments of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, each named with its members and the date it was adopted.

Every response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Placing a response in a cell is Hawkeye’s only editorial step. Responses are not ranked.
