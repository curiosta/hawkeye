Antarctica GAP

Where things stand now compared with an officially agreed target.

The targets here are set by the bodies of the Antarctic Treaty System: the Consultative Meetings, their Committee for Environmental Protection, and the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR).

Targets and latest values

Marine protected area network

Target
A representative network of marine protected areas by 2012. CCAMLR recalls the aim in accordance with the decision at the World Summit on Sustainable Development (WSSD) in 2002 to achieve a representative network of MPAs by 2012, (Conservation Measure 91-04 (2011) (opens in a new tab), preamble)
Baseline
The first CCAMLR marine protected area, the South Orkney Islands southern shelf MPA, was agreed in 2009 (CCAMLR, Spatial management (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2 MPAs (South Orkney Islands southern shelf, 2009; Ross Sea region, agreed 2016, in force 2017) in 9 planning domains; three more under consideration in domains 1, 3 and 7 (CCAMLR, 31 March 2023 (opens in a new tab)). At CCAMLR-44 a revised measure that included parts of the proposed Domain 1 MPA did not reach consensus (Fishery Report 2025 (opens in a new tab), published 2 March 2026).
Distance
Not computed. The target does not give a number of protected areas. The 2012 date has passed.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Liability annex (Annex VI) entering into force

Target
Entry into force once every entitled Consultative Party has approved: The Annex becomes effective upon the date on which Measure 1 adopted at the XXVIIIth Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meeting has been approved by all Consultative Parties entitled to attend the XXVIIIth Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meeting. (depositary status list (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Adopted 17 June 2005: Done: Stockholm June 17, 2005 (depositary status list (opens in a new tab))
Latest
19 of 28 approvals; Entry into force: not yet in force (depositary status, 6 October 2025). Hawkeye’s count from the depositary list (opens in a new tab) and the Antarctic Treaty Secretariat list of Parties (opens in a new tab).
Distance
9 approvals (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 28 − 19): Argentina, Belgium, Brazil, Bulgaria, China, India, Japan, the Republic of Korea and the United States.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Krill catch and the trigger level

Target
Catch in Area 48 would be limited to a “trigger level” of 620,000 tonnes in any fishing season until the Commission defined an allocation of this total catch limit between smaller management units, based on the advice from the Scientific Committee. (CCAMLR Fishery Report 2024 (opens in a new tab); Conservation Measure 51-01). The spatial limits in Conservation Measure 51-07 expired at the end of the 2024 season.
Baseline
498,350 tonnes in 2024, then the highest on record (Fishery Report 2024 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
624,918 tonnes in 2025 (Fishery Report 2025 (opens in a new tab), section 2.1); the fishery was closed on 1 August 2025 when the trigger level was reached (SC-CAMLR-44 (opens in a new tab), paragraph 2.18).
Distance
4,917 tonnes above the trigger level, as reported by the Scientific Committee (SC-CAMLR-44 (opens in a new tab), paragraph 2.13). The Fishery Report total gives 4,918 tonnes (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 624,918 − 620,000); the one-tonne difference is between the two sources.
Assessment by the tracking agency
The Scientific Committee noted record krill catches in Statistical Area 48 during the 2024/25 season, which exceeded the trigger level of 620 000 tonnes specified in CM 51-01 by 4 917 tonnes. (SC-CAMLR-44 (opens in a new tab), paragraph 2.13). No revised measure was agreed at CCAMLR-44 (Fishery Report 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Environmental Protocol membership

Target
The Paris Declaration (2021) encourages every Treaty Party to join: the parties encourage those States that are Party to the Antarctic Treaty but not yet Party to the Environmental Protocol, including its Annexes, as well as other instruments of the Antarctic Treaty system in accordance with their provisions, to accede to them. (Paris Declaration, ATCM XLIII (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
forty-one Parties at the time of this declaration (2021) (Paris Declaration (opens in a new tab))
Latest
42 of 58 Treaty Parties (Hawkeye’s count from the Antarctic Treaty Secretariat list of Parties (opens in a new tab), 18 October 2026)
Distance
16 Treaty Parties not yet Party (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 58 − 42): Costa Rica, Cuba, Denmark, Estonia, Guatemala, Hungary, Iceland, Kazakhstan, the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea, Mongolia, Papua New Guinea, San Marino, Saudi Arabia, Slovakia, Slovenia and the United Arab Emirates.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Climate Change Response Work Programme

Target
A process commitment with no number attached: CCRWP Vision: Taking into account the conclusions and recommendations from the ATME on Climate Change in 2010, the CCRWP provides a mechanism for identifying and revising goals and specific actions by the CEP to support efforts within the Antarctic Treaty System to prepare for, and build resilience to, the environmental impacts of a changing climate and the associated implications for the governance and management of Antarctica. (ATCM XLVI Final Report, Appendix 2 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Not applicable (no measured starting value)
Latest
The work plan covers five climate-related issues and runs from CEP 2025 to CEP 2029 (CCRWP (opens in a new tab)). At the 2026 meeting: To address rapid climate shifts, the Committee implemented its Climate Change Response Work Programme. (ATCM 48 – CEP 28 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. There is no numeric target.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 17–18 October 2026.

How this GAP analysis works

For a continent, the targets are those set by the regional or treaty bodies that govern it, plus the global targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
