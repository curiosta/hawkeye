A Aspirations

Antarctica has no countries and no permanent population. The Aspirations below are those of the Antarctic Treaty System: the Antarctic Treaty, the Protocol on Environmental Protection and the Convention on the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources, with the Climate Change Response Work Programme of the Committee for Environmental Protection. They are quoted in those instruments’ own words.

Peaceful use only

The Antarctic Treaty, signed 1 December 1959, in force 1961 · 58 Parties, 29 Consultative

The preamble records the Parties Recognizing that it is in the interest of all mankind that Antarctica shall continue for ever to be used exclusively for peaceful purposes and shall not become the scene or object of international discord; Article I(1): Antarctica shall be used for peaceful purposes only. (The Antarctic Treaty (opens in a new tab))

A natural reserve, devoted to peace and science

Protocol on Environmental Protection, signed 4 October 1991, in force 1998

The Parties commit themselves to the comprehensive protection of the Antarctic environment and dependent and associated ecosystems and hereby designate Antarctica as a natural reserve, devoted to peace and science. (Environmental Protocol (opens in a new tab), Article 2)

The mining ban in Article 7 is quoted on the SWOT Strengths page.

Conservation of Antarctic marine living resources

CAMLR Convention, drawn up at Canberra on 20 May 1980

The objective of this Convention is the conservation of Antarctic marine living resources. For the purposes of the Convention, the term ‘conservation’ includes rational use. (CAMLR Convention (opens in a new tab), Article II(1)–(2))

The krill catch limit and the marine protected area network are tracked on the GAP page.

Prepare for, and build resilience to, a changing climate

Climate Change Response Work Programme of the Committee for Environmental Protection, work plan from CEP 2025 to CEP 2029

CCRWP Vision: Taking into account the conclusions and recommendations from the ATME on Climate Change in 2010, the CCRWP provides a mechanism for identifying and revising goals and specific actions by the CEP to support efforts within the Antarctic Treaty System to prepare for, and build resilience to, the environmental impacts of a changing climate and the associated implications for the governance and management of Antarctica. (ATCM XLVI Final Report, Appendix 2 (opens in a new tab))

How this SOAR analysis works

For a continent, the Aspirations are goal statements of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, quoted word for word and labelled with each body’s members.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
