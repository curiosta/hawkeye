Targets and latest values

43% below 2005 by 2030

Target
(a) reducing Australia’s net greenhouse gas emissions to 43% below 2005 levels by 2030: (i) implemented as a point target; and (ii) implemented as an emissions budget covering the period 2021‑2030; (Climate Change Act 2022 (opens in a new tab), s 10(1)(a))
Baseline
2005 net emissions (the base year). The value has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Latest
Emissions for the year to March 2026 are estimated to be 455.6 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Mt CO 2 -e), a decrease of 1.6% (7.5 Mt CO 2 -e) compared with the previous year. Emissions in the year to March 2026 were 25.0% below 2005 levels (DCCEEW, 28 August 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The department also states: Australia has used 60% of its 2030 emissions budget.
Distance
18.0 percentage points short of the reduction target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 43 − 25)
Assessment by the tracking agency
The department projects that Australia will reduce its greenhouse gas emissions by 42% below 2005 levels by 2030 and be 3% below Australia’s 2021–2030 emissions budget. That is, Australia more than meets its 2030 target on a budget basis. (DCCEEW, Australia’s emissions projections 2025 (opens in a new tab))

62–70% below 2005 by 2035 (Paris pledge)

Target
Reduction in net national greenhouse gas emissions of 62–70% below 2005 levels by 2035, implemented as an emissions budget covering the period 2031–35. Indicative target values, based on net national emissions reported in 2025, are 1,248 to 1,395 Mt CO2-e. (Australia’s 2035 NDC (opens in a new tab), Annex A). An executive commitment under the Paris Agreement, submitted in September 2025; not in the Climate Change Act.
Baseline
2005 net emissions (the base year). The value has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Latest
25.0% below 2005 levels in the year to March 2026 (DCCEEW (opens in a new tab)); see the 2030 row.
Distance
Not computed. The target is a range and a budget for 2031–2035; no emissions in that period exist yet.
Assessment by the tracking agency
The department projects a 48% reduction on 2005 levels in 2035 and states: Over the period 2031 to 2035, emissions are 20-34% above the 2031–2035 emissions budget. It notes: The 2025 projections do not include policies announced with the 2035 target. (DCCEEW, emissions projections 2025 (opens in a new tab))

Net zero by 2050

Target
(b) reducing Australia’s net greenhouse gas emissions to zero by 2050. (Climate Change Act 2022 (opens in a new tab), s 10(1)(b))
Baseline
2005 net emissions (the base year of the other targets). The value has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Latest
455.6 Mt CO2-e in the year to March 2026, 25.0% below 2005 levels (DCCEEW (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
75.0 percentage points of the 2005 level short of net zero (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 100 − 25)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published. The department’s projections reach a 53% reduction on 2005 levels by 2040. (DCCEEW, emissions projections 2025 (opens in a new tab))

82% renewable electricity by 2030

Target
The Government’s target, described in its emissions projections as the federal government’s target to 82% on-grid renewable generation nationally, by 2030 (DCCEEW, emissions projections 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The Minister for Climate Change and Energy: One of the key pillars of our energy plan is the target of 82 per cent renewables in our energy mix by 2030 – up from around 33 per cent when we came into government. (Speech to the Lowy Institute, 21 November 2023 (opens in a new tab)). A government target, not a law.
Baseline
Around 33% in 2022, in the Minister’s words above. The official on-grid share for 2022 has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Latest
On-grid electricity, constituting over 92% of all electricity generated in Australia, reached 42.0% renewables in 2025. (Australian Energy Statistics 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
40.0 percentage points below the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 82 − 42)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Protect 30% of land and sea by 2030

Target
The Australian Government has set a national target to protect and conserve 30% of Australia’s landmass and 30% of Australia’s marine areas by 2030 (the ‘30 by 30’ target). (DCCEEW, Achieving 30 by 30 (opens in a new tab)). Also: In June 2025, the Environment Minister committed to ensuring 30% of Australia’s marine environment is highly protected by 2030.
Baseline
Not sourced yet (TBC)
Latest
Marine: Our marine protected areas cover 52% of Australian waters. About 24% is highly protected (no-take). Land: 25% of Australia’s land is protected. (DCCEEW, updated 22 July 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Marine: 52% against 30%, 22 percentage points above the target; highly protected 24% against the 30% commitment, 6 points below. Land: 25% against 30%, 5 percentage points below (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Our national 30 by 30 target for land is coming closer. (DCCEEW (opens in a new tab))

Close the life-expectancy gap by 2031 (Closing the Gap Target 1)

Target
Close the Gap in life expectancy within a generation, by 2031. (National Agreement on Closing the Gap, Targets (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Gap of 8.7 years (males) and 8.0 years (females), 2015–2017 (Productivity Commission (opens in a new tab), dataset CtG1A.1)
Latest
In 2020–2022 the gap in life expectancy for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander males and females was 8.8 years and 8.1 years, respectively. (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab), printed p. 20). The next estimate (2025–2027 data) is due in 2029.
Distance
The target is a gap of zero, so the distance is the gap itself: 8.8 years (males) and 8.1 years (females).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Life expectancy for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people is improving but the target to close the gap by 2031 is not on track to be met (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Healthy birthweight 91% by 2031 (Closing the Gap Target 2)

Target
By 2031, increase the proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander babies with a healthy birthweight to 91 per cent. (Closing the Gap, Targets (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
88.8% (2017) (Productivity Commission (opens in a new tab), dataset CtG2A.1)
Latest
In 2023, 88.9% of the 19,544 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander babies were born at a healthy birthweight, down slightly from 89.2% in 2022. (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
2.1 percentage points below the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 91 − 88.9)
Assessment by the tracking agency
The target shows no change since the baseline (2017), with the target of 91% of babies born at a healthy birthweight by 2031 not on track to be met; the Commission says this assessment is provided with a low level of confidence (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Preschool enrolment 95% by 2025 (Closing the Gap Target 3)

Target
By 2025, increase the proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children enrolled in Year Before Fulltime Schooling (YBFS) early childhood education to 95 per cent. (Closing the Gap, Targets (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
61.3% (2016) (Productivity Commission (opens in a new tab))
Latest
Nationally in 2025, 101.7% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children in the YBFS age cohort were enrolled in early childhood education. (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). A figure above 100% reflects how the population estimates used in the calculation are made.
Distance
Target met (Productivity Commission).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Target has been met (Productivity Commission, annual data report 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Work is under way to set a new Target 3.

Under-5 mortality (SDG 3.2)

Target
By 2030, end preventable deaths of newborns and children under 5 years of age, with all countries aiming to reduce neonatal mortality to at least as low as 12 per 1,000 live births and under-5 mortality to at least as low as 25 per 1,000 live births (UN SDG target 3.2 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
3.88 deaths per 1,000 live births (2015) (UN SDG database, series SH_DYN_MORT (opens in a new tab))
Latest
3.61 per 1,000 live births (2024) (UN SDG database (opens in a new tab), UN IGME estimates)
Distance
Below the ceiling of 25 per 1,000 named in the target, by 21.4 deaths per 1,000 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 25 − 3.61). The target’s first clause, ending preventable deaths, is not measured by a single number.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 19 October 2026. Quotations are given as published.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
