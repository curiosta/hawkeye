Australia PESTLE

The large outside forces acting on a place, in six groups: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental.

Political 3 points

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • A new defence strategy. The 2026 National Defence Strategy, released on 16 April 2026, replaced the 2024 strategy. The Department of Defence says: The 2024 NDS set out for the first time the concept of national defence and adopted the strategy of denial as the cornerstone of Defence planning. (Department of Defence (opens in a new tab)).
  • Spending to rise. In line with the common methodology adopted by NATO allies to measure defence expenditure, Defence funding is now projected to rise to approximately 3.0 per cent of GDP by 2033-34. (2026 National Defence Strategy (opens in a new tab), para 10.3). SIPRI puts military spending at US$35.3 billion in 2025, 1.9% of GDP and 1.2% of the world total (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · A more contested region

Governance and cooperation

  • Governance indicators. In the World Bank’s 2025 Worldwide Governance Indicators, Australia is higher than 94.8% of 213 economies on Government Effectiveness (estimate 1.75) and higher than 69.8% of 215 economies on Political Stability and Absence of Violence (estimate 0.78) (World Bank WGI, 2025 release (opens in a new tab)). Percentiles are Hawkeye’s calculations from the published estimates.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Effective government

Economic 3 points

Economy and finance

  • Growth. The Australian economy rose 0.4% in seasonally adjusted chain volume measures. In nominal terms, GDP rose 0.8%. (June quarter 2026); 2.1% through the year (ABS, 2 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Jobs. Unemployment was 4.7% in September 2026 (seasonally adjusted; trend 4.6%), with participation at 66.8% (ABS, 15 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Inflation and interest rates. The Reserve Bank raised the cash rate by 100 basis points in 2026 because inflation was elevated (RBA, Financial Stability Review, October 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Household debt was 178.4% of disposable income in the June quarter of 2026 (RBA, Table E2 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A large, growing economy · SWOT · Weaknesses · High household debt

Social 3 points

Human development

  • The Indigenous life-expectancy gap. 8.8 years for males and 8.1 years for females in 2020–2022. Nationally, the target to close the gap in life expectancy by 2031 is not on track to be met (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Human development. HDI 0.958, rank 7 of 193 (2023) (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · The Indigenous life-expectancy gap · SWOT · Strengths · Very high human development

Population and demographics

  • One in three born overseas. At 30 June 2025, Australia’s population included 8.8 million people who were born overseas. The proportion of Australia’s population born outside Australia was 32.0%. The largest groups were born in India, England, China and New Zealand (ABS, 29 April 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Technological 2 points

Energy transition

  • Renewable electricity. Renewables made up 39.5% of total electricity generation and 42.0% of on-grid generation in 2025, the highest on record (Australian Energy Statistics 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Record renewable electricity

Natural resources

  • A critical minerals strategy. The Critical Minerals Strategy 2023–2030 states: We will move up the critical minerals value chain and increase Australia’s footprint in downstream processing. (DISR, Critical Minerals Strategy, Vision and objectives (opens in a new tab)). Its Vision for 2030 is quoted on the SOAR page.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Mineral refining concentrated in a few countries · SWOT · Opportunities · Rising world demand for battery minerals

Legal 2 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Targets in law. Section 10(1) of the Climate Change Act 2022 sets the targets: (a) reducing Australia’s net greenhouse gas emissions to 43% below 2005 levels by 2030: (i) implemented as a point target; and (ii) implemented as an emissions budget covering the period 2021‑2030; (b) reducing Australia’s net greenhouse gas emissions to zero by 2050. (Climate Change Act 2022 (opens in a new tab), s 10(1)). The 2035 target of 62–70% is in Australia’s Paris Agreement pledge, not in the Act.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · High emissions per person

Economy and finance

  • A national interest framework for investment. The Future Made in Australia Act 2024 (No. 119, 2024, assented 10 December 2024) states: The National Interest Framework will support the Australian Government to make public investments that unlock private investment at scale in the national interest. (Future Made in Australia Act 2024 (opens in a new tab), Preamble).

Environmental 3 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Emissions. Emissions for the year to March 2026 are estimated to be 455.6 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Mt CO 2 -e), a decrease of 1.6% (7.5 Mt CO 2 -e) compared with the previous year. Emissions in the year to March 2026 were 25.0% below 2005 levels (DCCEEW, 28 August 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Fossil CO2 alone was 386.7 Mt in 2024 (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Warming. Land temperatures have risen by 1.51 ± 0.23 °C since 1910 (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · High emissions per person · SWOT · Threats · A warming climate and more fire weather

Biodiversity and ecosystems

  • Protected areas. Marine protected areas cover 52% of Australian waters (4.6 million km²) and 25% of the land is protected (DCCEEW, Achieving 30 by 30 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Half of its waters protected · SWOT · Weaknesses · Declining threatened species

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a country, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups: politics and government, the economy, society and population, technology, laws, and the environment. Official national sources come first.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact. Spanish quotations are given as published, with unofficial English translations.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
