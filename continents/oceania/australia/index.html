T

Threats

External · harmful

A warming climate and more fire weather

  • About 1.5 °C warmer since 1910. Australia’s climate has warmed by an average of 1.51 ± 0.23 °C since national records began in 1910. (CSIRO and Bureau of Meteorology, State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Longer fire seasons. There has been an increase in extreme fire weather, and a longer fire season, across large parts of the country since the 1950s. (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab)). The report is published every two years; a 2026 edition had not appeared by 19 October 2026.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Marine heatwaves and coral bleaching

  • Warmer seas. Sea surface temperatures have increased by an average of 1.08 °C since 1900. (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Bleaching in a La Niña year. The year with the highest average sea surface temperature on record was 2022, which was associated with a strong negative Indian Ocean Dipole event and mass coral bleaching in the Great Barrier Reef, which had never previously occurred in a La Niña event. (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 14).
  • More to come. The report projects Increased and longer-lasting marine heatwaves that will affect marine environments such as kelp forests and increase the likelihood of more frequent and severe bleaching events in coral reefs around Australia, including the Great Barrier Reef and Ningaloo Reef. (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Less cool-season rain in the south-east

  • About 9% less since 1994. In the south-east of Australia, there has been a decrease of around 9% in April to October rainfall since 1994. (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 8).
  • Falling streamflow in the Murray–Darling Basin. In the Murray–Darling Basin, 40 of 129 hydrologic reference stations show significantly declining streamflow trends since 1970 (State of the Climate 2024 (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 12). The rainfall figure above covers south-eastern Australia, not the Basin alone.

A more contested region

  • Major-power competition. The 2026 National Defence Strategy states: Intense competition between major powers – principally the United States and China – will be an enduring feature of our strategic environment. China continues to undertake the largest military build-up in the world today, without the transparency or strategic reassurance the region expects. (Department of Defence, 2026 National Defence Strategy (opens in a new tab), Overview).
  • Risk at a post-war high. The net effect is that Australia will face elevated levels of geopolitical risk over the coming decade and our exposure to force projection and military coercion will reach levels not seen since the Second World War. (2026 National Defence Strategy (opens in a new tab), para 1.4). Australia’s own defence spending is on the PESTLE page.

Also in PESTLE · Political

Mineral refining concentrated in a few countries

  • Record concentration. The International Energy Agency reports: Refining concentration across energy minerals reached new record levels in 2025. The average share of the top refined supplier stood reaching 70% in 2025, up from 68% in 2020. (IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Two main suppliers. The growth was driven by the top producers – Indonesia for nickel and China for most other key energy minerals – which together accounted for over three‑quarters of total supply growth between 2023 and 2025. (IEA, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological

O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Rising world demand for battery minerals

  • Battery demand up a third. Global battery demand grew by over 35% in 2025, surpassing 1.5 TWh, and Lithium demand has been particularly strong, increasing by around 25% per year on average over the past two years. (IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • The largest listed lithium producer. Australia mined an estimated 92,000 tonnes of lithium in 2025 (82,700 t in 2024), against a rounded world total of 290,000 t that excludes the United States, whose figure is withheld; that is about 32% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, not a USGS figure). Its reserves are 8,400,000 t (USGS, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological

High resource export earnings

  • A forecast peak in 2026–27. The September 2026 REQ forecasts resources and energy export earnings to rise to $422 billion in 2026–27, up from $403 billion in 2025–26 and then fall to $391 billion in 2027–28. (Australian dollars; forecasts) (Department of Industry, Science and Resources, 2 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Critical minerals growing. Critical minerals export earnings are forecast to rise from around $17 billion in 2025–26 to $26 billion in 2026–27. The department adds: Growing investment in AI infrastructure and the energy transition is also boosting demand for Australian resources. (DISR (opens in a new tab)). Total earnings are forecast to fall to $379 billion by 2030–31.

Free trade with the United Kingdom and India

  • The United Kingdom. The A-UKFTA was signed on 17 December 2021 and entered into force on 31 May 2023. The agreement has eliminated tariffs on over 99 per cent of Australian goods exports to the UK. (DFAT, Australia-United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement (opens in a new tab)). DFAT’s market snapshot on the same page puts trade with the UK at $35.9 billion in 2024.
  • India. The Australia-India Economic Cooperation and Trade Agreement entered into force on 29 December 2022. Over 90 per cent of Australian goods exports by value to India will have tariffs eliminated or reduced. (DFAT, AI-ECTA (opens in a new tab)). The cuts are phased. DFAT describes the fast-growing Indian market of over 1.4 billion people; talks on a wider agreement (CECA) have resumed but not concluded.
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

High emissions per person

  • Over three times the world average. Australia emitted 386.7 Mt of fossil CO2 in 2024, 1.0% of the world total, or 14.5 tonnes per person against a world average of 4.7 t (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); per person: Hawkeye’s arithmetic with UN WPP 2024 population). In Oceania only New Caledonia is higher per person.
  • All greenhouse gases. Emissions for the year to March 2026 are estimated to be 455.6 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Mt CO 2 -e), a decrease of 1.6% (7.5 Mt CO 2 -e) compared with the previous year. (DCCEEW, National Greenhouse Gas Inventory Quarterly Update: March 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The targets are on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Legal · PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · 2030 target

High household debt

  • 1.8 times income. Household debt was 178.4% of annualised household disposable income in the June quarter of 2026; housing debt alone was 134.9% (Reserve Bank of Australia, Statistical Table E2 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Higher interest rates. The Reserve Bank’s October 2026 Financial Stability Review states: Inflation has been elevated in 2026, reflecting domestic capacity pressures and higher input costs due to the Middle East conflict. As a result, the Monetary Policy Board has increased the cash rate by 100 basis points to help return inflation to target. (RBA, Financial Stability Review, October 2026 (opens in a new tab), Chapter 2).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

The Indigenous life-expectancy gap

  • About eight years. In 2020–2022 the gap in life expectancy for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander males and females was 8.8 years and 8.1 years, respectively. (Productivity Commission, Closing the Gap Annual Data Compilation Report, July 2026 (opens in a new tab), printed p. 20).
  • Not on track. Target 1: The life expectancy gap between Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people and non-Indigenous people is improving but the target of a life expectancy gap of zero is not on track to be met by 2031. (Productivity Commission, July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The full row is on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Closing the Gap, life expectancy

Declining threatened species

  • Threatened mammals down 38%. The 2021 State of the Environment report found that between 1995 and 2016, the abundance of threatened mammals that have been monitored decreased by an average of 38% (Figures 12 and 13) (State of the Environment 2021, Flora and fauna (opens in a new tab)).
  • Birds and plants too. The same report gives a decline of more than 60% in the Threatened Bird Index (1985–2017) and of 72% in the Threatened Plant Index (1995–2017) (State of the Environment 2021 (opens in a new tab)). These are the latest figures Hawkeye has confirmed; newer index releases have not been checked.

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Very high human development

  • 7th in the world. Australia’s Human Development Index is 0.958, ranking 7 of 193 countries (data year 2023), the highest in Oceania (UNDP Human Development Report 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Long lives. Life expectancy at birth is 84.2 years in 2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)); the HDR 2025 gives 83.9 years for 2023 (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social

Effective government

  • Around the 95th percentile. In the World Bank’s 2025 Worldwide Governance Indicators, Australia’s Government Effectiveness estimate is 1.75 (a score of 88.2 out of 100), higher than 94.8% of the 213 economies covered (World Bank WGI, 2025 release (opens in a new tab)). The percentile is Hawkeye’s calculation; the 2025 release no longer publishes percentile ranks.

Also in PESTLE · Political

Half of its waters protected

  • 52% of Australian waters. Our marine protected areas cover 52% of Australian waters. That’s 4.6 million square kilometres. About 24% of our waters, or 2.2 million square kilometres, are in highly protected ‘no-take’ areas. (DCCEEW, Achieving 30 by 30, updated 22 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A quarter of the land. The same page states that 25% of Australia’s land is protected. The 30 by 30 targets are on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · 30 by 30

A large, growing economy

  • Growth of 2.1% in a year. The Australian economy rose 0.4% in seasonally adjusted chain volume measures. in the June quarter of 2026; GDP was 2.1% higher than a year earlier (ABS, Australian National Accounts, June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • About A$2.93 trillion. GDP at current prices was about A$2,927 billion in 2025–26, Hawkeye’s sum of the four quarters published by the ABS (ABS (opens in a new tab), Table 1). The ABS annual figure is due on 23 October 2026.
  • Unemployment at 4.7%. In September 2026 the unemployment rate increased 0.1ppt to 4.7% in seasonally adjusted terms; in trend terms it remained at 4.6% (ABS, Labour Force, September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Record renewable electricity

  • 42% of grid power. On-grid electricity, constituting over 92% of all electricity generated in Australia, reached 42.0% renewables in 2025. This was up 3.9 percentage points from 2024, to be the highest level on record. (Australian Energy Statistics 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Mostly solar and wind. Renewables contributed 39.5% of total electricity generation in 2025, specifically solar (19.6%), wind (14.0%) and hydro (4.7%). (Australian Energy Statistics 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The 82% target is on the GAP page.
