Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Australia has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Link record renewable electricity to a larger share of the battery-mineral chain

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Record renewable electricity · SWOT · Opportunities · Rising world demand for battery minerals

      Use record renewable electricity to process and manufacture more of the battery-mineral chain in Australia, as world battery demand rises. Official Australian programmes already include Future Made in Australia, the Critical Minerals Strategy, and the Clean Energy Finance Corporation. This recommendation pairs that power-mix strength with battery-mineral demand. It is not a claim that no critical-minerals policy exists.

      Analogous case. The IEA reports: China has invested over USD 50 billion in new PV supply capacity – ten times more than Europe − and created as many as 300 000 manufacturing jobs across the solar PV value chain since 2011. Today, China’s share in all the manufacturing stages of solar panels (such as polysilicon, ingots, wafers, cells and modules) exceeds 80%. Those policies contributed to a cost decline more than 80% (IEA, Special Report on Solar PV Global Supply Chains, revised August 2022 (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. On-grid electricity reached 42.0% renewables in 2025, the highest level on record, and global battery demand grew by over 35% in 2025 (live SWOT). The IEA solar case shows that a strategic manufacturing build-out can create jobs and cut costs. The analogy is limited because the IEA case is solar modules, not battery-mineral refining or cell manufacture. Mineral refining is already concentrated in a few countries, which is a live threat. High resource-export earnings in the live SWOT are a forecast, and they include more than battery minerals, so they are not the opportunity used here.

  • WT Responses that reduce weaknesses and limit threats

    • Cut high emissions per person as the climate warms and fire weather rises

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · High emissions per person · SWOT · Threats · A warming climate and more fire weather

      Cut Australia’s high emissions per person, in a climate that is already warmer, with more fire weather. Official Australian programmes already include emissions targets in law and the Clean Energy Finance Corporation. This recommendation pairs that emissions weakness with the warming and fire-weather threat.

      Analogous case. The European Environment Agency, in its 9 November 2026 press release on the Trends and Projections report, states: In 2025, net EU greenhouse gas emissions under the scope of the European Climate Law dropped by 39% below 1990 levels, and This drop also came while gross domestic product and populations grew by 74% and 8% respectively over the same period. The figures for 2025 are preliminary. The same release records that the pace of cuts slowed in 2025, and that year-on-year emissions rose in domestic transport and buildings (EEA, 9 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Australia emitted 386.7 Mt of fossil CO2 in 2024, or 14.5 tonnes per person against a world average of 4.7 t, and official records show average warming of 1.51 ± 0.23 °C since 1910 (live SWOT). The EU cut net emissions 39% below 1990 while GDP grew. The 2025 figures are preliminary. The analogy is limited because electricity is only part of Australia’s emissions, and because transport and buildings in the EU still saw emissions rise in 2025. Emissions cuts do not at once reduce observed fire weather, marine heatwaves or coral bleaching.

Documented national responses 11

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • Emissions targets in law

    Adopted by Parliament, Climate Change Act 2022 (No. 37, 2022), in force 14 September 2022

    Section 3(aa) sets the object to advance an effective and progressive response to the urgent threat of climate change drawing on the best available scientific knowledge; section 10(1) sets 43% below 2005 levels by 2030 and net zero by 2050 (Climate Change Act 2022 (opens in a new tab)). Quotes of s 10(1) on the GAP page.

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · High emissions per person · GAP · 43% below 2005 by 2030 · GAP · Net zero by 2050

  • Future Made in Australia

    Adopted by Parliament, Future Made in Australia Act 2024 (No. 119, 2024), assented 10 December 2024

    Section 3(a): to establish a National Interest Framework to support the consideration of, and decision‑making in relation to, significant public investment that unlocks private investment at scale in the national interest; and (Future Made in Australia Act 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

  • National environment law

    Adopted by Parliament, Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (No. 91, 1999); amended by the Environment Protection Reform Act 2025 (No. 68, 2025)

    Section 3(1) objects include (a) to provide for the protection of the environment, especially those aspects of the environment that are matters of national environmental significance; and (b) to promote ecologically sustainable development through the conservation and ecologically sustainable use of natural resources; and (c) to promote the conservation of biodiversity; and (EPBC Act 1999 (opens in a new tab), Compilation No. 70).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Declining threatened species

  • National Defence Strategy 2026

    Adopted by Australian Government, Department of Defence, released 16 April 2026

    The 2026 NDS builds on these foundations by: Increasing efforts to develop greater self-reliance over the longer term so Defence can more ably and independently deter force projection against Australia. Through the 2026 IIP, the Government is investing around $425 billion over the decade (Department of Defence (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · A more contested region

  • Critical Minerals Strategy 2023–2030

    Adopted by Australian Government, Department of Industry, Science and Resources

    The strategy’s Vision for 2030 is quoted on the SOAR page. It also states: We will move up the critical minerals value chain and increase Australia’s footprint in downstream processing. (DISR (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Mineral refining concentrated in a few countries · SWOT · Opportunities · Rising world demand for battery minerals

  • Murray–Darling water management

    Adopted by Parliament, Water Act 2007 (No. 137, 2007), assented 3 September 2007

    Section 3(d)(i) includes the object to ensure the return to environmentally sustainable levels of extraction for water resources that are overallocated or overused (Water Act 2007 (opens in a new tab), Compilation No. 38).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Less cool-season rain in the south-east

  • Biosecurity

    Adopted by Parliament, Biosecurity Act 2015 (No. 61, 2015), assented 16 June 2015

    Section 4(b) includes the object to give effect to Australia’s international rights and obligations, including under the International Health Regulations, the SPS Agreement, the Ballast Water Convention, the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea and the Biodiversity Convention. (Biosecurity Act 2015 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Clean Energy Finance Corporation

    Adopted by Parliament, Clean Energy Finance Corporation Act 2012 (No. 104, 2012), assented 22 July 2012

    Section 3: The object of this Act is to establish the Clean Energy Finance Corporation to facilitate increased flows of finance into the clean energy sector and to facilitate the achievement of Australia’s greenhouse gas emissions reduction targets. (CEFC Act 2012 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Record renewable electricity

  • National Reconstruction Fund

    Adopted by Parliament, National Reconstruction Fund Corporation Act 2023 (No. 12, 2023), assented 11 April 2023

    Section 3: The object of this Act is to establish the National Reconstruction Fund Corporation to facilitate increased flows of finance into priority areas of the Australian economy. (NRFC Act 2023 (opens in a new tab)). Section 52 credits $5 billion on commencement and a further $10 billion before 2 July 2029. Seven priority areas are set by the NRFC (Priority Areas) Declaration 2026 (F2026L00417 (opens in a new tab)).

  • South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone

    Adopted by Treaty of Rarotonga, opened for signature 6 August 1985; Australia is a party ([1986] ATS 32)

    Article 3: Each Party undertakes: (a) not to manufacture or otherwise acquire, possess or have control over any nuclear explosive device by any means anywhere inside or outside the South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone; (IAEA INFCIRC/331 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Central bank objectives

    Adopted by Parliament, Reserve Bank Act 1959, as amended by the Treasury Laws Amendment (Reserve Bank Reforms) Act 2024 (No. 96, 2024), in force 1 March 2025

    Section 8AA(1): The overarching objective of the Bank is to promote the economic prosperity and welfare of the people of Australia both now and into the future. The Monetary Policy Board is to set monetary policy in the way that best contributes to: (i) price stability in Australia; and (ii) the maintenance of full employment in Australia; and (Reserve Bank Act 1959 (opens in a new tab), s 9B(1)(a)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · High household debt

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
