Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Papua New Guinea has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Use tuna-fishery revenue to fund rural electrification

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Few people with electricity · SWOT · Opportunities · A share of the world’s largest tuna fishery

      Use revenue from the shared tuna purse-seine fishery, through the Vessel Day Scheme already in force, to fund rural electrification. Official Papua New Guinean programmes already include the fisheries management law and the medium-term development plan. This recommendation pairs the access weakness with that fishery opportunity. Papua New Guinea is already a party to the Nauru Agreement, so this is not a membership recommendation.

      Analogous case. The Parties to the Nauru Agreement described the purse-seine fishery in 2016 as the fishery that is generating US$400 million a year to nine Pacific islands. The same official note states: Between 2010 and 2015, revenue to the islands rose from US$60 million to close to US$400 million. An independent review quoted there concluded: The purse seine VDS is a very successful fisheries management regime by any real world standard. (PNA, 29 October 2016 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Papua New Guinea is one of the least electrified countries in the world, with electricity access for less than 20% of people and grid penetration in rural areas of less than 4%, and it already has a share of the Parties to the Nauru Agreement fishery (live SWOT). The 2016 official note documents a large rise in regional purse-seine revenue under the Vessel Day Scheme. The analogy is limited because Papua New Guinea is already a party, and because the US$400 million figure is 2015 revenue for nine Pacific islands, not Papua New Guinea’s current receipt. Fishery revenue is not the same as household connections. Tuna moving east is a live threat and can reduce future revenue.

    Adopted by Government of Papua New Guinea (Department of National Planning and Monitoring), launched 7 July 2023

    Twelve Strategic Priority Areas aligned with Vision 2050 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 46). Quotes on the PESTLE and SOAR pages.

    Related GAP · A K200 billion economy and one million jobs by 2030

  • Environment Act 2000

    Adopted by National Parliament, Environment Act 2000 (No. 64 of 2000)

    Section 4(c) sets the aim to sustain the potential of natural and physical resources to meet the reasonably foreseeable needs of future generations, and safeguard the life-supporting capacity of air, water, land and eco-systems (Environment Act 2000, as enacted (opens in a new tab)).

  • Monetary policy mandate

    Adopted by National Parliament, Central Banking Act 2000 (consolidated to No 17 of 2021)

    Section 7(a) directs monetary policy at price stability and at promoting employment and growth, especially of the non-mineral and non-petroleum sector (Central Banking Act 2000 (opens in a new tab)). Quote on the PESTLE page.

  • Climate change management law

    Adopted by National Parliament, Climate Change (Management) Act 2015 (Act 19 of 2015), amended by Act 25 of 2021

    A regulatory framework for climate-compatible development and for implementing the UNFCCC and the Paris Agreement (Climate Change (Management) Act 2015, consolidated (opens in a new tab)). Quote on the PESTLE page.

  • National Forest Policy, 1991

    Adopted by Government of Papua New Guinea (PNG Forest Authority, National Forest Plan)

    Based on the Constitution’s fourth National Goal; main objective (i): Management and protection of the nation’s forest resource as a renewable natural assets. (National Forest Plan (opens in a new tab), s 2.0).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Large forests · SWOT · Strengths · Land held under customary tenure

    Adopted by National Parliament, Fisheries Management Act 1998 (No. 48 of 1998)

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
