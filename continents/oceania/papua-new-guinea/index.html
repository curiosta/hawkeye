T

Threats

External · harmful

Landslides

  • A common hazard. Landslides, in the National Adaptation Plan’s table of hazards: Are a common occurrence in the high mountainous regions of Papua New Guinea and frequently damage vital infrastructure, upland forests, and the homes and gardens of thousands of residents. (CCDA, National Adaptation Plan, April 2023 (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 20).
  • The Enga landslide, May 2024. A massive landslide occurred on the 24 May of 2024 at 3:00 AM local time in the southeastern area of Maip Muritaka Rural Local Level Government (LLG, Admin 3) in the Highlands´ Enga Province. (IOM, Rapid Assessment Report Round 1 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A fifth of the area affected. The two communities assessed reported a total of 7,849 individuals from 1,427 households affected, with 1,650 individuals from 315 households displaced. IOM estimated that this was at least 21% of the local government area’s population of about 26,450 (IOM (opens in a new tab)). This was a rapid first-round assessment of two communities; Hawkeye does not use a death toll because published estimates differed.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Sea-level rise

  • Islands already affected. The effects of sea-level rise are already apparent in the Carteret Islands and the Mortlock Islands, near the Solomon Islands, where much of the land is now underwater. (CCDA, National Adaptation Plan (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 20).
  • People already moved. islands like Manam and Carteret have already seen the displacement of communities due to environmental degradation and climate-related impacts. (National Adaptation Plan (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 47).
  • Up to 0.3 metres by the 2050s. By the 2050s, Papua New Guinea is likely to experience a sea-level rise of +0.2 to +0.3 meters (against the mean value of 1986-2005) (National Adaptation Plan (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 22, citing the World Bank, 2021).
  • Half a million coastal people. The plan states that nearly 500,000 people throughout 2,000 coastal villages are vulnerable to weather extremes and flooding. The country has 5,152 km of coastline and 40,000 km² of coral reef (National Adaptation Plan (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 11).

High risk of debt distress

  • Rated high risk. Papua New Guinea (PNG) remains at high risk of debt distress under the Low-Income Country Debt Sustainability Framework (LIC-DSF), with weak debt-carrying capacity. Both the risk of external debt distress and the overall risk are rated “High” (Joint IMF-IDA Debt Sustainability Analysis, May 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 1).
  • A bond falls due in 2028. A relatively large breach in 2028 arises due to the bullet payment for the US$500 million Eurobond, which was issued in 2018. (IMF-IDA DSA (opens in a new tab), para. 16).
  • Exports depend on commodities. The same analysis notes the share of commodities in total exports of goods and services exceeding 95 percent (IMF-IDA DSA (opens in a new tab), para. 15). The Asian Development Bank puts it this way: The minerals and energy sector dominates the economy, accounting for 88% of export earnings (ADB, December 2023 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Economic · GAP · K200 billion economy

Tuna moving east

  • Stocks may shift. Modelling by the Pacific Community (SPC) on skipjack and bigeye tuna suggests that in twenty years the sizes of the stocks will not be affected by climate change, but the distribution of the stocks may begin to shift towards the central and eastern Pacific. (World Bank, Pacific Possible: Tuna Fisheries, 2016 (opens in a new tab), executive summary, p. 9).
  • Up to 30% fewer skipjack by 2100. Under the high emissions (A2) scenario, in 2100 catches of skipjack for the western Pacific are estimated to decline further, by an average of over 20 percent and by as much as 30 percent for PNG. (World Bank, 2016 (opens in a new tab), p. 66). For 2035 the same table projects a 3% rise in skipjack catches for Papua New Guinea (Table 10, p. 67, from Bell and others, 2011).
  • A local counterweight. The report adds that increased rainfall and nutrient flow into the Bismarck Sea may retain concentrations of tuna in the Indonesia-PNG archipelagos (World Bank, 2016 (opens in a new tab), p. 67). These projections date from 2011 modelling; newer estimates have not yet been checked by Hawkeye.
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

An alliance with Australia

  • A treaty alliance since July 2026. On 8 July 2026 the two prime ministers announced the entry into force of the Pukpuk Treaty. Australia and Papua New Guinea are now formally allies. This is Papua New Guinea’s first alliance with any country, and Australia’s first in more than 70 years. (Prime Minister of Australia, 8 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Built on a 2023 security agreement. Today, the Prime Minister of Australia Anthony Albanese and the Prime Minister of Papua New Guinea James Marape announced the entry into force of the Australia – Papua New Guinea Bilateral Security Agreement. (joint statement, 12 December 2024 (opens in a new tab)). Since signing the Agreement in December 2023, Australia has responded quickly to support Papua New Guinea’s defence and internal security priorities as its security partner of choice.

Also in PESTLE · Political

A share of the world’s largest tuna fishery

  • A regional fishery. The Parties to the Nauru Agreement (PNA) control the world’s largest sustainable tuna purse seine fishery. PNA Members are Federated States of Micronesia, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Nauru/Naoero, Palau, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands and Tuvalu. (PNA (opens in a new tab)).
  • A large fishing zone. The fisheries Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) of 2.4 million square kilometres is the largest in the South Pacific covering an extended reef system and extensive coastline that supply about 18% of the World’s tuna catch (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 126).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Marine protection (SDG 14.5)

W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Maternal deaths

  • 189 deaths per 100,000 births. The UN’s maternal mortality estimate for Papua New Guinea is 189 per 100,000 live births in 2023 (World Bank WDI, UN MMEIG estimates (opens in a new tab)).
  • WHO’s figure. The maternal mortality ratio remains high at 171 deaths per 100,000 live births. (WHO Papua New Guinea, 29 July 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The development plan uses 171 as its 2020 baseline, sourced to the Demographic and Health Survey 2016–2018, and aims for 121 by 2027 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), SPA 3 indicator table and Table 10.4, printed p. 64).

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Maternal mortality (SDG 3.1)

Few people with electricity

  • One of the least electrified countries. PNG is one of the least electrified countries in the world—less than 20% of the population has access to electricity. Grid penetration in rural areas is less than 4%. (ADB Concept Paper, December 2023 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A jump in the latest figure. The World Bank series for access to electricity is 20.9% of the population in 2021, 19.0% in 2022, 20.5% in 2023 and 43.3% in 2024 (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab)). No reason for the 2024 jump has been published with the data.
  • The national target. The development plan aims for 70% of households by 2030; household access was 27% in 2021 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 154 and Table 10.3). The full row is on the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · Electricity access

Few police for the population

  • One police officer per 1,846 people. The Government plans to reduce the crime rate by 55% from 49 crimes committed per 100,000 people down to 22, improve ratio of police personnel to people from 1:1,846 to 1:1,200, reduce the back log of court cases by 30%, reduce the prison population and improve ratio of correctional officers to inmates by 2027 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), SPA 5, PDF p. 178).
  • A 2020 baseline. The plan’s indicator table gives the police-to-population ratio as 1:1,846 in 2020, sourced to the police National Crime Statistics Report, with a target of 1:1,200 by 2027 (PDF p. 181). A later measured ratio has not yet been confirmed at source.

Low human development

  • 160th of 193. Papua New Guinea’s Human Development Index is 0.576 (data year 2023), ranked 160 of 193 and the lowest of the 14 Oceania countries in the report (UNDP Human Development Report 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Five years of school on average. Adults have 5.0 mean years of schooling, and children can expect 11.5 years; life expectancy at birth was 66.1 years in 2023 (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Large forests

  • 78% forest cover. The climate pledge centres on maintaining PNG’s high forest cover, which it reports at 78 percent, citing a 2019 study of forest and land use change from 2000 to 2015 (Enhanced NDC, December 2020 (opens in a new tab), executive summary, p. i).
  • Part of the third-largest rainforest. PNG and Indonesia’s West Papua together is said to have the largest contiguous area of forest in the Asia-Pacific and the third largest tropical rainforest after the Amazon and the Congo forests. (PNG Forest Authority, National Forest Plan (opens in a new tab), p. 7).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Forests and land use

Land held under customary tenure

  • 97% customary land. Although Papua New Guinea has a land area of some 461 690 km2, it has been generally difficult to access it, either under customary tenure (97 percent), or alienated land which is administered by the Government (three percent) (Vision 2050 (opens in a new tab), s 20.11, PDF p. 65). Vision 2050 presents this as a difficulty of access to land.
  • A constitutional goal for resources. The 1991 Forest Policy is guided and based on the fourth goal of the constitution which states: “For Papua New Guinean’s natural resources and the environment to be conserved and used for the collective benefit of us all, and to be replenished for the benefit of future generations”. (National Forest Plan (opens in a new tab), s 2.0, p. 2).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Low fossil emissions

  • 0.02% of the world total. Papua New Guinea emitted 8.35 Mt of fossil CO2 in 2024, 0.02% of the world total of 38,599 Mt (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Under 1 tonne per person. That is 0.79 t per person, against a world average of 4.73 t (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); World Bank population for 2024).

No nuclear weapons

  • None held. At the start of 2026 nine states held about 12,187 nuclear weapons; Papua New Guinea is not one of them (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Small military spending. US$106.5 million in 2025, 0.35% of GDP (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)).
