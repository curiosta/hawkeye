Targets and latest values

Renewable share of on-grid capacity

Target
78% of on-grid installed capacity by 2030: the NDC commits to increasing the share of installed capacity of renewable energy from 30 percent in 2015 to 78 percent in 2030 for on-grid connection managed by PNG Power Limited; This target is conditional (Enhanced NDC, 2020 (opens in a new tab), pp. 23 and 25). The development plan sets yearly targets for the same measure: 40% (2023), 50% (2024), 60% (2025), 70% (2026) and 78% (2027) (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), SPA 10 indicator table, PDF p. 199).
Baseline
30% (2015) (NDC (opens in a new tab)); 30% (2020), source National Energy Authority (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 199)
Latest
Not confirmed at source yet (TBC): no regulator figure for on-grid installed capacity has been published. Related measures: Renewable resources currently produce about half of PPL’s power generation. (ADB, December 2023 (opens in a new tab)); for the whole country, The total installed capacity is estimated to be 580 MW and consist of 230MW hydro, 217 diesel, 82MW gas-fired and 53MW geothermal. (CCDA, Energy Mitigation Plan 2022–2025 (opens in a new tab), p. VI).
Distance
Not computed. The latest on-grid installed-capacity share has not been published; generation shares and national totals are different measures.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Forest and land-use emissions

Target
By 2030, the annual emission from deforestation and forest degradation due to agriculture expansion and commercial logging will be reduced by 10,000 Gg CO2 eq comparing to 2015 level. This will result in the LULUCF sub-sector moving from a net GHG source (1, 176 Gg CO2 eq) in 2015 to net GHG sink (-8,284 Gg CO2 eq) by 2030 (Enhanced NDC, 2020 (opens in a new tab), p. 23)
Baseline
Net source of 1,176 Gg CO2e in 2015 as stated in the NDC. The recalculated inventory in the First Biennial Transparency Report gives 2015 as a net sink of −4,868.32 Gg CO2e (BTR1, January 2026 (opens in a new tab), printed p. 22).
Latest
The net emissions from the LULUCF sector amounted to -14,269.79 Gg CO2 eq in 2022 compared to - 32,564.71 Gg CO2 eq in 2000. (First Biennial Transparency Report (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 16)
Distance
Not computed. The NDC baseline and the recalculated inventory series are not on the same basis.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

A K200 billion economy and one million jobs by 2030

Target
to grow PNG’s GDP to K200 billion and create one million additional jobs by 2030. Milestone for 2027: growing the PNG economy to K164 billion and improve PNG per capita income to K12,000 (or US$3,000 equivalent) by 2027 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF pp. 28 and 22)
Baseline
grow the economy from K110 billion in 2022 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 48)
Latest
K133.0 billion in 2025; The economy is expected to exceed K145.0 billion in 2026. (2026 National Budget, Volume 1 (opens in a new tab)). Jobs: no count of additional jobs has been published (TBC). The Treasury cites an index instead: According to BPNG’s employment index, total formal private sector employment grew by 2.6 per cent through the year to June quarter of 2025. (same volume, PDF p. 38).
Distance
67.0 billion kina below the 2030 GDP target (2025 value, current prices) (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 200 − 133)
Assessment by the tracking agency
We are well on target for a K200.0 billion economy by 2030 (Treasury, 2026 National Budget (opens in a new tab))

Roads and transport (Connect PNG)

Target
70% connectivity (Connect PNG) (mtdp.gov.pg (opens in a new tab)); 2027 targets include 15,000km total National roads, 48% access to air transport and 60% access to maritime transport (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 45)
Baseline
PNG has approximately 30,000 kilometres (km) of total road network, of which 8,740km is the National Road Network (NRN), whilst approximately 21,260km are Provincial and District roads. (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 144)
Latest
Works to date, as listed by the Department of Works and Highways (undated page): Rehabilitated over 3,000 km of national highways and Upgraded over 1,200 km of district and rural roads (Connect PNG (opens in a new tab)). A dated measure of connectivity has not been confirmed (TBC).
Distance
Not computed. The works figures are cumulative and undated, and are not the connectivity or network measures named in the targets.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Maternal mortality (SDG 3.1)

Target
By 2030, reduce the global maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 per 100,000 live births (UN SDG target 3.1 (opens in a new tab)). The national plan aims for 121 by 2027 (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), Table 10.4).
Baseline
323 per 100,000 live births (2000) (World Bank WDI, UN MMEIG (opens in a new tab))
Latest
189 (2023) (UN MMEIG (opens in a new tab)). WHO’s country office cites 171 (WHO, 29 July 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. SDG 3.1 is a global target, not a national one.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Under-5 mortality (SDG 3.2)

Target
By 2030, end preventable deaths of newborns and children under 5 years of age, with all countries aiming to reduce neonatal mortality to at least as low as 12 per 1,000 live births and under-5 mortality to at least as low as 25 per 1,000 live births (UN SDG target 3.2 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
72.0 per 1,000 live births (2000) (World Bank WDI, UN IGME (opens in a new tab))
Latest
39.4 (2024) (UN IGME (opens in a new tab))
Distance
14.4 deaths per 1,000 live births above the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 25 − 39.4)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Access to electricity

Target
As part of the overall objective to provide 70% electricity to our households by 2030 and achieve 100% by 2050; for 2027, a target of 40% of households from the current 19% (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 154)
Baseline
12.4% of households (2010), 16.7% (2016), 27% (2021) (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), Table 10.3, printed p. 64)
Latest
43.3% of the population (2024), after 20.5% in 2023 (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab)). The reason for the 2024 jump has not been published with the data.
Distance
Not computed. The target counts households; the latest series counts people.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Marine protection (SDG 14.5)

Target
By 2020, conserve at least 10 per cent of coastal and marine areas, consistent with national and international law and based on the best available scientific information (UN SDG target 14.5 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
0.2% of territorial waters (2013–2015) (World Bank WDI, UNEP-WCMC (opens in a new tab))
Latest
0.1% of territorial waters (2025) (World Bank WDI, UNEP-WCMC (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. The series covers territorial waters; the target’s “coastal and marine areas” are not defined the same way.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Drinking water (SDG 6.1)

Target
By 2030, achieve universal and equitable access to safe and affordable drinking water for all (UN SDG target 6.1 (opens in a new tab)). National target for 2027: 70% access to clean safe drinking water (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 45).
Baseline
43.9% using at least basic drinking water services (2015) (World Bank WDI, WHO/UNICEF JMP (opens in a new tab))
Latest
53.3% (2024) (WHO/UNICEF JMP (opens in a new tab))
Distance
Not computed. “Basic” service is a lower standard than the “safe” water named in both targets.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Top 50 in the Human Development Index by 2050

Target
To be among the top 50 countries in the United Nations Human Development Index (HDI) (Vision 2050 (opens in a new tab), executive summary, p. xiii)
Baseline
HDI 0.456 (2000) (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
HDI 0.576, rank 160 of 193 (data year 2023) (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
110 places below 50th (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 160 − 50); the deadline is 2050.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 18–19 October 2026. Quotations are given as published.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
