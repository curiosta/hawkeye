Papua New Guinea PESTLE

The large outside forces acting on a place, in six groups: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental.

Political 3 points

Governance and cooperation

  • Twelve priority areas. The MTDP IV identifies 12 Strategic Priority Areas (SPA - Figure 4.1) as the pillars that reflect the National objectives, goals and aspirations of Vision 2050, and to which Government investment will be aligned. (MTDP IV 2023–2027 (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 46). The plan was launched on 7 July 2023 (mtdp.gov.pg (opens in a new tab)).

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • Military spending. US$106.5 million in 2025 (K441.9 million), 0.35% of GDP (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)).
  • An alliance with Australia. The Pukpuk Treaty entered into force in July 2026 (Prime Minister of Australia, 8 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · An alliance with Australia · SWOT · Strengths · No nuclear weapons

Economic 4 points

Economy and finance

  • A K200 billion goal. The plan’s overall objective is to grow PNG’s GDP to K200 billion and create one million additional jobs by 2030. (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), s 2.1, PDF p. 28).
  • Growth in kina. The economy is expected to exceed K145.0 billion in 2026. This is K12.0 billion higher than the K133.0 billion economy in 2025 (2026 National Budget, Volume 1 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Living standards lost ground. From 2012 to 2018, real non-resource GDP per capita fell by 11%. Even with our record economic growth rates, it will take until 2029 before we are back to 2012 levels. (2025 National Budget, Volume 1 (opens in a new tab), Treasurer’s overview, PDF p. 5).
  • The central bank’s mandate. Section 7(a) of the Central Banking Act 2000 is to formulate and implement monetary policy with a view to achieving and maintaining price stability and promoting employment and economic growth, especially of the non-mineral and non-petroleum sector (Central Banking Act 2000, consolidated to 2021 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · High risk of debt distress

Social 3 points

Pandemics and health

  • Maternal mortality. 189 per 100,000 live births in 2023 (UN estimate) (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab)); WHO’s country office cites 171 (WHO, 29 July 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A young population. Median age 22.8 years and life expectancy 66.4 years in 2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Maternal deaths

Human development

  • Human development. HDI 0.576, rank 160 of 193 (data year 2023) (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Low human development

Technological 2 points

Energy transition

  • A renewable capacity target. The climate pledge commits to increasing the share of installed capacity of renewable energy from 30 percent in 2015 to 78 percent in 2030, for the on-grid system managed by PNG Power Limited; This target is conditional (Enhanced NDC, 2020 (opens in a new tab), pp. 23 and 25).
  • Access to electricity. 20.5% of the population in 2023 and 43.3% in 2024; no reason for the jump has been published with the data (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Few people with electricity

Legal 2 points

Biodiversity and ecosystems

  • Environment Act 2000. Its long title includes: (b) to regulate the environmental impacts of development activities in order to promote sustainable development of the environment and the economic, social and physical well-being of people by safeguarding the life-supporting capacity of air, water, soil and ecosystems for present and future generations and avoiding, remedying and mitigating any adverse effects of activities on the environment; (Environment Act 2000, as enacted (opens in a new tab)).

Climate change / global warming

  • Climate Change (Management) Act 2015. Being an Act to provide for a regulatory framework to— (a) promote and manage climate compatible development through climate change mitigation and adaptation activities; and (b) implement any relevant obligations of the State under applicable rules of international law and international agreements related to climate change including the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Paris Agreement; and (Climate Change (Management) Act 2015, consolidated to 7 February 2022 (opens in a new tab)). It was amended by Act 25 of 2021.

Environmental 3 points

Natural resources

  • Forest policy. The 1991 National Forest Policy is based on the Constitution’s fourth National Goal; its main objective (i) is Management and protection of the nation’s forest resource as a renewable natural assets. (PNG Forest Authority, National Forest Plan (opens in a new tab), s 2.0).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Land held under customary tenure · SWOT · Strengths · Large forests

Natural and cosmic hazards

  • A 1984 disaster law. Being an Act to make provision with respect to emergencies arising out of epidemics, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions and other disasters, and for related purposes. Section 3(1): The National Disaster Committee is hereby established. (Disaster Management Act 1984 (opens in a new tab)). No enactment of the bill for a National Emergency Management Authority, which would replace it, had been found as of 18 October 2026.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Landslides

Oceans

  • Fisheries law. The Fisheries Management Act 1998 is Being an Act to provide for and give effect to the National Goals and Directive Principles and in particular to promote the management and sustainable development of fisheries, and for related purposes, and section 25(b) reads: conserve the living resources for both present and future generations; (Fisheries Management Act 1998, as enacted (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · A share of the world’s largest tuna fishery

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a country, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups: politics and government, the economy, society and population, technology, laws, and the environment. Official national sources come first.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact. Spanish quotations are given as published, with unofficial English translations.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
