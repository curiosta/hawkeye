A Aspirations

Papua New Guinea’s long-term vision is Vision 2050, the Government’s long-term strategy first published in 2009. The Medium Term Development Plan IV sets the 2023–2027 steps towards it. Each is labelled with its status.

Vision 2050

Government long-term strategy, first published November 2009; whether Parliament adopted it has not been confirmed

The vision (Vision 2050 (opens in a new tab), s 4, printed p. 31): ‘We will be a Smart, Wise, Fair, Healthy and Happy Society by 2050.’ The mission (s 5.1, printed p. 32): ‘We will be ranked in the top 50 countries in the United Nations Human Development Index by 2050, creating opportunities for personal and national advancement through economic growth, smart innovative ideas, quality service and ensuring a fair and equitable distribution of benefits in a safe and secure environment for all citizens’; Vision 2050 is underpinned by seven Strategic Focus Areas, which are referred to as pillars:

  1. Human Capital Development, Gender, Youth and People Empowerment
  2. Wealth Creation
  3. Institutional Development and Service Delivery
  4. Security and International Relations
  5. Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change
  6. Spiritual, Cultural and Community Development
  7. Strategic Planning, Integration and Control

Pillar names as printed in the executive summary, p. xiii, where they are listed without numbers.

Medium Term Development Plan IV, 2023–2027

Government development plan, launched 7 July 2023; whether Parliament adopted it has not been confirmed

The Medium-Term Development Plan IV 2023-2027 (MTDP IV) is themed as “National Prosperity through Growing the Economy”. In consideration of the above, the Government has stated the following key objectives: i) To achieve a K200 billion PNG economy by 2030; ii) To create one million additional jobs; and iii) To improve the quality of life for all PNG citizens. (MTDP IV (opens in a new tab), PDF pp. 18 and 22). The MTDP IV identifies 12 Strategic Priority Areas (SPA - Figure 4.1) as the pillars that reflect the National objectives, goals and aspirations of Vision 2050, and to which Government investment will be aligned. (PDF p. 46). The twelve areas, as named in the plan’s contents:

  1. Strategic Economic Investment
  2. Connect PNG Infrastructure
  3. Quality and Affordable Health Care
  4. Quality Education and Skilled Human Capital
  5. Rule of Law and Justice
  6. National Security
  7. National Revenue and Public Finance Management
  8. Digital Government, National Statistics, and Public Service Governance
  9. Research, Science and Technology
  10. Climate Change and Environmental Protection
  11. Population, Youth and Women Empowerment
  12. Strategic Partnerships

How this SOAR analysis works

For a country, the Aspirations are its official goal statements, quoted word for word. Where a country has no single national vision document, the page names the official documents it quotes instead.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
