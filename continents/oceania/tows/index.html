Documented regional responses 8

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent

    Adopted by Pacific Islands Forum Leaders, 2022

    A long-term plan with seven thematic areas, a vision and ten Leaders’ commitments (2050 Strategy (opens in a new tab)). Vision quoted on the SWOT page.

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A shared long-term plan and security framework

  • Treaty of Rarotonga (South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone)

    Adopted by Concluded 6 August 1985; in force 11 December 1986

    Parties renounce nuclear explosive devices (UNTS Vol. 1445 (opens in a new tab), Art. 3). Text quoted on the SWOT page.

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A nuclear-free zone

  • Boe Declaration on Regional Security

    Adopted by Pacific Islands Forum Leaders, Nauru, September 2018

    Affirms an expanded concept of security and names climate change the single greatest threat to the region (Boe Declaration (opens in a new tab), paras 1(i) and 1(vii)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · A warming region · SWOT · Strengths · A shared long-term plan and security framework

  • Declaration on Preserving Maritime Zones

    Adopted by All 18 Pacific Islands Forum members, 6 August 2021

    Maritime zones notified under the Law of the Sea Convention continue to apply without reduction despite sea-level rise (Declaration (opens in a new tab)). Text quoted on the SWOT page.

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A common position on maritime zones · SWOT · Opportunities · A world court opinion on fixed maritime boundaries

  • Pacific Resilience Facility

    Adopted by Agreement signed in Honiara, 10 September 2025; in force 6 May 2026

    A Pacific-owned international organisation, based in Nuku’alofa, that makes grants for climate adaptation, disaster preparedness and rapid response (Agreement (opens in a new tab), Arts. 1 and 3; PIFS, 6 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A Pacific-owned resilience fund · SWOT · Opportunities · Partner money for the Pacific Resilience Facility

  • Noumea Convention

    Adopted by Adopted in Noumea, November 1986

    Parties undertake to seek further agreements to protect, develop and manage the marine and coastal environment of the Convention Area (Noumea Convention (opens in a new tab), Art. 4(1)). Text quoted on the PESTLE page.

  • South Pacific Forum Fisheries Agency Convention

    Adopted by Concluded in Honiara, 10 July 1979; in force 9 August 1979

    Set up the regional fisheries agency on the basis of its parties’ common interest in conserving and making optimum use of the region’s living marine resources (UNTS Vol. 1579 (opens in a new tab), preamble).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Island fleets take a growing share of the tuna catch · SWOT · Threats · Illegal and misreported tuna fishing

  • Nauru Agreement

    Adopted by Done at Nauru, 11 February 1982

    The Parties shall seek to establish a co-ordinated approach to the fishing of the common stocks in the Fisheries Zones by foreign fishing vessels (Nauru Agreement (opens in a new tab), Art. II).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Island fleets take a growing share of the tuna catch

Sources checked 19 October 2026. Figures for the Pacific islands use the UN group Oceania excluding Australia and New Zealand. Sources that cover a wider region are labelled.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a continent, the responses are documented instruments of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, each named with its members and the date it was adopted.

Every response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Placing a response in a cell is Hawkeye’s only editorial step. Responses are not ranked.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
