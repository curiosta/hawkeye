Oceania PESTLE

The large outside forces acting on a place, in six groups: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental.

Political 3 points

Governance and cooperation

  • A 2050 Strategy. Pacific Islands Forum Leaders endorsed the 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent in 2022; its vision is quoted on the SWOT page (2050 Strategy (opens in a new tab), p. 6)
  • Climate as the main security threat. The 2018 Boe Declaration calls climate change the single greatest threat to the livelihoods, security and wellbeing of the peoples of the Pacific (Boe Declaration (opens in a new tab), para 1(i))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A shared long-term plan and security framework

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • A nuclear-free zone. Parties to the 1985 Treaty of Rarotonga undertake not to acquire, possess or control any nuclear explosive device (Treaty of Rarotonga (opens in a new tab), Art. 3). No country in Oceania holds warheads (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A nuclear-free zone

Economic 3 points

Economy and finance

  • Growth forecasts cut. Economies in the Pacific face the largest downward revisions, with projections for both years cut by 0.3 percentage points, to 3% and 2.9%, on prolonged energy market disruptions and the expected effects of El Niño on mining and agriculture. (ADB, 23 September 2026 (opens in a new tab); forecasts for 2026 and 2027, ADB’s Pacific developing members)
  • Climate finance. The Blue Pacific 2050 Dashboard states that the region receives about US$220 million a year for climate resilience and adaptation, less than 0.22% of global climate finance (undated; dashboard (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Little climate finance reaching the region · SWOT · Opportunities · Partner money for the Pacific Resilience Facility

Water and food security

  • Tuna. Export value US$1.2 billion and access and licence fees US$502 million in 2024 (FFA and SPC, Tuna Fishery Report Card 2025 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · Tuna export markets · SWOT · Strengths · Island fleets take a growing share of the tuna catch

Social 3 points

Pandemics and health

  • Chronic disease. Premature deaths from non-communicable diseases: 30.9% in the Pacific islands against 8.6% in Australia and New Zealand, 2021 (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab))
  • Child survival. Under-five mortality 36.2 per 1,000 in the Pacific islands against 3.8 in Australia and New Zealand, 2024 (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · A large health gap with Australia and New Zealand

Human development

  • Reading. 53% of year six students met the minimum expected reading level in PILNA 2021; 47% did not (SPC, PILNA 2021 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Reading at the end of primary school

Technological 2 points

Technology and AI

  • Internet use. 25.6% of people in the Pacific islands used the internet in 2024 (12.2% in 2015), against 95.7% in Australia and New Zealand (UN SDG Global Database, series IT_USE_ii99 (opens in a new tab), ITU estimates)

Energy transition

  • Renewable capacity. 93.6 watts per person in the Pacific islands in 2024, against 1,526 for Oceania as a whole (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Little renewable generating capacity per person

Legal 3 points

Oceans

  • Maritime zones. The 2021 Forum declaration proclaims that members’ maritime zones shall continue to apply, without reduction, notwithstanding any physical changes connected to climate change-related sea-level rise. (Declaration on Preserving Maritime Zones (opens in a new tab))
  • Regional environment treaty. The Parties shall endeavour to conclude bilateral or multilateral agreements, including regional or sub-regional agreements, for the protection, development and management of the marine and coastal environment of the Convention Area. (Noumea Convention (opens in a new tab), Art. 4(1), adopted 1986)

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A common position on maritime zones · SWOT · Opportunities · A world court opinion on fixed maritime boundaries

Natural resources

  • A regional fisheries agency. The 1979 South Pacific Forum Fisheries Agency Convention records its parties’ common interest in the conservation and optimum use of the region’s living marine resources, in particular highly migratory species (FFA Convention (opens in a new tab), preamble; concluded in Honiara on 10 July 1979)

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Illegal and misreported tuna fishing · SWOT · Strengths · Island fleets take a growing share of the tuna catch

Environmental 3 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Heat and sea level. The South-West Pacific had its second-warmest year on record in 2025, and sea level rose by 3.7 mm a year over 1999–2025 (WMO, 7 July 2026 (opens in a new tab); WMO’s region includes maritime South-East Asia)

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · A warming region · SWOT · Threats · Rising seas and marine heatwaves

Water and food security

  • Drinking water. 62.6% of people in the Pacific islands had at least a basic drinking water service in 2024 (UN SDG Global Database (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Drinking water

Economy and finance

  • A resilience fund. The Pacific Resilience Facility entered into force on 6 May 2026; pledges stood at US$190 million against an initial target of US$500 million on 9 October 2026 (PIFS, 6 May 2026 (opens in a new tab); PIFS, 9 October 2026 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A Pacific-owned resilience fund · SWOT · Opportunities · Partner money for the Pacific Resilience Facility

Sources checked 19 October 2026. Figures for the Pacific islands use the UN group Oceania excluding Australia and New Zealand. Sources that cover a wider region are labelled.

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a continent, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups. Where a continent has no permanent population, government or national economy, as with Antarctica, the groups follow what its governing treaties and official bodies report on.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
