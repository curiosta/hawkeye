T

Threats

External · harmful

An Alpine Fault earthquake

  • A 75% chance in the next 50 years. A 2021 study, reported by GNS Science, puts the chance of a major quake on the fault at 75 percent in the next 50 years, compared to the previously estimated figure of 29 percent (GNS Science (opens in a new tab)).
  • Likely to be very large. The same study found an 82 percent chance that the next rupture could be similar to the last one, in 1717, which was about magnitude 8 (GNS Science (opens in a new tab); Howarth and others, Nature Geoscience, 2021).
  • A national response law. The Emergency Management Act 2026, in force since 4 September 2026, replaced the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002 (Emergency Management Act 2026 (opens in a new tab)). It is listed on the TOWS page.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Marine heatwaves and extreme weather

  • Coastal waters in heatwave most of 2022. In 2022 the coastal waters of the Western North Island spent an average of 88.5% of the year in marine heatwaves, the longest of the nine coastal regions; the Tasman Sea spent 61.1%, the longest of four oceanic regions (Stats NZ, Sea-surface temperature: Data to 2023 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Over $1 billion for one rebuild. Budget 2024 set aside More than $1 billion for the rebuild and recovery of communities affected by Cyclone Gabrielle and the 2023 Auckland Anniversary floods, including $939.3 million to repair roads. (Treasury, Budget 2024 at a Glance (opens in a new tab)). This is a budget allocation, not the total cost of the two events.

A harder strategic environment

  • Defence says conditions are worsening. The Ministry of Defence’s Defence Assessment 2021 identified that New Zealand’s strategic environment is deteriorating. The current Defence Force is designed for a relatively benign strategic environment, and not the challenges of increased strategic competition and the adverse effects of climate change that Defence Assessment 2021 identified. (Ministry of Defence, Ministerial Briefings for the 2025 Defence Capability Plan (opens in a new tab), Executive Summary).
  • The Indo-Pacific. The same briefings state: The Indo-Pacific is the central theatre of strategic competition with concerning flashpoints. (Ministry of Defence, July 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Small spending, no warheads. New Zealand spent US$2,855.7 million on its military in 2025, 1.1% of GDP and 0.10% of world military spending (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)). It holds no nuclear weapons; its nuclear-free law is on the PESTLE page.

O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Free trade with the European Union

  • In force since May 2024. The New Zealand – European Union Free Trade Agreement that entered into force on 1 May 2024 creates opportunities for exporters, MFAT wrote in a June 2024 market report (MFAT, Why Poland? (opens in a new tab)).
  • A large market. The same report notes that Poland provides access to 446 million European consumers. (MFAT, June 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

A growing space industry

  • Fast growth. MBIE reports that New Zealand’s space sector increased revenue by 53% in the 5 years to 2024 (MBIE (opens in a new tab)).
  • Its size. The space sector contributed $2.47 billion to the economy (financial year 2023/24) (MBIE (opens in a new tab)). This is the sector’s total economic contribution, not its revenue.
  • A law built for it. The Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act 2017, in force since 21 December 2017, states: The purpose of this Act is to— (a) facilitate the development of a space industry and provide for its safe and secure operation: (Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act 2017 (opens in a new tab), s 3). Section 3(e) was amended on 29 July 2025 (2025 No 38) to cover ground-based space infrastructure.

Also in PESTLE · Technological

Aid focused on the environment and the Pacific

  • Most aid supports the environment. In 2023-2024, New Zealand committed 57.8% of its total bilateral allocable ODA (USD 286.9 million) in support of the environment and the Rio Conventions, up from 39.9% in 2021-2022. The DAC average was 39%. (OECD Development Co-operation Profiles: New Zealand, June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Most aid goes to the Pacific. In 2024, 66.2% of New Zealand’s gross bilateral aid went to Oceania and to small island developing states; New Zealand has the highest share of bilateral aid going to small island states among the OECD’s Development Assistance Committee members (OECD, June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Total aid fell in 2025. Aid was 0.27% of gross national income in 2025 (preliminary), against the 0.7% international commitment; see the GAP page.

W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Children in material hardship

  • One in seven children. 14.3% of children lived in material hardship in the year ended June 2025, with no statistically significant change from the year before (measure (c)) (Stats NZ, 26 February 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Above the 2017/18 baseline. The baseline in 2017/18 was 13.3%; the government’s ten-year target is 6% by 2027/28 (MSD (opens in a new tab)). The full row is on the GAP page.
  • Subject to correction. On 29 September 2026 Stats NZ added a notice: Errors have been identified in the Household Income and Living Survey (HILS) data for the year ended June 2025, and The corrections for child poverty measures will be released in February 2027 (Stats NZ (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Child material hardship

Phosphorus in rivers

  • Most river length is affected. 36 percent of New Zealand’s river length had dissolved reactive phosphorus concentrations within band A, similar to natural conditions. The rest (64 percent) had elevated concentrations, making them more prone to eutrophication, which harms ecological communities. (Stats NZ, River water quality: phosphorus (opens in a new tab)).
  • One-fifth substantially elevated. 22 percent of New Zealand’s river length was substantially elevated above natural conditions (within NOF band D) (Stats NZ (opens in a new tab)). These are modelled figures for 2016–2020, published in April 2022.

Threatened seabirds and shorebirds

  • Nine in ten seabirds. 90 percent of seabird species (86 of 96) and 82 percent of shorebird species (14 of 17) are threatened with extinction or at risk of becoming threatened. (Stats NZ, Extinction threat to indigenous marine species (opens in a new tab)).
  • An older assessment. These birds are a subset of the bird species which were assessed in 2016; Stats NZ published the indicator in December 2021 (Stats NZ (opens in a new tab)). A newer bird assessment (2021) has been published by the Department of Conservation; its seabird figures have not yet been checked by Hawkeye.

Greenhouse gas emissions

  • Net emissions in 2024. Net emissions in 2024 were 54.8 Mt CO2e, 2 percent (1.1 Mt CO2e) lower than in 2023. Gross emissions were 75.8 Mt CO2e (Ministry for the Environment, Inventory 1990–2024 Snapshot (opens in a new tab)).
  • Below the 2006 peak. gross and net emissions increased steadily from 1990, reaching a peak in 2006 at 85.6 Mt CO2e and 64.6 Mt CO2e respectively (Ministry for the Environment (opens in a new tab)).
  • Farming is the largest source. Agriculture produced 53% and energy 38% of gross emissions in 2024; agriculture emissions were 9% higher and energy emissions 20% higher than in 1990 (Ministry for the Environment (opens in a new tab)).
  • The climate commission’s view. Current plans and policies are insufficient to meet the third budget or the 2050 target. (Climate Change Commission, 2026 monitoring report (opens in a new tab)). The targets are on the GAP page.

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Mostly renewable electricity

  • 85.5% renewable in 2024. Overall, 85.5 per cent of electricity in 2024 came from renewable sources, down from 88.1 per cent in 2023 (MBIE, Energy in New Zealand 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 12).
  • Records outside hydro. While hydro generation fell in 2024, generation from other renewable sources reached new records: geothermal generation reached 8,741 GWh, wind 3,919 GWh and solar 601 GWh (MBIE, Energy in New Zealand 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · Renewable energy

High human development

  • 17th in the world. New Zealand’s Human Development Index is 0.938, ranking 17 of 193 countries (data year 2023); in Oceania only Australia (0.958) is higher (UNDP Human Development Report 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Long lives. Life expectancy at birth is 82.4 years in 2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)); the HDR 2025 gives 82.1 years for 2023 (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social

A third of the land managed for conservation

  • A third of the country. The Department of Conservation Te Papa Atawhai (DOC) manages a third of New Zealand’s land area and is the largest provider of visitor activities. (DOC Annual Report 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 16).
  • 8.6 million hectares. We are responsible for 8.6 million hectares of land (nearly a third of New Zealand), 4.5 million hectares of water (DOC Annual Report 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 50).
  • Set down in law. Under the Conservation Act 1987 the Department’s functions include (a) to manage for conservation purposes, all land, and all other natural and historic resources, for the time being held under this Act (Conservation Act 1987 (opens in a new tab), s 6).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Fewer people smoking

  • Down to 6.8%. In 2024/25, the prevalence of daily smoking was 6.8%. This is similar to the previous year (6.9%), but down from 16.4% in 2011/12. (Ministry of Health, New Zealand Health Survey 2024/25 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Nearly halved in number. The estimated number of daily smokers nearly halved between 2011/12 and 2024/25, decreasing from 572,000 to 294,000. (Ministry of Health (opens in a new tab)).
  • Still above the goal for some groups. Daily smoking was 15.0% among Māori and 10.3% among Pacific peoples in 2024/25, against the Smokefree 2025 goal of under 5% for all population groups (Ministry of Health (opens in a new tab)); see the GAP page.

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Smokefree 2025

Marine reserves

  • 44 reserves (2015 data). We have 44 marine reserves. They cover 17,430km² or about 10 percent of the 14 biogeographic regions (defined by an area’s natural characteristics, including flora, fauna, and environmental conditions) that make up our territorial sea (out to the 12 nautical mile limit). (Stats NZ, Protection in the marine environment, October 2016 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Still 44 in December 2025. There are 44 marine reserves around the country, from the Kermadecs in the far north to the Subantarctic Islands in the deep south (DOC, 1 December 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Five more in 2026. The Government has completed the final steps to establish five new marine reserves as part of the South-East Marine Protection network, protecting 308 square kilometres of unique marine habitat and sea life. (DOC, 2 June 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Five marine reserves were gazetted on 28 May 2026 and came into effect on 1 July 2026. (DOC (opens in a new tab)). A current national total has not yet been confirmed at source.
