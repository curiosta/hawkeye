New Zealand PESTLE

The large outside forces acting on a place, in six groups: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental.

Political 3 points

Governance and cooperation

  • A nuclear-free law. The New Zealand Nuclear Free Zone, Disarmament, and Arms Control Act 1987 (assented 8 June 1987, administered by MFAT) prohibits, in section 5(1)(b), any act to aid, abet, or procure any person to manufacture, acquire, possess, or have control over any nuclear explosive device. (Nuclear Free Zone Act 1987 (opens in a new tab), s 5).
  • A general election on 7 November 2026. Official results are due on 27 November 2026 (New Zealand Parliament, Election 2026 Timeline (opens in a new tab)).

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • Military spending. US$2,855.7 million in 2025, 1.1% of GDP and 0.10% of the world total of US$2,886.6 billion (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)).

Economic 3 points

Economy and finance

  • Inflation above the target band. New Zealand’s consumers price index (CPI) increased 4.1 percent in the 12 months to the June 2026 quarter. The 4.1 percent increase follows a 3.1 percent increase in the 12 months to the March 2026 quarter. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand’s target band for the annual inflation rate is 1 to 3 percent. (Stats NZ, 21 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Modest growth. New Zealand’s gross domestic product (GDP) rose 0.2 percent in the June 2026 quarter, following a 0.9 percent increase in the March 2026 quarter; GDP per person rose 0.1% in the June 2026 quarter (Stats NZ, 17 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • The central bank’s single objective. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, s 9(1)(a), sets the economic objective of achieving and maintaining stability in the general level of prices over the medium term (Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 (opens in a new tab)). It was set by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Economic Objective) Amendment Act 2023 (2023 No 64), in force 20 December 2023.

Social 3 points

Human development

  • Child material hardship. 14.3% in the year ended June 2025, unchanged in statistical terms; Stats NZ says corrections to the underlying survey data for that year will be released in February 2027 (Stats NZ (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Children in material hardship

Pandemics and health

  • Daily smoking. 6.8% of adults in 2024/25, down from 16.4% in 2011/12; 15.0% among Māori, 10.3% among Pacific peoples, 5.7% among European/Other and 4.5% among Asian adults (Ministry of Health, NZ Health Survey 2024/25 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Life expectancy. 82.4 years at birth in 2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Technological 2 points

Energy transition

  • Renewable electricity. 85.5% of electricity was generated from renewable sources, down from 88.1% in 2023, with record wind (3,919 GWh) and solar (601 GWh) generation (MBIE, Energy in New Zealand 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Mostly renewable electricity

Technology and AI

  • A space law. The purpose of this Act is to— (a) facilitate the development of a space industry and provide for its safe and secure operation: (Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act 2017 (opens in a new tab), s 3). Administered by MBIE; in force 21 December 2017.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · A growing space industry

Legal 2 points

Governance and cooperation

  • Treaty claims. Section 6(1) of the Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975 sets out the claims that can be made to the Waitangi Tribunal. They can concern any Act (whether or not still in force), passed at any time on or after 6 February 1840, and the section ends: was or is inconsistent with the principles of the Treaty, he or she may submit that claim to the Tribunal under this section. (Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975 (opens in a new tab), s 6(1)). Administered by Te Puni Kōkiri.

Economy and finance

  • A new national interest test for foreign investment. Section 3 of the Overseas Investment Act 2005 was replaced on 6 March 2026 by the Overseas Investment (National Interest Test and Other Matters) Amendment Act 2025 (2025 No 81). Its purposes now include managing the risks to New Zealand’s national interest that are associated with overseas investment transactions, including national security and public order risks. and recognising the role of overseas investment in increasing economic opportunity by enabling the timely consent of less sensitive investments through an initial national interest risk assessment. (Overseas Investment Act 2005 (opens in a new tab), s 3(1)(c) and 3(2)).

Environmental 3 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Emissions. Net emissions in 2024 were 54.8 Mt CO2e, 2 percent (1.1 Mt CO2e) lower than in 2023. Gross emissions were 75.8 Mt CO2e, 53% from agriculture and 38% from energy (Ministry for the Environment (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Greenhouse gas emissions

Biodiversity and ecosystems

  • Conservation law. The functions of the Department are to administer this Act and the enactments specified in Schedule 1, and, subject to this Act and those enactments and to the directions (if any) of the Minister,— including (e) to the extent that the use of any natural or historic resource for recreation or tourism is not inconsistent with its conservation, to foster the use of natural and historic resources for recreation, and to allow their use for tourism: (Conservation Act 1987 (opens in a new tab), s 6).

Natural and cosmic hazards

  • A new emergency law. The Emergency Management Act 2026 (2026 No 53) replaced the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002 on 4 September 2026. The purpose of this Act is to promote and improve the management of hazards and to support the social, economic, cultural, and environmental well-being and safety of the public by providing for the following matters: (Emergency Management Act 2026 (opens in a new tab), s 3).

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a country, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups: politics and government, the economy, society and population, technology, laws, and the environment. Official national sources come first.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact. Spanish quotations are given as published, with unofficial English translations.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
