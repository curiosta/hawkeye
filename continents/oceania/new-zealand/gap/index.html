Targets and latest values

Net zero by 2050 (excluding biogenic methane)

Target
The target for emissions reduction (the 2050 target) requires that— net accounting emissions of greenhouse gases in a calendar year, other than biogenic methane, are zero by the calendar year beginning on 1 January 2050 and for each subsequent calendar year; and (Climate Change Response Act 2002 (opens in a new tab), s 5Q(1)(a))
Baseline
Not sourced yet
Latest
After removals from forestry are included, Aotearoa New Zealand’s projected net emissions (under target accounting rules) declined by 2.0% between 2023 and 2024. (Climate Change Commission, 2026 monitoring report (opens in a new tab)). The absolute value of this target measure has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC). The UN inventory figure, 54.8 Mt CO2e net in 2024, is a different measure (Ministry for the Environment (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. The absolute value of net accounting emissions excluding biogenic methane has not been confirmed at source.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Current plans and policies are insufficient to meet the third budget or the 2050 target. and Current policies are unlikely to be enough to meet the 2050 target unless further action is taken. (Climate Change Commission, 22 July 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Biogenic methane

Target
emissions of biogenic methane in a calendar year— are 10% less than 2017 emissions by the calendar year beginning on 1 January 2030; and are 14% to 24% less than 2017 emissions by the calendar year beginning on 1 January 2050 and for each subsequent calendar year. (Climate Change Response Act 2002 (opens in a new tab), s 5Q(1)(b); the 2050 range was amended on 17 December 2025 by 2025 No 80)
Baseline
2017 emissions (the base year). The value has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Latest
Not confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Distance
Not computed. No baseline or latest biogenic methane value has been confirmed at source.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Projections show the 2030 biogenic methane target will not be met. Government estimates show a 7.9% reduction in biogenic methane by 2030, compared with the 10% target. (Climate Change Commission, 22 July 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Renewable energy

Target
The Government is setting a target of 50 per cent of total final energy consumption (TFEC) coming from renewable sources by 2035. (first emissions reduction plan, May 2022 (opens in a new tab), Action 11.5.1). Whether this target is still in force in October 2026 has not been confirmed (TBC). The earlier goal of 100% renewable electricity by 2030 has been dropped: the 2025 energy package lists Removing the previous Government’s target of 100% renewable electricity by 2030 and cancelling the Lake Onslow pumped hydro scheme, which was deterring investment. (At a Glance: New Zealand’s Energy Package (opens in a new tab), p. 15)
Baseline
In 2020, Aotearoa New Zealand’s renewable energy share of TFEC was 28 per cent. (first emissions reduction plan (opens in a new tab))
Latest
Renewable share of total final energy consumption: not confirmed at source yet (TBC). For electricity only: 85.5% in 2024, down from 88.1% in 2023 (MBIE, Energy in New Zealand 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. The latest share of total final energy consumption has not been confirmed; the electricity share is a different measure.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Child material hardship

Target
6% of children in material hardship by 2027/28 (ten-year target); intermediate target 11% for 2026/27 (MSD (opens in a new tab)). Set under the Child Poverty Reduction Act 2018.
Baseline
13.3% (2017/18) (MSD (opens in a new tab))
Latest
14.3% (2024/25, measure (c)), with no statistically significant change on the year before (Stats NZ, 26 February 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Subject to correction: Stats NZ will release corrected child poverty figures in February 2027.
Distance
8.3 percentage points above the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 6 − 14.3)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Children in low-income households (before housing costs)

Target
5% of children in households below 50% of median income before housing costs (BHC50) by 2027/28 (ten-year target); intermediate target 12% for 2026/27 (MSD (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
16.5% (2017/18) (MSD (opens in a new tab))
Latest
12.6% (2024/25, measure (a)) (MSD (opens in a new tab); Stats NZ (opens in a new tab)). Subject to the same Stats NZ correction in February 2027.
Distance
7.6 percentage points above the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 5 − 12.6)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Smokefree 2025

Target
New Zealand’s goal is to be smokefree by 2025, with smokefree meaning that daily smoking prevalence is less than 5 percent for all population groups. (Ministry of Health (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
16.4% daily smoking (2011/12) (Ministry of Health (opens in a new tab))
Latest
6.8% (2024/25); Māori 15.0%, Pacific peoples 10.3% (Ministry of Health (opens in a new tab)). Data for the 2024/25 survey were collected from July 2024 to July 2025, so they do not cover all of 2025.
Distance
1.8 percentage points (all adults) above the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 5 − 6.8)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Development aid (SDG 17.2)

Target
0.7% of gross national income as official development assistance: SDG target 17.2 refers to the commitment by many developed countries to achieve the target of 0.7 per cent of gross national income for official development assistance (ODA/GNI) to developing countries (UN SDG framework (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Not sourced yet
Latest
New Zealand’s total official development assistance (ODA) (USD 681.1 million, preliminary data) decreased in 2025, representing 0.27% of gross national income (GNI). It was 0.31% in 2023 and 0.32% in 2024 (OECD, June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
0.4 percentage points of GNI below the 0.7% commitment (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 0.7 − 0.27)
Assessment by the tracking agency
New Zealand’s ODA is not in line with international commitments to achieve a 0.7% ODA/GNI ratio. (OECD (opens in a new tab))

First Paris Agreement target (NDC 1), 2021–2030

Target
The Nationally Determined Contribution of New Zealand is: To reduce net greenhouse gas emissions to 50 per cent below gross 2005 levels by 2030. The NDC put the budget provisionally at 571 Mt CO2e over 2021–2030 (NDC, updated November 2021 (opens in a new tab)); the Ministry for the Environment now gives a provisional emissions budget of 579 Mt CO 2 -e over the period 2021 to 2030 (Ministry for the Environment (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
Gross emissions in 2005: provisional estimate 86.62 Mt CO2e (second NDC, January 2025 (opens in a new tab), Table 1, based on the 1990–2022 inventory)
Latest
Not confirmed at source yet (TBC): the latest official projection of 2021–2030 emissions against the budget was read only in a search-engine copy of the Ministry’s page.
Distance
Not computed. The target is a ten-year budget; a confirmed total for 2021–2030 does not yet exist.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published. The Ministry states: New Zealand plans to use offshore mitigation with environmental integrity towards meeting our NDC (Ministry for the Environment (opens in a new tab)).

Predator Free 2025 goals

Target
Goal 1 of the first Predator Free strategy: By 2025, we will increase by 1 million hectares (from 2016 figures) the area of New Zealand mainland where predators are suppressed, through Predator Free 2050 projects. (DOC, Review of progress against the Predator Free 2025 goals (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
2016 figures (the goal’s own base)
Latest
Goal 1 was achieved in 2021; As at June 2025, this figure had increased to over 2.5 million hectares (DOC, March 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Goal 1 met (DOC). Of the seven 2025 goals, goals 2–6 are assessed as Achievable, but not by 2025.
Assessment by the tracking agency
The first Predator Free strategy, Towards a Predator Free New Zealand, set seven goals to achieve by 2025. These goals were deliberately ambitious, designed to significantly change how we manage predators in Aotearoa New Zealand. Two of the seven goals have been achieved. (DOC, March 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Marine protection (SDG 14.5)

Target
By 2020, conserve at least 10 per cent of coastal and marine areas, consistent with national and international law and based on the best available scientific information (UN SDG target 14.5 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
47.06% of marine Key Biodiversity Areas covered by protected areas (2015) (UN SDG database, series ER_MRN_MPA (opens in a new tab))
Latest
47.19% (2025) (UN SDG database (opens in a new tab)). This indicator measures the average share of each marine Key Biodiversity Area that is protected, not the share of all sea area. Stats NZ last reported 44 marine reserves covering 17,430 km² of the territorial sea (2015 data) (Stats NZ (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. The official indicator measures Key Biodiversity Area coverage, not the share of coastal and marine area named in the target.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 18–19 October 2026. Quotations are given as published.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
