Documented national responses 9

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • The 2050 climate target

    Adopted by Parliament, Climate Change Response Act 2002, s 5Q (inserted 2019; s 5Q(1)(b)(ii) amended 17 December 2025, 2025 No 80)

    Net accounting emissions other than biogenic methane at zero from 2050; biogenic methane 10% below 2017 by 2030 and 14% to 24% below by 2050 (Climate Change Response Act 2002 (opens in a new tab), s 5Q). Quotes on the GAP page.

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Greenhouse gas emissions · GAP · Net zero by 2050 (excluding biogenic methane) · GAP · Biogenic methane

  • Nuclear-free zone

    Adopted by Parliament, New Zealand Nuclear Free Zone, Disarmament, and Arms Control Act 1987 (1987 No 86)

    Section 5(1)(b) prohibits any act to aid, abet, or procure any person to manufacture, acquire, possess, or have control over any nuclear explosive device. (Nuclear Free Zone Act 1987 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Price stability mandate

    Adopted by Parliament, Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, as amended by the (Economic Objective) Amendment Act 2023 (2023 No 64)

    Section 9(1)(a): the economic objective of achieving and maintaining stability in the general level of prices over the medium term (Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Pest management

    Adopted by Parliament, Biosecurity Act 1993, Part 5 (s 54 replaced 18 September 2012); administered by the Ministry for Primary Industries

    Part 5 provides for national and regional pest and pathway management plans. Its purpose includes (a) the development of effective and efficient instruments and measures that prevent, reduce, or eliminate the adverse effects of harmful organisms on economic wellbeing, the environment, human health, enjoyment of the natural environment, and the relationship between Māori, their culture, and their traditions and their ancestral lands, waters, sites, wāhi tapu, and taonga; and (b) the appropriate distribution of costs associated with the instruments and measures. (Biosecurity Act 1993 (opens in a new tab), s 54).

  • Sustainable management of resources

    Adopted by Parliament, Resource Management Act 1991

    Section 5(1): The purpose of this Act is to promote the sustainable management of natural and physical resources. (Resource Management Act 1991 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Conservation estate

    Adopted by Parliament, Conservation Act 1987

    Section 6 sets the Department of Conservation’s functions, including managing land and natural and historic resources held under the Act and fostering recreation and allowing tourism where it is not inconsistent with conservation (Conservation Act 1987 (opens in a new tab), s 6). Quotes on the SWOT and PESTLE pages.

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A third of the land managed for conservation

  • Emergency management

    Adopted by Parliament, Emergency Management Act 2026 (2026 No 53), in force 4 September 2026; it replaced the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002

    Section 3(d) provides for requiring local authorities to co-ordinate, through Emergency Management Committees, planning, programmes, and activities related to emergency management across the stages of reduction, readiness, response, and recovery, and encouraging co-operation and joint action within those Committees: (Emergency Management Act 2026 (opens in a new tab), s 3). Some provisions start later, up to 2028.

    Related SWOT · Threats · An Alpine Fault earthquake

  • Treaty of Waitangi claims

    Adopted by Parliament, Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975; administered by Te Puni Kōkiri

    Section 6(1) provides for claims to the Waitangi Tribunal about any measure that was or is inconsistent with the principles of the Treaty, covering any Act (whether or not still in force), passed at any time on or after 6 February 1840 (Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Screening of overseas investment

    Adopted by Parliament, Overseas Investment Act 2005; s 3 replaced 6 March 2026 by 2025 No 81

    Section 3(1)(c): managing the risks to New Zealand’s national interest that are associated with overseas investment transactions, including national security and public order risks. (Overseas Investment Act 2005 (opens in a new tab)). Quote of s 3(2) on the PESTLE page.

Sources checked 18–19 October 2026. Quotations are given as published.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Placing a response in a cell is Hawkeye’s only editorial step. Responses are not ranked.
