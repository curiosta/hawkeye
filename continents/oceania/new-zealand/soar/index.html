A Aspirations

New Zealand has no single national vision document. The Aspirations below are the statutory 2050 climate target and the Government’s nine Government Targets and three priorities, each labelled with its status.

The 2050 target

Law (Climate Change Response Act 2002, s 5Q; latest version as at 4 September 2026)

(Climate Change Response Act 2002 (opens in a new tab), s 5Q(1)): The target for emissions reduction (the 2050 target) requires that— net accounting emissions of greenhouse gases in a calendar year, other than biogenic methane, are zero by the calendar year beginning on 1 January 2050 and for each subsequent calendar year; and emissions of biogenic methane in a calendar year— are 10% less than 2017 emissions by the calendar year beginning on 1 January 2030; and are 14% to 24% less than 2017 emissions by the calendar year beginning on 1 January 2050 and for each subsequent calendar year.

Section 5Q(1)(b)(ii) was amended on 17 December 2025 by the Climate Change Response (2050 Target and Other Matters) Amendment Act 2025 (2025 No 80).

The nine Government Targets

Government targets approved by Cabinet, launched 8 April 2024; not a law

The nine Government Targets focus the public sector on achieving improved results in health, education, law and order, work, housing and the environment. Government Targets are six-year aspirations for achieving better results on public services and priorities that matter to New Zealanders. The Targets will be delivered by 2030. The targets, as published (Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (opens in a new tab)):

  1. 95% of patients to be admitted, discharged, or transferred from an emergency department within six hours.
  2. 95% of people wait less than four months for elective treatment.
  3. 15% reduction in the total number of children and young people with serious and persistent offending behaviour.
  4. 20,000 fewer people who are victims of an assault, robbery, or sexual assault.
  5. 50,000 fewer people on Jobseeker Support Benefit.
  6. 80% of students are present for more than 90% of the term.
  7. 80% of Year 8 students at or above the expected curriculum level for their age in reading, writing and maths by December 2030.
  8. 75% reduction of households in emergency housing.
  9. New Zealand is on track to meet its 2050 net zero climate change targets, with total net emissions of no more than 290 megatonnes from 2022 to 2025 and 305 megatonnes from 2026 to 2030.

Target 9 is the Government’s target statement. The Climate Change Commission’s 2026 assessment is on the GAP page: Current plans and policies are insufficient to meet the third budget or the 2050 target. The Government Targets and priorities belong to the government in office since 2023; a general election is due on 7 November 2026 (New Zealand Parliament (opens in a new tab)).

The Government’s three priorities

Prime Minister’s statement, not a law

(Final 2024 Action Plan, September 2024 (opens in a new tab)): Our final action plan for 2024 will build on the previous action plans and continue to deliver on the Government’s core priorities of rebuilding the economy, restoring law and order, and delivering better public services.

How this SOAR analysis works

For a country, the Aspirations are its official goal statements, quoted word for word. Where a country has no single national vision document, the page names the official documents it quotes instead.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
