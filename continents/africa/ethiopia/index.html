T

Threats

External · harmful

High inflation

  • Inflation of 14.9%. The central bank’s committee noted that Inflation has begun to ease from a supply-driven peak, but at 14.9 percent in September it remains far above the Bank’s single-digit goal. (National Bank of Ethiopia, 3 November 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Energy prices push costs up. Non-food inflation rose to 15.1% from 12.2% in June 2026. In non-food inflation, energy items (electricity tariffs, kerosene and chopped firewood) pushed up the CPI. (National Bank of Ethiopia, 3 November 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Still in double digits. Headline inflation is expected to remain in double digits over the six-month forecast horizon (National Bank of Ethiopia, 3 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Economic · GAP · Inflation

People displaced inside the country

  • 2.4 million internally displaced people. At the end of 2025, UNHCR counted 2,378,000 internally displaced people in Ethiopia, the fifth most in Africa (UNHCR (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Refugees abroad. 164,215 refugees and 148,616 asylum-seekers from Ethiopia were outside the country at the end of 2025 (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)).

Drought in East Africa

  • 8.5 million people hit by drought in the region. Drought affected more than 8.5 million people in East Africa in 2025, WMO reports. The figure covers the region, not Ethiopia alone (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam

  • 5,150 megawatts. The dam was inaugurated on 9 September 2025. The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, which has been under construction for the past 14 years, has a power generation capacity of 5,150 megawatts from its 13 turbines. (Ethiopian Electric Power, 9 September 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Total capacity of 9,730 MW. The power company says its total installed generation capacity reached 9,730 MW during the fiscal year 2025/26 (Ethiopian Electric Power, 9 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Power for export. The ten-year plan aims To increase electricity export from 2,803 GWH to 7,184 GWH (Ten Years Development Plan 2021–2030 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · Power capacity, 2030

Debt relief and an IMF programme

  • An agreement with official creditors. Ethiopia signed a memorandum of understanding with its Official Creditor Committee on 2 July 2025. Its debt to them is rescheduled through a lowering of interest rates and extending maturities until FY 2038/39 (Paris Club (opens in a new tab)).
  • IMF support. The IMF Extended Credit Facility arrangement was approved on July 29, 2024 , in an amount equivalent to SDR 2.6 billion (about USD 3.4 billion) (Paris Club (opens in a new tab)).
  • Growth momentum. The central bank’s activity indicators signal robust growth momentum in FY 2026/27 (National Bank of Ethiopia, 3 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Millions without electricity

  • 57 million people without electricity. Ethiopia had the third-largest number of people without electricity in the world in 2024, after Nigeria and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A long way to the grid target. In 2020/21 the ten-year plan put grid coverage at 33% and aims to raise it to 96% (Ten Years Development Plan 2021–2030 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in GAP · Grid coverage, 2030

Child stunting

  • 39% of children stunted. Between 2015/16 and 2021/22, child stunting rates rose slightly from 38 percent to 39 percent, underscoring the persistent challenge of chronic malnutrition. (Ethiopia Voluntary National Review 2025 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Stunting, 2030

Low income and a large debt

  • US$1,142 per person. GDP per person was US$1,142 in 2025, 40th of 53 African economies (IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • US$31 billion of external debt. External debt was $31 billion as of end-June 2024 (DSA perimeter), half of it owed to multilateral lenders (Paris Club (opens in a new tab)).
S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Almost all electricity from renewables

  • Over 95% renewable. Renewable energy sources now provide more than 95 percent of electricity generation, and the 2023 ban on fossil fuel vehicle imports reinforces the country’s low-carbon path (Ethiopia Voluntary National Review 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Mostly hydro power. Installed capacity includes 9,213 MW of hydro power, 504 MW of wind, 25 MW of biomass and 7.3 MW of geothermal power (Ethiopian Electric Power, Generation (opens in a new tab)).

Tree planting

  • Over 40 billion trees. The Green Legacy Initiative has planted over 40 billion trees since 2019, contributing to an 8.1 percent increase in national forest cover. (Ethiopia Voluntary National Review 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Forest cover of 23.5%. The initiative has contributed to raising forest cover to 23.5 percent (Ethiopia Voluntary National Review 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Tree planting, 2030

A large and young population

  • 135.5 million people. Ethiopia had 135.5 million people on 1 July 2025, the second most in Africa after Nigeria (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A young country. The median age was 19.1 years in 2025, against 30.9 for the world (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

No central bank loans to the government

  • Over 27 months without direct advances. the Government has maintained its commitment to refraining from direct advances from the NBE during the quarter taking the refrain period to over 27 months, making the longest such period on record and contributing to price stability. (National Bank of Ethiopia, 3 November 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Exports growing. In July to September 2026, goods exports grew, particularly growth in gold and coffee (National Bank of Ethiopia, 3 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
