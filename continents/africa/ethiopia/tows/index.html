Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Ethiopia has no policy in that area.   • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Use new dam capacity to close the electricity-access gap

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Millions without electricity · SWOT · Opportunities · The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam

      Use power from the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam to connect more households, because generation that is already almost all renewable still leaves tens of millions of people without electricity. Official Ethiopian programmes already include the dam itself. This recommendation pairs the access weakness with that new capacity. Capacity is not the same as household access.

      Analogous case. Kenya’s official energy compact records a national result: Kenya has made significant strides in expanding electricity access, with national access levels rising from approximately 30% in 2014 to over 75% (65% on grid, 0.84% mini grid and 9.2% Standalone PV) as at 2024. This progress has largely been complemented by a strong focus on renewable energy development, which now accounts for 82% of the country’s total installed electricity generation capacity and 93% of actual energy consumption. (Kenya National Energy Compact 2025–2030 (opens in a new tab), figures as at 2024).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Ethiopia had the third-largest number of people without electricity in the world in 2024, at 57 million, while renewable sources already provide more than 95% of electricity generation (live SWOT). The dam, inaugurated on 9 September 2025, has a capacity of 5,150 megawatts (live SWOT). Kenya raised national access from about 30% in 2014 to over 75% in 2024 on a renewable-heavy system. The analogy is limited because a large leftover in Kenya still lacked access in 2024, and because Ethiopia’s gap of 57 million people is larger in absolute terms. New dam capacity does not automatically connect households. Last-mile grid work and off-grid supply still matter.

    • Use debt treatment and the nutrition programme to cut child stunting

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Child stunting · SWOT · Opportunities · Debt relief and an IMF programme

      Use official debt treatment, and the national nutrition programme already in place, to cut child stunting. Official Ethiopian programmes already include the nutrition programme and the 2 July 2025 memorandum with official creditors. This recommendation pairs the stunting weakness with that financing opportunity. It is not a claim that no nutrition policy exists.

      Analogous case. Peru ran a focused national effort against stunting during a period of growth. The World Bank records: Peru more than halved its stunting rates in eight years from 28 percent in 2008 to 13 percent in 2016. The same book states that it was targeted policies that played a pivotal role, while poverty fell from 58.7 percent in 2004 to 22.7 percent in 2014 (World Bank, Standing Tall, 2017 (opens in a new tab)). These years follow the book’s opening and Chart A1, not the foreword’s 2005-2016 span, which is inconsistent with those figures.

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Child stunting in Ethiopia rose slightly from 38% in 2015/16 to 39% in 2021/22 (live SWOT). Peru cut stunting from 28% to 13% in eight years through targeted policies during a period of growth. The analogy is limited because Peru’s result came from a focused nutrition effort, not from debt relief alone. Ethiopia’s stunting rate rose slightly in the latest official comparison in the live SWOT. Debt treatment can free public money, but it does not by itself feed children or improve diets.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
