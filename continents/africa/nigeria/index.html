T

Threats

External · harmful

Hunger in the north-east

  • 6.4 million people need food aid in three states. The latest Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis indicates that the situation in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States is particularly dire, with an estimated 6.4 million people, including more than 1.1 million in CH Phase 4 (Emergency) and 10,300 in CH Phase 5 (Catastrophe), requiring immediate life-saving food and livelihood aid. (FAO Nigeria, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Many causes at once. FAO lists Persistent conflict and insecurity, including insurgency, banditry, kidnappings, and farmer–herder conflicts, together with currency depreciation, rising inflation and climate shocks (FAO Nigeria (opens in a new tab)).

Millions forced from their homes

  • 3.5 million people displaced inside Nigeria. At the end of 2025, UNHCR counted 3,544,519 internally displaced people in Nigeria (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)).
  • Refugees abroad. 478,150 refugees and 56,176 asylum-seekers from Nigeria were outside the country at the end of 2025 (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)).

Flash floods

  • The Mokwa flood of May 2025. A flash flood hit Mokwa in Niger State on 28 May 2025 after heavy rain. NEMA reported 159 deaths, more than 98 people missing and more than 265 homes damaged (NEMA, 2 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Floods across Africa. WMO reports that flooding in Nigeria in May 2025 led to over 200 deaths (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Off-grid solar and gas for transport

  • Money for off-grid solar. For example, Nigeria’s Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) project has allocated USD 300 million for off-grid solar (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Gas-powered transport. The central bank welcomed the Presidential Initiative on National Affordable CNG Transit Programme, which it expects to lower transportation costs and support the continued decline in inflationary pressures (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Easing inflation and lower interest rates

  • Policy rate reset to 23%. On 22 September 2026 the Monetary Policy Committee decided to Reset the Monetary Policy Rate at 23 per cent. It said this was an operational reset, not a change of stance (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Inflation falling. Headline inflation (year-on-year) slowed to 15.39 per cent in August 2026 from 15.43 per cent in the preceding month, driven by both the food and core measures. (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Stronger banks. The committee noted the strengthening of the banking industry, following the successful recapitalisation programme (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Poverty and short lives

  • 133 million people in multidimensional poverty. 63% of persons living within Nigeria (133 million people) are multidimensionally poor. The figure is from the 2022 survey (NBS, 17 November 2022 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Poverty is highest in the north. 65% of the poor (86 million people) live in the North, while 35% (nearly 47 million) live in the South. (NBS (opens in a new tab))
  • The lowest life expectancy in Africa. Life expectancy at birth was 54.8 years in 2025, the lowest of the 58 areas of Africa (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Poverty, 2030

The largest number of people without electricity

  • 87 million people without electricity. Of the global population lacking access, three countries can claim nearly a third of them: the Democratic Republic of Congo, 85 million; Ethiopia, 57 million; and Nigeria, 87 million people (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The figures are for 2024.
  • Cooking with wood and charcoal. Over half of the population of Nigeria are multidimensionally poor and cook with dung, wood or charcoal, rather than cleaner energy. (NBS, 2022 survey (opens in a new tab))

Also in GAP · Electricity, 2030

A low income per person

  • US$1,223 per person. GDP per person was US$1,223 in 2025, 35th of 53 African economies, although Nigeria has the third-largest economy in Africa (IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Public debt rising in naira. Nigeria’s consolidated public debt stood at ₦166.79 trillion (31.78% of GDP) as of end-June 2026, up from ₦159.35 trillion at the end of March (CBN Economic Report, Q2 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Africa’s largest population

  • 237.5 million people. Nigeria had 237.5 million people on 1 July 2025, the most in Africa and 15.3% of the continent (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • A young country. The median age was 18.1 years in 2025, against 30.9 for the world. Women had 4.3 children on average (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Growth, reserves and a surplus

  • Growth of 4.43%. Real GDP grew by 4.43 per cent in the second quarter of 2026, from 3.89 per cent in the preceding quarter, reflecting improved performance in both the non-oil and oil sectors. (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Reserves at an 18-year high. Gross external reserves stood at US$55.25 billion on September 18, 2026, the highest in the last 18 years and sufficient to finance approximately 11.3 months of imports of goods and services. (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • A current account surplus. The current account surplus rose to US$7.54 billion in the second quarter of 2026, from US$4.49 billion in the first quarter (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Economic · GAP · Growth

Oil output rising

  • 1.70 million barrels a day. Total crude oil Crude Oil production, including condensates, increased by 9.68 per cent to 1.70 million Production barrels per day (mbpd), from 1.55 mbpd in the preceding quarter. This is the second quarter of 2026. The repeated words are part of the report’s page layout (CBN Economic Report, Q2 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Higher oil prices. The price of Bonny Light crude rose by 31.14% to US$108.45 a barrel in the second quarter of 2026 (CBN Economic Report, Q2 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Information technology drives growth

  • Technology leads the non-oil economy. The non-oil sector grew 4.31% in the second quarter of 2026, driven largely by increased activities in information and communications technology, crop production, real estate, livestock, financial services and trade subsectors (Central Bank of Nigeria, 22 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
