Africa PESTLE

The large outside forces acting on a place, in six groups: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental.

Political 1 point

Governance and cooperation

  • Guinea back in the African Union. On 22 January 2026, after a new constitution and a presidential election on 28 December 2025, the Peace and Security Council Decides to lift the suspension of the participation of the Republic of Guinea in the activities of the AU (AU Peace and Security Council, 22 January 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Economic 1 point

Economy and finance

  • Growth of 4.2% expected in 2026. Africa’s economies are projected to grow at 4.2 percent in 2026, moderating slightly from 4.4 percent in 2025, before rebounding to 4.4 percent in 2027. Inflation is projected at 10.4% in 2026 (African Development Bank, 26 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Strong growth · SWOT · Weaknesses · A small share of world output and a large funding gap

Social 1 point

Water and food security

  • 309 million hungry people. For the first time, more people face hunger in Africa than in Asia, 309 million against 292 million in 2025 (FAO, on SOFI 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Hunger · GAP · Hunger down to at most 5% by 2033

Technological 1 point

Energy transition

  • Electrification has slowed. progress in Sub-Saharan Africa has slowed significantly, and the pace of electrification must triple to achieve universal access by 2030 (Tracking SDG 7 press release, 24 June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Millions without electricity or clean cooking · GAP · Electricity for everyone by 2030 (Sub-Saharan Africa)

Legal 2 points

Economy and finance

  • 50 countries bound by the free trade agreement. 50 State Parties have ratified the AfCFTA agreement, and 48 have verified and adopted tariff schedules (AfCFTA Secretariat (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · The African Continental Free Trade Area

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • A treaty against nuclear weapons. The Treaty of Pelindaba has been in force since 15 July 2009, with 44 ratifications (African Union treaty status list (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · A nuclear-weapon-free zone

Environmental 2 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Faster warming and rising seas. Africa is warming faster than the world average, and sea level on some coasts is rising faster than the world average (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Warming faster than the world average

Natural and cosmic hazards

  • Early warnings. Throughout the African continent, there is growing commitment to the international Early Warnings for All initiative spearheaded by UN Secretary-General António Guterres. (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab))

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Floods and drought in 2025 · GAP · Early warnings for everyone by the end of 2027

Sources checked 12 November 2026. Several sources cover only Sub-Saharan Africa, which leaves out North Africa. Each one says so.

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a continent, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups. Where a continent has no permanent population, government or national economy, as with Antarctica, the groups follow what its governing treaties and official bodies report on.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact.

  • P

    Political

    Geopolitics, conflict and cooperation

  • E

    Economic

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Official sources, and labelled analysis. Hawkeye reports official and authoritative sources, quoted or attributed, with a link. Documented goals and programmes appear as those bodies stated them. TOWS strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice to any person or firm. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
