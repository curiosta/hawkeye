Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that Africa has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Build renewable power so growth and low emissions per person can rise together

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Strong growth · SWOT · Strengths · Low emissions per person · SWOT · Opportunities · Renewable power and off-grid solar

      Expand grid, mini-grid and standalone renewable power so that African growth can continue with low emissions per person. Official continental and national energy programmes already exist. This recommendation pairs those strengths with the renewable-power opportunity, and uses a documented national result.

      Analogous case. Kenya’s official energy compact states: Kenya has made significant strides in expanding electricity access, with national access levels rising from approximately 30% in 2014 to over 75% (65% on grid, 0.84% mini grid and 9.2% Standalone PV) as at 2024. This progress has largely been complemented by a strong focus on renewable energy development, which now accounts for 82% of the country’s total installed electricity generation capacity and 93% of actual energy consumption. It also states that Kenya leads the African continent in installed geothermal capacity and ranks sixth globally (Kenya National Energy Compact 2025–2030 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Africa already has low emissions per person and recent strong growth (live SWOT), and renewable power is a live opportunity. Kenya shows that access, geothermal and a high renewable share of generation can move together. The same compact records that about 25% of Kenyans still lack electricity, with a larger gap in rural areas, and that over 69% of households still rely on traditional biomass for cooking. Geothermal resources and grid finance are not evenly spread across the continent.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Close Africa’s electricity and clean-cooking gap with off-grid and grid renewables

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Millions without electricity or clean cooking · SWOT · Opportunities · Renewable power and off-grid solar

      Use off-grid solar, mini-grids and careful grid extension to reach the millions of Africans who still lack electricity or clean cooking. Official access programmes already exist. This recommendation pairs the live access weakness with the renewable-power opportunity.

      Analogous case. The same Kenyan compact records a rise in national electricity access from about 30% in 2014 to over 75% in 2024, with 9.2% from standalone photovoltaic systems, and a renewable share of 82% of installed capacity and 93% of electricity consumption (Kenya National Energy Compact 2025–2030 (opens in a new tab)). The compact is explicit about what remains: Approximately 25% of Kenyans still do not have access to electricity, with the rural areas disproportionately low on access. Over 69% of households rely on traditional biomass for cooking.

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Millions of Africans still lack electricity or clean cooking (live SWOT). The Kenyan mix of grid, mini-grid and standalone solar is a documented way to raise access while keeping a high renewable share. The limit is honest in the same source: even after that rise, a rural electricity gap and a much larger clean-cooking gap remain. Costs, connection fees and fuel habits will limit the pace in other countries, and a 75% access rate is not universal access.

    • Use coordinated debt relief so more public money can reach basic services

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · A small share of world output and a large funding gap · SWOT · Opportunities · The African Continental Free Trade Area

      Use coordinated debt relief, alongside the African Continental Free Trade Area, so that more public money can reach basic services rather than interest payments. AfCFTA and official debt work already exist. This recommendation pairs Africa’s funding-gap weakness with that trade opportunity, and uses a documented relief result.

      Analogous case. The World Bank states: The HIPC and related Multilateral Debt Relief Initiative (MDRI) programs have relieved 37 participating countries of more than $100 billion in debt. To date, 37 countries — 31 of them in Africa — have debt-relief for which they were eligible through the HIPC Initiative and the MDRI. The same brief warns: But challenges remain to ensure that debt burdens do not return to unsustainable levels. (World Bank, HIPC brief, 9 May 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. Africa still has a small share of world output and a large funding gap (live SWOT). Past relief did lift a large debt stock in 31 African countries. The World Bank’s own limit still applies: unsustainable burdens can return. AfCFTA is a trade opportunity, not a debt instrument, and relief alone will not close the funding gap if new borrowing and interest costs rise again.

Documented regional responses 5

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • The African Continental Free Trade Area

    Adopted by African Union, AfCFTA Secretariat (50 State Parties by February 2026)

    50 State Parties have ratified the agreement. 26 are ready to trade and 12 have told the Secretariat they trade under it (AfCFTA Secretariat (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Opportunities · The African Continental Free Trade Area · SWOT · Weaknesses · A small share of world output and a large funding gap

  • Early Warnings for All

    Adopted by United Nations, led by WMO, with national roadmaps in African countries

    A number of countries have committed to national roadmaps (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Floods and drought in 2025 · SWOT · Threats · Warming faster than the world average

  • The Treaty of Pelindaba

    Adopted by African Union member states (in force since 15 July 2009)

    44 of the 55 African Union member states have ratified the treaty or joined it (African Union treaty status list (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · A nuclear-weapon-free zone

  • The plan for 2024 to 2033

    Adopted by African Union, Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan

    The plan sets targets for 2033, among them to cut hunger to at most 5% and to raise households’ access to electricity to 80% (Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Hunger · SWOT · Weaknesses · Millions without electricity or clean cooking

  • The African Credit Rating Agency

    Adopted by Launched in January 2026, as reported by the African Development Bank

    The bank describes it as an important tool for addressing perceived biases in sovereign risk assessments (African Development Bank, 26 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

How this TOWS matrix works

For a continent, the responses are documented instruments of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, each named with its members and the date it was adopted.

Every documented regional response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
