Targets and latest values

Power-sector emissions 37% below business as usual by 2030

Target
For electricity generation, transmission and distribution, the NDC sets a mitigation target of 80,520 Gg CO2e by 2030, a 37% cut against business-as-usual emissions of 214,740 Gg CO2e that year (Egypt’s NDC, second update, 2023 (opens in a new tab), p. 12).
Baseline
87,694 Gg CO2e in 2015 (NDC (opens in a new tab), p. 12).
Latest
a substantial GHG emission reduction of 47,810 GgCO2e in 2022, with a decrease of 34.34% in emissions compared to BAU levels for that same year. Power-sector emissions were 91,403.40 Gg CO2e in 2022, against a business-as-usual level of 139,213.87 Gg (Egypt’s first Biennial Transparency Report (opens in a new tab), p. 23 and Table 45, p. 133).
Distance
2.7 percentage points short of the 37% cut (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 37 − 34.34). Each percentage is measured against business as usual for its own year; the 2030 business-as-usual level is higher than 2022’s.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Egypt’s own report to the UN climate secretariat: Egypt’s electricity sector is advancing steadily towards the 2030 target of 37% overall GHG emission reduction. (BTR1 (opens in a new tab), p. 23)

Renewables at 42% of electricity generation by 2030

Target
Installing additional renewable energy (RE) capacities to increase electricity generation contribution to be 42% of generation mix by 2030. (NDC (opens in a new tab), p. 13). The updated Vision 2030 annex sets 29.1% for 2025 and 40.3% for 2030 (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), p. 205), and its main text dates the 42% to 2035 (pp. 156, 185).
Baseline
8.37% in FY 2015/16 (BTR1 (opens in a new tab), Table 52). The Vision annex gives 20% for 2020/21, where BTR1 gives 12.19%; neither document explains the difference.
Latest
Renewable energy accounted for 12.07% of Egypt's electricity generation in 2022: 26,100 GWh of 216,252 GWh, from the electricity holding company’s data for FY 2022/23 (BTR1 (opens in a new tab), Table 52, p. 144). For FY 2023/24 the company lists 15,056.49 GWh of hydro and 11,630.628 GWh of new and renewable energy out of 229,284.23 GWh generated (EEHC Annual Report 2023/2024 (opens in a new tab)), about 11.64% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, same method).
Distance
30.4 percentage points below the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 42 − 11.64)
Assessment by the tracking agency
indicating slow progress toward the target of 42% by 2030. (BTR1 (opens in a new tab), p. 144)

Under-five mortality of 14.3 per 1,000 by 2030

Target
16.6 deaths per 1,000 live births in 2025 and 14.3 in 2030, with “Consultation with experts” named as the source of the targets (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), annex, p. 191).
Baseline
20.4 in 2019, as listed in the annex, which names no source for it (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), p. 191).
Latest
22.4 per 1,000 live births in 2024, with an uncertainty range of 16.3 to 30.6 (UN IGME (opens in a new tab)). The same series puts 2019 at 24.8.
Distance
Not computed. The plan’s 2019 baseline (20.4) does not match UN IGME’s estimate for 2019 (24.8), so they are not treated as the same measure.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Private investment at 65% of total investment by 2030

Target
50% in 2025 and 65% in 2030 for the share of private sector investments in total investments (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), annex, p. 201).
Baseline
28.2% in 2020/21 (annex, p. 201). The main text adds: there has been a resurgence in private investment, reaching 28.7% in 2021/22. (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), p. 117)
Latest
No outturn has been confirmed at source yet (TBC). The plan for FY 2025/26 projects an increase in private investments to reach around EGP 1.94 trillion, representing 63% of total investments compared to 37% for public investments. (Ministry of Planning, 18 May 2025 (opens in a new tab)). That is a projection, not a result.
Distance
Not computed. There is no confirmed actual share.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

National poverty rate of 21.9% by 2030

Target
25.8% in 2025 and 21.9% in 2030 for the share of people below the national poverty line (EGP 857 per month per person, in the annex’s definition) (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), annex, p. 190).
Baseline
29.7% in 2019/20, from CAPMAS’s Household Income, Expenditure and Consumption Survey (Egypt Vision 2030, updated edition (opens in a new tab), p. 190).
Latest
No newer survey figure has been confirmed at source (TBC). World Bank estimates indicate that the national poverty rate was 33.5 percent in 2021/22 (World Bank Macro Poverty Outlook, April 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. The only later figure is a World Bank estimate, not the CAPMAS survey the target is based on.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Haya Karima (Decent Life): the first phase

Target
The allocated budget for the first phase of the initiative is 350 billion EGP to implement approximately 23,000 projects in 1,477 villages across 52 centers in 20 governorates, benefiting 18 million citizens. (Ministry of Planning, 8 March 2025 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Not applicable: a programme of works.
Latest
the amount spent from the first-phase allocation by the end of December reached 298.3 billion EGP, with an implementation rate of 86.5%. A total of 16,590 developmental projects have been completed, and all developmental projects have been completed in 135 villages (Ministry of Planning, 8 March 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
About 6,400 of the approximately 23,000 projects were not reported complete, and EGP 51.7 billion of the allocation was unspent, at the end of December 2024 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
The ministry reports the 86.5% implementation rate quoted above (Ministry of Planning (opens in a new tab)).

Unemployment at 5% by 2030 (2016 strategy)

Target
The 2016 strategy lists Unemployment rate (%) 12.8 10 5: 12.8% at the time, 10% by 2020 and 5% by 2030 (Sustainable Development Strategy, 2016 (opens in a new tab), p. 16). The updated Vision 2030 annex sets no unemployment target.
Baseline
12.8%, the strategy’s current value in 2016.
Latest
the country’s annual unemployment rate fell to 6.6 percent in 2024 (State Information Service, citing CAPMAS, 26 April 2025 (opens in a new tab)). CAPMAS has announced a 2025 annual figure; it has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).
Distance
1.6 percentage points above the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 5 − 6.6)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 26 and 31 October 2026.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
