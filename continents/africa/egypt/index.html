T

Threats

External · harmful

Red Sea disruption and canal receipts

  • Receipts down 24.3%, then 45.5%. In FY 2023/24, Suez Canal transit receipts by 24.3 percent to only US$ 6.6 billion (against US$ 8.8 billion) (CBE, FY 2023/24 (opens in a new tab)). In FY 2024/25: The decline in Suez Canal transit receipts by 45.5 percent to only US$ 3.6 billion (against US$ 6.6 billion). Net tonnage fell 55.1% and transiting vessels 38.5% (CBE, External Position FY 2024/25 (opens in a new tab)).
  • The cause, as the Central Bank states it. The FY 2024/25 report links the fall to the Red Sea disruptions, which continue to affect global trade and push down the Suez Canal receipts. (CBE, FY 2024/25 (opens in a new tab), Overview).

Nile water and imported wheat

  • The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam. In a letter to the Security Council about the fifth filling of the dam, Egypt wrote: Egypt categorically rejects such unlawful unilateral Ethiopian actions, and that continued Ethiopian policies could result in an existential threat to Egypt (UN document S/2024/646 (opens in a new tab), 1 September 2024).
  • One river. The River Nile is the main source of water for Egypt, with an annual allocated flow of 55.5 km3/yr under the Nile Waters Agreement of 1959. (FAO AQUASTAT country profile (opens in a new tab)).
  • Wheat imports. Wheat import requirements are forecast at 13.5 million tonnes, about 8 percent above the average, mostly sourced from the Black Sea Region due to competitive prices. (FAO GIEWS, 30 June 2026 (opens in a new tab), 2026/27 marketing year).

Refugees from Sudan

  • Over a million registered. UNHCR Egypt lists 1,084,858 refugees and asylum-seekers registered as of 30 September 2026, of whom 846,556 (78.0%) are from Sudan (UNHCR Operational Data Portal (opens in a new tab)). At the end of 2025 Egypt hosted 1,098,301 refugees and asylum-seekers, fourth in Africa (UNHCR Refugee Data Finder (opens in a new tab)).

A low-lying, food-producing delta

  • Sea level and the Nile Delta. More than 30% of the Nile delta is a lowland area (levels lower than +2.00 m) and faces several risks such as erosion and flooding. About three-fifths of Egypt's food production is secured from agricultural land in the Nile Delta region. (Egypt’s NDC, second update, 2023 (opens in a new tab), p. 6).
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Renewables and interconnectors

  • NWFE energy pillar. The initiative aims to retire 5 GW of inefficient fossil-fuel capacity by 2025, invest in a just transition and accelerate Egypt's renewable development, including at least 10 GW of solar and wind energy by 2028 (EBRD, 8 November 2022 (opens in a new tab)). Whether the 2025 retirement took place has not been confirmed at source.
  • A link to Saudi Arabia. The interconnection will allow Saudi Arabia and Egypt to exchange up to 3,000 MW of power. It will run along a 1,350 km route (IEA policies database, updated April 2022 (opens in a new tab)). The commissioning dates on that page are 2022 expectations; operation has not been confirmed at source.
  • A link to Greece. The planned GREGY interconnector lists 3,000 MW transfer capacity, and says it would Replace amounts to 4.5 bcm of natural gas (GREGY project page (opens in a new tab)). It is a planned project, not built.

European finance and Gulf investment

  • A EUR 7.4 billion EU package. To underpin this partnership, the EU is working on a EUR 7.4 billion financial package for 2024-2027: EUR 5 billion of macro-financial assistance as concessional loans, EUR 1.8 billion of additional investments and EUR 600 million in bilateral grants, including EUR 200 million for migration (EEAS, The European Union and Egypt (opens in a new tab)). The page describes a package, not a single legal act.
  • Ras El-Hekma. Net foreign direct investment inflows rose to hit US$ 46.1 billion (of which, US$ 35.0 billion within the context of Ras-El Hekma agreement) in FY 2023/24 (Central Bank of Egypt, External Position FY 2023/24 (opens in a new tab)).
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

High public debt

  • About 90% of GDP. The IMF puts general government gross debt at 95.9% of GDP in 2023, 90.9% in 2024 and 86.8% in 2025 (IMF World Economic Outlook DataMapper (opens in a new tab)). The share of revenue spent on interest has not been confirmed at source yet (TBC).

Scarce water from outside

  • 570 m³ per person. there has been a sharp decline in the annual freshwater resources available per capita to shrink from 1,972 cubic meters per year in 1970 to 570 cubic meters per year in 2018. Egypt has 97% dependency ratio since its renewable water sources comes from outside its territory (Egypt’s NDC, second update, 2023 (opens in a new tab), p. 4).

Poverty and women’s work

  • A third below the national line. The official rate decreased from 32.5% in 2017/18 to 29.7% in 2019/20 (Egypt Vision 2030 (opens in a new tab), p. 53). The World Bank adds: World Bank estimates indicate that the national poverty rate was 33.5 percent in 2021/22 (World Bank Macro Poverty Outlook, April 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Few women in the labour force. Male economic participation reached 70.3 percent in 2024, while female participation rose to 16.9 percent, indicating that men’s participation remains over four times higher than that of women. (State Information Service, citing CAPMAS, 26 April 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Fossil-heavy power

  • 12.2% renewable electricity. Renewable sources produced 12.2% of Egypt’s electricity in 2021, the latest year in the World Bank series (World Bank WDI (opens in a new tab)).
  • 258 Mt of fossil CO2. Egypt emitted 258 Mt in 2024, second in Africa and 0.67% of the world total; 2.2 tonnes per person against a world 4.7 t (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab); Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

The Suez Canal

  • US$8.8 billion in canal receipts. The Central Bank’s balance-of-payments table shows Suez Canal receipts of US$8,759.6 million in FY 2022/23 and US$6,632.4 million in FY 2023/24 (Central Bank of Egypt, External Position FY 2023/24 (opens in a new tab)). The later fall is under Threats.

Large-scale solar

  • Benban. Egypt’s 2023 NDC lists among its renewable achievements the launch of Benban Solar Park (total of 1,465 MW) (Egypt’s NDC, second update, 2023 (opens in a new tab), p. 9).

A national cash-transfer programme

  • Takaful and Karama. The number of direct and indirect beneficiaries of the program in 2024 reached 21 million citizens, with 5.2 million households registered in total. (Arab Reform Initiative, April 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 3). These are the ministry’s figures; the same paper cites a World Bank figure of 11.3 million households, which it says contradicts them.

A large, young population

  • 118.4 million people, median age 24.5. Egypt has 118,365,994 people (1 July 2025), 1.44% of the world and third in Africa. The median age is 24.5 years against a world 30.9, and the fertility rate is 2.71 births per woman (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab); share: Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
