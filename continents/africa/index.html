T

Threats

External · harmful

Warming faster than the world average

  • Faster warming. The African continent is warming faster than the global average, and the rate of warming across the continent since 1991 is substantially higher than in any of the previous 30-year periods. (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • About 0.51 °C above the 1991 to 2020 average in 2025. Over land, 2025 ranked between the third and seventh warmest year on record, depending on the dataset (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Glaciers almost gone. Africa’s glaciers have lost more than 90% of their area since the late nineteenth century. On Mount Kilimanjaro, glacier area fell from 11.4 km² in 1900 to less than 1 km² (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Seas rising faster than the world average on some coasts. From 1999 to 2025, sea level rose about 5.6 mm a year in the Red Sea and 5.2 mm a year on the Indian Ocean coast, against a world average of 3.6 mm (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Floods and drought in 2025

  • At least 13 million people affected. Extreme weather and climate-related events affected at least 13 million people and led to over 3 000 reported fatalities in Africa in 2025 (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Floods were the most common hazard. Floods made up more than half of reported events. Flooding killed over 200 people in Nigeria in May and over 160 in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in April (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Drought in East Africa. Drought affected more than 8.5 million people in East Africa in 2025 (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Few warning systems. Only 40% of African countries have warning systems that cover several hazards (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental · GAP · Early warnings, 2027

Millions forced to flee war

  • Sudan is the largest source. At the end of 2025, 2,820,737 refugees and 940,297 asylum-seekers came from Sudan, 3,761,034 in all (UNHCR (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Other crises. South Sudan was the origin of 2,510,483 refugees and asylum-seekers, the Democratic Republic of the Congo 1,344,910 and Somalia 1,128,308 (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)).
  • Most stay in Africa. Uganda hosted 1,971,968 refugees and asylum-seekers, Chad 1,511,634 and Ethiopia 1,123,453 (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)).
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

The African Continental Free Trade Area

  • 50 State Parties. By February 2026, 50 countries had ratified the agreement, the AfCFTA Secretariat reports (AfCFTA Secretariat (opens in a new tab)).
  • Ready to trade. 26 State Parties have published their tariff cuts in national law and are ready to trade, and 12 have told the Secretariat that they trade under the agreement (AfCFTA Secretariat (opens in a new tab)).
  • A target for trade inside Africa. The African Union’s plan for 2024 to 2033 sets this target: Increase intra-Africa trade to 30% of total African trade (Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan (opens in a new tab)). AU-STATAFRIC writes: intra-African trade remains weak, averaging 11% of imports and 16% of exports over the period 2018–2024. The value of intra-African exports has fluctuated, but the share of intra-exports in the total returned to its 2018 level, standing at 16.9% in both 2018 and 2024. (AU-STATAFRIC, African Trade Statistics Yearbook 2025 (opens in a new tab)). An African Union press release on the 2025 Africa Integration Report says: Intra-African trade now accounts for almost 16% of total trade, compared to less than 10% two decades ago. (African Union, 3 October 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Legal · GAP · Intra-Africa trade, 2033

Renewable power and off-grid solar

  • 35% of electricity from renewables. Oceania and Europe ranked second and third, respectively, for their shares of renewable sources in electricity consumption (both around 40 percent), followed by Sub-Saharan Africa at 35 percent. (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Off-grid solar and mini-grids. Distributed renewable energy solutions, including off-grid solar and mini-grids, are a cost-effective solution for electricity access, already serving hundreds of millions of people. This is a statement about the world as a whole (Tracking SDG 7 press release, 24 June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Cooking with electricity. By 2030, electric cooking alone in Sub-Saharan Africa drives up electricity demand 16 percent from today (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Hunger

  • More hungry people than in Asia. Africa is now home to approximately 309 million hungry people, compared with 292 million people in Asia. It is the first time that the African continent has surpassed Asia in terms of numbers of people in hunger. (FAO, on SOFI 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • One in five people. The share of Africans facing hunger fell from 20.3% in 2024 to 20.0% in 2025 (FAO, on SOFI 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Healthy diets out of reach. Across the world, 2.69 billion people cannot afford a healthy diet, with Africa falling further behind (FAO, on SOFI 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social · GAP · Hunger, 2033

Millions without electricity or clean cooking

  • 563 million people without electricity. Sub-Saharan Africa remains the center of the global challenge, as it is home to 563 million of the 655 million people without electricity. The figures are for 2024 (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Most people cook with polluting fuels. Only about 22% of people in Sub-Saharan Africa had clean cooking in 2024. Some 970 (930–1,000) million people lack access in Sub-Saharan Africa alone, and that figure may reach 1 billion by 2027. (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Rural areas left behind. The number of people without electricity in rural Sub-Saharan Africa grew from 376 million in 2010 to 447 million in 2024 (Tracking SDG 7 press release (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · Electricity, 2030 · GAP · Clean cooking, 2030

A small share of world output and a large funding gap

  • 2.7% of world GDP. Africa had 18.8% of the world’s people in 2025 but 2.7% of world GDP at current prices (IMF WEO, Oct 2026 (opens in a new tab), UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • A funding gap of over US$1.3 trillion a year. The African Development Bank finds that the continent faces an annual gap exceeding $1.3 trillion to meet the Sustainable Development Goals (African Development Bank, 26 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • High inflation. Inflation is projected to stay elevated at 10.4 percent in 2026 (African Development Bank (opens in a new tab)).

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

A young and growing population

  • Median age of 19.3 years. Half of Africa’s people were younger than 19.3 in 2025, against 30.9 for the world (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 1.55 billion people. Africa had 1,549.9 million people on 1 July 2025, 18.8% of the world, growing by about 2.3% a year (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Births per woman. The fertility rate was 3.95 births per woman in 2025, against 2.24 for the world (UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Low emissions per person

  • 3.9% of world fossil CO2. Africa emitted 1,504 million tonnes of fossil CO2 in 2024, 3.9% of the world total of 38,599 million tonnes, with 18.8% of the world’s people (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • About 1.0 tonne per person. That is about a fifth of the world average of 4.7 tonnes (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • 2.9% of emissions since 1850. African countries emitted 54,127 million tonnes of fossil CO2 from 1850 to 2024, 2.9% of the world total (Global Carbon Budget 2025 (opens in a new tab). Hawkeye’s arithmetic).

A nuclear-weapon-free zone

  • The Treaty of Pelindaba. The African Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zone Treaty was adopted in Cairo on 11 April 1996 and entered into force on 15 July 2009 (African Union treaty status list (opens in a new tab)).
  • 44 ratifications. Of the 55 African Union member states, 52 have signed the treaty and 44 have ratified it or joined it (African Union treaty status list (opens in a new tab)).
  • Support from nuclear-armed states. France has ratified Protocols I, II and III. China, the United Kingdom and the Russian Federation have ratified Protocols I and II. (African Union treaty status list (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Legal

Strong growth

  • Among the fastest-growing regions. The continent remains among the world’s fastest-growing regions, with 22 countries projected to grow above 5 percent in 2025. (African Development Bank, 26 May 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Growth of 4.4% in 2025. The African Development Bank estimates growth of 4.4% in 2025 and projects 4.2% in 2026 and 4.4% in 2027 (African Development Bank (opens in a new tab)).
  • East Africa grows fastest. East Africa is projected to grow 5.9% in 2026, after 6.6% in 2025 (African Development Bank (opens in a new tab)).
