Targets and latest values

Unemployment at 6% by 2030

Target
The unemployment rate should fall from 24.9 percent in June 2012 to 14 percent by 2020 and to 6 percent by 2030. (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
24.9% in June 2012, as stated in the plan.
Latest
The official unemployment rate increased by 0,1 of a percentage point to 33,7% in Q3: 2026 (Stats SA, QLFS Q3 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
27.7 percentage points above the 6% target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 33.7 − 6).
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

24 million people employed by 2030

Target
Increase employment from 13 million in 2010 to 24 million in 2030. (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
13 million in 2010, as stated in the plan.
Latest
16,840 thousand employed in the third quarter of 2026 (Table A). Stats SA: the number of employed individuals increased by 101 000 to 16,8 million (Stats SA, QLFS Q3 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
7.2 million jobs short of the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 24 − 16.84)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

No households below R419 a person a month

Target
Reduce the proportion of households with a monthly income below R419 per person (in 2009 prices) from 39 percent to zero. (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
39% of households, as stated in the plan.
Latest
No current figure on the R419 line has been confirmed. Statistics South Africa’s nearest published line: the lower-bound poverty line (LBPL) – set at R1 300 per person per month (in 2023 prices) – fell to 37,9% in 2023 (Stats SA, 11 December 2025 (opens in a new tab)); that is a share of people, not households.
Distance
Not computed. The published lines and units differ from the plan’s.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

A Gini coefficient of 0.6

Target
Reduce inequality – The Gini coefficient should fall from 0.69 to 0.6. (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
0.69, as stated in the plan.
Latest
0.5405 for the 2022 survey, measured on consumption (World Bank PIP (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. The plan’s 0.69 is an income Gini; the World Bank figure is based on consumption, a different measure.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Life expectancy of at least 70 years

Target
Raise the life expectancy of South Africans to at least 70 years (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
In the official 2026 mid-year series, life expectancy at birth in 2010 was 58,7 years in total (males 56,1 years, females 61,1 years) (Appendix 2). The National Development Plan uses 2010 as the starting year for other enabling milestones in the same list as the 70-year life-expectancy goal. The 2026 release presents a revised series for 2002–2026 and states: comparisons between this model and previous series should not be made. (Stats SA, Mid-year population estimates 2026, 30 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
Life expectancy at birth for 2026 is estimated at 65,5 years for males and 71,0 years for females. Appendix 2 of the same release gives the total as 68,3 years in 2026, and 68,1 years in 2025 (Stats SA, Mid-year population estimates 2026, 30 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The official presentation of that release states: Total Life Expectancy (LE) at birth increased from 55,7 years in 2002 to 68,3 years in 2026. (Stats SA presentation, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
1.7 years short of the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 70 − 68.3)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

GDP growth averaging 5.4% a year

Target
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) should increase by 2.7 times in real terms, requiring average annual GDP growth of 5.4 percent over the period. (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
The plan’s enabling milestones include: Raise per capita income from R50 000 in 2010 to R120 000 by 2030. The same list starts employment from 13 million in 2010. The 5.4% average annual GDP growth target is “over the period” from this 2010 base (National Development Plan 2030 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
The annual increase in real GDP of 0,6% in 2024; 0.7% in 2023 (Stats SA, GDP Q4 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Growth in 2024 was 4.8 percentage points below the 5.4% average the plan requires (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 5.4 − 0.6). The cumulative 2.7-fold path has not been computed.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Emissions of 350–420 Mt CO2-eq in 2026–2030

Target
2030 South Africa's annual GHG emissions will be in a range from 350-420 Mt CO2-eq. 2026-2030 (Updated first NDC, September 2021 (opens in a new tab), Table 2)
Baseline
The updated first NDC does not set a base year. Table 3 (information to facilitate clarity, transparency and understanding) states: This section is not applicable to the South African NDC, since the first NDC and this update, do not define mitigation targets in relation to a reference point, but as a fixed level GHG emissions range in 2025 and 2030. Therefore, each entry below is marked “Not applicable”. The row for a reference or base year is marked Not applicable, as above. (Updated first NDC, September 2021 (opens in a new tab), Table 3).
Latest
2022 inventory: 478,300 Gg CO2e excluding land use (LULUCF) and 435,120 Gg including it; the LULUCF sector was a sink of 43,181 Gg. On the NDC’s own accounting (including land use, excluding natural disturbances) the 2022 figure is 394.3 Mt (First Biennial Transparency Report, December 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. The target applies to 2026–2030; the 2022 NDC-basis figure of 394.3 Mt is inside the 350–420 Mt range.
Assessment by the tracking agency
South Africa is on track to meet its 2025 and 2030 NDC target. (BTR1, December 2024 (opens in a new tab))

17,742 MW wind and 8,288 MW solar PV by 2030

Target
The installed capacity mix for 2030 in the Integrated Resource Plan 2019, Table 5: wind 17,742 MW (22.53%) and solar PV 8,288 MW (10.52%) (IRP 2019 (opens in a new tab)). A planning mix, not a law.
Baseline
IRP 2019 Table 5, “Current Base”: solar PV 1,474 MW and wind 1,980 MW (IRP 2019, as gazetted 18 October 2019, Table 5 (opens in a new tab)). The same table states the 2030 installed totals already used as the target: solar PV 8,288 MW and wind 17,742 MW.
Latest
By the end of June 2025, the total grid-installed capacity of projects was 6 618 MW from 94 REI4P and three risk mitigation IPP power plants. Of this installed capacity, 6 587 MW was in commercial operation. (NERSA Renewable Energy Monitoring Report, Issue 26 (opens in a new tab)). The report gives no split by wind and solar capacity, and the total includes other technologies.
Distance
Not computed. The latest figure covers one procurement programme and all technologies, not total wind and solar capacity.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Eskom plant availability at 70%

Target
Eskom’s own target: stabilising generating plant availability at 70% over the medium term; its outlook lists MEDIUM TERM: FY2027 TO FY2031 Stabilise EAF at 70% (Eskom Integrated Report 2026 (opens in a new tab)). A company target, not a law.
Baseline
60.60% in the year to March 2025 (Eskom Integrated Report 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
65.16% Energy availability factor (2025: 60.60%) for the year to March 2026 (Eskom Integrated Report 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
4.8 percentage points below the target (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 70 − 65.16)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published. Eskom’s short-term aim is to Achieve EAF of 68% in FY2027 (Eskom (opens in a new tab)).

Non-revenue water 15% below business as usual by 2030

Target
Reduce Non Revenue Water (NRW) and water losses in all municipalities to 15% below the business as usual (1.1.1), with the Department of Water and Sanitation and CoGTA responsible and 2030 as the date (National Water and Sanitation Master Plan, 2019 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Non-revenue water levels in municipalities are estimated at an average of 41% (NWSMP, 2019 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
The Minister of Water and Sanitation told the National Assembly: The current national average NRW was 47.3% as at June 2024 which is equivalent to 2,121,347,794 kl/annum. (Minister of Water and Sanitation, written reply to question 2851, 15 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
The official national average at June 2024, 47.3%, is 6.3 percentage points above the 41% municipal average stated in the 2019 plan (Hawkeye’s arithmetic). The plan’s target is 15% below business as usual. This page does not restate that as a single official percentage, so the remaining distance to the 15%-below-BAU level is not computed here.
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published

Sources checked 26 and 31 October 2026. Quotations are given as published; Statistics South Africa writes decimals with a comma.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
