A Aspirations

The African Union speaks for the whole continent: its 55 member states cover every African country. The Aspirations below quote its long-term plan, Agenda 2063, and the plan for its second decade, in the Union’s own words.

The Africa We Want by 2063

Agenda 2063, adopted by the African Union in 2015

Agenda 2063 is the blueprint and master plan for transforming Africa into the global powerhouse of the future. Its seven aspirations (African Union, Agenda 2063 (opens in a new tab), African Union, Agenda 2063 overview (opens in a new tab)):

  1. A prosperous Africa based on inclusive growth and sustainable development
  2. An integrated continent, politically united and based on the ideals of Pan-Africanism and the vision of Africa’s Renaissance
  3. An Africa of good governance, democracy, respect for human rights, justice and the rule of law
  4. A peaceful and secure Africa
  5. An Africa with a strong cultural identity, common heritage, shared values and ethics
  6. An Africa, whose development is people-driven, relying on the potential of African people, especially its women and youth, and caring for children.
  7. Africa as a strong, united, resilient and influential global player and partner.

Seven Moonshots for 2033

Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan 2024–2033, launch version, February 2024

The plan turns each aspiration into a goal for 2033, which it calls a Moonshot. Among them (Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan (opens in a new tab)):

  1. Moonshot 1: Every AU Member State attains at least middle-income status.
  2. Moonshot 2: Africa is more integrated and connected.
  3. Moonshot 3: Public institutions are more responsive.
  4. Moonshot 4: Africa resolves conflicts amicably.
  5. Moonshot 6: African citizens are more empowered and more productive.
  6. Moonshot 7: Africa is a strong and influential global player.

The plan words Moonshot 5 in two slightly different ways, so it is left out of this list. For Moonshot 1, the World Bank writes: For the current 2027 fiscal year, low-income economies are defined as those with a GNI per capita, calculated using the World Bank Atlas method, of $1,175 or less in 2025 (World Bank income groups, FY2027 (opens in a new tab)). On that list, 21 of the 54 AU member states that the World Bank classifies are low-income economies (Hawkeye’s arithmetic from the World Bank country list). The Sahrawi Republic is not on the list.

How this SOAR analysis works

For a continent, the Aspirations are goal statements of the regional and treaty bodies its countries belong to, quoted word for word and labelled with each body’s members.

SOAR is built from other pages: Strengths and Opportunities link to the SWOT items, and Results are the matching rows of the GAP analysis. Only the Aspirations are new, and they are always quotations.

Official sources, and labelled analysis. Hawkeye reports official and authoritative sources, quoted or attributed, with a link. Documented goals and programmes appear as those bodies stated them. TOWS strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.
