Targets and latest values

Hunger down to at most 5% by 2033

Target
The African Union’s plan sets this target for 2033: Reduce the proportion of the population who suffer from hunger to at most 5% (Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan (opens in a new tab), Moonshot 1). The UN target for 2030 begins: By 2030, end hunger (UN global indicator framework (opens in a new tab), target 2.1).
Baseline
2023: 20.1% of people in Africa, according to official Table 2.1 of SOFI 2026 (prevalence of undernourishment). The same table gives 16.1% in 2015, 20.3% in 2024 and 20.0% in 2025 (the 2025 figure is marked as a projection based on a nowcast) (FAO, SOFI 2026, Table 2.1 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
20.0% in 2025, about 309 million people (FAO, on SOFI 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
15.0 percentage points to go by 2033 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
For the world as a whole, FAO says improvements are insufficient to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals by 2030 (FAO, on SOFI 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Electricity for everyone by 2030 (Sub-Saharan Africa)

Target
By 2030, ensure universal access to affordable, reliable and modern energy services (UN global indicator framework (opens in a new tab), target 7.1).
Baseline
565 million people without electricity in Sub-Saharan Africa in 2010 (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
563 million in 2024, 86% of the 655 million people in the world without electricity (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
563 million people still to connect by 2030.
Assessment by the tracking agency
progress in Sub-Saharan Africa has slowed significantly, and the pace of electrification must triple to achieve universal access by 2030 (Tracking SDG 7 press release, 24 June 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Clean cooking for everyone by 2030 (Sub-Saharan Africa)

Target
The same UN target 7.1, universal access to affordable, reliable and modern energy services (UN global indicator framework (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
Not given in this row (TBC).
Latest
About 22% of people in Sub-Saharan Africa had clean cooking in 2024, and about 970 million did not (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
About 78 percentage points, or about 970 million people (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
The report says the number without clean cooking may reach 1 billion by 2027 (Tracking SDG 7, 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Early warnings for everyone by the end of 2027

Target
Early Warnings for All aims to ensure that everyone on Earth is protected from hazardous weather, water, or climate events through life-saving early warning systems by the end of 2027 (WMO, Early Warnings for All (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
Not given in this row (TBC).
Latest
Only 40% of African countries have warning systems that cover several hazards (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
About 60% of countries still to cover, with about 13 months left (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Assessment by the tracking agency
WMO says Africa faces critical gap in early warning systems but is making progress (WMO, State of the Climate in Africa 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Intra-Africa trade to 30% by 2033

Target
Increase intra-Africa trade to 30% of total African trade (Agenda 2063 Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan (opens in a new tab), Moonshot 1, Strategic Objective 1.1).
Baseline
AU-STATAFRIC gives the same intra-export share in 2018 as in 2024, 16.9% of total exports (AU-STATAFRIC, African Trade Statistics Yearbook 2025 (opens in a new tab)). A 2023 baseline for total trade is not given in this row (TBC).
Latest
Intra-African exports were 16.9% of total African exports in 2024 (AU-STATAFRIC, African Trade Statistics Yearbook 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The African Union, writing about total trade, says: Intra-African trade now accounts for almost 16% of total trade, compared to less than 10% two decades ago. (African Union, 3 October 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
The plan’s 30% target is a share of total African trade. The yearbook’s 16.9% figure is a share of exports, so a distance in percentage points is not calculated from that figure alone.
Assessment by the tracking agency
AU-STATAFRIC says intra-African trade remains weak, averaging 11% of imports and 16% of exports over the period 2018–2024 (AU-STATAFRIC, African Trade Statistics Yearbook 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Sources checked 12 November 2026. Several sources cover only Sub-Saharan Africa, which leaves out North Africa. Each one says so.

How this GAP analysis works

For a continent, the targets are those set by the regional or treaty bodies that govern it, plus the global targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
