Ten of the eleven rows are targets of the European Union. The EU has 27 member states and 60.1% of Europe’s people (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)). Targets and latest values

EU net greenhouse gas emissions, 2030

Target
In order to reach the climate-neutrality objective set out in Article 2(1), the binding Union 2030 climate target shall be a domestic reduction of net greenhouse gas emissions (emissions after deduction of removals) by at least 55 % compared to 1990 levels by 2030. (European Climate Law, Article 4(1) (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
1990, the base year in the law
Latest
The EU reduced its net GHG emissions by 37.7% between 1990 and 2024 (Eurostat, SDG 13, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab)). This figure follows the scope of EU climate policy, which includes estimated emissions from international aviation and shipping.
Distance
17.3 percentage points short of the 55% cut (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 55 − 37.7)
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat: However, over the next six years GHG emissions will need to fall even faster for the EU to reach its net reduction target of 55% by 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 13, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))

EU net greenhouse gas emissions, 2040

Target
In order to reach the climate-neutrality objective set out in Article 2(1), the binding Union 2040 climate target shall be a reduction of net greenhouse gas emissions (emissions after deduction of removals) by 90 % compared to 1990 levels by 2040. (Regulation (EU) 2026/667, new Article 4(3) of the Climate Law (opens in a new tab)). From 2036 the law allows international credits of up to 5 % of 1990 Union net emissions, corresponding to a domestic reduction of net greenhouse gas emissions by 85 % compared to 1990 levels by 2040. The regulation entered into force on 7 April 2026.
Baseline
1990, the base year in the law
Latest
A 37.7% cut from 1990 to 2024 (Eurostat, SDG 13, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))
Distance
52.3 percentage points short of the 90% cut (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 90 − 37.7)
Assessment by the tracking agency
None published yet. Eurostat’s 2026 edition still describes the 2040 target as only provisionally agreed. It became law on 7 April 2026.

EU climate neutrality, 2050

Target
Union-wide greenhouse gas emissions and removals regulated in Union law shall be balanced within the Union at the latest by 2050, thus reducing emissions to net zero by that date, and the Union shall aim to achieve negative emissions thereafter. (European Climate Law, Article 2(1) (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Domestic net emissions, 1990: 4,608.5 Mt CO2-equivalent (Eurostat, env_air_gge (opens in a new tab), international transport excluded)
Latest
2024: 2,786.2 Mt CO2-equivalent (same source), 39.5% below 1990 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic)
Distance
2,786.2 Mt above net zero (Hawkeye’s arithmetic)
Assessment by the tracking agency
See the 2030 row. No separate assessment for 2050 was found.

EU carbon removals by land and forests, 2030

Target
The 2030 Union target for net greenhouse gas removals shall be 310 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent as a sum of the values of the greenhouse gas net emissions and removals by Member States in 2030 set out in column D of Annex IIa, and shall be based on the average of its greenhouse gas inventory data for the years 2016, 2017 and 2018 as submitted in 2020. (Regulation (EU) 2023/839, Article 1(4) (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
Net removals were 382.9 Mt CO2-equivalent at their peak in 2009 and 236.3 Mt in 2019 (Eurostat, sdg_13_21 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
In absolute numbers, net removals amounted to 231.0 million tonnes (Mt) of CO2-equivalent in 2024. (Eurostat, SDG 13, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))
Distance
79.0 Mt short of 310 Mt (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 310 − 231.0)
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat: Carbon removals in the EU have decreased in recent years and remain far from the target (Eurostat, SDG 13, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab), section heading)

EU renewable energy share, 2030

Target
Member States shall collectively ensure that the share of energy from renewable sources in the Union’s gross final consumption of energy in 2030 is at least 42,5 %. The next sentence adds an aim: Member States shall collectively endeavour to increase the share of energy from renewable sources in the Union’s gross final consumption of energy in 2030 to 45 %. (Directive (EU) 2023/2413, Article 1(2) (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
19.89% of gross final energy consumption (2019) (Eurostat, sdg_07_40 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
26.2% (2025, provisional). 25.24% in 2024 (Eurostat, sdg_07_40 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
16.3 percentage points short of 42.5% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 42.5 − 26.2), and 18.8 points short of the 45% aim
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat, on 2024 data: Nevertheless, even faster growth will be needed across the EU to meet the 42.5% target in 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 7, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))

EU energy consumption, 2030

Target
Member States shall collectively ensure a reduction of energy consumption of at least 11,7 % in 2030 compared to the projections of the 2020 EU Reference Scenario so that the Union’s final energy consumption amounts to no more than 763 Mtoe. A second limit is only indicative: Member States shall make efforts to collectively contribute to the indicative Union primary energy consumption target amounting to no more than 992,5 Mtoe in 2030. (Directive (EU) 2023/1791, Article 4(1) (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
2019: final energy consumption 968.7 Mtoe, primary energy consumption 1,353.2 Mtoe (Eurostat, sdg_07_10 and sdg_07_11 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
2024: final 900.4 Mtoe, primary 1,193.7 Mtoe (same source)
Distance
Final energy use is 137.4 Mtoe above the binding 763 Mtoe. Primary energy use is 201.2 Mtoe above the indicative 992.5 Mtoe (Hawkeye’s arithmetic)
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat: Thus, additional improvements in energy efficiency and consumption patterns are necessary to ensure the EU meets both of its 2030 energy consumption targets. (Eurostat, SDG 7, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))

EU employment rate, 2030

Target
At least 78% of the population aged 20 to 64 should be in employment by 2030 (European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan, COM(2021) 102 (opens in a new tab)). This is a headline target in a Commission action plan, not a law.
Baseline
72.7% (2019) (Eurostat, sdg_08_30 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
76.1% (2025). Men 80.9%, women 71.3% (Eurostat, sdg_08_30 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
1.9 percentage points short of 78% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 78 − 76.1)
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat: The EU is thus well placed to reach its target of 78% by 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 8, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))

People at risk of poverty or social exclusion in the EU, 2030

Target
The number of people at risk of poverty or social exclusion should be reduced by at least 15 million by 2030 (COM(2021) 102 (opens in a new tab)), compared with 2019. A Commission action plan, not a law.
Baseline
95.5 million people, 21.8% of the population (2019) (Eurostat, sdg_01_10 (opens in a new tab))
Latest
92.7 million, 20.9% (2025) (Eurostat, sdg_01_10 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
2.8 million fewer people since 2019, against a cut of 15 million. That leaves 12.2 million to go (Hawkeye’s arithmetic)
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat, on 2024 data: Thus, the EU is not on track to meet its target of lifting at least 15 million people out of poverty or social exclusion by 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 1, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))

Young people not in work, education or training in the EU, 2030

Target
The action plan aims to decrease the rate of young people neither in employment, nor in education or training (NEETs) aged 15-29 from 12.6% (2019) to 9%, namely by improving their employment prospects. (COM(2021) 102 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
12.8% (2019) in Eurostat’s current series (Eurostat, sdg_08_20 (opens in a new tab)). The plan’s 12.6% is the 2021 version of the same figure.
Latest
11.0% (2025). Men 10.0%, women 12.1% (Eurostat, sdg_08_20 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
2.0 percentage points above 9% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 11.0 − 9)
Assessment by the tracking agency
Eurostat: However, the rate of decline has slowed since 2023, and further efforts seem necessary for the EU to reach its NEET target of 9% by 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 8, 2026 edition (opens in a new tab))

EU nature restoration, 2030

Target
This Regulation establishes a framework within which Member States shall put in place effective and area-based restoration measures with the aim to jointly cover, as a Union target, throughout the areas and ecosystems within the scope of this Regulation, at least 20 % of land areas and at least 20 % of sea areas by 2030, and all ecosystems in need of restoration by 2050. (Nature Restoration Regulation, Article 1(2) (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
An assessment of the state of nature shows that 81% of habitats, 39% of birds and 62% of non-bird species of community interest are in a poor or bad state (EEA, Europe’s environment 2025 (opens in a new tab)). These assessments cover the reporting period 2013 to 2018.
Latest
No official measure of the area under restoration has been published. The Commission wrote on 1 September 2026: Member States submit their draft national restoration plans to the European Commission today (1 Sep), marking an important milestone in the implementation of the EU Nature Restoration Regulation. Over the coming months, the Commission, with support from the European Environment Agency, will assess the drafts and provide observations to Member States. Final national restoration plans are to be completed within one year. (European Commission, 1 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed. There is still no official latest value for the area under restoration.
Assessment by the tracking agency
EEA, written before those submissions: There is no information yet on which an assessment can be made as to whether the targets of the Nature Restoration Regulation are expected to be met, since the first national restoration plans will be submitted only in 2026. (EEA, Europe’s environment 2025 (opens in a new tab)). The Commission’s 1 September 2026 article does not yet assess whether the 2030 area targets will be met.

SDG targets on track in the UNECE region (Europe, North America and Central Asia)

Target
The 2030 Agenda targets. UNECE: Out of the total of 169 SDG targets, progress for the UNECE region can be measured towards 127 (UNECE, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Baseline
21 targets on track in the 2025 assessment, as implied by the 2026 report (below)
Latest
If the current path is followed, the UNECE region will achieve only 19 targets by 2030. This is 15 per cent of the assessed targets, and a decline by two targets compared to the estimation from last year. (UNECE, 2026 (opens in a new tab))
Distance
108 of the 127 measurable targets are not on track (Hawkeye’s arithmetic: 127 − 19)
Assessment by the tracking agency
UNECE: The current trend is in reverse for 26 targets (20 per cent), which is up by 6 targets compared to the estimation from last year. (UNECE, 2026 (opens in a new tab))

Sources checked 20 October and 12 November 2026.

How this GAP analysis works

For a continent, the targets are those set by the regional or treaty bodies that govern it, plus the global targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
