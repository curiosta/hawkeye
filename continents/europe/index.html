T

Threats

External · harmful

War and rearmament

  • Europe drove the world rise. The main contributor to the global increase in military spending in 2025 was a 14 per cent rise in Europe to $864 billion. Spending by Russia and Ukraine continued to grow in the fourth year of the war in Ukraine, while ongoing rearmament efforts by European NATO members led to the sharpest annual growth in spending in Central and Western Europe since the end of the cold war. (SIPRI, 27 April 2026 (opens in a new tab))
  • Three in ten dollars. The 40 states of the UN Europe region with data spent US$854.5 billion in 2025, 29.6% of the world’s US$2,887 billion (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)). SIPRI’s own Europe region, a different set of countries, gives US$864 billion.
  • Half the world’s nuclear warheads. Russia 5,420, France 370 and the UK 225: 6,015 in total, 49.4% of the world’s 12,187 in January 2026 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, SIPRI Yearbook 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Political

The fastest-warming continent

  • Twice the global pace. Since the 1980s, Europe has been warming twice as fast as the global average, making it the fastest-warming continent on Earth. (C3S and WMO, European State of the Climate 2025 (opens in a new tab))
  • Already above 2 °C. However, the average annual temperature over the European continent has already increased by 2.19–2.26 °C during this decade (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • The cost so far (EU-27). Over the period 1980 to 2024, weather- and climate-related losses accounted for a total of EUR 822.6 billion. Hydrological events such as floods were the major cause of climate-related losses in 2024, contributing to EUR 31.1 billion of the EUR 40.5 billion total for the year. (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Dependence on imported energy

  • More than half of EU energy is imported (EU-27). Despite continuous growth in renewable energy sources within the EU over the past decade, fuel imports from non-EU countries remained an important energy source for the EU in 2024, contributing to 57.3% of gross available energy (GAE) — as measured by net imports (imports minus exports). (Eurostat, SDG 7 (opens in a new tab))
  • Oil and gas most of all. Net imports were highest for oil and petroleum products (96.6% imported) and natural gas (85.0% imported), followed by solid fuels (predominantly coal, 34.2% imported). (Eurostat, SDG 7 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Economic

Refugees from wars nearby

  • Almost a third of the world’s refugees. Countries in the UN Europe region hosted 8,894,867 refugees at the end of 2025, 31.3% of the 28,461,306 under UNHCR’s mandate, and 10.2 million with asylum-seekers (Hawkeye’s arithmetic, UNHCR Refugee Data Finder (opens in a new tab)).
  • The largest hosts. Germany 2,655,191 refugees, Poland 990,469, France 788,419, the UK 581,072 and Spain 511,079 (UNHCR (opens in a new tab)). Türkiye, which hosts more than any of them, is counted in Asia.

O

Opportunities

External · helpful

Renewable energy keeps growing

  • A quarter of EU energy, nearly half its electricity. Renewable energy use in the EU has grown steadily overall, almost doubling from 13.9% of gross final energy consumption in 2009 to 25.2% in 2024. Reductions in costs due to economies of scale and greater competition, more efficient technologies, supply chain improvements and renewable energy support schemes have driven this growth. (Eurostat, SDG 7 (opens in a new tab)) (EU-27 figures; the EU is 60.1% of Europe’s population, Hawkeye’s arithmetic)
  • Electricity leads. The share of renewables in electricity generation experienced the most pronounced growth, reaching 47.5% in 2024. The shares of renewables in heating and cooling and in transport were lower, at 26.7% and 11.2% in 2024, respectively. (Eurostat, SDG 7 (opens in a new tab))
  • The 2030 target is still ahead. Nevertheless, even faster growth will be needed across the EU to meet the 42.5% target in 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 7 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Technological · GAP · EU renewables 2030

Green bonds and climate finance

  • Record corporate green-bond share. In 2024, green bonds made up 12.8% and 4.2% of total bond issuance by corporates and governments respectively, compared with 5.0% and 1.2% in 2019. While for corporates the share of green bonds in 2024 was the highest on record, for governments the share fell in 2024 compared with the peak of 6.1% in 2022. (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • EUR 31.7 billion of public climate finance. Total EU public finance contributions (including all 27 Member States as well as the EU institutions) increased from about EUR 12.9 billion in 2014 to EUR 31.7 billion in 2024. This equals roughly USD 34.3 billion contribution to the global target. (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • The largest contributor. In 2024, the EU, its Member States and the EIB together were the biggest contributors of public climate finance to developing countries worldwide. (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))

New trade agreements

  • Mercosur signed, interim agreement applying. On 9 January 2026, the Council adopted the two decisions authorising the signature of the EMPA and the iTA. On 17 January 2026, the European Union and Mercosur signed the EMPA and the iTA. The iTA provisionally applies as of 1 May 2026. (European Commission, EU-Mercosur agreement (opens in a new tab)). EMPA is the EU-Mercosur Partnership Agreement; iTA the interim Trade Agreement.
  • New Zealand in force. The EU-New Zealand trade agreement entered into force on 1 May 2024. (European Commission, EU trade relations with New Zealand (opens in a new tab))

A single rulebook for AI

  • The AI Act (EU policy, an internal enabler). Article 1(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689: The purpose of this Regulation is to improve the functioning of the internal market and promote the uptake of human-centric and trustworthy artificial intelligence (AI), while ensuring a high level of protection of health, safety, fundamental rights enshrined in the Charter, including democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection, against the harmful effects of AI systems in the Union and supporting innovation. (EU AI Act, Art. 1(1) (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Technological

Integrating capital markets

  • The Strategic Agenda 2024–2029 (EU policy, an internal enabler). To unlock the necessary investment potential, we will accelerate financial integration by achieving the Capital Markets Union and completing the Banking Union. We will create truly integrated European capital markets, which are accessible and attractive to all citizens and businesses and benefit all Member States. (European Council, EUCO 15/24 (opens in a new tab), annex, p. 20)
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

The oldest population of any continent

  • A median age of 42.8. Half of Europe’s people were older than 42.8 years in 2025, against 30.9 for the world. That is the highest of the UN’s continent groups (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Fewer births, a shrinking population. 1.41 births per woman and a population growth rate of −0.10% in 2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

Also in PESTLE · Social

Forests absorb less carbon (EU-27)

  • A shrinking land sink. Between 2009 and 2024, GHG net removals from land use and forestry fell by 39.7% in the EU. (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • Why. The strong decline in forest carbon sinks has been attributed to several trends, including slowdowns in net afforestation and forest biomass growth, and increases in tree mortality and timber harvesting (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • Far from the 2030 target. Net removals were 231.0 million tonnes of CO2-equivalent in 2024, against an EU target of at least 310 million tonnes by 2030 (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab)). The Europe GAP page tracks this target.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Research spending below target (EU-27)

  • Stuck near 2.2%. After prolonged stagnation between 2000 and 2007, the EU’s R&D intensity increased slowly, reaching 2.28% in 2020. It declined again slightly in the following years and stood at 2.24% in 2024. (Eurostat, SDG 9 (opens in a new tab))
  • The 3% ambition. With a gap of 0.76 percentage points, the EU remains far from its ambition of raising R&D expenditure to 3% of GDP by 2030. (Eurostat, SDG 9 (opens in a new tab))
  • Revised since. Eurostat’s database now estimates 2.26% for 2024 and 2.25% for 2023 (Eurostat, sdg_09_10 (opens in a new tab), updated 3 November 2026).

Also in PESTLE · Technological

Poverty and social exclusion (EU-27)

  • One in five. In 2024, 93.3 million persons, equalling 21.0% of the EU population, were at risk of poverty or social exclusion. (Eurostat, SDG 1 (opens in a new tab))
  • Latest. 92.7 million people, 20.9%, in 2025 (Eurostat, sdg_01_10 (opens in a new tab)). 2.8 million fewer than in 2019, against a target of 15 million fewer by 2030 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • More children at risk. The number of children (persons aged less than 18 years) who are at risk of poverty or social exclusion amounted to 19.5 million in 2024, corresponding to 24.2% of this age group. This is an increase compared with 2019, when 19.1 million children (23.7%) were at risk of poverty or social exclusion across the EU. (Eurostat, SDG 1 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Social

An investment gap (EU-27)

  • EUR 750 to 800 billion a year. The Draghi report, written for the European Commission, found that a minimum annual additional investment of EUR 750 to 800 billion is needed, corresponding to 4.4-4.7% of EU GDP in 2023 (Draghi report, Part A, 2024 (opens in a new tab), chapter 5). It is an independent report, not an official statistic.

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Long lives

  • 79.5 years. Life expectancy at birth in Europe was 79.5 years in 2025, six years above the world’s 73.5 (UN World Population Prospects 2024 (opens in a new tab), Hawkeye’s arithmetic).

A large economy with record employment (EU-27)

  • EUR 18.9 trillion. EU GDP was EUR 18,884.6 billion in 2025 at current prices (Eurostat, nama_10_gdp (opens in a new tab)).
  • Income has recovered. The economy rebounded from the recession as early as 2021, and real GDP per capita reached EUR 34 110 in 2025. Between 2019 and 2025, the EU’s real GDP per capita grew by 5.7%. (Eurostat, SDG 8 (opens in a new tab))
  • Record employment. In 2025, it reached a record high of 76.1%, which is a 3.4 percentage points increase since 2019 when it amounted to 72.7% and a 9.1 percentage point increase since 2010. (Eurostat, SDG 8 (opens in a new tab), the EU employment rate, ages 20 to 64)
  • Long-term unemployment at a low. In 2024, the rate reached the lowest value on record, at 1.9%, and remained at this level in 2025. (Eurostat, SDG 8 (opens in a new tab))

Also in PESTLE · Economic · PESTLE · Social

Emissions falling (EU-27)

  • Down by more than a third. The EU reduced its net GHG emissions by 37.7% between 1990 and 2024 (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • Faster in recent years. The rate of reduction has accelerated in recent years, with a particularly strong drop of 8.2% in 2023 followed by a more moderate 2.5% decline in 2024. Over the short-term period between 2019 and 2024, emissions thus fell by 15.8%. (Eurostat, SDG 13 (opens in a new tab))
  • Not yet enough for 2030. The EU target is a 55% cut by 2030. The Europe GAP page tracks it.
