T

Threats

External · harmful

The Russia–Ukraine war

  • Europe’s deadliest region in 2025. SIPRI reports: The security picture in Europe in 2025 was shaped primarily by the continuing Russia–Ukraine war, which meant that it was the region with the highest number of conflict-related fatalities. It estimates 78,350 conflict-related deaths in Ukraine in 2025, including Russian and North Korean personnel fighting there (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), ch. 3).
  • A divided continent. Russia’s annexation of Crimea and invasion of Ukraine marked the return of an East–West divide in Europe and the collapse of hopes for integrating Russia into a post–cold war European security order. (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), ch. 1).
  • Russian military spending. SIPRI estimates Russia’s military spending at US$190 billion in 2025, up 5.9% in real terms and 7.5% of its GDP (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab); a SIPRI estimate). Russia’s nuclear arsenal is compared with France’s under Strengths.
  • France’s response in law. The 2026 update of the military programming law sets the objective of raising the national defence effort de 2 % du produit intérieur brut en 2025 à 2,5 % au minimum en 2030, avec l'objectif d'atteindre 3,5 % à l'horizon 2035 (unofficial translation: “from 2% of gross domestic product in 2025 to at least 2.5% in 2030, with the objective of reaching 3.5% by 2035”) (Loi n° 2026-791 of 16 August 2026 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). The updated programme totals €435.7 billion for 2024–2030 (Loi n° 2023-703 (opens in a new tab), Art. 4 as amended).

Global warming: heat, heavy rain and drought

  • +4 °C in mainland France by 2100. Since January 2026 the reference warming trajectory for adaptation has been set in regulation: I. - Pour la France métropolitaine, réchauffement de 2 °C à l'horizon 2030, réchauffement de 2,7 °C à l'horizon 2050, réchauffement de 4 °C à l'horizon 2100. (unofficial translation: “I. For metropolitan France, warming of 2 °C by 2030, warming of 2.7 °C by 2050, warming of 4 °C by 2100.”) (Arrêté du 23 janvier 2026 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1).
  • Tied to world warming. Ces niveaux de réchauffement correspondent à un réchauffement mondial de 1,5 °C à l'horizon 2030, de 2 °C à l'horizon 2050 et de 3 °C à l'horizon 2100. (unofficial translation: “These levels of warming correspond to global warming of 1.5 °C by 2030, 2 °C by 2050 and 3 °C by 2100.”) (Arrêté du 23 janvier 2026 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). The trajectory depends on world emissions; France’s own share of world fossil CO2 is in the table below.
  • Extremes described as the new norm. The third national adaptation plan (PNACC-3), finalised on 10 March 2025 with 52 measures, opens: Records de chaleur, précipitations torrentielles, sécheresses dévastatrices : ces phénomènes ne sont malheureusement plus des exceptions. Ils sont notre nouvelle norme. (unofficial translation: “Heat records, torrential rain, devastating droughts: unfortunately these phenomena are no longer exceptions. They are our new norm.”) (PNACC-3 press kit, 10 March 2025 (opens in a new tab), foreword).
  • 2024 as an example. 2024 illustre clairement ce dérèglement du climat. C’est à la fois l’une des cinq années les plus chaudes depuis le début des mesures en 1900, l’une des dix années les plus pluvieuses à l’échelle nationale et l’une des moins ensoleillées depuis 30 ans. (unofficial translation: “2024 clearly illustrates this disruption of the climate. It is at once one of the five hottest years since measurements began in 1900, one of the ten wettest years nationally and one of the least sunny in 30 years.”) (PNACC-3 press kit (opens in a new tab), foreword).

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

Dependence on imported fossil fuels

  • Almost all fossil fuels imported. La production nationale primaire représente 63 % de l’approvisionnement en énergie du territoire en 2025. Si la France importe désormais la quasi-totalité des énergies fossiles qu’elle consomme et en a progressivement diversifié la provenance géographique, elle est exportatrice nette d’électricité. (unofficial translation: “Domestic primary production accounts for 63% of the country’s energy supply in 2025. While France now imports almost all the fossil fuels it consumes and has gradually diversified where they come from, it is a net exporter of electricity.”) (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 95). Fossil fuels were 40.2% of primary energy in 2025 (SDES, Bilan énergétique 2025 (opens in a new tab); provisional).
  • An energy bill of €45.8 billion. France’s energy bill atteint 45,8 Md€ en 2025 et est divisée par près de trois par rapport au record atteint en 2022, à la suite des tensions géopolitiques liées à la guerre en Ukraine. (unofficial translation: “reached €45.8 billion in 2025 and is almost three times lower than the record reached in 2022, after the geopolitical tensions linked to the war in Ukraine.”) (SDES (opens in a new tab), p. 23). This net bill includes the credit from electricity exports; RTE, using customs data, puts the cost of fossil-fuel imports alone at €53 billion in 2025 (RTE, Bilan électrique 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Where the imports come from. Les importations de gaz, notamment de gaz naturel liquéfié depuis les États-Unis ou la Russie, demeurent élevées. (unofficial translation: “Gas imports, notably of liquefied natural gas from the United States or Russia, remain high.”) Crude oil came mainly from the United States (22%), Kazakhstan (15%), Nigeria (12%) and Norway (10%) in 2025 (SDES, Bilan énergétique 2025 (opens in a new tab); provisional).

An EU excessive deficit procedure and rising interest costs

  • An EU procedure since July 2024. The Council of the EU decided: From an overall assessment it follows that an excessive deficit exists in France due to non-compliance with the deficit criterion. (Council of the EU, document ST 12166/24 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1; adopted on 26 July 2024). The European Commission lists the procedure as ongoing (European Commission (opens in a new tab)). The deadline set for correcting the deficit has not been confirmed at source for this page (TBC).
  • Interest costs rising with borrowing and rates. INSEE reports: La dépense d’intérêts continue d’augmenter fortement (+11,2 % après +13,9 % en 2024). En hausse de 6,5 Md€, elle s’établit à 2,2 % du PIB. Cette hausse est principalement portée par l’État (+6,8 Md€), en raison de l’accroissement du volume d’emprunt couplé à une hausse du taux d’intérêt moyen. (unofficial translation: “Interest spending continues to rise sharply (+11.2% after +13.9% in 2024). Up €6.5 billion, it stands at 2.2% of GDP. The increase is driven mainly by the State (+€6.8 billion), because of the growing volume of borrowing combined with a rise in the average interest rate.”) (INSEE, Informations rapides n° 78, 27 March 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The deficit and debt themselves are under Weaknesses.

Forced displacement abroad and asylum applications

  • 117.8 million people forcibly displaced worldwide. UNHCR counts 117.8 million forcibly displaced people at the end of 2025, of whom 68.7 million were internally displaced and 35.6 million were refugees and others in need of international protection (UNHCR Refugee Data Finder (opens in a new tab), Global Trends 2025 data). Whether this is a record has not been checked for this page (TBC).
  • 145,210 applications in France in 2025. Au 31 décembre 2025, l'Office français de protection des réfugiés et apatrides (Ofpra) a enregistré 145 210 demandes de protection internationale, dont 730 demandes de statut d’apatride. Cela représente une baisse de 5,5 % par rapport à 2024 (153 715 demandes). (unofficial translation: “At 31 December 2025, the French Office for the Protection of Refugees and Stateless Persons (Ofpra) had registered 145,210 applications for international protection, including 730 applications for stateless status. This is a fall of 5.5% compared with 2024 (153,715 applications).”) (OFPRA, 27 January 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The largest groups of applicants came from Afghanistan (13,800), the Democratic Republic of the Congo (13,240), Haiti (12,600) and Ukraine (12,310); OFPRA’s figures are provisional and include minors. Opportunities

External · helpful

Neighbouring countries’ demand for French electricity

  • A record 92.3 TWh of net exports. Le solde net de la France en 2025 s’est élevé à 92,3 TWh dans le sens des exportations. Il a été, pour la deuxième année consécutive, le plus élevé depuis le début des échanges entre la France et les autres pays européens, dépassant le solde de 2024 (89,0 TWh), dans un contexte globalement similaire. (unofficial translation: “France’s net balance in 2025 was 92.3 TWh in the direction of exports. For the second year running it was the highest since exchanges between France and other European countries began, exceeding the 2024 balance (89.0 TWh), in a broadly similar context.”) (RTE, Bilan électrique 2025: Échanges (opens in a new tab)).
  • Europe’s largest net exporter. La France est restée le premier exportateur net d’électricité d’Europe en volume, comme presque tous les ans au cours des trois dernières décennies (unofficial translation: “France remained Europe’s largest net exporter of electricity by volume, as in almost every year of the past three decades”) (RTE (opens in a new tab)). RTE puts exports at 17% of French production and their net value at €5.4 billion in 2025. SDES’s energy balance, on a different basis, gives 94 TWh (SDES (opens in a new tab)).
  • Demand from neighbours. Le solde annuel des échanges entre l’Allemagne et ses voisins est devenu importateur depuis 2023 (28 TWh dans le sens des importations en 2024, 22 TWh en 2025) (unofficial translation: “Germany’s annual balance of exchanges with its neighbours has been an import balance since 2023 (28 TWh of imports in 2024, 22 TWh in 2025)”). France’s net balances in 2025 were 26.2 TWh with Italy, 23.1 TWh with Germany and Belgium together, 22.6 TWh with Great Britain, 20.1 TWh with Switzerland and 0.2 TWh with Spain (RTE (opens in a new tab)).

Foreign investors and international visitors

  • 1,878 foreign investment decisions in 2025. En 2025, 1 878 décisions d’investissement étrangères ont été enregistrées, permettant la création ou le maintien de 47 734 emplois. (unofficial translation: “In 2025, 1,878 foreign investment decisions were recorded, creating or maintaining 47,734 jobs.”) (Business France, 29 January 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The counting method changed in 2025, so the total is not compared with earlier years.
  • Mostly from the EU. L’Union européenne demeure la principale source de ces investissements étrangers, avec 72% des projets et 56% des emplois. (unofficial translation: “The European Union remains the main source of this foreign investment, with 72% of projects and 56% of jobs.”) 25% des décisions recensées et 46% des emplois projetés concernent des priorités directement ciblées par ce plan stratégique, tels que l’intelligence artificielle, la santé, les mobilités décarbonées ou les énergies renouvelables. (unofficial translation: “25% of the decisions recorded and 46% of the planned jobs concern priorities directly targeted by this strategic plan, such as artificial intelligence, health, low-carbon mobility or renewable energy.”) (Business France (opens in a new tab)).
  • €77.5 billion from international visitors in 2025. The economy ministry reports that, en hausse de 9% par rapport à 2024, la France enregistre 77,5 milliards d'euros de recettes internationales, un record historique, ainsi qu'un solde positif de la balance des paiements de 20,1 milliards d' euros (unofficial translation: “up 9% on 2024, France recorded €77.5 billion of international receipts, an all-time record, and a positive balance-of-payments balance of €20.1 billion”), and En 2025, la France a accueilli 102 millions de touristes internationaux (contre 100 millions en 2024). (unofficial translation: “In 2025, France welcomed 102 million international tourists (against 100 million in 2024).”) (Ministère de l’Économie, 19 February 2026 (opens in a new tab), using Banque de France data).
  • “Most visited” is the ministry’s description. The ministry’s release calls France the most visited country in the world; an independent UN Tourism ranking has not been checked for this page (TBC). The same release gives Spain’s international tourism receipts as €105 billion, above France’s (Ministère de l’Économie (opens in a new tab)).

Foreign demand for arms

  • The second-largest arms exporter. Arms exports by France rose by 21 per cent between 2016–20 and 2021–25 and accounted for 9.8 per cent of the global total; SIPRI ranks France second among exporters for 2021–25 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), ch. 7).
  • Growing world demand. The volume of international transfers of major arms in the five-year period 2021–25 was the highest since the end of the cold war, having risen by 9.2 per cent compared with 2016–20. There are strong indications that demand for major arms is growing, which will probably lead to further increases in global arms flows in the coming years. Arms imports by states in Europe more than trebled between 2016–20 and 2021–25 (+210 per cent) (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), ch. 7).

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

Public deficit and debt, and a goods trade deficit

  • A deficit of 5.1% of GDP in 2025. En 2025, le déficit public s’établit à 152,5 Md€ après 169,1 Md€ en 2024, soit 5,1 % du produit intérieur brut, après 5,8 %. (unofficial translation: “In 2025, the public deficit was €152.5 billion after €169.1 billion in 2024, or 5.1% of gross domestic product, after 5.8%.”) (INSEE, Insee Première n° 2106, 29 May 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The EU procedure on the deficit and the rising cost of interest are under Threats.
  • Debt of 115.7% of GDP at the end of 2025. La dette publique s’établit à 115,7 % du PIB, après 112,6 % en 2024. (unofficial translation: “Public debt stands at 115.7% of GDP, after 112.6% in 2024.”) That is €3,460.5 billion (INSEE (opens in a new tab)). In 2025 France had the third-highest debt ratio in the EU27, after Greece and Italy, and the fourth-largest deficit, after Romania, Poland and Belgium (Eurostat, April 2026 notification (opens in a new tab)).
  • Latest quarter: 119.0% at the end of June 2026. Exprimée en pourcentage du produit intérieur brut (PIB), elle s’établit à 119,0 % du PIB, après 117,5 % au premier trimestre 2026. (unofficial translation: “Expressed as a percentage of gross domestic product (GDP), it stands at 119.0% of GDP, after 117.5% in the first quarter of 2026.”) Debt was €3,595.5 billion at the end of the second quarter of 2026 (INSEE, Informations rapides n° 239, 29 September 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • A goods trade deficit of €69.2 billion in 2025. Customs report that en 2025, le solde commercial FAB/FAB en valeur augmente de 10,0 milliards d’euros par rapport à 2024 et s’établit à - 69,2 milliards d’euros (unofficial translation: “in 2025, the FOB/FOB trade balance in value rose by €10.0 billion compared with 2024, to −€69.2 billion”), and that Malgré le redressement du solde commercial, celui-ci reste, en 2025, inférieur de 11,1 milliards d’euros à son niveau de 2019, principalement en raison de la dégradation du solde des produits manufacturés, en particulier des produits agroalimentaires, des matériels de transport, notamment des automobiles, et des produits informatiques, électroniques et optiques. (unofficial translation: “Despite the recovery of the trade balance, in 2025 it remains €11.1 billion below its 2019 level, mainly because of the deterioration in the balance of manufactured goods, in particular agri-food products, transport equipment, notably cars, and computer, electronic and optical products.”) (DGDDI, 9 February 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
  • Excluding energy and military equipment: −€46.4 billion. Hors énergie et matériel militaire, le solde se détériore de -3,1 Md€ pour s’établir à -46,4 Md€ (unofficial translation: “Excluding energy and military equipment, the balance worsened by €3.1 billion to −€46.4 billion”) (DG Trésor, 6 February 2026 (opens in a new tab)). France’s share of world goods exports was 2.6% in 2025, 0.4 points below 2019 (DGDDI, annual analysis 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Services showed a surplus of €55.5 billion and the current account a deficit of €13.1 billion, 0.4% of GDP (DG Trésor (opens in a new tab)).

Renewable energy behind France’s own targets

  • 24.0% in 2025 against a 33% goal for 2030. La part des énergies renouvelables, telle que calculée selon la méthodologie de la directive (UE) 2023/2414, dite directive RED III, atteint 24,0 % de la consommation finale brute d’énergie en 2025. (unofficial translation: “The share of renewable energy, calculated with the method of Directive (EU) 2023/2414, known as RED III, reaches 24.0% of gross final energy consumption in 2025.”) (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 81). The Energy Code sets the objective De porter la part des énergies renouvelables à 23 % de la consommation finale brute d'énergie en 2020 et à 33 % au moins de cette consommation en 2030 ; (unofficial translation: “To raise the share of renewable energy to 23% of gross final energy consumption in 2020 and to at least 33% of that consumption in 2030;”) (Code de l’énergie, art. L100-4, I, 4° (opens in a new tab)). The calculation method changed to RED III in 2025.
  • The only EU member state to miss its 2020 target. Eurostat: When looking at the national targets, 26 Member States met or exceeded them for 2020. France, by contrast, fell short of its target (-3.9 pp). (Eurostat, 19 January 2022 (opens in a new tab)). France reached 19.1% in 2020 against its 23% target (Eurostat, nrg_ind_ren (opens in a new tab)).
  • 14th of 27 in the EU. On Eurostat’s basis France was at 23.9% in 2025 (provisional), against 26.2% for the EU27, 14th of 27 (Eurostat (opens in a new tab)). This is about renewable energy across all uses; electricity, which is almost entirely low-carbon, is under Strengths.

Also in GAP · Renewable energy share

Research and development below the legal goal

  • 2.18% of GDP in 2024. En 2024, la dépense intérieure de R&D de la France s’établit à 64,1 milliards d’euros en valeur, soit une augmentation de 1,8 % en volume après une légère baisse en 2023 (-0,5 %). L’effort de recherche est de 2,18 % en 2024, stable par rapport à 2023. (unofficial translation: “In 2024, France’s domestic R&D expenditure was €64.1 billion in value, an increase of 1.8% in volume after a slight fall in 2023 (−0.5%). The research effort is 2.18% in 2024, unchanged from 2023.”) (MESR-SIES, Note Flash n° 16, July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). Business R&D was 1.43% of GDP and government R&D 0.75%.
  • A legal goal of at least 3%, with no year. The research programming law sets l'objectif de porter les dépenses intérieures de recherche et développement des administrations et des entreprises à au moins 3 % du produit intérieur brut annuel et les dépenses intérieures de recherche et développement des administrations à au moins 1 % du produit intérieur brut annuel au cours de la décennie suivante (unofficial translation: “the objective of raising domestic research and development expenditure by government and business to at least 3% of annual gross domestic product, and domestic research and development expenditure by government to at least 1% of annual gross domestic product, over the following decade”) (Loi n° 2020-1674 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). In 2024 France was 0.82 points below 3%, and government R&D 0.25 points below 1% (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
  • Below the EU average. In 2024 R&D was 2.18% of GDP in France, against 2.26% in the EU27 (estimate), 3.12% in Germany and 3.53% in Sweden (Eurostat, rd_e_gerdtot (opens in a new tab)).

Poverty at its highest level since the series began

  • 15.4% in 2024. En 2024, 9,8 millions de personnes occupant un logement ordinaire en France métropolitaine vivent sous le seuil de pauvreté monétaire (1 337 euros par mois). Le taux de pauvreté est stable par rapport à 2023 à 15,4 % et reste ainsi à son plus haut niveau depuis 1996, date de début de la série. (unofficial translation: “In 2024, 9.8 million people living in ordinary housing in metropolitan France were below the monetary poverty line (€1,337 a month). The poverty rate is unchanged from 2023 at 15.4% and so remains at its highest level since 1996, when the series began.”) (INSEE, Insee Première n° 2117, 9 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). The figures cover metropolitan France and ordinary housing only.
  • Inequality also at a series high. L’ indice de Gini augmente pour la deuxième année consécutive à 0,302 (+0,005 point) et atteint en 2024 un maximum depuis 1996 (figure 2). (unofficial translation: “The Gini index rose for the second year running to 0.302 (+0.005 points) and in 2024 reached its highest since 1996 (figure 2).”) (INSEE (opens in a new tab)).
  • Children and single-parent families. En 2024, le taux de pauvreté des familles monoparentales diminue de 0,3 point, à 34,0 %. (unofficial translation: “In 2024, the poverty rate of single-parent families fell by 0.3 points, to 34.0%.”) En 2024, 22,4 % des enfants vivent sous le seuil de pauvreté. (unofficial translation: “In 2024, 22.4% of children lived below the poverty line.”) The poverty rate was 13.8% in 2017 and 14.4% in 2022 (INSEE (opens in a new tab), figure 3). The EU measure of poverty or social exclusion is on the GAP page.

Strengths

Internal · helpful

Nuclear and low-carbon electricity

  • 68.1% of electricity from nuclear in 2025. Nuclear plants produced 373.0 TWh of a total of 547.5 TWh (RTE, Bilan électrique 2025 (opens in a new tab), p. 21, figure 2.2). Over the last ten years nuclear’s share has ranged between 63% and 77%.
  • A record for low-carbon power. Nuclear and renewable output reached in 2025 un maximum historique, soit 521,1 TWh. Cela représente 95,2 % de l’électricité produite sur le territoire métropolitain (unofficial translation: “2025 an all-time high of 521.1 TWh. That is 95.2% of the electricity produced in metropolitan France”) (RTE (opens in a new tab)). The 95.2% refers to metropolitan France. Net exports of this power are under Opportunities.

Also in PESTLE · Environmental

An independent nuclear deterrent

  • 370 nuclear warheads. SIPRI counts France’s total inventory at 370 warheads in January 2026, of which 290 are in the military stockpile and 280 are deployed. It is the fourth-largest inventory, after Russia (5,420), the United States (5,042) and China (620) (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab), table of world nuclear forces).
  • Defence policy founded on deterrence. La politique de défense de la France est fondée sur le socle de la dissuasion nucléaire, renouvelée dans la logique de la juste suffisance et de la crédibilité, et sur le modèle d'armée d'emploi qui assure des capacités d'intervention et de projection autonome face à une agression ou une menace d'agression sur ses intérêts nationaux et stratégiques. (unofficial translation: “France’s defence policy rests on nuclear deterrence, renewed according to the logic of strict sufficiency and credibility, and on an operational-army model that provides autonomous intervention and projection capabilities against aggression or threat of aggression to its national and strategic interests.”) (Loi n° 2023-703 (opens in a new tab), Art. 2, version in force from 19 August 2026).
  • Two deterrent components. La crédibilité opérationnelle est également illustrée par la disponibilité permanente de deux composantes aux caractéristiques très complémentaires : invulnérabilité, discrétion pour la Composante nucléaire océanique (CNO) et démonstrativité, réversibilité pour la Composante nucléaire aéroportée (CNA). (unofficial translation: “Operational credibility is also shown by the permanent availability of two components with highly complementary characteristics: invulnerability and discretion for the oceanic nuclear component (CNO), and demonstrability and reversibility for the airborne nuclear component (CNA).”) (Ministère des Armées, February 2026 (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 11).
  • Four ballistic-missile submarines. Elle repose sur une escadrille de 4 SNLE de la classe « Le Triomphant », basés à l’Île Longue, disposant de trois lots de 16 missiles M51, dont la portée est de l’ordre de 10 000 km. (unofficial translation: “It rests on a squadron of 4 ballistic-missile submarines of the Le Triomphant class, based at Île Longue, with three sets of 16 M51 missiles, whose range is about 10,000 km.”) La permanence à la mer d’au moins un SNLE garantit la possibilité d’effectuer une frappe « en second ». (unofficial translation: “Having at least one such submarine at sea at all times guarantees the ability to carry out a second strike.”) (Ministère des Armées (opens in a new tab), PDF p. 11).

Long lives, health-cost coverage and tertiary education

  • Very high human development. France’s HDI and life expectancy are in the key facts above. Among the 27 EU countries, France ranks 12th on the HDI but 3rd on life expectancy, after Italy and Spain (UNDP HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab), data year 2023; Hawkeye’s ranking).
  • Life expectancy in 2025. L’espérance de vie à la naissance augmente toutefois : elle s’établit à 85,9 ans pour les femmes et à 80,3 ans pour les hommes. (unofficial translation: “Life expectancy at birth nevertheless rises: it stands at 85.9 years for women and 80.3 years for men.”) (INSEE, Insee Première n° 2087, 13 January 2026 (opens in a new tab); provisional).
  • A legal right to health-cost coverage. Toute personne travaillant ou, lorsqu'elle n'exerce pas d'activité professionnelle, résidant en France de manière stable et régulière bénéficie, en cas de maladie ou de maternité, de la prise en charge de ses frais de santé dans les conditions fixées au présent livre. (unofficial translation: “Any person working or, when not in work, residing in France in a stable and lawful way has their health costs covered in case of illness or maternity under the conditions set out in this book.”) (Code de la sécurité sociale, art. L160-1 (opens in a new tab)).
  • 55.8% of 25–34-year-olds with a tertiary degree. In 2025, against 44.8% in the EU27; France ranks 6th of 27. The series has a break in 2024 (Eurostat, edat_lfse_03 (opens in a new tab)). The EU-level benchmark for 2030 is on the GAP page.

A large economy

  • GDP of €2,991.1 billion in 2025. Le PIB augmente de 0,8 % en 2025 en volume et atteint 2991,1 milliards d'euros. (unofficial translation: “GDP rose by 0.8% in volume in 2025 and reached €2,991.1 billion.”) (INSEE, Insee Première n° 2105, 29 May 2026 (opens in a new tab), figure 1). Growth has been revised to 1.6% for 2023 and 1.5% for 2024.
  • Seventh in the world, second in the EU. In current US dollars France’s GDP was the seventh largest in the world in 2025, after the United States, China, Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom and India (IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026 (opens in a new tab); the value is in the key facts above), and 15.8% of EU27 GDP, second after Germany (Eurostat, nama_10_gdp (opens in a new tab); 2025 provisional).
