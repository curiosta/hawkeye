Hawkeye analysis

Strategy cells on this page are Hawkeye’s own analysis. Each recommendation pairs a verified strength or weakness with a verified opportunity or threat, and compares it with a documented case from another place. The precedent facts are sourced. The pairing, the transfer and the recommendation are Hawkeye’s work, not official policy and not advice to any person or firm.

The documented responses listed lower on this page remain official programmes or instruments, each confirmed at their official source. Several of those responses already work in the same area as a recommendation. A recommendation is a pairing and a lesson from elsewhere. It is not a claim that France has no policy in that area. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.

The four cells

  • SO Responses that use strengths to pursue opportunities

    • Use low-carbon nuclear power to supply more electricity to neighbouring countries

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Nuclear and low-carbon electricity · SWOT · Opportunities · Neighbouring countries’ demand for French electricity

      Use France’s nuclear and low-carbon electricity to meet more demand from neighbouring countries, while keeping domestic security of supply. Official French programmes already include new-nuclear construction and the low-carbon strategy. This recommendation pairs that power strength with the export opportunity.

      Analogous case. Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (amended 11 November 2025), states that Norway had record power production of 158 TWh in 2024 and et eksportoverskudd (eksport minus import av kraft) på 18,4 TWh (an export surplus of 18.4 TWh). Vann- og vindkraft er dominerende i norsk kraftproduksjon, og står samlet for rundt 98 prosent av produksjonen. (Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. France already produced 373.0 TWh of nuclear electricity in 2025, 68.1% of 547.5 TWh, and neighbour demand is a live opportunity (live SWOT). Norway shows that a low-carbon system can run a large annual export surplus (18.4 TWh in 2024). The analogy is limited because Norway’s 2024 surplus also followed high reservoir inflows, and about 98% of Norwegian production is hydro and wind, not nuclear. France’s nuclear share has ranged between 63% and 77% over ten years, so annual export volume can swing with outages. Official DESNZ figures also show that the UK was a net importer from Europe in 2024, which is demand, not a guarantee of a lasting price or volume.

  • ST Responses that use strengths to reduce threats

    • Cut imported fossil-fuel use by electrifying transport on low-carbon nuclear power

      Pairs SWOT · Strengths · Nuclear and low-carbon electricity · SWOT · Threats · Dependence on imported fossil fuels

      Use France’s low-carbon electricity to electrify more road transport, so that imported oil and other fossil fuels can fall. Official French programmes already include the green industry law and the low-carbon strategy. This recommendation pairs that power strength with the import threat.

      Analogous case. The Norwegian Ministry of Transport, in “Norway is electric” (last updated 15 January 2025), states: At the end of 2024, electric vehicles (EVs) held an 88% market share in the market for new passenger cars in Norway, resulting in EVs making up 28% of the total passenger car stock. For new urban busses, the EV market share reached 72% by the end of 2024. The same page records a large fiscal cost for the incentives (Norwegian Ministry of Transport, 15 January 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (amended 11 November 2025), states: Energibruken i veitransport gikk ned 3,7 prosent i 2024 selv om de totale kjørelengdene for norsk-registrerte kjøretøy var omtrent uendret fra året før. Road-transport energy use fell 3.7% in 2024 even though total distances driven were about unchanged. Electric vehicles and assumed electric driving by plug-in hybrids stod for til sammen 29 prosent av de totale kjørelengdene i 2024, against 26% in 2023 and 2% in 2015 (Statistics Norway, 16 June 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. France already has a high nuclear share of electricity, and imported fossil fuels are a live threat (live SWOT). Norway shows that electric vehicles can take most new-car sales and go with a fall in road-energy use. The analogy is limited by Norway’s costly incentives and by France’s own lag in renewable-energy targets (a live weakness), which matters for total energy, not only electricity. Electricity share is not the share of all energy used in transport and heat.

  • WO Responses that use opportunities to reduce weaknesses

    • Close the gap on France’s own renewable-energy targets, using cases that already raised the electricity share

      Pairs SWOT · Weaknesses · Renewable energy behind France’s own targets · SWOT · Opportunities · Neighbouring countries’ demand for French electricity

      Raise the renewable-energy share toward France’s own targets, so that exportable low-carbon electricity is not only nuclear. Official French programmes already include the APER law on faster renewable projects. This recommendation pairs that live target gap with neighbour demand.

      Analogous case. Portugal, a smaller economy, raised the renewable share of electricity production while cutting energy import dependence. The Directorate-General for Energy and Geology (DGEG) states: A dependência energética em 2024 foi de 64,3%, tendo descido 2,4 p.p. face a 2023. Energy dependence in 2024 was 64.3%, 2.4 percentage points lower than in 2023. O contributo das fontes de energia renovável (%FER) no consumo final bruto de energia, em 2024, foi de 36,5%, sendo o contributo das fontes de energia renovável na produção de eletricidade (%FER-E), de 65,8%. The renewable share of electricity production was 65.8% in 2024. (DGEG, Balanço Energético Nacional 2024 (opens in a new tab), published 31 October 2025).

      Why it transfers, and the limit. France is behind its own renewable-energy targets, while neighbours demand French electricity (live SWOT). Portugal recorded a 65.8% renewable share of electricity production in 2024. The analogy is limited because Portugal’s figure is electricity production, not all final energy, and France’s live weakness is about renewable-energy targets that cover more than electricity. Portugal still had 64.3% energy import dependence in 2024. Nuclear remains France’s main low-carbon source, and a renewable rise will need grid space and sitting decisions that the official APER law already addresses.

Documented national responses 12

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • Military programming law 2024–2030

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2023-703 of 1 August 2023, as amended by Loi n° 2026-791 of 16 August 2026

    Art. 2: La politique de défense de la France est fondée sur le socle de la dissuasion nucléaire, renouvelée dans la logique de la juste suffisance et de la crédibilité, et sur le modèle d'armée d'emploi qui assure des capacités d'intervention et de projection autonome face à une agression ou une menace d'agression sur ses intérêts nationaux et stratégiques. (unofficial translation: “France’s defence policy rests on nuclear deterrence, renewed according to the logic of strict sufficiency and credibility, and on an operational-army model that provides autonomous intervention and projection capabilities against aggression or threat of aggression to its national and strategic interests.”) Art. 4: Le total du budget de la programmation militaire actualisée est de 435,7 milliards d'euros. (unofficial translation: “The total budget of the updated military programme is €435.7 billion.”) The 2023 text had set €413.3 billion (Loi n° 2023-703, consolidated (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · An independent nuclear deterrent

  • Update of the military programming law, 2026

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2026-791 of 16 August 2026 (Conseil constitutionnel decision 2026-907 DC: in conformity)

    Art. 1 sets the objective of raising the national defence effort de 2 % du produit intérieur brut en 2025 à 2,5 % au minimum en 2030, avec l'objectif d'atteindre 3,5 % à l'horizon 2035 (unofficial translation: “from 2% of gross domestic product in 2025 to at least 2.5% in 2030, with the objective of reaching 3.5% by 2035”). Art. 9: la France vise à consacrer 1,5 % de son produit intérieur brut à l'horizon 2035 à des dépenses permettant de renforcer la sécurité et de préserver les valeurs démocratiques. (unofficial translation: “France aims to devote 1.5% of its gross domestic product by 2035 to spending that strengthens security and preserves democratic values.”) (Loi n° 2026-791 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related GAP · Defence effort at least 2.5% of GDP in 2030 · SWOT · Threats · The Russia–Ukraine war

  • France 2030 investment plan

    Adopted by The President presented it on 12 October 2021; ongoing, reported in the annex to the 2027 draft budget

    The annex lists 10 objectives, the first being Faire émerger en France d’ici 2030 des réacteurs nucléaires de petite taille, innovants et avec une meilleure gestion des déchets (unofficial translation: “To develop in France by 2030 small, innovative nuclear reactors with better waste management”) (Annex to the 2027 draft budget (opens in a new tab), p. 17). In 2021 the Élysée described Un plan qui suit 10 objectifs pour mieux comprendre, mieux vivre, mieux produire en France à l'horizon 2030. (unofficial translation: “A plan following 10 objectives to understand better, live better and produce better in France by 2030.”) (Élysée, 12 October 2021 (opens in a new tab)). The 2023 press kit says 54 milliards d'euros sont investis sur cinq ans (unofficial translation: “€54 billion is invested over five years”) (info.gouv.fr, 11 December 2023 (opens in a new tab), p. 7). Spending to date is on the PESTLE page.

  • Faster renewable-energy projects (APER law)

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2023-175 of 10 March 2023, Art. 19, creating Energy Code art. L211-2-1

    Les projets d'installations de production d'énergies renouvelables au sens de l'article L. 211-2 du présent code ou de stockage d'énergie dans le système électrique, y compris leurs ouvrages de raccordement aux réseaux de transport et de distribution d'énergie, sont réputés répondre à une raison impérative d'intérêt public majeur, au sens du c du 4° du I de l'article L. 411-2 du code de l'environnement, dès lors qu'ils satisfont à des conditions définies par décret en Conseil d'Etat. (unofficial translation: “Renewable-energy production or electricity-storage projects, including their connections to transmission and distribution networks, are deemed to meet an imperative reason of overriding public interest, within the meaning of the Environment Code, where they meet conditions set by decree of the Conseil d’État.”) (Code de l’énergie, art. L211-2-1 (opens in a new tab); Loi n° 2023-175 (opens in a new tab)). Art. 15 sets up renewable-energy acceleration zones. The conditions decree has not been checked (TBC).

  • Faster construction of new nuclear reactors

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2023-491 of 22 June 2023 (some articles struck down by Conseil constitutionnel decision 2023-851 DC)

    Art. 1 removes the cap on nuclear’s share of electricity from the Energy Code. Art. 7 II: Le présent titre s'applique à la réalisation de réacteurs électronucléaires, y compris de petits réacteurs modulaires, dont l'implantation est envisagée à proximité immédiate ou à l'intérieur du périmètre d'une installation nucléaire de base existante [...] et pour lesquels la demande d'autorisation de création [...] est déposée au cours des vingt ans qui suivent la promulgation de la présente loi. (unofficial translation: “This title applies to building nuclear reactors, including small modular reactors, planned next to or within an existing basic nuclear installation [...] for which the application for authorisation [...] is filed within twenty years of this law’s promulgation.”) (Loi n° 2023-491 (opens in a new tab)). The number of new reactors is not set in this law.

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Nuclear and low-carbon electricity

  • Green industry law

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2023-973 of 23 October 2023

    Art. 2: Pour accélérer la transition écologique et la décarbonation de l'industrie, l'Etat élabore une stratégie nationale pour une industrie verte pour la période 2023-2030. (unofficial translation: “To accelerate the ecological transition and the decarbonisation of industry, the State draws up a national green-industry strategy for 2023–2030.”) (Loi n° 2023-973 (opens in a new tab)). Whether the strategy has been adopted has not been confirmed at source (TBC).

  • Third national low-carbon strategy (SNBC 3)

    Adopted by Government: décret n° 2026-636 of 16 July 2026, replacing the 2020 strategy (décret n° 2020-457)

    Art. 1: Les documents de la stratégie nationale bas-carbone dans sa troisième version sont adoptés (unofficial translation: “The documents of the national low-carbon strategy in its third version are adopted”) (Décret n° 2026-636 (opens in a new tab), read on the INERIS official-texts portal). Art. 2 sets carbon budgets, excluding carbon sinks, of 342, 262 and 194 Mt CO2e a year for 2024–28, 2029–33 and 2034–38. Its 2030 target and the Energy Code’s are compared on the GAP page.

    Related GAP · Greenhouse gas emissions 40% (law) or 50% (strategy) below 1990 by 2030

  • Climate and Resilience law: land take

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2021-1104 of 22 August 2021, art. 191

    Art. 191 sets the national objective of no net land artificialisation in 2050 and requires total land consumption in the ten years after promulgation (August 2021 to August 2031) to be less than half that of the previous ten years (Loi n° 2021-1104 (opens in a new tab); full text on the GAP page). The implementing rules as amended in 2023 have not been checked (TBC).

    Related GAP · Land take in 2021–2031 less than half that of 2011–2021

  • Third national climate adaptation plan (PNACC-3)

    Adopted by Government: presented in final form on 10 March 2025 (52 measures)

    The plan has five axes: protecting the population; the resilience of territories, infrastructure and essential services; adapting human activities; protecting natural and cultural heritage; and mobilising the nation (PNACC-3 press kit (opens in a new tab), p. 3). It is based on the reference warming trajectory of +3 °C worldwide and about +4 °C in mainland France in 2100 (Ministère de la Transition écologique (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Global warming: heat, heavy rain and drought

  • Reference warming trajectory made regulatory

    Adopted by Government: arrêté of 23 January 2026 (JORF of 25 January 2026), taken under décret n° 2026-23 (decree text not opened, TBC)

    Art. 1 sets warming levels for metropolitan France of 2 °C by 2030, 2.7 °C by 2050 and 4 °C by 2100, corresponding to world warming of 1.5 °C, 2 °C and 3 °C, with separate levels for each overseas territory (Arrêté du 23 janvier 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Threats · Global warming: heat, heavy rain and drought

  • National biodiversity strategy 2030 (SNB 2030)

    Adopted by Government: presented by the Prime Minister on 27 November 2023

    Measure 1: D'ici à 2030, 10 % du territoire national sera placé sous protection forte. (unofficial translation: “By 2030, 10% of the national territory will be placed under strong protection.”) (SNB 2030 press kit (opens in a new tab)). The 30% protection goal has not been confirmed in a French official text for this page (TBC).

  • Programming law for the Ministry of the Interior (LOPMI)

    Adopted by Parliament: Loi n° 2023-22 of 24 January 2023 (programming 2023–2027)

    Annexed report: Transformer l'institution pour être à la hauteur des attentes des citoyens : telle est l'ambition qui fédère l'ensemble des agents et forces du ministère de l'intérieur pour les cinq prochaines années. (unofficial translation: “Transforming the institution to meet citizens’ expectations: that is the ambition uniting all the ministry’s staff and forces for the next five years.”) le budget du ministère de l'intérieur sera augmenté de 15 milliards d'euros sur la période 2023-2027 pour la mise en œuvre de ces mesures nouvelles. (unofficial translation: “the ministry of the interior’s budget will be increased by €15 billion over 2023–2027 to carry out these new measures.”) (Loi n° 2023-22 (opens in a new tab)).

Sources checked 2026-11-12. French quotations are given as published; English translations are unofficial.

How this TOWS matrix works

Every documented national response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Those responses are not ranked. Strategy cells, where filled, are Hawkeye’s own analysis, based on analogous cases. They are not official policy and not advice.
