Each row sets a target from an official French text against the latest official figure. Read it with four labels in mind. Row 1 shows two 2030 climate targets: −40% in the Energy Code and −50% in the third national low-carbon strategy. Row 7’s target year (2025) has passed. Targets and latest values

Greenhouse gas emissions 40% (law) or 50% (strategy) below 1990 by 2030

Target
Law: De réduire les émissions de gaz à effet de serre de 40 % entre 1990 et 2030 et d'atteindre la neutralité carbone à l'horizon 2050 en divisant les émissions de gaz à effet de serre par un facteur supérieur à six entre 1990 et 2050. (unofficial translation: “To reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 40% between 1990 and 2030 and to reach carbon neutrality by 2050 by dividing greenhouse gas emissions by a factor of more than six between 1990 and 2050.”) (Code de l’énergie, art. L100-4, I (opens in a new tab), 1°). Strategy: Réduire de 50 % nos émissions sur le territoire français d'ici 2030, par rapport à 1990. (unofficial translation: “Reduce our emissions on French territory by 50% by 2030, compared with 1990.”) (SNBC 3 (opens in a new tab), adopted by décret n° 2026-636 (opens in a new tab) of 16 July 2026).
Baseline
1990: 546.9 million tonnes CO2 equivalent, excluding carbon sinks (Citepa, Secten 2026 long series (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2024: Hors puits de carbone, les émissions de GES s’élèvent à 367 Mt CO 2 e en 2024 (unofficial translation: “Excluding carbon sinks, GHG emissions amount to 367 Mt CO2e in 2024”) (Citepa, Secten 2026 (opens in a new tab)), 32.9% below 1990. First estimate for 2025: 359 Mt, 34.3% below 1990 (Citepa (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
2024: 31.3 Mt above the −40% level (328.1 Mt) and 85.9 Mt above the −50% level (273.5 Mt).
Assessment by the tracking agency
The High Council on Climate: Par rapport à la baisse pré-estimée en 2025, pour respecter le 3e budget carbone (2024-2028), le rythme doit s'accéler et au moins doubler, pour atteindre en moyenne plus de 4 %/an en 2026, 2027 et 2028. (unofficial translation: “Compared with the estimated fall in 2025, to meet the third carbon budget (2024–2028) the pace must accelerate and at least double, to reach an average of more than 4% a year in 2026, 2027 and 2028.”) (HCC, Rapport annuel 2026 (opens in a new tab), ch. 3; the misspelling is in the source). Citepa: Toutefois avec un budget défini à 342 Mt CO 2 e/an pour la période 2024-2028, le rythme de réduction doit s'accélérer pour les années à venir avec des baisses annuelles de 5 % à 6 % attendues à partir de 2026. (unofficial translation: “However, with a budget set at 342 Mt CO2e a year for 2024–2028, the pace of reduction must accelerate in the coming years, with annual falls of 5% to 6% expected from 2026.”) (Citepa (opens in a new tab)).

Final energy consumption 20% below 2012 by 2030

Target
De réduire la consommation énergétique finale de 50 % en 2050 par rapport à la référence 2012, en visant les objectifs intermédiaires d'environ 7 % en 2023 et de 20 % en 2030. (unofficial translation: “To reduce final energy consumption by 50% in 2050 compared with the 2012 reference, aiming at intermediate objectives of about 7% in 2023 and 20% in 2030.”) (Code de l’énergie, art. L100-4, I (opens in a new tab), 2°). The energy programming decree (PPE3, décret n° 2026-76 (opens in a new tab)) sets −29% for 2030 (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 49).
Baseline
2012, the reference year in the law.
Latest
2025: la baisse réalisée atteint 9,8 % en 2025 (unofficial translation: “the fall achieved reaches 9.8% in 2025”) (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 49; mainland France, climate-corrected, non-energy uses excluded).
Distance
10.2 percentage points short of −20%, and 19.2 points short of the PPE3’s −29%. Whether the 2023 intermediate objective was met has not been confirmed at source (TBC).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Renewables at least 33% of gross final energy consumption by 2030

Target
De porter la part des énergies renouvelables à 23 % de la consommation finale brute d'énergie en 2020 et à 33 % au moins de cette consommation en 2030 ; (unofficial translation: “To raise the share of renewable energy to 23% of gross final energy consumption in 2020 and to at least 33% of that consumption in 2030;”) (Code de l’énergie, art. L100-4, I (opens in a new tab), 4°).
Baseline
2005: 9.3%; 2020: 19.1%, against the 23% set for 2020 (Eurostat, nrg_ind_ren (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2025: 24.0% (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 81, RED III method); 23.9% on Eurostat’s basis (provisional).
Distance
9.0 percentage points below 33% (SDES basis). The calculation method changed in 2025.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Fossil primary energy 40% below 2012 by 2030

Target
De réduire la consommation énergétique primaire des énergies fossiles de 40 % en 2030 par rapport à l'année de référence 2012, en modulant cet objectif par énergie fossile en fonction du facteur d'émissions de gaz à effet de serre de chacune. (unofficial translation: “To reduce primary energy consumption of fossil fuels by 40% in 2030 compared with the 2012 reference year, modulating this objective by fossil fuel according to the greenhouse gas emission factor of each.”) (Code de l’énergie, art. L100-4, I (opens in a new tab), 3°). PPE3 sets 2030 targets per fuel against 2012: gas −36%, oil −42%, coal −84% (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 49).
Baseline
2012, the reference year in the law.
Latest
2025, fall since 2012 (SDES, Chiffres clés de l’énergie 2026 (opens in a new tab), p. 49): gas 24.9%, oil 24.6%, coal 67.7%. The combined fall for all fossil fuels, the measure in the law, has not been confirmed at source (TBC).
Distance
Per fuel, to the PPE3 targets: gas 11.1, oil 17.4 and coal 16.3 percentage points. Combined gap TBC.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Land take in 2021–2031 less than half that of 2011–2021

Target
Afin d'atteindre l'objectif national d'absence de toute artificialisation nette des sols en 2050, le rythme de l'artificialisation des sols dans les dix années suivant la promulgation de la présente loi doit être tel que, sur cette période, la consommation totale d'espace observée à l'échelle nationale soit inférieure à la moitié de celle observée sur les dix années précédant cette date. (unofficial translation: “To reach the national objective of no net land artificialisation in 2050, the pace of land artificialisation in the ten years after this law is promulgated must be such that, over that period, total land consumption observed nationally is less than half that observed over the ten years before that date.”) (Loi n° 2021-1104 (opens in a new tab), art. 191; promulgated 22 August 2021).
Baseline
1 January 2011 to 1 January 2021: 226,734 hectares of natural, agricultural and forest land used, so half is 113,367 hectares (Hawkeye’s sum of the commune file, Cerema data (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
1 January 2021 to 1 January 2025: 72,658 hectares (Hawkeye’s sum, Cerema data (opens in a new tab)). L'année 2024 constitue le niveau calculé le plus bas jamais atteint depuis 2011 avec 15 119 ha d'ENAF consommés sur le territoire national. (unofficial translation: “2024 is the lowest level calculated since 2011, with 15,119 ha of natural, agricultural and forest land consumed nationally.”) (Portail de l’artificialisation, 30 June 2026 (opens in a new tab); 2024 is provisional).
Distance
64.1% of the half-allowance used in four of the ten years (calendar-year approximation; the law’s period starts on 22 August 2021).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Defence effort at least 2.5% of GDP in 2030

Target
de 2 % du produit intérieur brut en 2025 à 2,5 % au minimum en 2030, avec l'objectif d'atteindre 3,5 % à l'horizon 2035 (unofficial translation: “from 2% of gross domestic product in 2025 to at least 2.5% in 2030, with the objective of reaching 3.5% by 2035”) (Loi n° 2026-791 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). The 2023 law’s step of 2% between 2025 and 2027 remains in its Art. 3 (Loi n° 2023-703 (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
2017: 1.78% of GDP (NATO core defence expenditure, NATO (opens in a new tab)); 1.85% (SIPRI (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
NATO core defence expenditure: 2.04% of GDP in 2024 and 2025 (estimate), 2.22% in 2026 (estimate) (NATO, Defence Investment 2026 (opens in a new tab), Table 3). SIPRI: 2.03% in 2025, US$68.0 billion (SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (opens in a new tab)). The two measures and the law’s “effort national de défense” are defined differently.
Distance
The 2% step was met in 2025 on both measures. To 2.5% in 2030: 0.28 points from NATO’s 2026 estimate; 0.47 points from SIPRI’s 2025 figure.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Development aid 0.7% of gross national income in 2025 (target year passed)

Target
La France consacrera 0,55 % de son revenu national brut à l'aide publique au développement en 2022. Elle s'efforcera d'atteindre 0,7 % du revenu national brut en 2025 (unofficial translation: “France will devote 0.55% of its gross national income to official development assistance in 2022. It will strive to reach 0.7% of gross national income in 2025”) (Loi n° 2021-1031 (opens in a new tab), art. 2, III, 2.). The law set indicative steps of 0.61% (2023) and 0.66% (2024). Whether a newer national target exists for after 2025 has not been confirmed at source (TBC).
Baseline
2022: the 0.55% target was met: France met its domestic commitment to achieve a 0.55% ODA/GNI ratio in 2022 (OECD (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
France provided USD 14.5 billion (preliminary data) of ODA in 2025, 0.42% of GNI; 0.49% in 2023 and 0.48% in 2024 (OECD, Development Co-operation Profiles: France (opens in a new tab), 16 June 2026).
Distance
2025: 0.28 percentage points short of 0.7%. Also below the 2023 and 2024 steps.
Assessment by the tracking agency
OECD: France voted further cuts in its 2025 budget putting off track its efforts towards its international and European Union (EU) commitments to achieve a 0.7% ODA/GNI ratio by 2030. (OECD (opens in a new tab)).

1.1 million fewer people at risk of poverty or social exclusion by 2030 (France’s EU-pillar target)

Target
France’s national 2030 target under the European Pillar of Social Rights: −1,100 thousand people at risk of poverty or social exclusion, against 2019 (European Commission, State of play on the national targets for 2030 (opens in a new tab), annex as of 16 June 2022). The EU-wide target is at least 15 million fewer people at risk of poverty or social exclusion.
Baseline
2019: 11,716,000 people, 18.8% of the population (Eurostat, ilc_peps01n (opens in a new tab)). Target level: 10,616,000 (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Latest
2025: 13,863,000 people, 20.8% (Eurostat (opens in a new tab)). The series has breaks in 2020 and 2022.
Distance
2025: 2,147,000 more than in 2019 and 3,247,000 above the target level (series breaks apply).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

At least 45% of 25–34-year-olds with a tertiary degree by 2030 (EU-level benchmark)

Target
The share of 25-34 year-olds with tertiary educational attainment should be at least 45%, by 2030. (Eurostat, policy context (opens in a new tab), reproducing Council Resolution 2021/C 66/01). This is an EU-level benchmark, not French law.
Baseline
2012: 42.6% (Eurostat, edat_lfse_03 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2025: 55.8%, against 44.8% for the EU27 (Eurostat (opens in a new tab)). The series has a break in 2024.
Distance
10.8 percentage points above the benchmark.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

R&D at least 3% of GDP, government at least 1% (no year set)

Target
avec l'objectif de porter les dépenses intérieures de recherche et développement des administrations et des entreprises à au moins 3 % du produit intérieur brut annuel et les dépenses intérieures de recherche et développement des administrations à au moins 1 % du produit intérieur brut annuel au cours de la décennie suivante (unofficial translation: “with the objective of raising domestic research and development expenditure by government and business to at least 3% of annual gross domestic product, and domestic research and development expenditure by government to at least 1% of annual gross domestic product, over the following decade”) (Loi n° 2020-1674 (opens in a new tab), Art. 1). The law sets no year.
Baseline
2016: 2.22% of GDP, of which government 0.78% (annexed report of Loi n° 2020-1674 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2024: 2.18%, of which government 0.75% (MESR-SIES, Note Flash n° 16 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
0.82 percentage points below 3%; government R&D 0.25 points below 1%.

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
