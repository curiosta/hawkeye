Documented national responses 11

Listed in no particular order. None is ranked.

  • Strategic Defence Review 2025 and Defence Investment Plan 2026

    Adopted by Ministry of Defence (review published 2 June 2025) and Ministry of Defence with HM Treasury (plan published 30 June 2026); both under the 2024–2026 government

    Defence Investment Plan: From 27-28 onwards, the UK will spend 2.7% of GDP on core NATO defence spending, solidifying its position as the NATO Alliance’s third-largest cash spender, behind only the US and Germany. Alongside NATO allies, the UK has committed to reach 3.5% of GDP on defence spending by 2035. It puts the total MOD budget for 2026–27 to 2029–30 at £297.7 billion (DIP funding explainer (opens in a new tab)). Earlier, the 2025 review’s introduction by the then Prime Minister said: We are delivering our commitment to spend 2.5% of GDP on defence, accelerating it to 2027, and we have set the ambition to reach 3% in the next Parliament, subject to economic and fiscal conditions. (Strategic Defence Review 2025 (opens in a new tab), PDF p.5).

    Related GAP · Defence spending 2.7% of GDP from 2027–28 and 3.5% by 2035 · SWOT · Threats · Russia’s war and threats to the UK · SWOT · Strengths · A continuous nuclear deterrent and large defence budget

  • Climate Change Act 2008: net zero by 2050

    Adopted by Parliament: Climate Change Act 2008 (c. 27), Royal Assent 26 November 2008; the 2050 target was raised to 100% by SI 2019/1056, in force since 27 June 2019

    s.1(1): It is the duty of the Secretary of State to ensure that the net UK carbon account for the year 2050 is at least 100% lower than the 1990 baseline. (Climate Change Act 2008, s.1 (opens in a new tab)).

  • Sixth and seventh carbon budgets

    Adopted by Secretary of State by order, each draft approved by both Houses: SI 2021/750 (in force 24 June 2021) and SI 2026/695 (in force 26 June 2026)

    The carbon budget for the 2033-2037 budgetary period is 965,000,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Carbon Budget Order 2021 (opens in a new tab), art. 2). The carbon budget for the 2038-2042 budgetary period is 535,000,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Carbon Budget Order 2026 (opens in a new tab), art. 2).

    Related GAP · Sixth and seventh carbon budgets (2033–2037 and 2038–2042)

  • Energy Act 2023

    Adopted by Parliament: Energy Act 2023 (c. 52), Royal Assent 26 October 2023; commenced in stages

    Scope, from the long title: An Act to make provision about energy production and security and the regulation of the energy market, including provision about the licensing of carbon dioxide transport and storage; about commercial arrangements for carbon capture and storage and for hydrogen production and transportation; about new technology, including low-carbon heat schemes and hydrogen grid trials; about the Independent System Operator and Planner; and other matters (Energy Act 2023 (opens in a new tab)). Commencement by part has not been checked (TBC).

  • Great British Energy

    Adopted by Parliament: Great British Energy Act 2025 (c. 16), Royal Assent 15 May 2025, in force the same day

    GOV.UK: Great British Energy will be a new, publicly owned, clean energy company. (GOV.UK, Great British Energy (opens in a new tab)). Its statutory objects, s.3(2), are restricted to facilitating, encouraging and participating in— (a) the production, distribution, storage and supply of clean energy, (b) the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions from energy produced from fossil fuels, (c) improvements in energy efficiency, (d) measures for ensuring the security of the supply of energy, and measures against slavery and human trafficking in its business (Great British Energy Act 2025, s.3 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Strengths · Low-carbon power and Europe’s largest offshore wind fleet

  • The UK’s Modern Industrial Strategy

    Adopted by Department for Business and Trade: CP 1451, published 23 June 2025 (after the Invest 2035 Green Paper of October 2024); published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government

    Our modern Industrial Strategy provides a new approach for a new era. It is robust, strategic, and unashamedly long-term: a 10-year plan to make the UK the best country to invest in anywhere in the world. (PDF p.8). It backs eight sectors: backing eight sectors (the IS-8) with the highest potential (Industrial Strategy, CP 1451 (opens in a new tab), PDF p.11). The Green Paper was a consultation that ran from 11 October to 25 November 2024 (GOV.UK, Invest 2035 (opens in a new tab)).

    Related GAP · Public R&D investment of £22.6 billion by 2029/30 (partial) · SWOT · Opportunities · Inward investment in artificial intelligence

  • National Wealth Fund

    Adopted by HM Treasury: the UK Infrastructure Bank has operated as the National Wealth Fund since 14 October 2024; published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government

    The National Wealth Fund will catalyse tens of billions of pounds of private investment into in the UK’s clean energy and growth industries, including green hydrogen, carbon capture and gigafactories. (the “into in” is in the source). The National Wealth Fund will have a total of £27.8 billion (HM Treasury, 14 October 2024 (opens in a new tab)). Whether legislation for a broader mandate has passed has not been checked (TBC).

  • Environment Act 2021: species abundance target

    Adopted by Parliament: Environment Act 2021 (c. 30), Royal Assent 9 November 2021; target set by SI 2023/91 (England)

    s.3(1): The Secretary of State must by regulations set a target (the “species abundance target”) in respect of a matter relating to the abundance of species. (Environment Act 2021, s.3 (opens in a new tab)). The regulations set it for England: The 2030 species abundance target is that the overall relative species abundance index on the specified date indicates that the decline in the abundance of species has been halted. (SI 2023/91, reg. 11(1) (opens in a new tab)).

    Related GAP · Halt the decline in species abundance by 2030 (England) · SWOT · Weaknesses · Declining wildlife and waterbody health

  • AI Security Institute

    Adopted by Government body, part of the Cabinet Office (formerly the AI Safety Institute)

    The AI Security Institute’s mission is to equip governments with a scientific understanding of the risks posed by advanced AI. We conduct research and develop and test mitigations. (GOV.UK, AI Security Institute (opens in a new tab)). An institute rather than a law or plan; the date of the change of name has not been checked (TBC).

    Related SWOT · Opportunities · Inward investment in artificial intelligence · SWOT · Threats · Cyber attacks by states and criminals

  • NHS Long Term Workforce Plan (England)

    Adopted by NHS England: published 30 June 2023; a plan, not a statute. A 10 Year Workforce Plan was pending on 19 October 2026 (TBC)

    Those actions fall into three clear priority areas: train, retain and reform (PDF p.5). It commits to Double the number of medical school training places, taking the total number of places up to 15,000 a year by 2031/32 (PDF p.7). This Plan sets out modelling of NHS workforce demand and supply over a 15-year period and the resulting shortfall. (NHS England, Long Term Workforce Plan (opens in a new tab)).

    Related SWOT · Weaknesses · Hospital waiting times (England)

  • Planning and Infrastructure Act 2025

    Adopted by Parliament: Planning and Infrastructure Act 2025 (c. 34), Royal Assent 18 December 2025; commencement began with SI 2025/1370

    Scope, from the long title: An Act to make provision about infrastructure; to make provision about town and country planning; to make provision for a scheme, administered by Natural England, for a nature restoration levy payable by developers; to make provision about development corporations; to make provision about the compulsory purchase of land; to make provision about environmental outcomes reports; and for connected purposes. (Planning and Infrastructure Act 2025 (opens in a new tab)). Its operative provisions have not been read for this page.

Sources checked 20 October 2026. Quotations are given as published. Documents of the 2024–2026 government are labelled as GOV.UK labels them; Andy Burnham has been Prime Minister since 20 July 2026.

How this TOWS matrix works

For a country, the responses are documented national laws, decrees, plans and programmes, each named with the body that adopted it.

Every response is attributed to the body behind it and links to its official text. Placing a response in a cell is Hawkeye’s only editorial step. Responses are not ranked.
