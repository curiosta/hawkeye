Each row sets a target from an official UK text against the latest official figure. Read it with four labels in mind. Several targets come from documents of the 2024–2026 government (the Plan for Change, the Child Poverty Strategy, the Industrial Strategy and the Post-16 white paper), which GOV.UK labels as published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government; Andy Burnham has been Prime Minister since 20 July 2026. Rows on species, hospital waits and higher-level learning cover England only. The child-poverty row is a modelled reduction, not a rate target, and the R&D row is partial. Distances are Hawkeye’s arithmetic where target and latest use the same measure; where a figure has not been confirmed at source, the cell says TBC.

Targets and latest values

Greenhouse gas emissions at least 68% below 1990 by 2030 (NDC)

Target
In its NDC the UK commits to reducing economy-wide greenhouse gas emissions by at least 68% by 2030 compared to 1990 levels. (UK 2030 Nationally Determined Contribution (opens in a new tab), updated September 2022). The base year is 1995 for fluorinated gases; international aviation and shipping are excluded.
Baseline
1990: 790.8 MtCO2e (DESNZ, Provisional UK greenhouse gas emissions statistics 2025 (opens in a new tab), Table 1a).
Latest
2025 (provisional): 366.6 MtCO2e, territorial, excluding international aviation and shipping. DESNZ writes: In 2025, total UK net territorial greenhouse gas emissions are provisionally estimated to be 367 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (MtCO2e), a decrease of 2% (7 MtCO2e) from 2024 and 54% (424 MtCO2e) from 1990. (DESNZ, Provisional UK greenhouse gas emissions statistics 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
2025: 53.6% below 1990 against at least 68%, so 14.4 percentage points short. On the DESNZ basis, 68% below 1990 is 253.1 Mt, so 2025 is 113.5 Mt above it. Approximate, because the NDC uses 1995 for fluorinated gases (TBC).
Assessment by the tracking agency
The Climate Change Committee: The CBGDP reaches a reduction versus the 1990 baseline of 65% by 2030, compared to the target of 68%. This leaves a gap of 26 MtCO2e of further emissions reductions required to achieve the 2030 NDC. (CBGDP is the government’s Carbon Budget and Growth Delivery Plan pathway; CCC, Progress report 2026 (opens in a new tab), 24 June 2026.)

Greenhouse gas emissions at least 81% below the base year by 2035 (NDC)

Target
The target year level is an at least 81% reduction in total net GHG emissions and removals in 2035 compared to the base year. (UK 2035 Nationally Determined Contribution (opens in a new tab), 30 January 2025).
Baseline
1990: 790.8 MtCO2e (DESNZ, Provisional UK greenhouse gas emissions statistics 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2025 (provisional): 366.6 MtCO2e, territorial, excluding international aviation and shipping. DESNZ writes: In 2025, total UK net territorial greenhouse gas emissions are provisionally estimated to be 367 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (MtCO2e), a decrease of 2% (7 MtCO2e) from 2024 and 54% (424 MtCO2e) from 1990. (DESNZ, Provisional UK greenhouse gas emissions statistics 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
27.4 percentage points short (DESNZ basis, approximate).
Assessment by the tracking agency
The Climate Change Committee notes that, after adjustments to the latest emissions inventory, the government’s pathway narrowly misses the 2035 NDC (CCC, Progress report 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

Sixth and seventh carbon budgets (2033–2037 and 2038–2042)

Target
Sixth: The carbon budget for the 2033-2037 budgetary period is 965,000,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Carbon Budget Order 2021 (opens in a new tab), art. 2). Seventh: The carbon budget for the 2038-2042 budgetary period is 535,000,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (Carbon Budget Order 2026 (opens in a new tab), art. 2, in force 26 June 2026).
Baseline
Annual averages: 193.0 Mt (sixth) and 107.0 Mt (seventh) (Hawkeye’s arithmetic).
Latest
The budget periods have not started. The Climate Change Committee puts 2025 emissions at 407 MtCO2e including the UK’s share of international aviation and shipping (CCC, Progress report 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
Not computed: whether the budgets’ accounting matches the CCC’s 407 Mt basis has not been confirmed (TBC).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Performance against targets: the CBGDP pathway outperforms the Fourth (2023 to 2027) and Fifth (2028 to 2032) Carbon Budgets and narrowly achieves the Sixth Carbon Budget (2033 to 2037) but falls short of the UK’s 2030 Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC). (CCC, Progress report 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

At least 95% low-carbon electricity generation by 2030

Target
We will measure this by being on track to achieving at least 95% of low carbon generation by 2030, in line with advice from the National Energy System Operator. (Plan for Change, CP 1210 (opens in a new tab), 5 December 2024, PDF p.43; published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government).
Baseline
2024: 64.7%, the record (DUKES 2026: 0.4 points above 2025).
Latest
2025: The share of generation coming from low carbon sources (nuclear plus renewables) fell slightly to 64.3 per cent in 2025, 0.4 percentage points lower than the record high share seen in 2024. (DESNZ, DUKES 2026 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
30.7 percentage points below 95%. DUKES covers the UK; the milestone’s geography (Great Britain or the UK) has not been re-checked (TBC).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Defence spending 2.7% of GDP from 2027–28 and 3.5% by 2035

Target
From 27-28 onwards, the UK will spend 2.7% of GDP on core NATO defence spending, solidifying its position as the NATO Alliance’s third-largest cash spender, behind only the US and Germany. Alongside NATO allies, the UK has committed to reach 3.5% of GDP on defence spending by 2035. (Defence Investment Plan funding explainer (opens in a new tab), 30 June 2026).
Baseline
2017: 2.06% of GDP (NATO core defence expenditure; NATO, Defence Investment 2026 (opens in a new tab), Table 3). SIPRI: 1.94%.
Latest
NATO core: 2.28% (2024), 2.32% (2025, estimate), 2.56% (2026, estimate) (NATO (opens in a new tab), Table 3). SIPRI: 2.35% in 2025, US$89.0 billion (SIPRI (opens in a new tab)). NATO and SIPRI use different definitions.
Distance
To 2.7%: 0.38 points from the 2025 estimate and 0.14 points from the 2026 estimate (NATO basis). To 3.5% by 2035: 1.18 points from 2025.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Official development assistance at 0.7% of gross national income

Target
It is the duty of the Secretary of State to ensure that the target for official development assistance (referred to in this Act as “ODA”) to amount to 0.7% of gross national income (in this Act referred to as “the 0.7% target”) is met by the United Kingdom in the year 2015 and each subsequent calendar year. (International Development (ODA Target) Act 2015, s.1(1) (opens in a new tab)). Section 2 requires a statement to Parliament when the target is not met.
Baseline
The UK spent 0.7% of its GNI on ODA between 2013 to 2020. (FCDO (opens in a new tab)).
Latest
2025 (provisional): UK ODA spend was £13,036 million, a decrease of £1,046 million (7.4%) from 2024; the provisional ODA:GNI ratio was 0.43% (the UK spent 0.43% of its GNI on ODA), compared with 0.50% in 2024 (FCDO, provisional UK ODA spend 2025 (opens in a new tab)).
Distance
0.27 percentage points short of 0.7%.
Assessment by the tracking agency
FCDO: In February 2025, the UK government took the decision to reduce ODA spending to 0.3% of GNI by 2027 to fund an increase in defence spending and has stated its commitment to restoring ODA spending at the level of 0.7% of GNI as soon as fiscal circumstances allow. (FCDO (opens in a new tab)).

Halt the decline in species abundance by 2030 (England)

Target
The 2030 species abundance target is that the overall relative species abundance index on the specified date indicates that the decline in the abundance of species has been halted. (Environmental Targets (Biodiversity) (England) Regulations 2023, reg. 11(1) (opens in a new tab)). The date: The specified date for the species abundance target must be 31 December 2030. (Environment Act 2021, s.3(2) (opens in a new tab)).
Baseline
1970 = 100 (Defra all-species index).
Latest
Between 1970 and 2024 the index of change in relative abundance of species in England declined by around 40%; More recently, between 2019 and 2024, the relative abundance index declined by around 11% (Defra, Indicators of species abundance in England (opens in a new tab), 28 April 2026; an official statistic in development).
Distance
No numeric distance: the test is whether the 2030 index is at or above the 2029 index. The latest direction is down.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Defra assesses the all-species indicator as “Deteriorating” over the long, medium and short term (Defra (opens in a new tab)).

92% of patients waiting no longer than 18 weeks (England)

Target
This Parliament, our health milestone is to end hospital backlogs by delivering our ambitious milestone of meeting the NHS standard that 92% of patients should wait no longer than 18 weeks from referral to start consultant-led treatment of non-urgent health conditions. (Plan for Change, CP 1210 (opens in a new tab), PDF p.31; published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government).
Baseline
June 2025: 61.5% (derived from the 4.3-point rise to June 2026 in the NHS England notice).
Latest
June 2026: 7.3 million pathways waiting; In 65.8% of cases the patient had been waiting up to 18 weeks, thus not meeting the 92% standard. (NHS England, RTT press notice (opens in a new tab), 13 August 2026). August 2026 figures not read at source (TBC).
Distance
26.2 percentage points below 92%.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

550,000 fewer children in relative low income after housing costs by the end of this Parliament (modelled)

Target
Taken together, action outlined in this strategy will lift 550,000 children out of relative low income after housing costs by the end of this parliament. (Child Poverty Strategy (opens in a new tab), 5 December 2025; published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government). A modelled reduction against a counterfactual, not a rate target.
Baseline
2010/11: 27.2% of children in relative low income after housing costs (DWP, HBAI (opens in a new tab), table 1.4a).
Latest
Financial year ending 2025: 27.4% (4.03 million children); year ending 2024: 27.7% (4.04 million) (DWP, HBAI FYE 1995–2025 (opens in a new tab), tables 1.4a and 1.4b).
Distance
No distance: the commitment is a modelled reduction, not a level.
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

Two-thirds of young people in higher-level learning by age 25 (England)

Target
We will measure progress against our target for two-thirds (66.7%) of young people to participate in higher-level learning – academic, technical, or apprenticeships – by age 25. This will include a sub-target of at least 10% of young people going into level 4 or 5 study including apprenticeships by 2040. (DfE, Post-16 education and skills white paper (opens in a new tab), 20 October 2025; published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government).
Baseline
38.9% for the 2001/02 cohort (derived from the DfE’s 12.0-point increase).
Latest
50.9% for the latest cohort (15-year-olds in 2013/14); levels 4 and 5: 5.7% (DfE, Participation measures in higher education (opens in a new tab), 29 January 2026).
Distance
15.8 percentage points below 66.7%; levels 4–5 are 4.3 points below 10% (target year 2040).
Assessment by the tracking agency
Not confirmed at source for this page (TBC).

How this GAP analysis works

For a country, the targets are those set in its own laws and official commitments, plus the global Sustainable Development Goal targets that apply to it.

Each row quotes the target word for word and gives the latest official value with its year. A distance is worked out only where the target and the latest value measure the same thing. An “on track” or “off track” label appears only where the agency that tracks the target publishes one. Hawkeye does not assign its own.
