Political 4 points

Governance and cooperation

  • A new Prime Minister in July 2026. Andy Burnham has been Prime Minister since 20 July 2026, (GOV.UK, Andy Burnham’s first speech as Prime Minister, 20 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)). GOV.UK records: Sir Keir Starmer was Prime Minister from 5 July 2024 to 20 July 2026. (GOV.UK, Sir Keir Starmer (opens in a new tab)). GOV.UK labels documents of the previous two years as published under the 2024 to 2026 Starmer Labour government (GOV.UK (opens in a new tab)).
  • A large Labour majority. On 20 October 2026 Labour had 404 of the 650 MPs. UK Parliament states: The Labour party forms a majority government. Their current working majority is 167. This takes into account the non-voting Speaker and their deputies, and non-sitting Sinn Féin MPs. (UK Parliament, State of the parties (opens in a new tab)). Labour won 411 seats with 33.7% of the vote at the general election of 4 July 2024 (UK Parliament, election results (opens in a new tab)).

Conflict and nuclear weapons

  • A permanent seat on the UN Security Council. The Republic of China, France, the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, and the United States of America shall be permanent members of the Security Council. (UN Charter (opens in a new tab), Art. 23).
  • Military spending and nuclear weapons. SIPRI writes: The UK’s military spending amounted to $89.0 billion in 2025, which was 2.0 per cent less than in 2024 but 32 per cent more than in 2016. The UK allocated 2.4 per cent of its GDP to the military in 2025—still slightly short of the objective of 2.5 per cent that the government plans to reach by 2027. (SIPRI Fact Sheet, April 2026 (opens in a new tab), p.9). The Defence Investment Plan (30 June 2026) has since set 2.7% from 2027–28 (see the GAP page). SIPRI counts 225 UK nuclear warheads in January 2026 (SIPRI Yearbook 2026 summary (opens in a new tab)).

Economic 2 points

Economy and finance

  • Public debt at 93.8% of GDP. Public sector net debt was £2,985.5 billion at the end of August 2026: Debt at the end of August 2026 was equivalent to 93.8% of GDP, 1.3 percentage points lower than a year earlier and at levels last seen in the early 1960s. The public sector borrowed £134.3 billion in the financial year ending March 2026, equivalent to 4.4% of GDP. (ONS, Public sector finances, August 2026 (opens in a new tab); provisional).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Public debt, and productivity concentrated in London

Economy and finance

  • A large services surplus. The trade in services surplus widened to 6.4% of GDP (£185.5 billion) in 2024, from 5.8% of GDP (£160.0 billion) in 2023. Financial services made up £74.7 billion of it (£69.8 billion in 2023) (ONS, Pink Book 2025 (opens in a new tab), Table 3.1).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Financial services · SWOT · Opportunities · World demand for UK services, and new trade agreements

Social 2 points

Pandemics and health

  • Hospital waits in England. At the end of June 2026, 7.3 million referral-to-treatment pathways were waiting (about 6.2 million patients). In 65.8% of cases the patient had been waiting up to 18 weeks, thus not meeting the 92% standard. (NHS England, June 2026 data (opens in a new tab)). The standard is in the NHS Constitution; it is not statutory.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Hospital waiting times (England)

Human development

  • Very high human development. HDI 0.946, rank 13 of 193 (data year 2023). Life expectancy at birth is 81.3 years in the HDR (2023) and 81.6 years in the UN’s estimate for 2025 (UNDP, HDR 2025 (opens in a new tab); UN WPP 2024 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Education, human development and a free health service

Technological 2 points

Technology and AI

  • A state AI institute. The AI Security Institute (formerly the AI Safety Institute) is part of the Cabinet Office: The AI Security Institute’s mission is to equip governments with a scientific understanding of the risks posed by advanced AI. We conduct research and develop and test mitigations. (GOV.UK, AI Security Institute (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Opportunities · Inward investment in artificial intelligence

Economy and finance

  • Productivity: London and the rest. In 2023, output per hour worked in London was 28.5% above the UK average and in the South East 7.7% above; All other regions showed output per hour worked levels as below the UK average. Wales was lowest, at 15.1% below (ONS, Regional and subregional labour productivity, 2023 (opens in a new tab)). Productivity is an economic measure; it is listed here as the research placed it.

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Public debt, and productivity concentrated in London

Legal 2 points

Climate change / global warming

  • Net zero by 2050 in law. It is the duty of the Secretary of State to ensure that the net UK carbon account for the year 2050 is at least 100% lower than the 1990 baseline. (Climate Change Act 2008, s.1(1) (opens in a new tab), as amended by SI 2019/1056, in force since 27 June 2019).

Economy and finance

  • The UK–EU trade agreement. The OBR assumes the Trade and Cooperation Agreement, in effect since 1 January 2021, will reduce long-run productivity by 4 per cent relative to remaining in the EU. Both exports and imports will be around 15 per cent lower in the long run than if the UK had remained in the EU. (OBR, Brexit analysis (opens in a new tab); assumptions behind the March 2026 forecast).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Threats · Barriers to trade with the EU

Environmental 2 points

Energy transition

  • Record offshore wind. Offshore wind generation in 2025 was up by 6.6 per cent on 2024 to a new record of 52.0 TWh, despite reduced wind speed. Renewable sources accounted for 52.1 per cent of generation in 2025, and wind 29.5% (DESNZ, DUKES 2026 (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Strengths · Low-carbon power and Europe’s largest offshore wind fleet · SWOT · Opportunities · World investment in clean energy

Biodiversity and ecosystems

  • Wildlife and water. By 2023 the UK priority-species index had fallen to 38% of its 1970 value (JNCC (opens in a new tab)). In England, all monitored species fell by around 40% between 1970 and 2024 (Defra (opens in a new tab)), and 14.3% of waterbodies were at good ecological status in 2025, a figure not comparable with 2019’s 16.3% (Environment Agency (opens in a new tab)).

On the SWOT page SWOT · Weaknesses · Declining wildlife and waterbody health

How this PESTLE analysis works

For a country, PESTLE covers the large forces acting on it in six groups: politics and government, the economy, society and population, technology, laws, and the environment. Official national sources come first.

Each factor has up to five sourced points; where fewer have been confirmed at source, fewer are shown. Points link to the SWOT item that covers the same fact. Spanish quotations are given as published, with unofficial English translations.

  • P

    Political

  • E

    Growth, debt, trade and inequality

  • S

    Social

    Population, health, education and society

  • T

    Technological

    Technology, AI, energy and digital access

  • L

    Legal

    International law and treaties

  • E

    Environmental

    Climate, biodiversity, oceans, land and water

Reporting, not recommending. Hawkeye reports; it never recommends. Goals, aspirations and strategies appear only as stated by official or authoritative bodies, quoted or attributed, with a link. Nothing here is Hawkeye’s own view. Every figure links to its source and gives the year it refers to.
